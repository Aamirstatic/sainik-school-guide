## Most marks are lost quietly

Last year, a parent called us after the AISSEE result with a puzzle: her son had scored well in Maths, done "fine" everywhere else, and still missed the merit list. The answer was in the section scores. He had cleared the aggregate easily — and failed one 50-mark section by two questions. The paper does not forgive a weak section, however strong the rest is.

That is the pattern behind most lost marks. Children rarely lose their exam on questions that are too hard. They lose it on blanks, on one neglected section, on a paper finished in a panic, on revision that never looks back at what went wrong. Seventy days is enough to fix all of it. Here are the seven mistakes, what each one costs, and the fix.

## 1. Leaving questions blank

There is **no negative marking** in AISSEE. A wrong answer scores zero. A blank answer also scores zero. So a blank is never the safe option — it is a guaranteed zero where a guess had a chance. This is the easiest fix on this list: it costs nothing and it is worth doing from the very next mock. The rule for your child is simple — no bubble stays empty. If a question is genuinely unknown, eliminate what is obviously wrong, pick the best of what is left, and move on.

## 2. Ignoring the 25% section floor

The qualifying rule is **25% in every section and 40% in aggregate** — 120 out of 300 for Class 6, 160 out of 400 for Class 9 — and it does not apply to SC/ST candidates. The trap is that parents read mock scores as one number. A child scoring 190 out of 300 looks safe. But if 20 of those marks came from GK out of 50, the paper is failed, whatever the total says. From today, read every mock score section by section. The floor in a 50-mark section is 12.5 marks, so **7 correct answers (14 marks)** clears it — a small, concrete target your child can actually aim at. Our [worked examples of the 25% rule](/blog/aissee-2027-qualifying-marks-25-percent-rule-worked-examples/) show exactly how the arithmetic plays out.

## 3. Maths-only preparation

Maths is half the Class 6 paper and half the Class 9 paper — and it gets far more than half the study time in most homes. The other half is **150 marks in Class 6 and 200 marks in Class 9**, spread across sections that are easier to improve quickly than Maths is. A child who moves GK from 20 to 40 gains the same 20 marks as seven extra Maths questions — and GK takes less effort to lift. Give the non-Maths sections **45 to 60 minutes a day**, every day, not as an afterthought. Our guide to [banking the other 150 marks](/blog/aissee-class-6-beyond-maths-other-150-marks-2026-11-21/) shows how to split that time.

## 4. No full timed mocks

Chapter-wise practice is comfortable. A 150-minute paper is not. Children who have never sat a full paper discover in the exam hall that their concentration fades after an hour, that bubbling answers takes longer than they thought, and that the last section gets rushed. The fix is one full paper every week — **about 10 papers in the 70 days left** — each in one sitting, under real timing, with an OMR sheet and the phone in another room. The score matters less than the pattern: which questions eat time, which section always runs short. Our [OMR and exam-day guide](/blog/aissee-2027-omr-answer-sheet-bubbling-rough-work-exam-day-guide/) covers the bubbling routine that saves the last ten minutes.

## 5. Random guessing

Attempting everything is right; guessing blindly is wasteful. Teach your child the three-step routine: **eliminate** the options that are clearly wrong, **mark** the question and move on to the sure ones, and **return** to the marked ones with whatever time is left. Two eliminated options turn a guess into a real chance, and the mark-and-return habit stops one hard question from eating ten minutes that three easy questions needed.

## 6. GK left for the last week

General Knowledge is the section most often postponed — "we will do it in January" — and January is when everything is being revised at once. GK is 50 marks with its own floor, and it rewards small daily doses: 15 minutes a day on current affairs and static facts, every day, compounds into real knowledge. A last-week cram does not. If your child reads one news summary a day from now, that is 70 days of GK by exam day.

## 7. Revision without error review

This is the mistake that makes the other six permanent. A mock test that is scored and filed away teaches nothing. Keep an **error notebook**: every wrong answer from every mock, written down with the right answer and the reason it went wrong, and re-solved within 48 hours. After three mocks, the notebook shows the real syllabus — the same three or four chapter types, again and again. That is what the remaining weeks should be spent on, not another chapter your child already knows.

## The 70-day fix, week by week

**Weeks 1–2 (23 November–6 December):** Submit the AISSEE form first — it closes 2 December, late window 3–6 December with Rs 500 extra — then start the Sunday mock routine and the error notebook. Identify the weakest section from the first two mocks.

**Weeks 3–6 (7 December–3 January):** The weakest section gets the extra daily hour. GK becomes a daily 15-minute habit. One mock every Sunday, error review every Monday.

**Weeks 7–9 (4–24 January):** Two mocks a week now, with the error notebook driving revision. The exam city is announced on 11 January — check it the day it appears.

**Final week (25–31 January):** Admit card on 25 January — download it, check every detail, print two copies. No new chapters. Light revision, one last full mock early in the week, and rest the day before. The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**.

None of these fixes needs a new book or a new course. They need a decision: the next 70 days are spent fixing what mock tests actually show, not repeating what already works. Start with the error notebook tonight.

## FAQs

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**
No. Wrong answers and blank answers both score zero, so your child should attempt every question in the paper, including the ones they are unsure about. A guess costs nothing and can gain marks.

**What is the minimum score needed in each AISSEE section?**
25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate — 120 out of 300 for Class 6 and 160 out of 400 for Class 9. In a 50-mark section, 25 percent is 12.5 marks, so 7 correct answers (14 marks) clear the floor. The 25 percent and 40 percent conditions do not apply to SC and ST candidates.

**How many full mock tests should my child take before AISSEE 2027?**
At least one full paper every week from now until the exam — about 10 papers in the 70 days left — each in one sitting under real timing, with an OMR sheet. The point is not the score; it is finding which questions eat time and which sections keep failing.

**Should my child guess answers in AISSEE 2027?**
Yes, with a method. Since there is no negative marking, every question should be attempted. Eliminate the obviously wrong options first, mark the question, and come back to it after finishing the sure ones — never leave a bubble empty.

**How much time is left for AISSEE 2027 preparation?**
70 days from 22 November 2026 to the exam on Sunday, 31 January 2027. That is enough to fix one weak section properly, build a mock-test routine, and revise the whole syllabus once — if the days are used on the fixes below instead of on more of the same.

**Which section do most children neglect in AISSEE?**
General Knowledge and the non-Maths sections. In Class 6, Language, Intelligence and GK together are 150 of the 300 marks; in Class 9, the four non-Maths sections are 200 of the 400. Each section also has its own 25 percent floor, so neglecting one can fail the whole paper.
