> **Last verified: 2 October 2026.** The quota rules below are CONFIRMED from the official AISSAC 2025 SOP (Sainik Schools Society) — the governing document for counselling. The 2027 SOP will be released after the AISSEE 2027 result; re-check formats then.

If your family wears the uniform, the Sainik School system holds a real, defined seat for your child — but only if your paperwork survives verification. In the 33 established Sainik Schools, **25% of the balance seats (after SC/ST/OBC allocation) are reserved for wards of serving personnel of the Indian Army, Indian Navy and Indian Air Force, and ex-servicemen** — in both the home-state and other-states quotas (CONFIRMED — AISSAC SOP §13(b)). That is a big number. It is also a number people lose every year to wrong formats and late paperwork. Here is the exact document list, where each paper comes from, and when it gets checked.

## First: are you actually in the DEF category?

The SOP's definition is narrow and worth reading twice: **serving personnel of the Army, Navy and Air Force, plus ex-servicemen**. Two things parents commonly assume, wrongly:

- **New Sainik Schools don't necessarily follow this quota.** The SOP is explicit: there is no SSS reservation policy for the partnership-mode schools — reservation there is the school management's decision (CONFIRMED — SOP §14). Your DEF claim is strongest at the 33 established schools.
- **Paramilitary isn't in the DEF definition.** The physical-reporting checklist asks "Whether Paramilitary ward (Assam Rifles/Coast Guard)" — a separate question, not the DEF quota. How paramilitary wards are treated is something to confirm in the AISSEE 2027 bulletin, not assume.

If you're unsure where your child stands in the overall reservation picture, our [defence quota policy guide](/blog/sainik-school-sc-st-defence-quota-2027/) walks through the full 67/33 domicile matrix with a worked example.

## The document checklist (from the official SOP)

| # | Document | Who needs it | Issued by | Checked at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Certificate of Service** (SOP Appendix H format) | Wards of serving personnel | Competent authority of the parent's unit | Application upload (2026 pattern) + physical reporting |
| 2 | **e-PPO / PPO** (relevant proof from the Pension Payment Order) | Wards of ex-servicemen | Issued through the pension system | Application upload (2026 pattern) + physical reporting |
| 3 | **Date-of-birth proof from the concerned Record Office** | Defence personnel families | The parent's Record Office | Physical reporting verification |
| 4 | Domicile / residence certificate (original) | Everyone | Competent state authority | Physical reporting |
| 5 | Study certificate (Class V / Class VIII) | Everyone | School principal / headmaster | Physical reporting |
| 6 | Photo ID + address proof (student, father, mother/guardian) | Everyone | Government-issued | Physical reporting |

Items 1–3 are the defence-specific ones — CONFIRMED from the AISSAC SOP's Appendix J document checklist and Appendix H. Items 4–6 apply to every candidate but matter just as much; a missing domicile original kills a DEF claim as surely as a wrong service certificate.

## What the serving certificate must contain (Appendix H)

The SOP doesn't just ask for "a certificate" — it prescribes the format. The serving certificate must certify:

- the parent's **service number, rank, and name**;
- that they are the **father/mother of the named candidate**;
- that they are **currently in service in the Indian Army, Indian Navy, or Indian Air Force**;
- signed by the **competent authority with appointment stamp**, plus the **seal of the unit**.

At the AISSEE application stage, the 2026 pattern also required a defence service certificate upload "issued by the commanding officer or competent military authority" that **must show current service status** (Collegedunia, 2026 cycle). So "serving" is the operative word — a certificate that doesn't establish current service is exactly the kind of thing verification officers send back.

For ex-servicemen, the SOP's instruction is short and absolute: **submit relevant proof from the Pension Payment Order — the PPO or e-PPO**. Keep both the original and multiple attested copies; this is the single most common document to be requested, re-requested, and re-verified.

## The timeline: when to start

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| **Now (October 2026)** | Request the serving certificate from the unit / locate the PPO. Records offices have their own pace — this is the step that cannot be rushed in December. |
| **When the 2027 bulletin releases (expected Oct)** | Check whether the DEF definition or formats changed; download any new prescribed proforma. |
| **During the application window** | Upload the defence certificate with the form (2026 pattern). Name spelling must match the birth certificate exactly. |
| **After the result (expected ~Mar 2027)** | Register on the AISSAC portal; keep originals ready for physical reporting. |
| **Physical reporting** | Originals verified. Admission stays **provisional** until this clears. |

Why the urgency now? Because the SOP's parent undertaking is blunt: **admission is provisional subject to successful document verification, and if certificates are found incorrect at any stage, the school reserves the right to cancel the admission automatically** (CONFIRMED — SOP Appendix J). A seat won on merit, lost on paperwork, is the most painful outcome in this whole process.

## Three mistakes defence families make

1. **Assuming the quota applies everywhere.** It is defined for the 33 established schools. For new partnership schools, ask the school — the SOP leaves it to management.
2. **Bringing a service certificate in the wrong format.** A letter from a colleague or an old posting certificate isn't the Appendix H format. Get the unit's competent authority to issue it on the prescribed proforma.
3. **Forgetting the Record Office birth proof.** Civil birth certificate in hand, defence families skip the Record Office one — but the SOP checklist lists it separately for defence personnel, so the verifier will too.

**Who qualifies for the defence quota in Sainik Schools?**

Wards of serving personnel of the Indian Army, Navy and Air Force, and ex-servicemen — per the official AISSAC SOP. In the 33 established schools, 25% of the balance seats (after SC/ST/OBC allocation) are reserved for this category.

**What is the serving certificate and who issues it?**

The AISSAC SOP's Appendix H format certifies the parent's service number, rank, name and current service in the Army, Navy or Air Force. It must be signed by the competent authority with the unit seal.

**What document do ex-servicemen submit?**

Relevant proof from the Pension Payment Order — the PPO or e-PPO — as stated in the AISSAC SOP.

**Do I also need a birth certificate from the Record Office?**

The AISSAC document checklist specifically lists date-of-birth proof issued by the concerned Record Office for defence personnel, in addition to the civil birth certificate.

**Do paramilitary wards get the defence quota?**

The SOP defines the DEF quota for serving Army, Navy and Air Force personnel and ex-servicemen. The reporting checklist asks about paramilitary wards separately — confirm their treatment in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin.

**What happens if my defence certificate has an error?**

Admission is provisional until documents are verified at physical reporting. If certificates are found incorrect at any stage, the school can cancel the admission automatically — so get the format right the first time.
