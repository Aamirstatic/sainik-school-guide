Here's something most parents discover embarrassingly late: the AISSEE Class 6 paper has **no Science section at all**. Open the paper and you'll find four sections — Mathematics, Language, Intelligence, General Knowledge. So where does science live? Inside GK, quietly, in about 4 to 5 questions per paper.

And here's why those 4–5 questions punch above their weight. A child needs at least 25% in *each* section to qualify (40% aggregate overall, per the AISSEE rules). For GK, 25% of 50 marks is 12.5 — which, with 2-mark questions, effectively means getting 7 questions right. A student who has prepared history and geography but blanks the science questions is suddenly fighting to cross that sectional cutoff. I've seen children lose a Sainik School seat by 2 marks sitting entirely inside the GK section. Don't let that be your child.

## Where the science questions actually come from

NTA doesn't publish a fixed GK syllabus, so this is reconstructed from previous papers (2018–2026) and consistent education-portal analyses:

- **Base text:** NCERT Class 5 EVS (*Looking Around*) — most science-flavoured GK questions trace back to its chapters.
- **The gap EVS doesn't fill:** basic physics — light, shadows, sound, force. EVS touches these lightly; a Class 6-level general science reading (any school textbook chapter on light and sound) closes it.
- **The split that repeats:** human body systems and plants/animals first, basic physics second, environment third. Chemistry barely appears at this level.

Notice the pattern? NTA loves the **body, the plant, the sky, and water**. Everything a 10-year-old can observe. Abstract definitions almost never make it into the paper; observation-based questions do. When you revise, ask your child "what happens if…" questions, not "define…" ones.

## The 3-week coverage plan

Honestly, this whole topic needs about 30 minutes a day for three weeks — not a separate "science subject" timetable.

- **Week 1 — the High cluster:** Read the 5 High-priority chapters end-to-end from the EVS book (not a guidebook — the actual NCERT lines). After each chapter, your child explains the concept aloud in 2 minutes. If they can't explain digestion without reading, they don't know it.
- **Week 2 — Medium + physics gap:** Finish the Medium chapters, then read one Class 6-level chapter each on light/shadow and sound. Draw every diagram: the eye, the ear, the water cycle, the solar system. AISSEE science questions are diagram-friendly — children who have drawn it, answer it.
- **Week 3 — question attack:** Solve only the science-flavoured questions from 3–4 previous AISSEE GK sections. Wrong answers become one-line facts on a revision sheet (e.g. "Malaria — female Anopheles mosquito — stagnant water"). Revise the sheet, not the chapters.

One worked example, so you see the flavour. A previous paper asked roughly: *which gas do plants absorb from air for photosynthesis?* A child who read *Seeds and Seeds* and *Blow Hot, Blow Cold* answers "carbon dioxide" in three seconds. A child who memorised definitions from a guidebook hesitates. Observation beats memorisation — that's the whole secret of this section.

## FAQs

**Is there a separate Science section in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
No. The Class 6 paper has Mathematics (50 questions, 150 marks), Language (25/50), Intelligence (25/50) and General Knowledge (25/50). Science questions — about 4 to 5 per paper — sit inside the GK section.

**Which NCERT book covers AISSEE Class 6 science questions?**
NCERT Class 5 EVS (*Looking Around*) is the base text. Add basic physics reading (light, shadow, sound, force) at Class 6 level, since EVS covers those topics only lightly.

**How many science questions come in AISSEE Class 6 GK?**
About 4 to 5 of the 25 GK questions, worth 8 to 10 marks. Small — but they often decide the 25% sectional cutoff in GK, so they matter more than their marks suggest.

**Which science chapters should I finish first for AISSEE Class 6?**
Start with digestion (From Tasting to Digesting), plants and seeds (Seeds and Seeds), water (Every Drop Counts, Experiments with Water) and the solar system (Sunita in Space). Then breathing (Blow Hot, Blow Cold). That covers the bulk of what repeats.

**Does AISSEE Class 6 science ask chemistry questions?**
Almost never. The Class 6 science questions concentrate on biology (human body, plants, animals) and basic physics. Chemistry-type questions are rare at this level — save serious chemistry for the Class 9 paper.
