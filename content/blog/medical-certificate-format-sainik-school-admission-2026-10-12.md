## The short answer first

One of the most common questions in our inbox: *"Where do we get the medical certificate for Sainik School admission — and in what format?"*

Here is the honest answer: **there is no medical certificate for you to arrange in advance, and no official pre-admission format for parents to download and fill.** The fitness certificate is issued by the **school's own medical board** after it examines your child — usually at the school itself or a designated hospital — following seat allotment in AISSAC counselling. Your job is not to produce a certificate. Your job is to show up with a child who is healthy, prepared, and with a complete health file.

That said, the question behind the question is a good one, and this guide answers it fully: when the medical happens, who issues what, what the board actually checks, and what you should be doing *now* — while the exam (31 January 2027, per the NTA calendar) is still over three months away.

## Where the medical fits in the admission sequence

Understanding the sequence removes most of the anxiety. For AISSAC 2026, the order ran roughly like this — and the 2027 cycle is EXPECTED to follow the same shape:

| Step | When | What happens |
|------|------|--------------|
| 1 | 31 Jan 2027 | AISSEE written exam (CONFIRMED tentative per NTA calendar) |
| 2 | Feb–Mar 2027 | Result, provisional answer key process |
| 3 | Apr–May 2027 | AISSAC e-counselling and seat allotment |
| 4 | May–Jun 2027 | Physical document verification at the allotted school |
| 5 | May–Jun 2027 | **Medical examination by the school's medical board** |
| 6 | Jun 2027 | **Fitness certificate issued by the board** → admission confirmed |

Two things stand out. First, the medical is the *final* gate — your child's seat is provisional until the board declares them fit. Second, the "certificate" that matters is generated at step 6 by the board itself. See our [counselling-day document checklist](/blog/aissee-counselling-day-document-checklist-2026-10-12/) for the full document list that travels with your child to the school.

## So who issues the medical certificate?

The school's medical board — typically comprising the school's medical officer and visiting specialists — conducts the examination and issues the fitness certificate on its own forms. After allotment, schools send parents their **own medical forms** (in 2026 these arrived as a multi-page set to be filled and carried in original), which the board completes during the examination.

This means:

- **You do not need a certificate from your local doctor or civil surgeon** for the admission itself. Don't spend money getting one "just in case" — the school won't ask for it.
- **You do need your child's existing health records.** Glasses prescriptions, vaccination cards, records of any ongoing treatment, allergy notes — these travel in your health file and inform the board's assessment.
- **If your child is under a specialist's care** (asthma, orthopaedic follow-up, anything ongoing), get a written summary from that specialist *before* the medical. A specialist's opinion carries real weight when the board is assessing an edge case — this is confirmed practical wisdom from the AISSAC process, not a trick.

## What the medical board actually checks

The examination is a general fitness assessment for residential school life and physical training. Based on the standard process:

| Area | What's checked |
|------|---------------|
| General health | Height, weight, age-appropriate physical development |
| Vision | Eyesight, colour vision — glasses are fine, declare them |
| Hearing | Basic hearing assessment |
| Dental | General dental health — a pre-checkup is cheap insurance |
| Skin | Visible skin conditions |
| Chest & heart | Chest measurement, heart and lung check |
| Limbs & posture | Flat foot, knock knees, spinal alignment, joint mobility |
| General | Any condition affecting residential school life or physical training |

Now the honesty clause we repeat in every medical article: many websites publish exact numbers — "vision must be 6/6," "minimum height X cm" — as universal rules. **They are not.** Boards exercise judgment, standards can vary between schools, and borderline cases are assessed individually. The board is the only authority. Anyone quoting precise cutoffs as official is guessing; don't build your child's health plan on guesses. Our [medical test guide](/blog/sainik-school-medical-test-2027/) goes deeper into each area.

## The five fixable issues to handle now

Most medical complications aren't dramatic — they're fixable things nobody addressed in time. With the medical months away (it follows the April–May 2027 counselling), you have a golden window:

1. **Vision.** The single most common finding. Get your child's eyes tested *now* — not in January, and certainly not the week before the medical. If glasses are prescribed, get them early so the child is comfortable wearing them.
2. **Weight.** Significantly underweight or overweight is one of the more common board comments. Regular meals and daily outdoor play over several months fix most cases — see our [physical fitness guide](/blog/sainik-school-physical-test-guide/).
3. **Dental.** A checkup and cleaning before the medical is cheap insurance, and many parents simply never think of it.
4. **Skin conditions.** Treatable ones should be treated early. Don't wait for the medical to discover them.
5. **Posture and feet.** Flat foot, knock knees, mild spinal issues — a paediatric orthopaedic consultation *now* tells you whether it's nothing or something that needs attention. Many mild cases are perfectly acceptable to boards; only the board decides, but going in informed beats going in scared.

**The one rule above all: disclose everything.** Hiding a condition is far worse than having one. Non-disclosure discovered later can jeopardise admission — the board needs the information to care for your child.

## Build your health file this month: a checklist

You can't prepare the certificate — but you can prepare everything around it. Do this in October–November, calmly:

- [ ] Full eye test done; glasses procured if prescribed
- [ ] Dental checkup and cleaning completed
- [ ] General paediatric checkup — height, weight, overall development
- [ ] Vaccination record located and up to date
- [ ] Specialist summaries collected for any ongoing condition (asthma, allergies, orthopaedic)
- [ ] Glasses prescription (if any) in the file
- [ ] Three recent passport photographs kept with the health folder
- [ ] Daily 30-minute outdoor activity started

## FAQs

**Do I need a medical certificate before filling the AISSEE 2027 form?**

No. No medical certificate is required at the application stage. The medical examination happens after seat allotment, conducted by the school's own medical board.

**Who issues the medical fitness certificate for Sainik School admission?**

The school's medical board issues it after examining your child at the school or a designated hospital. Admission is confirmed only after the board declares the candidate medically fit.

**Is there an official medical certificate format I must download?**

There is no universal pre-admission format for parents to fill. After allotment, schools send their own medical forms, which you complete and carry to the medical examination.

**Are children with glasses rejected in the medical test?**

Correctable vision with glasses is generally acceptable. Get the prescription early and declare it honestly at the medical examination.

**What if my child has a treatable condition like dental issues or mild skin problems?**

Most such issues are fixable and should be addressed months before the medical. Boards assess whether the child can safely handle residential school life and physical training; disclose everything honestly.

**Can we appeal if the medical board declares the child unfit?**

Schools typically have a review process. Ask the school office immediately and in writing — timelines are tight, so act fast.
