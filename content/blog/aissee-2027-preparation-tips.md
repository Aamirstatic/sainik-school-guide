The All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE) 2027 is one of India's most competitive entrance examinations for school students. Conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA), AISSEE determines admission to Class 6 and Class 9 at 33+ traditional Sainik Schools and 69 new PPP-mode schools across India. With approximately 4 lakh students competing for approximately 10,000 seats, the competition is intense — a selection rate of roughly 2.5%.

Success in AISSEE requires more than intelligence. It demands a disciplined, structured preparation strategy that covers the entire syllabus, builds exam-taking skills, and develops the mental resilience needed to perform under pressure. This comprehensive guide provides a complete 6-month preparation strategy, subject-wise tips, a daily routine, mock test planning, and the [best books for AISSEE](/blog/best-books-aissee-2027/) 2027.

## 6-Month AISSEE 2027 Study Plan

A structured 6-month plan ensures complete syllabus coverage with adequate time for practice and revision. Starting from July 2026 for the January 2027 exam:

### Month 1 (July 2026): Foundation Building

**Objective:** Complete the entire NCERT syllabus for the relevant class

| Subject | Activity | Daily Time |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Mathematics | Read NCERT textbook chapter by chapter; solve all examples and exercises | 1.5 hours |
| English | Read NCERT stories and poems; practice grammar basics | 1 hour |
| GK / Science / Social Studies | Read NCERT EVS (Class 6) or Science and Social Studies (Class 9) | 45 minutes |
| Intelligence | Begin with basic reasoning patterns from R.S. Aggarwal | 30 minutes |

**Daily Target:** 3.5 to 4 hours of focused study

**Month 1 Milestones:**
- Complete 50% of NCERT Mathematics
- Complete 30% of NCERT English
- Begin GK/Science/Social Studies reading
- Solve 10 reasoning questions daily

### Month 2 (August 2026): Foundation Completion

**Objective:** Complete the remaining NCERT syllabus

| Subject | Activity | Daily Time |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Mathematics | Complete remaining NCERT chapters; solve all exercises | 1.5 hours |
| English | Complete NCERT reading; begin vocabulary building (10 words daily) | 1 hour |
| GK / Science / Social Studies | Complete NCERT reading; begin making notes | 45 minutes |
| Intelligence | Increase to 15 to 20 reasoning questions daily | 30 minutes |

**Daily Target:** 4 hours of focused study

**Month 2 Milestones:**
- Complete 100% of NCERT Mathematics
- Complete 100% of NCERT English
- Complete 100% of NCERT GK/Science/Social Studies
- Build vocabulary of 300+ new words

### Month 3 (September 2026): Deep Practice

**Objective:** Topic-wise practice from reference books; begin sectional tests

| Subject | Activity | Daily Time |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Mathematics | Topic-wise practice from Arihant guide; solve 30 to 40 problems daily | 1.5 hours |

| GK / Science / Social Studies | Make topic-wise notes; read current affairs monthly | 45 minutes |
| Intelligence | Topic-wise reasoning practice; focus on weak areas | 30 minutes |

**Daily Target:** 4 to 4.5 hours on weekdays; 5 to 6 hours on weekends

**Month 3 Milestones:**
- Complete topic-wise Mathematics practice for 50% of topics
- Solve 5 sectional tests
- Build vocabulary of 500+ words
- Begin current affairs notes

### Month 4 (October 2026): Intensive Practice

**Objective:** Complete topic-wise practice; begin full-length mock tests

- Solve 4 full-length mock tests

## Subject-Wise AISSEE Preparation Tips

### Mathematics Preparation Strategy

Mathematics carries 50% weightage in both Class 6 and Class 9 AISSEE. It is the single most important subject.

**Key Topics for Class 6:**

| Topic | Weightage | Priority |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Number System (HCF, LCM, Fractions, Decimals) | 35–40% | Highest |
| Geometry (Lines, Angles, Triangles, Circles) | 20–25% | High |
| Mensuration (Area, Perimeter) | 15–20% | High |
| Arithmetic (Ratio, Percentage, Profit/Loss) | 10–15% | Medium |
| Algebra (Introduction) | 5–10% | Medium |

**Key Topics for Class 9:**

| Topic | Weightage | Priority |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Algebra (Polynomials, Linear Equations) | 25–30% | Highest |
| Geometry (Triangles, Circles, Quadrilaterals) | 25–30% | Highest |
| Number System (Rational, Irrational) | 15–20% | High |
| Mensuration (Surface Area, Volume) | 10–15% | Medium |
| Statistics and Probability | 5–10% | Medium |

**Pro Tips for Mathematics:**
1. **Master NCERT first:** Every example and exercise must be solved
2. **Create a formula sheet:** Write all formulas on one page; revise daily
3. **Practice mental math:** Speed matters in competitive exams
4. **Time yourself:** Practice solving questions within time limits from day one
5. **Learn shortcuts:** Basic Vedic mathematics techniques save valuable time
6. **Solve previous year papers:** Understand the question format and difficulty level

### English Language Preparation

English tests grammar, vocabulary, and reading comprehension.

**Focus Areas:**

| Area | Topics | Practice Method |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Reading Comprehension | Unseen passages, inference questions | One passage daily |
| Grammar | Tenses, articles, subject-verb agreement, prepositions | Grammar exercises from reference book |
| Vocabulary | Synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutions | 10 new words daily |
| Sentence Correction | Error identification, sentence improvement | Practice sets from Arihant guide |
| Writing | Paragraph writing, letter writing (Class 9) | Weekly writing practice |

**Daily English Practice Routine:**
- Read one newspaper article and summarize in 3 to 5 sentences
- Learn 10 new words with their usage in sentences
- Practice one comprehension passage
- Solve 10 grammar questions

**For Class 6 (General Knowledge):**

| Topic | Focus Areas |

| Geography | States, capitals, rivers, mountains, neighbours |

**For Class 9 (General Science + Social Studies):**

**Preparation Tips:**
- Make topic-wise notes for quick revision

### Intelligence / Mental Ability Preparation

The Intelligence section tests logical reasoning and is one of the most scoring sections with proper practice.

**Topics and Frequency:**

| Topic | Frequency in AISSEE | Difficulty |
|-------|-------------------|-----------|
| Analogies (Word and Number) | High | Easy to Medium |
| Classification (Odd One Out) | High | Easy |
| Series Completion | High | Medium |
| Coding-Decoding | Medium | Medium |
| Blood Relations | Medium | Medium |
| Direction Sense | Low to Medium | Easy |

## Mock Test Strategy

Mock tests are arguably the most important component of AISSEE preparation. They build exam temperament, improve time management, and identify weak areas.

### Mock Test Schedule

| Period | Mock Test Type | Frequency |
|--------|---------------|-----------|
| Month 1–2 | Topic-wise tests (after completing each chapter) | 2 to 3 per week |
| Month 3–4 | Sectional tests (one subject at a time) | 1 per week |
| Month 4–5 | Full-length mock tests | 1 per week |
| Month 5–6 | Full-length mock tests under exam conditions | 2 to 3 per week |

### How to Analyze Mock Tests

After every mock test, spend equal time analyzing the results:

1. **Calculate the score** and compare with previous attempts
2. **Categorize wrong answers:**
   - Silly mistakes (careless errors — fixable with attention)
   - Conceptual gaps (did not know the concept — requires study)
   - Time pressure errors (knew the answer but ran out of time — requires speed practice)
3. **Track progress** in a notebook or spreadsheet
4. **Re-attempt wrong questions** within 24 hours without looking at solutions
5. **Revise related concepts** immediately

|----------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| Month 3 | 180–200 (60–67%) | 240–280 (60–70%) |
| Month 4 | 200–220 (67–73%) | 280–320 (70–80%) |
| Month 5 | 220–250 (73–83%) | 300–350 (75–88%) |
| Month 6 | 250+ (83%+) | 340+ (85%+) |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Why It Is Harmful | Solution |
|---------|-------------------|----------|
| Ignoring NCERT textbooks | 70–80% of AISSEE questions are based on NCERT content | Complete NCERT thoroughly before using reference books |
| Studying without a plan | Random studying leads to gaps in preparation | Follow the structured 6-month plan |
| Not practicing previous year papers | Misses understanding of actual exam difficulty and format | Solve at least 5 previous year papers |
| Neglecting time management | Students who know answers but run out of time score poorly | Practice under timed conditions from the start |
| Skipping revision | Without regular revision, learned material is forgotten | Schedule weekly revision sessions |
| Over-relying on coaching | Self-study with good books is equally effective and more flexible | Use coaching as supplement, not replacement |
| Ignoring health and sleep | Poor nutrition and sleep reduce concentration | Ensure 8 to 9 hours sleep, balanced diet, and daily exercise |
| Last-minute cramming | Adds stress without adding knowledge; causes confusion | Complete all new topics at least 2 weeks before the exam |

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the best time to start AISSEE 2027 preparation?

The ideal time to start preparation is 6 to 8 months before the exam. For the January 2027 exam, starting from June or July 2026 provides ample time to complete the syllabus, practice thoroughly, and revise effectively. However, even 3 to 4 months of focused preparation can yield good results if the student follows a structured study plan and dedicates sufficient daily hours.

### Is coaching necessary for AISSEE preparation?

Coaching is not mandatory. Many students clear AISSEE through self-study using NCERT textbooks, standard reference books, and previous year papers. However, coaching can benefit students who need structured guidance, doubt-clearing support, or a competitive peer environment. Online coaching platforms have made quality preparation accessible from home. The decision should be based on the student's learning style and self-discipline level.

### How many hours should I study daily for AISSEE?

For school-going students, 4 to 5 hours of focused daily study on weekdays and 6 to 8 hours on weekends is sufficient for AISSEE preparation. Quality matters more than quantity — a focused 3-hour session is more productive than 6 hours of distracted studying. Take regular breaks (5 to 10 minutes every 45 to 50 minutes) to maintain concentration.

### What is a good score in AISSEE to get admission?

The cutoff score varies by category, school, and year. Generally, scoring 75 to 80% of total marks puts a candidate in a safe zone for most Sainik Schools. For Class 6 (out of 300), aim for 230+ marks. For Class 9 (out of 400), aim for 300+ marks. Top schools may have higher cutoffs. The absence of negative marking means students should attempt all questions.

### Can I prepare for AISSEE without NCERT books?

While technically possible, it is strongly recommended to use NCERT textbooks as the primary study material. AISSEE questions are largely based on the NCERT syllabus, and many questions are directly derived from NCERT content. Use NCERT as the foundation and supplement with specialized AISSEE preparation books for practice questions and exam-specific strategies.

### Is there negative marking in AISSEE?

No. There is no negative marking in AISSEE. Each correct answer earns full marks, and incorrect or unanswered questions do not result in any deduction. This means students should attempt all questions, even when uncertain. Educated guessing — eliminating obviously wrong options and selecting from the remaining — is a valid strategy.
