## The question every parent asks — and why the usual answers fail

"Kitne marks lane honge?" It is the most-asked question in every AISSEE parent group, and it gets the worst answers: a WhatsApp forward quoting last year's closing of some school, a coaching institute's "expected cutoff" banner, a YouTube thumbnail promising the 2027 cutoff in advance. None of these help, because a cutoff is not a property of the exam — it is a property of *one school, one category, one year's applicant pool*. The number that got a boy into one school's general list last year tells you almost nothing about what gets a girl into another school's home-state list this year.

There is, however, a legitimate way to use past data. It does not produce a magic number. It produces a **target range with a method behind it** — and that is far more useful for planning study time.

## Step 1: Know the two bands that actually exist

Forget "the cutoff". There are two separate bands:

1. **The qualifying floor.** Per the AISSEE 2026 bulletin (the 2027 bulletin is not out yet; this pattern is expected to repeat): **25% in each section plus 40% in aggregate**. SC/ST candidates are exempt from these minimums. This floor is fixed by the rules — the only part of the system you can count on before the exam.
2. **The school-wise closing merit.** Published after the exam in NTA's final merit lists and AISSAC's counselling allotment rounds. This moves every year with applicant numbers, vacancy counts, and reservation splits (67% home-state / 33% other-states, with SC/ST/OBC-NCL and defence-ward quotas layered on).

The floor is the same for every school. The closing merit is different for every school-category combination. Anyone who quotes a single "AISSEE cutoff" without naming the school, class, and category is skipping the part that matters.

## Step 2: Translate the floor into real marks

This is the arithmetic most parents never do. Here is the Class 6 and Class 9 paper structure from the 2026 pattern (CONFIRMED, expected to repeat), with the qualifying floor converted into raw marks:

| Section | Class 6 (300 marks) | Section floor (25%) | Class 9 (400 marks) | Section floor (25%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 150 (50 questions × 3) | 37.5 | 200 (50 questions × 4) | 50 |
| Language/English | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 |
| Intelligence | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 |
| General Knowledge / Science+GK | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 |
| Social Studies | — | — | 50 (25 × 2) | 12.5 |
| **Aggregate** | **300** | **120 (40%)** | **400** | **160 (40%)** |

Read this table carefully, because it contains the most underrated fact in AISSEE prep: **Maths is half the paper** — 150 of 300 marks in Class 6, 200 of 400 in Class 9. There is no other section with comparable leverage. A child who raises Maths accuracy by 10 percentage points gains 15 marks in Class 6 (or 20 in Class 9) — more than the *entire* section floor of any other subject. This is why a target score must be built **section by section**, not as one big number.

## Step 3: Build your target from official data, not rumours

Here is the method — four actions, in order:

1. **Pull the official closing data.** Download the NTA's final merit list PDFs and the AISSAC counselling allotment lists from the official portals (`exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society/` and `pesa.ncog.gov.in`). Find your child's category and shortlist schools — not one school, three or four across preference rounds. Note the *closing* position, not the opening one. ✅ Official sources only.
2. **Convert positions into section-aware targets.** You do not know the exact closing marks for next year, so work in bands: set your target at **the qualifying floor plus a study buffer**, with the buffer weighted toward Maths. A practical buffer many counsellors use is 25–35% above the aggregate floor, distributed by section weight — but treat this as a planning heuristic, not a prediction of any school's cutoff. 🔶 Expected approach — the buffer is a judgement call, not an official figure.
3. **Check section floors first, aggregate second.** The single most common way capable children miss the merit list is not a low total — it is failing the 25% floor in *one* section while scoring well overall. When you review mock tests, flag the weakest section's floor before you celebrate the total.
4. **Reality-check against mocks, monthly.** A target is only useful if it changes behaviour. If your child consistently scores below the Maths floor in mocks, that section — not "more mock tests" — is where the next month of study time goes.

## The five mistakes that waste a year

- **Treating last year's closing as this year's cutoff.** Applicant numbers and vacancies change; a 2026 closing is a reference point, not a forecast.
- **Quoting cutoffs without school + category.** Cutoffs differ by school, class, category, and home-state vs other-state quota — a bare number is meaningless.
- **Aggregate-only thinking.** Clearing 40% aggregate while missing the 25% section floor in one subject means disqualification from the merit list.
- **Ignoring the no-negative-marking rule.** With no negative marking (CONFIRMED), every unattempted question is wasted — attempt-all is free, and the target should assume it.
- **Building targets on rumoured numbers.** If a number does not trace to an NTA or AISSAC document, it is entertainment, not data.

## Your target-score worksheet

Run this checklist once a month with your child's latest mock results:

- [ ] Write down the section floors from the table above for your child's class.
- [ ] Mark which sections currently sit *below* their floor in mocks — these are priority zero.
- [ ] Set a personal target per section: floor + buffer, with Maths carrying the largest buffer.
- [ ] Convert section targets into **question counts** (e.g., Class 6 Maths: how many of 50 questions must be correct?) — counts are what a child can feel during a test.
- [ ] Allocate weekly study time in proportion to section weight, not in proportion to comfort.
- [ ] Re-run this worksheet after every full mock; retire the target only when it is met three mocks in a row.

## FAQs

**What are the qualifying marks for AISSEE 2027?**
Per the AISSEE 2026 information bulletin, the expected pattern for 2027 is 25 percent marks in each section plus 40 percent in aggregate. That works out to 120 out of 300 for Class 6 and 160 out of 400 for Class 9, on top of the per-section floor.

**Is meeting the qualifying marks enough to get into a Sainik School?**
No. Qualifying marks only make your child eligible for the merit list. Actual admission depends on the school-wise merit position, which shifts every year with applicant numbers and vacancies, so your target score should sit comfortably above the floor.

**Which section matters most for the AISSEE target score?**
Mathematics. It carries 150 of 300 marks in the Class 6 paper and 200 of 400 marks in the Class 9 paper — half the total in both. A section-wise target built around Maths strength is the single most efficient way to raise a mock score.

**Where can I find official AISSEE cutoff or closing marks?**
The official sources are the NTA AISSEE portal and the AISSAC counselling portal, which publish merit and allotment lists. Treat any cutoff number from WhatsApp forwards, coaching ads, or YouTube thumbnails as unverified until it traces back to one of those.

**Does AISSEE have negative marking?**
No. Per the AISSEE 2026 pattern, expected to repeat in 2027, there is no negative marking — wrong answers and blanks both score zero, so attempting every question is always the rational strategy.
