## Everyone talks about Maths. English is the paper that ends campaigns.

If your child is sitting RIMCEE on **Sunday, 6 December 2026**, you have probably heard one number a hundred times: Maths carries **200 of the 400 written marks**. Half the paper. So the whole house drills Maths — and honestly, that is correct. Maths deserves the most hours.

But here is the number nobody repeats at the dinner table: **62.5**. That is the minimum your child must score in English — 50% of 125 — to stay in the race at all. RIMCEE does not work on total marks alone. A child must clear **50% in each paper separately** to be called for the Viva Voce. A boy can score 190 out of 200 in Maths and still go home if his English is 55.

And English is the paper most Class 7 children are least prepared for — not because they do not know English, but because it is the only fully descriptive paper. Two hours of writing by hand: a composition, a letter, a picture description, comprehension answers in full sentences, grammar. There is no option to tick. No lucky guess. The examiner reads what your child actually wrote.

Sixteen days is not enough to learn English. It is exactly enough to learn to *write* an exam in English. That is what this plan does.

## The paper at a glance

| Paper | Questions | Marks | Duration | Must score at least |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English (descriptive) | 45 | 125 | 2 hours | 62.5 (50%) |
| General Knowledge (MCQ) | 75 | 75 | 1 hour | 37.5 (50%) |
| Mathematics (descriptive) | 30 | 200 | 1 hour 30 minutes | 100 (50%) |
| **Written total** | **150** | **400** | **4 hours 30 minutes** | — |
| Viva Voce (interview) | — | 50 | — | 25 (50%) |

Two things jump out. First, English gets the longest sitting — two full hours, more than Maths — because writing takes time. Second, the qualifying line is per paper, not overall. English is not the paper to "manage somehow". It is the paper to clear with margin.

## What the English paper actually asks for

The 45 questions fall into five familiar blocks: a composition or essay, a letter, picture writing, reading comprehension, and grammar. None of this is exotic. It is Class 6 and 7 CBSE/NCERT level. What makes it hard is that every answer is written out — spelling, punctuation, sentence formation and handwriting all count, because a tired examiner cannot award marks to an answer they cannot read.

Last year, I watched a bright boy lose his RIMC chance on exactly this. His Maths was strong, his GK was fine, and his English composition was a good essay — written so slowly that he never reached the last two grammar questions. The paper does not reward the best essay. It rewards the finished paper.

## The 16-day plan: 20 November to 5 December

Sixteen days, about 45 minutes of English a day, on top of Maths and GK. The rule for all sixteen days: **write by hand, with a timer**. Reading about writing does not count.

**Days 1–4 (20–23 Nov): Grammar first, because it is the cheapest marks.** One topic a day — tenses, articles and prepositions, subject-verb agreement, then direct-indirect speech and active-passive voice. Twenty questions a day from the Class 7 grammar book, corrected the same evening. Grammar questions are short, and every corrected mistake is a mark that stays corrected.

**Days 5–8 (24–27 Nov): Writing, one essay and one letter a day.** Alternate the essay topics: "My Favourite Teacher", "A Visit to a Historical Place", "The Importance of Discipline", "My Aim in Life". Letters alternate too: formal (to the Principal, to the Editor) and informal (to a friend, to a grandfather). Time each piece — 25 minutes for the essay, 15 for the letter — and count the words afterwards. The target is simple: finish inside the time, in readable handwriting, with a clear beginning, middle and end.

**Days 9–12 (28 Nov–1 Dec): Comprehension and picture writing.** One unseen passage a day, answered in full sentences, not single words. One picture description a day — describe what is happening, not just what is visible. This is also when the RIMCEE admit card is expected (29 November to 1 December), so download it the evening it appears and check the name, photograph, centre and reporting time — [our city-slip guide]({{< relref "rimcee-2026-city-slip-out-check-exam-city-2026-11-19.md" >}}) explains the difference between the slip and the admit card.

**Days 13–15 (2–4 Dec): Two full English mocks, in two hours, on paper.** Sit at a table, no phone, 45 questions, 2 hours, pen and paper. Mark strictly: would a stranger understand this sentence? The first mock will feel slow. The second will feel possible. That gap is the whole point of the plan.

**Day 16 (5 Dec): Rest the hand.** Read one good essay aloud, pack the bag — admit card, Aadhaar, two blue or black ballpoint pens — and stop studying by the evening. A tired hand writes slowly on exam day.

## The five mistakes that cost English marks

| Mistake | What it costs | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starting with the essay and running out of time | The last 10 questions go blank | Do grammar and comprehension first, essay last |
| Writing the essay without a 2-minute plan | Rambling, no ending | Three lines of plan: beginning, two points, ending |
| One-word answers in comprehension | Half marks, even when correct | Full sentences, always |
| Ignoring handwriting | Marks the examiner cannot give | Slow down 10% — legible beats fast |
| Leaving questions blank | Free marks lost — there is no negative marking | Attempt everything, even imperfectly |

The last row matters most. There is **no negative marking** in RIMCEE. A guessed grammar answer and a half-finished letter both beat a blank. Teach your child that a blank is the only certain zero on the paper.

## What happens after 6 December

The written result is expected around February 2027 (🔶 EXPECTED — the exact date has not been announced), and boys who clear 50% in each paper are called for the **Viva Voce, worth 50 marks**, tentatively in April 2027. The viva tests the same thing the English paper tests, out loud: can this boy express himself clearly? The sixteen days you spend on writing now are also the first sixteen days of viva preparation — the same sentences, spoken instead of written.

## FAQs

**What is the RIMCEE 2026 English paper pattern?**

English is a descriptive paper of 125 marks with 45 questions in 2 hours. It covers composition and essay writing, letter writing, picture writing, reading comprehension and grammar. It is the longest paper of the three by time.

**How many marks does my child need in English to qualify for the RIMCEE Viva Voce?**

At least 50 percent in each paper separately — 62.5 out of 125 in English, 100 out of 200 in Maths and 37.5 out of 75 in General Knowledge. Scoring high in Maths does not compensate for failing English.

**Is there negative marking in RIMCEE 2026?**

No. There is no negative marking in the RIMCEE written examination, so every question should be attempted — including every English question, even if the answer is imperfect.

**When is the RIMCEE 2026 exam and when does the admit card come?**

The Stage-I written exam is on Sunday, 6 December 2026. The exam city slip was released on 17 November 2026, and the admit card is expected 5 to 7 days before the exam, around 29 November to 1 December 2026.

**Can English really improve in 16 days before RIMCEE?**

Yes, more than any other paper — because English here is a writing skill, not a syllabus to memorise. One timed essay and one letter a day for two weeks changes handwriting speed, planning and confidence more than any amount of reading about English does.
