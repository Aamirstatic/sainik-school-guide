Every evening, in lakhs of homes, the same quiet battle plays out. The child finishes school, opens the schoolbag, and stares at two piles: the school homework that must be done tonight, and the AISSEE practice the parent is asking about. Something has to give — and usually it is the child's mood, then the parent's patience, and finally nobody studies at all.

Here is the unpopular truth I tell every parent who asks me: **your child's school homework is not the enemy of AISSEE preparation. It is most of it.** The Class 6 paper tests maths, language, intelligence and general knowledge — the exact subjects your child studies in school every day. The problem is never that school takes time away from AISSEE prep. The problem is that nobody planned the week, so every day becomes a negotiation.

## Stop choosing. Start scheduling.

The parents who manage this well do not make daily decisions. They make one decision — the weekly plan — and then follow it. When Monday evening's maths homework takes forty minutes, nobody argues about whether AISSEE practice happens, because Tuesday's block is already on the wall.

A practical rule that works: **school owns the weekdays, AISSEE owns the weekends, and revision sneaks into the cracks.** Concretely:

- **Weekday school days:** homework first, then 30–45 minutes of AISSEE revision (formulae flashcards, 10 mental-maths questions, one reading passage). This is maintenance, not new learning.
- **Weekends:** two focused AISSEE blocks of 90–120 minutes each, one for maths and one for language or GK. This is where new topics and full practice sets live.
- **Daily:** fifteen minutes of reading anything — newspaper, storybook, school textbook. Reading speed is the hidden engine of the AISSEE paper, and it cannot be built in the last month.

## A sample weekly plan (adapt it, don't adopt it)

Every family is different, so treat this as a template to adjust around your child's school timetable:

| Day | After school | Evening block | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | School homework | 30 min: mental maths (tables, squares, fractions) | Light day — settle into the week |
| Tuesday | School homework | 30 min: English reading + vocabulary | Read one passage aloud |
| Wednesday | School homework | 45 min: AISSEE maths topic of the week | Weekend's new topic, first pass |
| Thursday | School homework | 30 min: GK facts (states, capitals, simple science) | Use school SST chapters where they overlap |
| Friday | School homework | Free evening / play | Rest is part of the plan, not a reward |
| Saturday | AISSEE block 1 (90–120 min): maths practice set | AISSEE block 2 (60–90 min): language or intelligence | Homework done before or after, at child's pace |
| Sunday | Revision: 60 min of the week's mistakes | Family time | End the week light, not exhausted |

Notice what Friday evening does: nothing. A plan with no rest in it is a plan your child will abandon by the third week. The [daily timetable guide](/blog/aissee-daily-timetable-class-6/) goes deeper on structuring single days if you need it.

## When school must win

There are weeks when AISSEE preparation has to shrink, and fighting that is a mistake. During school exams and class tests, **school comes first** — completely, unapologetically. A child who bombs a school test loses confidence, and a rattled child cannot solve a single intelligence question well. The same applies when the child is genuinely unwell or exhausted.

But shrink does not mean vanish. Keep a thread alive: ten minutes of mental maths, one paragraph of reading. The habit survives even when the volume drops, and restarting after the school exam feels like resuming, not starting over. If you want the fuller reasoning on keeping pressure healthy, our [motivation guide](/blog/motivate-child-aissee-without-pressure/) covers it from the parent's side.

## The Class 9 difference

Class 6 parents can relax a little — the syllabus overlap with school is enormous, and school work genuinely doubles as prep. Class 9 is different: the paper is 400 marks with separate English, science and social science sections, in English medium only, and the maths goes deeper than most school syllabi at that pace. Class 9 aspirants usually need real weekday depth — not just revision — on two or three school days a week. If your child is in Class 9, add one extra 60-minute subject block on Wednesday and one on Saturday, and guard them.

## Five warning signs the week is overloaded

Watch for these instead of waiting for marks to drop:

1. Homework that used to take an hour now takes two — the brain is tired, not slow.
2. The child starts "forgetting" the AISSEE books — avoidance is a signal, not laziness.
3. Sleep drops below eight hours regularly — nothing destroys learning like this.
4. School teachers mention inattention — the first system to complain is school.
5. You are negotiating every single evening — the plan has collapsed; rebuild it, don't enforce harder.

When two or more appear, cut first from the extras: extra tuition sheets, random app quizzes, third-party mock marathons. Never cut sleep, play, or school homework. Our [Class 6 study plan](/blog/sainik-school-study-plan-class-6/) shows how a lighter week can still keep the syllabus moving.

## One conversation that fixes most of it

Sit with your child on Sunday evening — not with a lecture, but with a blank weekly grid. Fill it together: school first, then AISSEE blocks, then the free evenings they choose. Children who co-own the plan follow it; children who are handed the plan fight it. Ten minutes of planning on Sunday buys you five evenings without arguments. That is the entire method.

## FAQs

**Should my child skip school homework to prepare for AISSEE?**
No. School homework reinforces the same maths and language fundamentals the AISSEE tests, and skipping it creates school stress that leaks into exam prep. The fix is a weekly plan that assigns clear time blocks to each, not choosing between them.

**How many hours a day should a Class 6 child study for AISSEE?**
Most children do well with about 1 to 1.5 focused hours on school days plus longer blocks on weekends. Consistency across months matters far more than any single long study session.

**When should school take priority over AISSEE preparation?**
During school exams and class tests, school must come first. A child who falls behind in school loses confidence, which damages AISSEE prep too. Reduce AISSEE study to a light revision habit during exam weeks rather than stopping completely.

**How do I stop the daily fight about homework versus AISSEE study?**
Fix a weekly timetable together and put it on the wall. When the schedule is decided in advance, it is no longer a daily negotiation. Children resist ambiguous demands; they follow visible plans.

**Is weekend-only AISSEE preparation enough?**
For most Class 6 aspirants, short daily revision plus focused weekend blocks works well. Class 9 students, whose syllabus is wider, usually need some weekday depth too. The key is that revision never gaps for more than a day or two.

**What is the first thing to cut when the week gets overloaded?**
Cut passive extras first — extra tuition sheets, random mock tests, and scrolling through study apps. Never cut sleep or school homework. Sleep deprivation and school backlogs destroy preparation faster than any missed practice set.
