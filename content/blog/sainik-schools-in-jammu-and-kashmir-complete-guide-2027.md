## Jammu and Kashmir's one Sainik School — and why that changes the strategy

Jammu and Kashmir has one school under the Sainik Schools Society: **Sainik School Nagrota, in Jammu**. It is a fully residential, CBSE-affiliated school, and it admits to Classes 6 and 9 through one exam only — the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE), conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA). There is no school-level test, no walk-in admission, and no separate J&K entrance exam for this school.

That single-school fact shapes everything for J&K parents. In a state with two or three Sainik Schools, counselling preferences spread the risk. Here, the preference list in AISSAC counselling still matters — you will list schools across India — but the home-UT advantage is concentrated on one school, and the competition for it is concentrated too. The good news is the other side of the same coin: the 67% home-state pool (explained below) means a J&K-domicile child is competing first against other J&K children for most of Nagrota's seats, not against the whole country.

If you are reading this in the AISSEE 2027 window, the immediate point is simple: applications opened on 3 November 2026 and close on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**, with a late-fee window from 3 to 6 December. The exam is on **31 January 2027**. Everything in this guide is built around that calendar.

## The 67% J&K quota — and what it means for your child

Under the standard Sainik School reservation pattern, **67% of seats are reserved for candidates from the state or Union Territory where the school is located** — here, Jammu and Kashmir — and the remaining 33% are open to candidates from other states and Union Territories. Within those pools, the standard pattern reserves 25% of seats for children of service personnel including ex-servicemen, 15% for Scheduled Castes and 7.5% for Scheduled Tribes.

For a J&K family, the practical meaning is this: your child's domicile certificate is not paperwork, it is the key to the larger pool. Get it ready before the form window closes, and make sure the domicile details on the form match the certificate exactly. A child from another state can still win a Nagrota seat through the 33% all-India pool, but a J&K child who files without a valid domicile claim gives up the advantage the system is designed to give them.

One honest note for families in the Kashmir valley: Nagrota is in the Jammu region, and the school is fully residential from Class 6. The distance from home is real, and it is worth discussing with your child before admission, not after. Families who talk about boarding life honestly beforehand have a far easier first term than families who discover the adjustment in April.

## Age limits and the exam pattern

The age rules follow the standard AISSEE pattern, confirmed for 2027:

- **Class 6:** 10 to 12 years as of 31 March 2027, and studying in Class 5.
- **Class 9:** 13 to 15 years as of 31 March 2027, and studying in Class 8 in a recognised school.

Boys and girls can both apply for Classes 6 and 9. Admission directories note that the number of seats for boys is higher than for girls — confirm the 2027 position in the bulletin rather than assuming a ratio.

The written exam is pen-and-paper, multiple-choice, with no negative marking:

| Class | Subjects and marks | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Class 6 | Mathematics 150, GK (Science and Social Studies) 50, Language 50, Intelligence 50 | 300 |
| Class 9 | Mathematics 200, English 50, Intelligence 50, General Science 50, Social Studies 50 | 400 |

After the written test, shortlisted candidates go through e-counselling and then a medical examination; admission is confirmed only after passing the medical fitness test. The minimum qualifying standard used in recent cycles has been 25% in each subject with 40% in aggregate for general and defence categories — treat that as the pattern to aim above, not as a guarantee of a seat, since final selection is by merit and the seat matrix.

## What J&K parents should do this week

1. **File the AISSEE form now, not in the late window.** The late fee is only Rs 500, but the late window is also when the portal is busiest and mistakes are hardest to fix calmly. See our [AISSEE 2027 notification guide](/news/aissee-2027-notification-out-applications-open-exam-jan-31-2026-11-03/) for the full schedule.
2. **Get the domicile certificate ready.** It unlocks the 67% pool, and it is the one document families scramble for when a window is about to close.
3. **List Nagrota honestly in counselling.** In AISSAC counselling you list preferences, but allotment follows merit and the seat matrix, not preference alone. List schools in the order you would actually accept — including a realistic second choice outside J&K, since the home school has only one intake.
4. **Check the fee and eligibility details before paying.** Read our [AISSEE 2027 fee and eligibility guide](/news/aissee-2027-fee-eligibility-age-limit-2026-11-03/) so the form fee, the school fee and the age rules are all clear before you submit.
5. **Talk to your child about boarding, honestly.** The routine, the hostel life and the discipline are the point of a Sainik School, and they are also the adjustment. A child who has never stayed away from home needs preparation, not just a form.

J&K gives its parents one clear, historic school and a home-UT pool that genuinely helps them. Use the remaining weeks before 2 December to file carefully, document the domicile claim properly, and prepare your child for the test and the boarding life that follows it — then, when the form is in, the waiting is for merit, not for paperwork.

## FAQs

**How many Sainik Schools are there in Jammu and Kashmir?**

One school under the Sainik Schools Society: Sainik School Nagrota in Jammu. It admits to Classes 6 and 9 through AISSEE. Check the AISSEE bulletin for any approved New Sainik Schools in Jammu and Kashmir.

**What is the home-state quota for Sainik School Nagrota?**

Under the standard Sainik School reservation pattern, 67% of seats are reserved for candidates domiciled in Jammu and Kashmir, and 33% for candidates from other states and Union Territories. Confirm the exact 2027 seat matrix in the AISSEE bulletin.

**What are the age limits for Sainik School Nagrota?**

For Class 6, age 10 to 12 years as of 31 March 2027, studying in Class 5. For Class 9, age 13 to 15 years as of 31 March 2027, studying in Class 8. Both boys and girls can apply for Classes 6 and 9.

**How many seats does Sainik School Nagrota have in Class 6?**

Around 70 seats for Class 6, as reported by admission directories. That is a reported figure, not a bulletin figure — the 2027 seat matrix in the AISSEE Information Bulletin is the authority.

**What is the fee at Sainik School Nagrota?**

No confirmed 2027 fee figure was found this run. Careers360's 2027 fees list reports Rs 1,75,230 plus Rs 3,000 caution money, but other directories report different figures. Treat them as reported only and check the school's own fee notice before budgeting.

**How do Jammu and Kashmir parents apply for Sainik School Nagrota?**

Through the national AISSEE route only: apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society between 3 November and 2 December 2026, with a late-fee window from 3 to 6 December 2026. The exam is on 31 January 2027.
