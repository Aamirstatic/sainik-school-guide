## Everybody trains for Maths. The merit list is decided elsewhere

Ask any AISSEE parent where the marks are, and they will say Maths. They are right — and they are missing the point. Maths is 150 of the 300 marks in the Class 6 paper. The other three sections — Language, Intelligence and General Knowledge — are 25 questions and 50 marks each. That is 75 questions and **150 marks**. Half the paper.

And here is what actually happens in most homes: Maths gets an hour a day, a tutor, and a stack of worksheets. The other three sections get whatever time is left over, which is usually none. Then the result comes, and the child who scored 130 in Maths still misses the cutoff — because one of the smaller sections fell below the floor.

Honestly, the other 150 marks are the easiest marks in the paper. Maths questions take minutes each and punish a single careless step. A GK fact is either known or not, a Language question is a rule applied in thirty seconds, and an Intelligence question is a pattern spotted in a minute. The children who clear AISSEE comfortably are rarely the ones with the highest Maths score. They are the ones who banked the other 150.

## The paper, in one table

| Section | Questions | Marks | Marks per question | Floor to clear (25%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 50 | 150 | 3 | 38 marks (13 correct) |
| Language | 25 | 50 | 2 | 14 marks (7 correct) |
| Intelligence | 25 | 50 | 2 | 14 marks (7 correct) |
| General Knowledge | 25 | 50 | 2 | 14 marks (7 correct) |
| **Total** | **125** | **300** | — | **120 marks aggregate (40%)** |

Two things in that table deserve a second look. First, the floor: 25% of 50 marks is 12.5 marks, and since each question is worth 2 marks, a child needs **7 correct answers (14 marks)** in each small section to clear it. Six correct answers is 12 marks — below the floor, and the paper fails even if the total is above 120. Second, the aggregate: 40% of 300 is 120 marks. A child needs both — every section above its floor, and the total above 120. (The 25% and 40% conditions do not apply to SC and ST candidates in the existing Sainik Schools.)

## What 20 marks in the other half is actually worth

Work it through with real numbers. Take two children, both scoring 110 out of 150 in Maths:

- **Child A** scores 30 in each of the three small sections: 110 + 90 = **200 out of 300**.
- **Child B** scores 18 in each: 110 + 54 = **164 out of 300**.

Same Maths score, 36 marks apart. That gap is the difference between a comfortable merit position and a nervous wait. Now flip it: a child who lifts each small section from 20 to 30 gains 30 marks — the equivalent of ten extra correct Maths questions, each worth 3 marks. Ten Maths questions is a hard week's work. Ten marks in GK is a month of reading one page of current affairs a day.

There is no negative marking, so every question in these sections is a free attempt. A guessed answer costs nothing and sometimes gains 2 marks. Children should be told this plainly: in the small sections, never leave a question blank.

## The 10-week plan for the other 150

There are ten weeks left — our [10-week countdown calendar](/blog/aissee-2027-10-week-countdown-calendar-23-november-to-exam-2026-11-21/) maps the whole period. Inside it, give the three small sections about **45 to 60 minutes a day**, on top of Maths. A rhythm that works:

| Day | Language (30 min) | Intelligence (30 min) | GK (15 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Grammar rules: tenses, articles | Series and coding, 20 questions | 5 current-affairs facts + revision |
| Tuesday | Comprehension passage, timed | Odd one out, analogy | Static GK: states, capitals, rivers |
| Wednesday | Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms | Blood relations, direction sense | 5 current-affairs facts + revision |
| Thursday | Grammar drill + error spotting | Puzzles, seating arrangement | Static GK: history dates, civics |
| Friday | Comprehension passage, timed | Mixed mock of 25 questions | 5 current-affairs facts + revision |
| Saturday | Weak-topic revision | Weak-topic revision | Full GK quiz, 25 questions |
| Sunday | **Full mock, 150 minutes, 2 PM** | — | Mock analysis: which section leaked marks? |

Three rules make this work. First, **Language is a rulebook, not a talent** — tenses, articles and prepositions follow patterns, and a child who drills one rule a day for ten weeks walks in knowing them. Our [Class 6 Language section strategy](/blog/aissee-class-6-language-section-strategy-50-marks/) lists the rules that repeat most. Second, **Intelligence rewards speed, not study** — twenty questions a day, timed, beats a theory book. Our [Intelligence section strategy](/blog/aissee-class-6-intelligence-section-strategy-50-marks/) has the question types to drill. Third, **GK is two subjects, not one** — static facts (states, rivers, national symbols) and current affairs (the last three months). Our [GK section strategy](/blog/aissee-class-6-gk-section-strategy-50-marks/) and this morning's [current-affairs roundup](/blog/aissee-2027-gk-current-affairs-october-november-2026-10-stories/) cover both halves.

## The three mistakes that cost the other 150

**Leaving GK to the last week.** GK cannot be crammed — 25 questions span history, geography, science, sports and the news. Fifteen minutes a day for ten weeks beats five hours the night before, every time.

**Treating the floor as a target.** A child aiming for 7 correct answers in a section will land on 6 on a bad day — and fail the paper. Aim for 15 correct in each small section in mocks. The cushion is the point.

**Skipping the small sections in mocks.** Children who practise Maths in isolation never learn the real skill: switching gears. In the hall, the paper jumps from a Maths sum to a grammar question to a pattern puzzle. Sunday mocks should always be the full 150 minutes, all four sections, in order. If Maths is eating your child's time, our [Maths speed drills](/blog/aissee-2027-maths-speed-drills-71-days-2026-11-21/) will help — but the fix for a leaking paper is rarely more Maths.

## A small story

Last year, a parent called us the evening the answer key came out. Her son had attempted 48 of the 50 Maths questions and was sure of most of them. But he had left 9 GK questions blank — "I didn't know them, so I skipped them." There was no negative marking. Nine blank questions were 18 marks thrown away, and his GK total landed below the floor. He missed the merit list by 11 marks. The blanks cost him more than any question he got wrong.

Tell your child this week: in AISSEE, a guess is never punished and a blank is never rewarded. Attempt everything. Bank the other 150.

## FAQs

**How many marks is each section in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
Mathematics is 50 questions for 150 marks, and Language, Intelligence and General Knowledge are 25 questions for 50 marks each. The paper totals 125 questions and 300 marks in 150 minutes.

**What is the minimum score needed in each AISSEE Class 6 section?**
25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate — 120 out of 300. In a 50-mark section, 25 percent is 12.5 marks, so a child needs 7 correct answers (14 marks) to clear the floor. The 25 percent and 40 percent conditions do not apply to SC and ST candidates in the existing Sainik Schools.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**
No. Wrong answers and blanks both score zero, so your child should attempt every question in all four sections, including the ones they are unsure about.

**In which language can my child write the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
The Class 6 paper can be written in English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu or Urdu. The Class 9 paper is in English only.

**Can the GK section alone change an AISSEE result?**
Yes. GK is 50 marks — the same as Language or Intelligence — and a child who scores 40 in GK instead of 20 gains 20 marks, the same as seven extra Maths questions. GK also has its own 25 percent floor, so ignoring it can disqualify an otherwise good paper.

**How much weekly time should the three non-Maths sections get?**
About 45 to 60 minutes a day across the three sections: roughly three 30-minute Language sessions, three 30-minute Intelligence sessions and 15 minutes of GK every day, alongside Maths practice. The exact split should follow your child's mock-test scores.
