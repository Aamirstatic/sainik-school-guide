## The mistake nobody notices until counselling

Every admission season, a handful of strong AISSEE candidates hit a wall that has nothing to do with their maths score. Their application form says one spelling of their name; their birth certificate says another. Or the birth certificate says 14 March 2015 and the school diary says 14 March 2016. At the application stage nobody stops you. At document verification — after the written exam, after the merit list — these mismatches can end a candidature.

Why does NTA care so much about this one document? Because the birth certificate is the **primary proof of date of birth**, and the entire AISSEE eligibility structure hangs on age: Class 6 applicants must be **10 to 12 years old** and Class 9 applicants **13 to 15 years old**, both as of **31 March 2027** (CONFIRMED from the 2026 information bulletin pattern). Get the DOB wrong on the form and the age-band calculation is wrong; get it inconsistent across documents and the verification officer has no way to know which version is true.

The good news: this is entirely fixable, and October — with the AISSEE 2027 notification still awaited — is the ideal month to do it.

## The four mismatches that matter

Before you fix anything, figure out which problem you actually have. Not all mismatches are equal:

| # | Mismatch | How serious | What fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spelling difference** between birth certificate and school/Aadhaar records (e.g. "Shreya" vs "Shreyha") | High — the most common rejection trigger | Correction application at the registering authority with a notarised affidavit |
| 2 | **DOB difference** between birth certificate and school records | Critical — affects age eligibility | Correction at the registering authority; hospital discharge summary is the strongest supporting proof |
| 3 | **Father's name spelled differently** across documents | Medium-high — NTA asks for parents' names on the form | Same correction route; check against the father's own documents too |
| 4 | **No name on the certificate** (only "Baby of X") | Medium — common for older registrations | Name-inclusion application at the registrar; supported by school records and affidavit |

Notice the theme: **the birth certificate is the anchor document**. When two documents disagree, the one issued at the time of birth wins. That is why the correction happens there, not by adjusting your Aadhaar to match the school diary.

## How corrections actually work in India

Birth registration in India runs through the Civil Registration System (CRS). The correcting authority is wherever the birth was originally registered — the **Municipal Corporation** (urban areas), **Nagar Palika / Nagar Panchayat** (smaller towns), or the **Gram Panchayat / Tehsil** (rural areas). The national CRS portal (crsorgi.gov.in) and most state portals let you start the process online; some jurisdictions still require a physical visit.

The standard correction kit, assembled from the legal guides linked below:

- [ ] The **original birth certificate** (the one with the error)
- [ ] A **notarised affidavit** on stamp paper, stating the incorrect entry, the correct entry, and the reason for the change
- [ ] **Parents' identity proofs** (Aadhaar is the most widely accepted)
- [ ] Supporting proof of the correct detail — **hospital discharge summary** for DOB errors, **school records** for name spelling
- [ ] The **application fee** (varies by state, roughly in the **Rs 20–Rs 500** band — EXPECTED, confirm locally)
- [ ] For **major name changes**, some states require a **gazette notification** (sometimes after newspaper publication) or even a court order — this is the heavier route, and exactly why small corrections should be done now

A practical point from the guides: if the child is above one year of age or the request is a completely new first name rather than a spelling fix, registrars are likelier to ask for the affidavit or the gazette step first. Complete that before filing the correction, not after.

## What NOT to do

Three traps parents fall into, all avoidable:

**1. Filling the form with a "compromise" spelling.** Writing a third version of the name that matches neither document does not split the difference — it creates a third mismatch. Fill the form exactly as the corrected birth certificate reads.

**2. Correcting the wrong document.** Updating Aadhaar to match the school diary feels easier, but the birth certificate remains the anchor. At verification, an inconsistent set is an inconsistent set regardless of which piece you polished.

**3. Assuming the correction window will save you.** NTA does open a short correction window after applications close, but [limited fields are editable and the window is days, not weeks](/news/rimcee-2026-correction-window-live-closes-october-11-2026-10-09/). A birth certificate correction cannot be completed inside it.

## FAQs

**Why does AISSEE care about the birth certificate?**
It is the primary proof of date of birth. NTA checks it against the age eligibility band — 10 to 12 years for Class 6 and 13 to 15 years for Class 9 as of 31 March 2027 — and document verification at counselling can reject a candidature over a mismatch.

**How do I correct a name or date of birth on a birth certificate in India?**
Apply to the municipal corporation, Nagar Palika, or Gram Panchayat where the birth was registered. You will need the original certificate, a notarised affidavit, parents' identity proofs, and supporting records like hospital discharge papers or school records. Many states accept applications online through their CRS portal.

**How long does a birth certificate correction take?**
It varies by state and municipality. Most urban corporations issue the corrected certificate in about 7 to 30 working days; cases needing a gazette notification or court order for major changes can take a month or more, so start now and do not wait for the AISSEE notification.

**What if the name on the birth certificate and the school diary differ?**
Fix the birth certificate first, then get the school record updated to match. Never fill the AISSEE form with a third version of the name — NTA cross-checks documents, and mismatches are the most common reason forms are sent back.

**Is a gazette notification needed for every correction?**
No. Simple spelling corrections at the registering authority usually need only an affidavit and supporting documents. A gazette notification is typically needed only for a substantial name change or where the local registrar specifically requires it.
