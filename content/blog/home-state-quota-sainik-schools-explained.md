> **Last verified: 13 October 2026.** The 67%/33% split and the reservation percentages below are CONFIRMED from the AISSAC counselling Standard Operating Procedure for the Sainik Schools Society's 33 schools. Seat counts in the worked examples are **illustrative examples**, not published figures — they exist to show how the maths works.

When parents first hear about AISSEE counselling, one question follows quickly: *"We are from Bihar but my child wants Sainik School Purulia — whose quota are we in?"* The answer decides how many seats you are competing for, who your competitors are, and sometimes whether a dream school is realistic. Here is the home-state quota, decoded properly — with the maths worked out so you never have to guess again.

![Students at a school morning assembly in uniform](/images/inbody/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-explained.webp)

## The one number that matters: 67 vs 33 (CONFIRMED)

Under the AISSAC (All India Sainik Schools Admission Counselling) SOP, every one of the 33 Sainik Schools divides its seats like this:

| Seat pool | Share | Who competes |
|---|---|---|
| **Home-state quota** | **67%** of seats | Candidates holding domicile of the state where the school is located |
| **Other-states quota** | **33%** of seats | Candidates from every other state (including yours) |

"Home state" is always defined by the **school**, not the student. Sainik School Purulia's home state is West Bengal; Sainik School Rewa's is Madhya Pradesh. A student from Patna applies to Purulia in the **33% other-states pool** — and to Sainik School Nalanda in the **67% home-state pool**. [Which school to pick as your first preference is a separate decision — our AISSAC preference guide covers it](/blog/how-to-choose-school-preferences-aissac-counselling/).

Two things parents get wrong here: first, that a Bihar-domiciled child can somehow also enter a West Bengal home-state pool (they cannot); second, that the 67% is "easy" (it is competitive too — you are up against the entire state's merit list).

## How the reservation layers sit inside the split (CONFIRMED)

The state split is the first division. Inside each school's total, the category reservations apply as well — CONFIRMED per the counselling SOP:

- **SC: 15%** of seats
- **ST: 7.5%** of seats
- **OBC-NCL: 27%** of seats
- **Defence wards:** 25% of the **remaining** seats, reserved for children of serving and ex-servicemen (this "remaining" pool is what is left after the SC/ST/OBC quotas are carved out)

Girls compete within the same pools — there is no separate gender quota, and the 10% girls intake policy operates through the same counselling.

## Three worked examples (illustrative numbers)

These examples use round seat counts purely to show the arithmetic. Actual published intake per school for 2027 will come with the notification and the [seats matrix](/blog/sainik-school-seats-2027-all-schools-matrix/).

**Example 1 — A school with 90 Class VI seats.**

- Home-state pool: 67% of 90 ≈ **60 seats** (West Bengal domicile only, if the school is Purulia).
- Other-states pool: 33% of 90 ≈ **30 seats** (everyone else in India).
- Inside the 90: SC ≈ 14 seats, ST ≈ 7, OBC-NCL ≈ 24. Of what remains (≈45), defence wards take 25% — about 11 seats.

**Example 2 — A defence-category child from Uttar Pradesh targeting Sainik School Mainpuri (home state: UP).**

She lands in the 67% pool — good. Within it, she also benefits from the defence-women/ward reservation layer as applicable. Her realistic competition set is UP-domiciled children in her category, not the all-India list. That is the quota working as designed: it rewards applying where your domicile is.

**Example 3 — A general-category boy from Karnataka targeting Sainik School Bijapur vs Sainik School Rewa.**

- At Bijapur (home state: Karnataka), he is in the 67% pool.
- At Rewa (home state: Madhya Pradesh), he is in the 33% pool — competing with applicants from 27+ states for a third of the seats.

This is why [our West Bengal guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-west-bengal-guide-2027/) stresses knowing your own state's school first: the home-state pool is roughly twice as large as the other-states pool, and your competition is narrower.

## The domicile certificate: the document that unlocks your pool

Here is where the quota gets personal. Counselling uses the **candidate's own domicile certificate** — not the father's, not the family's ancestral claim. Common mistakes that have cost real seats:

1. **No domicile in the child's name.** Apply now; [state-wise portal processes are listed here](/blog/domicile-certificate-online-apply-state-wise-process-2026-10-11/).
2. **Domicile from the wrong state.** If you moved recently, check which state your child's certificate actually names — that decides your 67% pool. [Our domicile guide](/blog/domicile-certificate-sainik-school-guide/) walks through edge cases.
3. **Assuming two home states.** You get one — the state on the certificate.
4. **OBC-NCL certificate from the wrong financial year.** The one-year/financial-year validity norm applies here too.

Start the paperwork in October-November, not at counselling time. Certificates take weeks, and counselling document verification accepts no "it is in process".

## What the quota does not do

- It does not override merit. Every pool has its own merit list; you still need to clear the category cutoff.
- It does not apply between New Sainik Schools (the 67/33 split is for the 33 Sainik Schools under the Society).
- It does not help at [counselling day document verification](/blog/aissee-counselling-day-document-checklist-2026-10-12/) — a missing domicile certificate there can cost you the pool entirely.

## A parent's checklist for October

- [ ] Confirm which state your child's domicile certificate names.
- [ ] Identify your home-state Sainik School and check its [2027 seats](/blog/sainik-school-seats-2027-all-schools-matrix/) when published.
- [ ] Decide 2–3 other-state schools you genuinely want, knowing they are the 33% pool.
- [ ] Get category (SC/ST/OBC-NCL/defence) documents in order alongside domicile.
- [ ] Rank preferences in AISSAC with the pool maths in mind — home-state school first is usually the highest-probability strategy.

The home-state quota is the single biggest structural advantage an AISSEE candidate gets. Used deliberately, it turns "apply everywhere" anxiety into a short, sensible list — and October, before the form window opens, is exactly when to draw that list up.
