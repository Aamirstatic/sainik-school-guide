Let me be honest before anything else: **the last 10 days are not now.** As I write this, AISSEE 2027 is 105 days away — the exam is **31 January 2027** ✅ (NTA exam calendar, 16 Sept 2026, tentative). If your child started a "final 10 days" plan today, they'd peak in October and burn out by December.

So why publish this now? Because the final stretch is the part families botch the most — and it's the part nobody plans until they're inside it. Bookmark this. Use it from **21 January 2027**. What follows is a taper: the deliberate winding-down that athletes use before a race, adapted for a 10-year-old with an OMR sheet.

> **Last verified:** 18 October 2026 · Exam date **31 Jan 2027** ✅ (NTA calendar, tentative) · Pattern: Class 6 — 150 min, 125 questions, 300 marks; Class 9 — 180 min, 150 questions, 400 marks; **no negative marking** ✅ (2026 bulletin, expected repeat) · Qualifying: 25% per section + 40% aggregate ✅ (2026 bulletin).

## Why the taper matters more than the grind

Here's what I see every year: a child prepares decently for three months, then in the last ten days the parents panic — four mocks a day, new chapters at midnight, YouTube "last-minute important questions" till 1 AM. The child walks into the exam hall exhausted, makes silly errors in the first 30 minutes, and loses marks they had already earned.

The taper flips that. Think of it like a runner's last week before a marathon: you don't run farther, you run *less*, protect sleep, and arrive fresh. A Class 6 paper is 150 minutes and 300 marks with no negative marking — the skill being tested in the final days isn't knowledge anymore. It's **steadiness**.

## The 10-day taper: day by day

Countdown assumes the exam on 31 January 2027 (Sunday pattern aside — adjust if NTA shifts the date when the bulletin releases).

| Day | Date | Focus | Hours | The rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 21 Jan | Full mock #1 (last full paper), then full error review | 5 | Note every silly error in an [error log](/blog/aissee-mock-test-analysis-error-log-method/) |
| 9 | 22 Jan | Revise only Day-10 errors + weakest formula sheet | 4 | No new topics, no new book |
| 8 | 23 Jan | Short drills: 45 min maths, 30 min language | 3.5 | Timed, but relaxed — no scoring |
| 7 | 24 Jan | Full mock #2 (last one ever), error review same day | 5 | This is the final full paper. After this, none |
| 6 | 25 Jan | Revise Day-7 errors + GK quick facts | 3.5 | GK is read-only now — no new current-affairs |
| 5 | 26 Jan | Intelligence + language drills, 30 min each | 3 | Stop all full mocks from today |
| 4 | 27 Jan | One error-log pass: every mistake ever noted, reread once | 3 | No new questions at all today |
| 3 | 28 Jan | Light revision: formula sheet + 20 questions, untimed | 2.5 | If a topic still feels shaky, leave it — really |
| 2 | 29 Jan | Pack bag, visit centre route if possible, early dinner | 1 | One 20-minute fun revision game, nothing more |
| 1 | 30 Jan | Rest day | 0 after lunch | Admit card, ID, pens, watch packed; sleep by 10 PM |

Two mocks in ten days, not ten mocks. Notice the direction: hours shrink, sleep grows, and the last three days are nearly study-free. That is the whole point. If you haven't built the pre-taper base yet, the [30-day revision plan](/blog/30-day-revision-plan-aissee-2027/) is the phase that comes right before this one — and the [Class 6/Class 9 syllabus breakdown](/blog/aissee-2027-syllabus-class-6-class-9/) tells you exactly what "already prepared" should cover.

## The "stop doing" checklist

Print this and put it on the fridge from 21 January:

- [ ] **Stop starting new chapters.** Anything untouched by Day 10 stays untouched. A half-learned chapter earns nothing and costs confidence.
- [ ] **Stop comparing.** The neighbour's child doing "8 hours daily" is either exaggerating or peaking too early. Both are their problem, not yours.
- [ ] **Stop night study.** Sleep is when memory consolidates — a tired brain drops the easy 50 marks in Section 1 (Mathematics is 150 of 300 in Class 6; you can't afford a foggy start).
- [ ] **Stop new materials.** No new guidebook, no new YouTube channel, no "2027 most expected" PDF forwarded in a group. The sources your child already trusts are enough.
- [ ] **Stop score-talk.** Don't ask "how many did you get right" after drills in the final week. Ask "did you enjoy it" — the goal is a calm child, and calm children attempt more questions (remember: no negative marking).
- [ ] **Stop changing the routine.** Same wake time, same meals, same study corner. Novelty is the enemy of steadiness.

## What parents do in the taper (the hard part)

The taper is harder on parents than on children, because your job shifts from *pushing* to *protecting*. Two scenes I keep replaying: the father who cancelled a family wedding trip on 28 January because "every hour counts" — his son remembered the resentment, not the revision. And the mother who, in the final week, simply started sitting nearby with her own book while her daughter studied — no questions, no hovering. The daughter later said those quiet evenings were when she felt most ready.

Your taper job list: keep the house calm, keep meals on time, keep visitors and phone calls away from the study corner, and keep your own anxiety out of your voice. If you're worried, talk to another adult — not to the child.

## The 30 January evening routine

This is the most valuable 6 hours of the entire preparation, and it contains zero studying:

1. **4 PM** — bag packed: admit card (downloaded when NTA releases it, expected mid-January 🔶), school ID, two black/blue ball pens, a simple wristwatch, water bottle.
2. **5 PM** — if the exam centre is unfamiliar, drive the route once. Knowing the gate removes morning panic.
3. **7 PM** — normal dinner. Nothing heavy, nothing new, nothing "special brain food."
4. **8 PM** — twenty minutes of anything pleasant: a walk, a favourite story, cards. Then phones away.
5. **10 PM** — lights out. If the child can't sleep, that's fine — lying still in the dark still rests the brain. No "one last revision," ever.

Morning of the 31st: wake at the usual time, normal breakfast, reach the centre 45 minutes early, and remind them of exactly one thing — *attempt every question; nothing is deducted for a wrong answer.*

## FAQs

**When do the last 10 days before AISSEE 2027 start?**
AISSEE 2027 is tentatively scheduled for 31 January 2027, so the final 10-day stretch runs 21–30 January 2027. Bookmark this plan now; don't start it in October.

**Should my child study new chapters in the last 10 days?**
No — that is the first taper rule. Only revise what is already prepared. New material in the final stretch adds anxiety without adding marks.

**How many hours should a child study in the final 10 days?**
Taper down, not up: roughly 5 hours at Day 10 shrinking to near-zero by Day 2, protecting sleep throughout. The table above gives the day-by-day shape.

**Should we stop mock tests in the last week?**
Yes. Take the last full mock about 7 days out, then stop full papers entirely. Final days get short 30–45 minute drills and error-log review only.

**What should the child do on the evening of 30 January?**
Pack everything (admit card, ID, pens, watch), eat a normal dinner, and sleep by 10 PM. No studying after dinner — preparation is done; rest is the strategy now.
