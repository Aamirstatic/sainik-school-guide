# Silly OMR Mistakes That Cost Students Their Sainik School Seat

> **🔄 Last verified: 28 September 2026** — ✅ **CONFIRMED**: AISSEE is a pen-and-paper, OMR-based exam (multiple-choice questions; Class VI: 150 minutes / 300 marks; Class IX: 180 minutes / 400 marks — per the AISSEE 2026 cycle and widely reported pattern). There is **no negative marking**: wrong and blank answers both score zero. AISSEE 2027 is proposed for **31 January 2027** (tentative, per NTA's 16 Sept 2026 calendar).

Every January, after the AISSEE, the same story surfaces in coaching centres across India: a bright student who "knew the paper" but scored far below their mock average. The questions weren't the problem. The OMR sheet was.

AISSEE is old-school — pen, paper, and a bubble sheet. Nobody loses marks because the Intelligence section was unfair. They lose marks because of bubbling errors, numbering shifts, and three panicked minutes at the end. The good news: every one of these mistakes is mechanical, and mechanical mistakes can be trained out of a student in two weeks.

## The 8 Mistakes That Actually Happen

### 1. The question-number shift (the big one)

This is the mistake that destroys scores. A student skips question 47, solves 48, and marks 48's answer in 47's row. Everything after that is off by one — ten correct answers can score zero. It usually happens right after a skipped question or a page turn.

**The fix:** when you skip a question, draw a small circle around its number in the *question paper* and keep your finger on the OMR row while bubbling. After every 25 questions, stop for five seconds and re-match the last question number.

### 2. Half-darkened bubbles

The scanner reads fully darkened circles. Lightly shaded, half-moon, or tick-marked bubbles may be read as blank. Students in a hurry often shade like they're colouring — quick diagonal strokes that leave the circle's edges white.

**The fix:** fill the circle completely, edge to edge, with steady pressure. Practise on printed OMR practice sheets, not in a notebook. Speed comes from the wrist, not from sloppiness.

### 3. Two bubbles for one question

Overwriting happens when a student changes their mind: the first bubble gets a cross or a scribble, and a second bubble is darkened. Scanners frequently reject the question entirely — and some treat double-marked answers as invalid, scoring zero even if one bubble was right.

**The fix:** decide before you bubble. If you must change an answer, follow the invigilator's instruction exactly — and know that correction fluid or blade-scratching will likely invalidate the sheet.

### 4. Bubbling everything at the end

Some students solve the whole paper and plan to "bubble in the last 15 minutes." Then the bell comes early, or the invigilator collects sheets section-wise, or 15 minutes turns out to be enough for only half the sheet. Fifty solved questions with zero bubbles is fifty zeroes.

**The fix:** bubble as you go — solve a question, darken its circle immediately. If you prefer batching, do it in blocks of 20–25, never more. [Pair this with a proper attempt strategy]({{< relref "aissee-paper-attempt-strategy.md" >}}) so bubbling never becomes a separate crisis.

### 5. Wrong roll number or booklet series

Before a single question is answered, students have to darken their roll number and question-booklet series on the OMR. A single wrong digit here can mean the sheet is scored against the wrong candidate — or not scored at all.

**The fix:** fill the roll number first, in a calm moment, and read each digit back twice. Match the booklet series (A/B/C/D) to the question paper in your hand before darkening anything else.

### 6. Rough work on the OMR sheet

Under pressure, students scribble calculations in the OMR's margins. Stray marks near the timing tracks or answer areas can confuse the scanner — and rough work belongs in the question booklet anyway.

**The fix:** do all rough work in the question paper booklet. Keep the OMR sheet clean, flat, and dry from the first minute. No water bottle on the same desk corner.

### 7. Leaving questions blank "to come back to"

Remember: there is **no negative marking** in AISSEE. A blank and a wrong answer both score zero — so a blank is never safer than a guess. Students who leave 10 questions "for later" and run out of time hand away free marks. [Read the full honest answer on negative marking]({{< relref "aissee-negative-marking-honest-answer.md" >}}).

**The fix:** attempt every question. For genuine guesses, eliminate obviously wrong options first — even a 50/50 guess doubles your expected score on that question versus leaving it blank.

### 8. No final verification pass

Most students use the last minutes to solve one more hard question. The highest-ROI use of the last 8–10 minutes is verification: re-checking that what you *meant* to mark is what is *actually* marked.

## The 3-Minute Pre-Submit Checklist

Run through this in the final minutes, in order. Print it, practise it in mocks, make it muscle memory:

| # | Check | How |
|---|-------|-----|
| 1 | Roll number & booklet series | Read each digit off the admit card, twice |
| 2 | Question-number alignment | Last question solved = last row bubbled; spot-check 3 random rows |
| 3 | Skipped questions | Every skipped question now has an attempted answer (no negative marking — guess) |
| 4 | Bubble completeness | Scan down each column: no half-shaded or tick-marked circles |
| 5 | Double marks | No question has two darkened bubbles or correction marks |
| 6 | Sheet condition | No tears, no fluid, no water damage; edges intact |

## How to Train This at Home

Buy or print 10–15 blank OMR practice sheets (125 rows for Class VI, 150 for Class IX). For the next two weeks, every mock your child takes must be on a real OMR sheet, timed strictly — 150 minutes for Class VI, 180 for Class IX — with the 3-minute checklist at the end. After each mock, score *two* numbers: the knowledge score (what they solved right) and the OMR score (what the sheet actually captured). When those two numbers match for three mocks in a row, the mechanical risk is gone.

One more thing parents underestimate: the pen. Let your child find, in practice, the exact blue or black ballpoint pen they will use on exam day — one that flows smoothly without pressing hard, because pressing hard for 150 minutes causes hand fatigue, and fatigue causes mistake #2. Buy five of the same pen. It is the cheapest insurance in this entire exam.

## FAQs

**Does AISSEE have an OMR sheet?**
Yes. AISSEE is conducted in pen-and-paper mode with an OMR answer sheet — confirmed for AISSEE 2026, and the 2027 cycle is expected to follow the same pattern. Class VI gets a 150-minute paper; Class IX gets 180 minutes.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE for wrong OMR bubbles?**
No. Wrong answers and blank answers both score zero — nothing is deducted. This means you should attempt every question, but it also means a wrongly bubbled answer is indistinguishable from a wrong answer.

**What pen should be used to fill the AISSEE OMR sheet?**
Use a blue or black ballpoint pen as instructed in the information bulletin and admit-card instructions. Never use pencil or gel pens that smudge, and do not carry correction fluid into the hall — a torn or whitened OMR may not be scanned.

**What is the most common OMR mistake in AISSEE?**
The question-number shift — answering question 47's response against question 48's row. It happens after skipping a question or losing your place, and it can corrupt a whole block of answers. Finger-tracking and row re-checks prevent it.

**How much time should AISSEE students reserve for bubbling and re-checking?**
Bubble as you go rather than saving all bubbling for the end, and keep the last 8–10 minutes exclusively for verification: roll number, question numbers, darkened circles, and skipped-question handling.

**Can a damaged OMR sheet be replaced in the exam hall?**
Invigilators may replace a torn or misprinted sheet at their discretion, but a sheet you damaged yourself through overwriting or correction fluid is usually not replaced. Protect the sheet from the first minute — no rough work on it.
