## Admit card day is not the day to discover a problem

Every January, the same three phone calls reach schools and coaching centres: "The photo on the card is not my child's photo." "The centre is 200 km away." "They have spelled her name wrong." Every one of these is fixable or manageable — but only if you act the day you download the card, not on exam morning.

Here is the uncomfortable truth behind most admit card panic: parents download the card, glance at the centre name, and file it away. The errors sit there unread until the child is standing at the gate. This guide is built around a different habit — a ten-minute check the day the card appears — and a fix for each of the three classic problems.

Start with the companion guides on this site: our [AISSEE 2027 admit card guide](/blog/aissee-2027-admit-card-guide/) covers downloading and exam-day documents in full, and our [exam city choice guide](/blog/aissee-2027-exam-city-choice-pick-centres-wisely-2026-10-23/) explains how centres are allotted in the first place. This article is the troubleshooting half — what to do when something on the card is wrong.

## The ten-minute check: run it the day the card appears

NTA follows a two-step pattern: an **advance intimation of the examination city** comes first, and the **admit card** follows. The city slip tells you where you are going; the admit card is the document that gets your child through the gate. Check both, but check the admit card hardest, in this order:

| # | Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Candidate name, exactly as on the application | Gate staff match it against photo ID |
| 2 | Date of birth | A wrong DOB is the hardest error to explain away |
| 3 | Photograph and signature | A missing or wrong photo triggers extra verification |
| 4 | Centre name, full address and reporting time | The centre you memorised may not be the centre printed |
| 5 | Exam date, paper and instructions printed on the card | Reporting late is not excused by a misread |

If all five match, print two copies, file one with the confirmation page, and stop worrying. If anything does not match, find your problem below.

## Problem 1: the photo is wrong, blurry or missing

This is the most common admit card problem and the least dangerous — if you handle it early. Photos go wrong at upload time: a low-resolution scan, a photo taken against a busy background, or a file that got swapped between siblings' applications.

**What to do:** Contact NTA through the helpline published on the AISSEE portal the same day you spot the error. State the application number, the exact problem, and ask what correction step applies. Then prepare the backup kit regardless of the reply: the printed admit card, the application confirmation page, a valid photo ID, and **two recent passport-size photographs** of the candidate. At the centre, identity is verified from the documents as a set, not from the printed photo alone — a child with a wrong photo but matching ID, confirmation page and fresh photographs is verifiable; a child with only the card is not.

**What not to do:** Do not paste a new photo over the printed one, do not edit the PDF, and do not reprint a doctored card. A visibly altered admit card looks worse than a wrong photo — it looks like a forgery.

## Problem 2: the centre is far away, or not the one you expected

This one hurts, because the fix parents want does not exist. **Once the admit card is issued, the allotted centre is final.** NTA does not entertain centre-change requests after allotment, and no helpline call, email or visit will move your child to a nearer school.

So the work shifts from changing the centre to reaching it safely:

- **Visit the route before exam week.** If the centre is in another town, do a trial run or at least confirm bus and train timings for exam morning. January mornings in North India mean fog — build in an extra hour.
- **Book stay the week the city slip arrives, not the week the admit card arrives.** The advance city intimation exists precisely so families can plan travel early. Families who wait for the admit card pay more for rooms and get worse ones.
- **Read the full centre address, not just the town name.** Two schools in the same town with similar names have sent more than one family to the wrong gate. Match the centre code and address on the card, not your memory of the choice list.

And for the next cycle, remember how allotment works: centres are allotted from the choices and availability at the time of application, and our [exam city choice guide](/blog/aissee-2027-exam-city-choice-pick-centres-wisely-2026-10-23/) explains how to choose wisely while the form is still open. Once the card is out, that window is closed.

## Problem 3: the name, date of birth or category is wrong

This is the problem to take most seriously, because it splits into two very different cases.

**Minor spelling variation** — say "Anjali" printed as "Anjli", or a missing middle name — usually does not block entry when the photo ID and confirmation page agree with each other. Still flag it to NTA the same day, and carry the documents that prove the correct spelling.

**Wrong name, wrong date of birth, or wrong category** is different. Date of birth decides eligibility itself (Class 6 is 10–12 years and Class 9 is 13–15 years as on 31 March 2027), so a wrong DOB is not a cosmetic error — it can raise an eligibility question at verification. Flag it immediately through the NTA helpline, keep a written record of the complaint (date, reference number, name of the person spoken to), and carry the birth certificate to the centre along with everything else.

One rule covers all three problems: **never wait for exam day to raise an error.** Corrections take time, helplines get busy in exam week, and a problem flagged on download day has days to be resolved. A problem flagged at the gate has minutes.

## The exam-day backup kit

Whatever the problem, the kit is the same. Pack it the night before, in two bags if possible:

- [ ] Printed admit card (two copies)
- [ ] Application confirmation page
- [ ] Valid photo ID of the candidate
- [ ] Two recent passport-size photographs
- [ ] Birth certificate (only if a name or DOB error is under dispute)
- [ ] A pen, and the reporting time written on paper — not just on a phone

Reach the centre at least 45 minutes before the reporting time. Early arrival buys you the one thing no helpline can give you on exam morning: time to sort out a verification question calmly.

## FAQs

**My photo on the AISSEE admit card is wrong or missing. What do I do?**

Do not wait for exam day. Contact NTA through the helpline on the portal the same day you spot it, and carry the confirmation page, a valid photo ID and two recent photographs to the centre as backup. The centre staff verify identity from your documents, not from the printed photo alone.

**Can I change my AISSEE exam centre after the admit card is released?**

No. Once the admit card is issued, the allotted centre is final and NTA does not entertain centre-change requests. Plan travel to the allotted centre instead of chasing a change that will not come.

**My name is misspelled on the AISSEE admit card. Will I be stopped at the gate?**

A minor spelling variation usually does not block entry if your photo ID and confirmation page match, but a wrong name or date of birth must be flagged to NTA immediately. Do not assume it will be sorted at the centre.

**When is the AISSEE 2027 admit card released?**

The AISSEE 2027 notification has not been released yet, so no admit card date is confirmed. Based on the previous cycle, the card is expected around mid-January 2027, shortly before the January exam. The advance city intimation slip comes first.

**What should I check the moment I download the AISSEE admit card?**

Check five things in order: the candidate name and date of birth, the photograph and signature, the exam centre name and address, the exam date and reporting time, and the instructions printed on the card. Flag any error the same day.

**What documents must I carry with the AISSEE admit card?**

Carry the printed admit card, the application confirmation page, a valid photo ID of the candidate, and two recent passport-size photographs. Keep a photocopy of everything in a separate bag.
