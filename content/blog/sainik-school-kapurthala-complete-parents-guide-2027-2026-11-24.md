## A palace that became a school

If you are a parent in Punjab filling the AISSEE 2027 form this week, there is really only one school on your list — because Punjab has only one Sainik School under the Sainik Schools Society. And what a school it is. **Sainik School Kapurthala** sits inside the **Jagatjit Palace**, a French Renaissance building modelled on the Palace of Versailles, built in 1908 by Maharaja Jagatjit Singh of Kapurthala. Your child would not just study in a boarding school. He or she would live, eat, play and parade in a palace.

The school was inaugurated on **8 July 1961** by **V. K. Krishna Menon**, then Defence Minister — the man who conceived the Sainik School idea itself. It was the **seventh Sainik School established in India**, one of the founding institutions of the whole system. Sixty-five years later, that history is not decoration. It is the reason the school exists: to prepare boys — and now girls — academically, physically and mentally for the **National Defence Academy**. Its motto says it plainly: **"Knowledge, Humility and Discipline."**

## The NDA record — and what it actually means

Let us be straight about the number everyone quotes. Standard school profiles report that **more than 1,150 Kapurthala cadets have become officers** in the Indian armed forces through the NDA. Treat the exact lifetime tally as reported rather than officially audited — an older newspaper profile put the count above 700 at the time it was written, and the figure has grown since. But the direction is not in doubt: this is one of the strongest NDA pipelines of any Sainik School in the country.

There is a second record that tells you something different, and arguably more useful, about the place. Kapurthala is reported to be **the only Sainik School to have held the Defence Minister's Trophy for nine consecutive years**. That trophy is not won by one brilliant batch. It is won by a school that produces results year after year, across academics, sports and training. For a parent, that consistency matters more than any single year's headline number.

The alumni network — the Saikapians — runs through the services and beyond, and in a residential school, that network starts in the dormitory. Twelve houses named after Indian personalities, from Subhash and Bhagat to Nalwa and Ranjit, give every cadet a second family from the first week.

## Seats for 2027-28: 120 and 27

For the 2027-28 session, the AISSEE 2027 bulletin lists **120 Class 6 seats** and **27 Class 9 seats** at Kapurthala. The figures are tentative, as they are for every school.

| Entry | Seats (2027-28, tentative) |
|---|---|
| Class 6 | 120 (including 12 girls' seats under the 10% rule) |
| Class 9 | 27 |

The home-state rule is where Punjab parents have the edge. **67% of seats are reserved for Punjab-domicile candidates** and 33% for other states — so a Punjab-domicile child competes for roughly two-thirds of those 120 Class 6 seats, while a child from another state chases the remaining third. We explained how the school you pick decides your home-state status in our [school-choice guide](/news/aissee-2027-8-days-left-school-choice-home-state-quota-zero-seat-rule-2026-11-24/). Kapurthala's Class 6 intake of 120 is among the largest in the country — the same as Kunjpura, and behind only a handful of schools like Tilaiya at 140. Our [Punjab guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-punjab-complete-guide-2027/) explains the state's position in full: one school, one shot, and a very deep NDA tradition. And a warning worth heeding: the fee you pay is not the form. Our [fee-deadline piece](/news/aissee-2027-fee-deadline-2-december-not-3-december-2026-11-24/) explains why the payment must succeed by 2 December, 11:50 PM — not 3 December, as some result sites claim.

## Fees: what is reported, and what is not verified

No verified 2027-28 fee figure for Kapurthala is published. Two admission portals report the 2025-26 figures in close agreement: ezyschooling lists a **yearly fee of ₹1,79,622** (₹1,82,622 in the first year, with one-time charges), and a second portal reports about **₹1,82,143** a year. The breakdown both report is consistent — tuition about ₹1,16,922, meal charges ₹41,300, horse riding ₹5,400, and the rest in miscellaneous and imprest charges.

That agreement makes **roughly ₹1.8 lakh a year** a reasonable planning figure, but it is still reported, not official: fees are revised periodically by the Board of Governors, and some schools raise them by up to 10% a year. Do not budget to the rupee on it. The only figure worth budgeting from is the school's own fee notice, which you should ask for at the time of admission. Fee concessions exist for SC/ST and defence wards — ask the school office which ones your child qualifies for, rather than assuming.

## Campus life: what a day actually feels like

Kapurthala's campus is the thing that sets it apart from every other Sainik School on the list. About **245 acres** of green, open ground around the palace, with the palace building itself still serving as the administrative and academic hub. Around it: smart classrooms, science laboratories, a computer lab, a library, hostels organised around the twelve-house system, a cadets' mess with vegetarian and non-vegetarian options, a medical centre, and sports infrastructure that includes football, hockey, cricket and basketball grounds, a swimming pool, a gymnasium, NCC training and drill grounds — and horse riding, which is part of the fee structure here, not an optional extra.

A typical weekday runs from early-morning PT through classes, games in the evening and supervised prep at night. It is a full, physical, regimented day, and that is the point — but it is also the thing to weigh honestly. If your child hates early mornings and structured routine, no palace and no NDA record will make them happy here. If they thrive on it, Kapurthala will feel like it was built for them. It was, quite literally — a palace turned into a school, for exactly this purpose.

## Who Kapurthala suits — and who should think twice

Kapurthala suits a Punjab-domicile child who wants the NDA and is ready for a fully residential, disciplined life. The home-state quota, the 120-seat Class 6 intake and the school's position on the Kapurthala–Jalandhar road — an easy drive from Jalandhar, Ludhiana and Amritsar — make it the most practical first choice for most Punjab families. For families outside Punjab, the outside-state pool is only a third of the seats, and the competition there is fierce — but the 27 Class 9 seats, small as they are, are a second door for an older child who is ready.

Think twice if your child is set on a school closer to home in another state. And if your daughter is applying for Class 9, check the bulletin's Appendix VI-A1 for the exact girls' figure before you decide; for Class 6 girls, the position is clearer — 12 seats under the 10% rule.

## How to apply for 2027-28

Admission is only through **AISSEE**, run by NTA. Apply online at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society** by **Wednesday, 2 December 2026, 11:50 PM**. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027 (born 1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017); Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 (born 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014) and a passed Class 8. The fee is **₹850** for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards and **₹700** for SC/ST, with a late window from 3 to 6 December at an extra ₹500. The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027, at 2:00 PM** — 150 minutes for Class 6, 180 for Class 9, no negative marking in either. If you are still weighing Kapurthala against the other founding schools, our profiles of [Kunjpura](/blog/sainik-school-kunjpura-complete-parents-guide-2027-2026-11-24/), [Tilaiya](/blog/sainik-school-tilaiya-complete-parents-guide-2027-2026-11-24/), [Ghorakhal](/blog/sainik-school-ghorakhal-complete-parents-guide-2027-2026-11-24/) and [Kazhakootam](/blog/sainik-school-kazhakootam-complete-parents-guide-2027-2026-11-24/) use the same format, so you can compare them side by side.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Kapurthala have for 2027-28?**

Kapurthala has 120 Class 6 seats and 27 Class 9 seats for 2027-28. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin's seat appendices. By the 10 percent girls' rule, 12 of the 120 Class 6 seats are for girls.

**When was Sainik School Kapurthala founded?**

It was inaugurated on 8 July 1961 by the then Defence Minister V. K. Krishna Menon, making it the seventh Sainik School established in India. Its motto is Knowledge, Humility and Discipline, and its campus is the Jagatjit Palace, built in 1908.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Kapurthala?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Kapurthala?**

No verified 2027-28 fee figure is published. Admission portals report a total annual fee of about Rs 1,79,622 to Rs 1,82,622 for 2025-26, so treat roughly Rs 1.8 lakh as a planning figure — the 2027-28 figure may be higher, as fees are revised periodically. Confirm the current figure from the school before budgeting.

**Does Sainik School Kapurthala admit girls?**

Yes, since the 2021-22 academic session, when girls began joining Sainik Schools. For 2027-28, 12 of the 120 Class 6 seats are for girls under the 10 percent rule. Class 9 girls' seats are few and listed school-wise in the bulletin.

**How strong is Kapurthala's NDA record?**

More than 1,150 Kapurthala cadets are reported to have become officers in the armed forces through the NDA — one of the strongest records of any Sainik School. The school is also reported to be the only one to have held the Defence Minister's Trophy for nine consecutive years.
