## The question I hear every October

"My child is good at sports. Should we aim for a Sainik School, or a sports school?" Parents ask this assuming it's one choice. It isn't — it's a question about what your child's day will look like for the next seven years. Let me lay out both systems honestly, because they reward completely different children.

A Sainik School is a defence-run residential school (33 established ones under the Sainik Schools Society, plus New Sainik Schools in PPP mode) whose founding purpose is preparing students for the National Defence Academy and officer careers. Admission is through one door only: the **AISSEE written exam** — 150 minutes of Maths, language, intelligence and GK for Class 6; 180 minutes including science and social science for Class 9. No physical trial. No sports certificate. Just the OMR sheet.

A sports school is built around the opposite idea: the day is organised for **professional sports training**, with academics fitted around it. Take Motilal Nehru School of Sports (MNSS), Rai, in Haryana — established 1973 by the Haryana government, fully residential, co-educational, CBSE-affiliated, spread over 250+ acres. Its main entry is Class 5 (100 seats), with lateral entry for Outstanding Sportspersons in Classes 6, 8, 9 and 11. Selection runs through the **PESAT** (Physical Efficiency and Sports Aptitude Test — running, strength, jumping, fitness), then a written exam in English, Hindi, Maths and GK, then a medical test and merit list. A physically gifted child with average marks has a genuine chance here that the AISSEE paper would never give them.

## Side-by-side: what actually differs

| Feature | Sainik School (AISSEE route) | Sports School (e.g., MNSS Rai) |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | Ministry of Defence / Sainik Schools Society; New Sainik Schools are PPP partnerships | State governments, trusts or private managements — varies school to school |
| Entry classes | Class 6 and Class 9 | Varies: MNSS Rai's main entry is Class 5, with lateral entry in 6, 8, 9, 11 |
| Selection | One national written exam (AISSEE), MCQ on OMR, **no negative marking** | Physical trials (PESAT) + written test + medical; sports achievements count for OSP seats |
| Age band (Class 6) | 10–12 years as on 31 March (PROJECTED for 2027) | MNSS Rai Class 6: 10–12 years (its Class 5 band is 9–11) |
| Board & medium | English-medium, CBSE-affiliated | MNSS Rai is CBSE-affiliated, English-medium; varies by school |
| Daily focus | Parade, PT, academics, NDA-oriented grooming | Sports coaching (hockey, swimming, athletics, shooting, wrestling at Rai), academics around training hours |
| Girls | 10% of Class 6 vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more (33 schools) | Co-educational residential at Rai and most government sports schools |
| Natural career path | NDA, armed forces, also UPSC and engineering (see our piece on [Sainik School graduates beyond defence](/blog/careers-after-sainik-school-beyond-defence-2026-10-08/)) | Professional sports, sports quotas in government jobs, defence, police and railway recruitment |
| Indicative cost | Varies by school — check each prospectus; new PPP schools publish their own schedules | MNSS Rai: reported ~Rs 1.5–1.8 lakh/year all-in (subsidised, EXPECTED — confirm on the school site) |

One honest note on cost: neither system is "cheap" for a fully residential education, and neither publishes a single national fee. Treat any fee number you read online — including the MNSS figures above — as indicative, and confirm against the school's current prospectus before deciding.

## How to decide: the 5-question checklist

Forget the prestige debate. Answer these about your actual child:

1. **Does your child love running, jumping and competing — without being told?** A sports school is wasted on a child who merely "likes games period". It rewards the kid who is restless without physical activity.
2. **Is your child academically strong and disciplined with a desk?** That child fits the AISSEE system, where the single written exam is everything.
3. **What does the child say they want to be?** "Army officer" and "athlete" are different dreams. Both systems respect the other — sports school students can attempt the NDA after Class 12, and Sainik Schools run serious PT and games too — but the *centre of gravity* differs.
4. **How does your family handle injury risk and uncertainty?** A sports career can be ended by one injury. Academics-plus-defence is the steadier floor. Be honest with yourselves here.
5. **Can your child live in a hostel from age 10–11?** Both are residential. Both run on routine, roll call and mess timings. If boarding itself is the worry, solve that question first — read our comparison of [Sainik School vs a good private CBSE school](/blog/sainik-school-vs-private-cbse-school-honest-comparison/) for the day-scholar alternative.

## A word on doing both

You don't have to pick today. Sports school entry tests include a written paper — English, Hindi, Maths and GK at MNSS Rai — which means AISSEE preparation doubles as preparation for that half of the trials. The extra investment is physical: daily running, body-weight strength work and one chosen sport played seriously. A child who prepares both ways keeps two doors open, and the discipline transfers. ## FAQs

**What is the main difference between a Sainik School and a sports school?**

Sainik Schools are defence-run residential schools aimed at officer careers (NDA route), with admission through the written AISSEE exam. Sports schools are built around professional sports training, and admission usually involves physical fitness trials plus a written test.

**Does a sports school student still get good academics?**

Yes — most recognised sports schools are CBSE-affiliated and run regular academics alongside training. At MNSS Rai, for example, students follow the CBSE curriculum plus a written entrance test that includes English, Hindi, Maths and GK.

**Which is harder to get into — Sainik School or a sports school?**

Both are competitive in different ways. Sainik School admission is a single nationwide written exam (AISSEE) with heavy demand per seat. Sports schools select through physical trials like the PESAT test, which favours athletic children even if their academics are average.

**Can a sports school student join the NDA?**

Yes. NDA entry depends on clearing the UPSC NDA exam after Class 12, not on which school you attended. Sports school students with strong academics can and do apply — but Sainik School students get daily NDA-oriented grooming.

**How much do sports schools cost compared to Sainik Schools?**

It varies widely. Government-run sports schools like MNSS Rai (Haryana) are subsidised, with reported annual costs around Rs 1.5 to 1.8 lakh including boarding. Sainik School fees also differ by school — old established schools versus new PPP schools have different fee schedules, so always check the school prospectus.

**Can a child do both — prepare for AISSEE and sports trials?**

Yes, and many families do. Sports school entry tests include a written component (English, Maths, GK), so AISSEE preparation overlaps well. The extra piece is physical conditioning for trials — daily running, strength and flexibility work.
