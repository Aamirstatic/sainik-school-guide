## Two exams, two different childhoods

If you are a parent of a Class 5 child this year, you have probably heard both names in the same conversation: AISSEE and JNVST. Both are national entrance exams. Both lead to free-or-subsidised residential schooling. Both are talked about in the same breath at parent-teacher meetings.

But they lead to genuinely different schools — and the exam your child finds "easy" depends a lot on what kind of learner they are. Here's an honest comparison, so you can decide whether to aim at one, or sensibly attempt both.

## The two systems at a glance

| | AISSEE (Sainik Schools) | JNVST (Navodaya Vidyalayas) |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | National Testing Agency (NTA) for the Sainik Schools Society, Ministry of Defence | Navodaya Vidyalaya Samiti (NVS), Ministry of Education |
| Admission to | Class 6 and Class 9, Sainik Schools and New Sainik Schools | Class 6 (plus Class 9 and 11 lateral entry) in Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas |
| 2027 exam date | 31 January 2027 | 28 November 2026 (Class 6) |
| Application window | 3 November – 2 December 2026 (late window 3–6 December, Rs 500 extra) | Closed for 2027-28 (last date 31 July 2026, extended into August) |
| Fee | Rs 850 (Gen/OBC/Defence), Rs 700 (SC/ST) | Free registration |
| School character | Military-style residential schooling, NDA-oriented | Academic residential schooling, one JNV per district |
| Negative marking | None in either exam | None in either exam |

The dates alone tell you something useful: the two exams don't clash. JNVST Class 6 happens on 28 November 2026, AISSEE on 31 January 2027. A child can attempt both, and many families do — the JNVST in November becomes a real-exam rehearsal for the AISSEE in January.

## The papers, side by side

**AISSEE Class 6** is the bigger paper: 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks. Mathematics carries half the marks (50 questions, 150 marks), with Language, Intelligence and General Knowledge at 25 questions and 50 marks each. It rewards a child who is strong in maths and can sustain concentration for two and a half hours.

**JNVST Class 6** is shorter and more focused: 120 minutes, 80 questions, 100 marks. The first section combines Mental Ability (20 questions) with Environmental Studies (20 questions) for 50 marks in 60 minutes; Arithmetic and Language are 20 questions and 25 marks each in 30 minutes apiece. Each correct answer is worth 1.25 marks.

| | AISSEE Class 6 | JNVST Class 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 150 minutes | 120 minutes |
| Questions | 125 | 80 |
| Total marks | 300 | 100 |
| Maths weight | 150 marks (50%) | 25 marks (Arithmetic) + reasoning in Mental Ability |
| Qualifying rule | 25% per section + 40% aggregate (not applicable to SC/ST) | Minimum 14 in Mental Ability + EVS, 7 in Arithmetic, 7 in Language |
| Language of paper | 13 languages (Class 6) | Regional languages as opted |

Honestly, most guidebooks overcomplicate this choice. The real question is simpler: is your child a maths-heavy, long-paper child, or a quick-reasoning, short-paper child? A child who scores well on pattern puzzles and mental ability but tires in a long maths paper may find JNVST kinder. A child who loves maths and wants a military career will find AISSEE more rewarding — and the 150-mark maths section is where they can pull away from the pack.

## What school life actually differs on

This is where parents should look past the exam and at the next six years. Sainik Schools are run under the Ministry of Defence with a deliberately military character: discipline, physical training, house systems and an orientation toward the armed forces. That is the point of the school, not a side effect — if your child dreams of the NDA, this is the pipeline built for it.

Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas are academic residential schools under the Ministry of Education, one per district, with a strong record in board exams and competitive exams. They are not military schools, and they don't pretend to be. For a child whose goal is strong academics in a residential setting, JNV is an excellent destination in its own right.

Neither is a consolation prize for the other. They are different answers to different ambitions, and treating one as the "backup" for the other usually means preparing half-heartedly for both.

## A practical plan if you attempt both

- **November:** JNVST Class 6 is on 28 November 2026, and admit cards were released on 28 October 2026 — if your child registered, download the admit card now and use the exam as a full rehearsal: same morning routine, same OMR discipline.
- **December:** Finish the AISSEE form well before 2 December (11:50 PM). December is also your window to fix AISSEE-specific gaps the JNVST exposed — especially maths depth and GK.
- **January:** AISSEE is on 31 January 2027. Exam-city information comes on 11 January and admit cards from 25 January — diary both dates the day you submit the form.
- **After both:** Results and counselling run separately. AISSEE counselling (AISSAC) and JNV selection follow their own processes — track each on its official portal, not on rumour.

## So, which one?

If the dream is the uniform — NDA, the armed forces, a military-school life — AISSEE is the exam that matters, and JNVST is a useful rehearsal along the way. If the dream is a strong, free residential education with excellent academics, JNVST is the destination, and AISSEE is still worth attempting for the extra option it gives. And if your child is genuinely undecided, attempting both costs one extra exam morning and gives two completely different futures to choose from. That is a good trade.

## FAQs

**Can my child attempt both AISSEE and JNVST in the same year?**

Yes. They are run by different bodies for different school systems, with different exam dates — JNVST Class 6 is on 28 November 2026 and AISSEE is on 31 January 2027 — so a child can sit both, provided they meet each exam's eligibility rules.

**Which exam is tougher, AISSEE or JNVST?**

They test different things. AISSEE Class 6 is a longer, broader paper — 125 questions for 300 marks over 150 minutes, including a full maths section and general knowledge. JNVST Class 6 is shorter — 80 questions for 100 marks over 120 minutes, built around mental ability, arithmetic and language. Toughness depends on your child's strengths, not on the paper alone.

**Is there a fee for JNVST?**

No. JNVST registration is free, while AISSEE 2027 charges Rs 850 for General, OBC and Defence category candidates and Rs 700 for SC and ST candidates.

**Do both exams have negative marking?**

No. Neither AISSEE nor JNVST deducts marks for wrong answers, so attempting every question costs nothing in both exams.

**Which exam should a defence-aspiring child choose?**

AISSEE, because Sainik Schools are run by the Sainik Schools Society under the Ministry of Defence and are designed to prepare students for the NDA and other officer-training academies. JNVST leads to Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalayas, which are strong academic residential schools under the Ministry of Education but are not military schools.

**What is the qualifying rule in each exam?**

AISSEE requires 25 percent in each section and 40 percent overall, except for SC and ST candidates. JNVST Class 6 requires minimum marks in each section: 14 in Mental Ability plus Environmental Studies, 7 in Arithmetic and 7 in Language.
