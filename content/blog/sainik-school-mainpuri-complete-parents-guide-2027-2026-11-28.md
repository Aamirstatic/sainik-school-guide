## Western UP's Sainik School — and the youngest of the four

Here is the fact that shapes everything else in this guide: **Sainik School Mainpuri has 90 Class 6 seats and 12 Class 9 seats for 2027-28.** Ninety is a solid intake — not the biggest in the country, but a real one, and 60 of those 90 seats belong to Uttar Pradesh children. For a family in Mainpuri, Etah, Farrukhabad, Kannauj or Auraiya, this is the Sainik School that was built for them.

Mainpuri is also the newest of Uttar Pradesh's four Sainik Schools. Lucknow is the old one, founded in 1960. Jhansi and Amethi came later. Mainpuri became functional on **22 July 2019**, when its first batch of Class 6 walked in — the 30th Sainik School in India. That newness cuts both ways, and this guide is honest about both sides: a young school with modern buildings and a small, close-knit campus, and a young school still writing its NDA record.

The setting matters too. The campus sits in Nauner Kharra village on the Agra–Mainpuri Road, about 12 km from Mainpuri railway station and bus stand. Mainpuri district is agricultural heartland — flat, fertile, and deeply connected to the land — and the region's families have sent sons to the Army for generations. The school was created to give that tradition a structured path: NDA preparation, in a residential school, close to home.

## The story — a school the Army asked for, and UP agreed to build

Mainpuri did not appear overnight. The memorandum of agreement between the Ministry of Defence and the Government of Uttar Pradesh was signed on **30 April 2015**. Four years of building followed, and on **22 July 2019** the school became functional with its first batch of Class 6, under its first principal, Lt Col Agnivesh Pandey. It comes directly under the Sainik Schools Society, Ministry of Defence — the same body that runs all 33 schools.

Why Mainpuri? Look at the map. Western Uttar Pradesh — Mainpuri, Etah, Farrukhabad, Kannauj, Auraiya — has one of the strongest military recruitment traditions in the country, yet until 2019 it had no Sainik School of its own. Families who wanted this path sent their children to Lucknow or Jhansi, hours away. Mainpuri closed that gap. It is the fourth Sainik School in Uttar Pradesh, alongside Lucknow, Jhansi and Amethi, and the one that serves the western districts.

The first batch is still young — the school has only been running for seven years, so its first Class 12 batch passed out only recently. That is worth knowing before you choose it, and we come back to it in the honest questions at the end.

## Seats for 2027-28 — the numbers that decide your child's chances

The AISSEE 2027 bulletin seat matrix is tentative — it can change before e-counselling — but it is the best picture available before the 2 December form deadline:

| Entry | Seats (2027-28, tentative) |
| --- | --- |
| Class 6 — total | **90** |
| Class 6 — boys, Uttar Pradesh domicile | 55 |
| Class 6 — boys, other states | 25 |
| Class 6 — girls (5 UP, 5 other states) | 10 |
| Class 9 — total | **12** |
| Class 9 — boys (2 UP, 5 other states) | 7 |
| Class 9 — girls (1 UP, 4 other states) | 5 |

Two things deserve your attention. First, the home-state quota does real work here: **67 percent of seats are for Uttar Pradesh-domicile children** — 60 of the 90 Class 6 seats. If your child holds a UP domicile, the competition is for those 60 seats, not the headline 90. The domicile certificate is what proves the claim, so sort it before the form, not after.

Second, the category split inside the 55 UP boys' seats: SC 8, ST 4, OBC-NCL 15, Defence 6 and General 22 — the standard reservation pattern, with 25 percent of the remainder for defence wards. And a useful detail from the 2025 counselling: the other-state seats at Mainpuri went to children from just four states — Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra and Rajasthan. If you are applying from outside UP, that is the real competition set.

Class 9 is the narrow door here — 12 seats, against 90 in Class 6. If your child is in Class 8 now, Mainpuri's Class 9 door is one of the smaller ones in the country. Apply, by all means, but put your main hope in the Class 6 window if you still have it.

## What it costs — and what the fee actually buys

A new Class 6 cadet pays **Rs 2,23,212 for 2026-27** (Rs 2,21,712 for SC/ST), as per the school's official fee document. The pieces:

| Item | Amount (2026-27) |
| --- | --- |
| Tuition fee (Rs 1,16,923 × 110%) | Rs 1,28,615 |
| Dietary charges (Rs 179.99 per day × 295 days) | Rs 53,097 |
| Clothing (as per actuals) | Rs 30,000 |
| Pocket money | Rs 3,000 |
| Incidental charges | Rs 1,500 |
| Laundry charges (as per actuals) | Rs 4,000 |
| Caution money (Gen/OBC/Def; SC/ST Rs 1,500) | Rs 3,000 |
| **Total, General** | **Rs 2,23,212** |
| **Total, SC/ST** | **Rs 2,21,712** |

Classes 7 to 12 pay **Rs 2,00,212** — clothing drops to Rs 10,000 and there is no caution money. Two honest notes. First, the fee sheet is titled "Proposed Fee Structure for AY-2026-27" and signed by the Administrative Officer — it is the school's own document, but fees rise every year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure with the school before you pay. Second, the fee includes diet: cadets live on campus and eat in the mess, so the Rs 53,097 dietary charge is not an extra on top of the total — it is inside it. No instalment schedule is printed on the sheet; ask the school office how the payment is split across the year.

The exam itself: Class 6 is 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks (Maths 50 questions for 150 marks; Language, Intelligence and GK 25 questions for 50 marks each). Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks (Maths 200). Both are pen-and-paper OMR with no negative marking, and the qualifying bar is 25 percent in each section and 40 percent overall. Apply only on the official portal, exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society — NTA has no agents, and anyone offering to file the form for a fee is not acting for NTA.

## The honest questions to ask before you choose Mainpuri

**Is it too new?** This is the fair question, and the honest answer is: it depends what you are buying. If you want a school with decades of NDA selections to point at, Mainpuri cannot show you that yet — its first batches are still early in their journey, and no headline NDA record has been publicly reported. What it can show you is the full preparation — NDA-oriented academics, NCC, physical training — on a modern campus, close to home, in a region that lives and breathes the Army. Judge it on that, not on a league table that does not exist yet.

**Are you a UP family that hasn't applied because the seats feel out of reach?** Sixty of the 90 Class 6 seats are reserved for UP children. The home-state quota is the single biggest advantage a Mainpuri family has in this exam — bigger than any coaching centre. Use it.

**What if your child is a girl?** She would join a school with 10 Class 6 girls' seats and 5 in Class 9 this year, in a system where girls have been admitted since 2021-22. Read our [girls' quota guide](/blog/girls-quota-sainik-schools-explained-2026-10-22/) for how the 10-percent rule works across all 33 schools.

**How does Mainpuri compare with the other UP schools?** Lucknow is the old, established name; Jhansi and Amethi sit in between; Mainpuri is the youngest and the closest for western UP families. All four admit through the same AISSEE, charge broadly similar fees, and reserve 67 percent of seats for UP domicile. The choice is mostly about distance, and about whether you prefer an old school's record or a young school's campus. Read our [Uttar Pradesh schools guide](/states/uttar-pradesh/) to compare all four before you fill in your school preferences.

Mainpuri is not the oldest Sainik School, the biggest, or the most famous. It is the one built for western UP — and for a family in Mainpuri district, that is the point. The school is twelve kilometres away. The seats are there. The form closes on 2 December.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Mainpuri have for 2027-28?**
Mainpuri has 90 Class 6 seats — 55 for Uttar Pradesh boys, 25 for boys from other states, and 10 for girls (5 Uttar Pradesh, 5 other states) — and 12 Class 9 seats (7 boys and 5 girls). The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Mainpuri founded?**
It became functional on 22 July 2019, when its first batch of Class 6 started, under a memorandum of agreement signed between the Ministry of Defence and the Government of Uttar Pradesh on 30 April 2015. It is the 30th Sainik School in India and the newest of Uttar Pradesh's four Sainik Schools.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Mainpuri?**
A new Class 6 cadet pays Rs 2,23,212 for 2026-27 (Rs 2,21,712 for SC/ST), as per the school's official fee document: tuition Rs 1,28,615, dietary charges Rs 53,097, clothing Rs 30,000, pocket money Rs 3,000, incidental charges Rs 1,500, laundry Rs 4,000 and caution money Rs 3,000. Classes 7 to 12 pay Rs 2,00,212. Fees rise every year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure with the school before paying.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Mainpuri?**
Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026 (11:50 PM). Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8. There is no direct admission or management quota.

**Do Uttar Pradesh children get preference at Sainik School Mainpuri?**
Yes — 67 percent of seats are reserved for Uttar Pradesh-domicile children and 33 percent for other states. For Class 6, that means 60 of the 90 seats (55 boys and 5 girls) are for UP children. A UP domicile certificate is what proves the claim, so keep it ready before the form goes in.

**Can girls study at Sainik School Mainpuri?**
Yes. Mainpuri has 10 Class 6 seats for girls in 2027-28 (5 for Uttar Pradesh girls, 5 for other states) and 5 Class 9 seats for girls. Unfilled girls' seats go to boys of the same category, as the bulletin provides.
