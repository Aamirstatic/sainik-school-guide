## Half the paper, one section

Here is the number every Class 9 parent should know before anything else: **Mathematics is 200 of the 400 marks in AISSEE 2027.** Fifty questions, four marks each, half the paper. Your child could answer every other section perfectly and still lose the merit list on Maths alone — or rescue an average paper with a strong Maths score. No other section comes close.

Last year, a parent asked me whether her son should spend his final weeks on Science, "because there is so much to revise." Honestly? Science is 50 marks. Maths is 200. The revision hours should follow the marks, not the syllabus size. That is the whole strategy in one sentence — and the rest of this article is how to spend those hours well.

## The paper you are actually sitting

| Section | Questions | Marks each | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 50 | 4 | **200** |
| Intelligence | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| English | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| General Science | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| Social Science | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| **Total** | **150** | — | **400** |

The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027, from 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM** — 180 minutes, pen-and-paper, OMR sheet, multiple-choice questions, **no negative marking**. The paper is English only. And there are two floors your child must clear: **25% in each section** (50 marks in Maths) and **40% in aggregate** (160 out of 400). The section floor is the one that quietly eliminates good students — a child who scores 180 overall but only 45 in Maths still fails the section rule. SC and ST candidates are exempt from the minimum-marks rule in the 33 Sainik Schools, but everyone else must clear both floors.

Do the arithmetic on one question. Each Maths question is worth 4 marks — a full 1% of the paper. A careless sign error costs the same as getting an entire Science question wrong twice over. That is why this section rewards accuracy over speed, and why the habits below matter more than any new topic in the final weeks.

## The topic map — what to revise, in order

The official syllabus sits in Appendix III of the NTA bulletin. These are the topics it lists for Class 9 Maths, in the order I would revise them:

1. **Algebra first** — rational numbers, linear equations, squares and cubes. This is the biggest block, and linear equations appear in both direct and word-problem form. If your child can set up an equation from a sentence, half the section is already won.
2. **Geometry next** — triangles, Pythagoras theorem, basic geometry. Our earlier piece on [where algebra meets geometry](/blog/aissee-class-9-maths-where-algebra-meets-geometry-2026-10-23/) shows how the two topics feed each other; a triangle question is often an algebra question in disguise.
3. **Number work** — percentages, profit and loss, simple and compound interest. These are the "banker" questions: straightforward, fast, and worth the same 4 marks as the hardest geometry problem. Never leave them for last.
4. **Measurement and data** — mensuration, data handling, statistics and probability. Mensuration rewards children who have memorised the formulae cold; probability rewards those who read the question slowly.

Notice what is not on the list: Class 9 Maths does not reward exotic topics. It rewards the Class 8 syllabus, done accurately, under time pressure.

## A 180-minute budget that actually works

| Block | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics — first pass | 55 | Every question, in order, no lingering. Mark the stuck ones and move on. |
| English | 20 | Comprehension first, then grammar. |
| Intelligence | 20 | Analogies and patterns — fast or skip. |
| General Science | 25 | Steady pace; do not re-read the question twice. |
| Social Science | 25 | Dates and facts — answer from memory, not deliberation. |
| Mathematics — second pass | 15 | Return to the marked questions with fresh eyes. |
| Checking + OMR bubbling | 20 | Verify every bubble against the question number. |

Two things about this budget. First, Maths gets two passes, not one — the second pass is where the 4-mark questions you skipped get rescued, and it is worth 15 minutes of anyone's paper. Second, the last 20 minutes are sacred. OMR errors — bubbling the answer for question 47 against row 48 — are the most expensive mistakes in the whole exam, and they happen to children who rush the final minutes. Our [paper attempt strategy](/blog/aissee-2027-paper-attempt-strategy-150-180-minute-plan/) explains the three-pass method in detail; this budget is the Class 9 version of it.

## Two worked examples — the kind of questions that decide ranks

**Example 1 — linear equations (the word problem).** *The sum of two numbers is 48. One number is 6 more than twice the other. Find the numbers.*

Let the smaller number be x. Then the larger is 2x + 6. So x + (2x + 6) = 48, which gives 3x = 42, so x = 14, and the larger number is 34. Check: 14 + 34 = 48. The habit to build: always write "let x be…" before touching the numbers. Children who skip that line are the ones who set up the wrong equation and then solve it perfectly.

**Example 2 — Pythagoras (the disguised question).** *A ladder 13 m long leans against a wall. Its foot is 5 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach?*

The ladder, the wall and the ground form a right triangle with hypotenuse 13 and base 5. Height² = 13² − 5² = 169 − 25 = 144, so height = 12 m. This is a 5-12-13 triple — and recognising the common triples (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17) turns a two-minute calculation into a ten-second answer. Drill the triples the way you drill tables.

## Five mistakes that cost the most marks

1. **Skipping the rough-work step.** Mental maths feels faster; it is also where sign errors live. One line of working per question, every time.
2. **Spending 5 minutes on a 4-mark question.** If a question resists two attempts, mark it and move on. The second pass exists for exactly this.
3. **Forgetting the section floor.** A child who "hates Maths" and plans to compensate in Science is planning to fail. Fifty marks in Maths is non-negotiable.
4. **Bubbling in batches without checking.** Bubble as you go, and use the final 20 minutes to match question numbers to rows. One slipped row can cost 20 marks.
5. **Revising new topics in the last fortnight.** From 17 January onwards, no new chapters. Only mocks, error notebooks and formula revision — the same final-phase rhythm as our [Class 6 Maths strategy](/blog/aissee-class-6-maths-section-strategy-150-marks/).

## The weekly rhythm for the last 11 weeks

From now to the exam, Maths needs four focused sessions a week: two on algebra and geometry, one on number work and mensuration, and one full 50-question Maths set under a 55-minute timer. Every Sunday, one full 150-question paper in 180 minutes, phone in another room, with the OMR sheet. Review every error the same evening — not the next day, when the reasoning has gone cold. In the final week, cut the volume in half and double the revision of the formula sheet and the error notebook. Sleep properly the two nights before the exam; a rested child outscores a tired one, every single time.

## FAQs

**How many marks is Mathematics in AISSEE Class 9?**

200 marks — 50 questions at 4 marks each, exactly half of the 400-mark paper. It is the biggest section by far, and the one that decides the merit list.

**How much time should I give Mathematics in the AISSEE Class 9 exam?**

About 70 minutes of the 180 minutes. That leaves roughly 20 minutes for English, 20 for Intelligence, 25 each for General Science and Social Science, and 20 minutes for checking and OMR bubbling.

**What is the minimum qualifying mark in Class 9 Mathematics?**

25 percent of the section — 50 marks out of 200 — plus 40 percent in aggregate, which is 160 out of 400. SC and ST candidates are exempt from the minimum marks rule in the 33 Sainik Schools.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027 Class 9?**

No. The NTA bulletin states there is no negative marking for a wrong response, so attempt every question — a guess costs nothing and a blank scores nothing.

**Is the AISSEE Class 9 paper in English only?**

Yes. Class 9 is English only — unlike Class 6, which offers 13 language mediums. The medium cannot be changed after the form is submitted.

**Which Maths topics matter most for AISSEE Class 9?**

Algebra and geometry carry the most weight — linear equations, triangles, Pythagoras, squares and cubes — followed by rational numbers, percentages, profit and loss, interest, mensuration, data handling, statistics and probability.
