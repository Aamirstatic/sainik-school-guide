## Nagaland's only Sainik School — and a very good year to apply

Here is the fact that shapes everything else in this guide: **Sainik School Punglwa has 120 Class 6 seats and 53 Class 9 seats for 2027-28.** The Class 6 intake ties it with several of the biggest schools in the country. The Class 9 intake is more unusual still — 53 seats is the second-largest Class 9 intake in India, after only Chhingchhip's 90. Most Sainik Schools take 10 to 30 children in Class 9. Punglwa takes 53.

That matters because Class 9 entry is usually the harder door. Fewer seats, older children, a tougher paper. At Punglwa, the door is wider than almost anywhere else — and for a family in the Northeast, it is also the closest door. Punglwa is Nagaland's only Sainik School, and for many families in Nagaland, Manipur and Arunachal Pradesh, it is the school their child can actually reach.

And the timing is good. In the same month your child would be filling the form, Punglwa has just put two cadets on the NDA final merit list — the school's best result in years. This guide tells you what the school is, what it costs, what the seats look like, and the honest questions to ask before you choose it.

## A school the people of Nagaland asked for

Punglwa is unusual among Sainik Schools because it was not simply handed down by Delhi. Its establishment was the result of an initiative by the Nagaland Chapter of the Old Boys' Association of Sainik Schools, led by Chief Minister Neiphiu Rio — himself an alumnus of Sainik School Purulia in West Bengal. The foundation stone was laid by Rio on 16 February 2004, and the school was inaugurated three years later, on 12 May 2007, by then Raksha Mantri A.K. Antony.

The PIB release from that week is worth quoting for what it says about intent: the school was set up "to provide better educational facilities to the children of far flung areas" and to meet "the aspirations of the mostly tribal people" of the region. The campus was built by the National Building Construction Corporation, with over Rs 14 crore from the Ministry for Development of North-Eastern Region and a Rs 2 crore grant from the state government.

That origin still shows. The school is administered by the Sainik Schools Society under the Ministry of Defence, and the Chief of Staff, 3 Corps, chairs its Local Board of Administration — a reminder that this is a frontier school in a frontier state, with the Army's eastern command literally next door.

One correction worth making plainly, because older pages on the internet — including an earlier profile on this very website — get it wrong: **Punglwa was founded in 2007, not 1970.** The 1970 date belongs to no Sainik School in Nagaland; it appears to be a confusion with the older schools of the region. The 2007 inauguration is confirmed by the PIB, the school's own website, and the CBSE affiliation record. We have corrected our own profile accordingly.

The state has not forgotten the school, either. On 21 November 2026, Chief Minister Rio pledged a personal contribution of Rs 50 lakh to the Old Boys' Association's Nagaland chapter, announced a state-provided prefabricated multipurpose building for the association's complex, and proposed a committee to study financial assistance and entrance-exam coaching for deserving students from economically weaker families. That committee is a proposal, not a scheme — no scholarship or fee waiver exists yet — but it is a sign that the school sits high on the state's agenda. Our full report is here: [Nagaland CM pledges Rs 50 lakh to revamp Sainik School Punglwa](/news/nagaland-cm-50-lakh-sainik-school-punglwa-revamp-2026-11-22/).

The home-state split is worth understanding, because Nagaland's numbers look different from other states. Of the 74 home-state boys' seats in Class 6, the bulletin lists SC 0, ST 5, OBC-NCL 0, Defence 17 and General 52. There are no SC or OBC-NCL home-state seats because those categories barely exist in Nagaland's population — the reservation matrix follows the state's demography. What stands out instead is the defence share: **17 of the 74 home-state boys' seats are for defence wards**, one of the highest defence allocations in the country. If your child is a defence ward with Nagaland domicile, Punglwa is one of the most favourable schools in India to apply to.

The 67% home-state quota is the whole story for Nagaland families: 80 of the 120 Class 6 seats are reserved for Nagaland-domiciled children, competing only against each other. A child from Kohima, Dimapur or Peren with a Nagaland domicile is playing a very different game from a child applying from Assam or Manipur. And in Class 9, the home-state advantage is even stronger — 45 of the 53 seats.

One detail the bulletin itself insists on: list Punglwa as a choice even if no vacancy is shown for your category. Seats open up when cadets leave, and they are redistributed by the reservation rules. Across the 33 established Sainik Schools there are 3,160 Class 6 seats this year — Punglwa's 120 are among the largest shares of them.

A few things to note before you budget. First, this is the school's own **proposed** fee document for **2026-27** — the latest the school has published — and diet, clothing and incidental charges are marked as charged on actuals, with any surplus or deficit carried to the cadet's ledger. Second, there is a variance you should know about: Careers360 and Ezyschooling still quote **₹1,76,624**, which is the 2025-26 figure. The ₹1,93,516 figure is the newer one, and the gap between the two years is roughly the 10% annual rise the Sainik Schools Society allows — so the honest way to budget is to expect the 2027-28 figure to be higher still, and to confirm it from the school before paying.

Third, the Nagaland government offers a scholarship to state-domicile candidates on conditions, plus a central share, adjusted on receipt — but as with other schools, the full fee is payable first and the scholarship is adjusted later. Don't plan on it reducing the first bill.

For context, Punglwa sits in the middle of the pack. Goalpara, the cheapest of the 33, charges ₹1,77,560 for 2026-27; older schools like Purulia are above ₹2 lakh. Budget roughly ₹2 lakh for the first year, all in.

## The honest question: what has Punglwa actually achieved?

This is the section that changed most in November — and it changed for the better.

On 7 November 2026, Nagaland Post reported that **two Punglwa cadets had made the final merit list of the NDA-157 course: Cadet Dev Dagar with All India Rank 61 and Cadet Anshik Yadav with All India Rank 125**. Both now head to the National Defence Academy at Khadakwasla. The officiating principal, Lt Col Amit Chaudhary, called it the result of "sustained hard work, discipline and dedication by both the institution and the cadets."

That result did not come out of nowhere. A month earlier, on 13 October, NEWire reported that **nine Punglwa cadets had cleared the written stage of the NDA (II) 2026 examination** — seven from the current batch and two senior cadets — following the school's own announcement from Peren on 2 October. Dev Dagar was one of those two senior cadets. Nine written clears, two final selections: that is a conversion rate any school would be proud of, and it is the first time in years that Punglwa's NDA story has had a final-merit-list ending rather than just a written-stage one.

Keep the scale honest, though. Punglwa is a young school — nineteen years old — and its NDA pipeline is still thin compared with the old giants. Kunjpura put twelve cadets on the same NDA-157 and TES-56 lists. Punglwa put two. But for a school in one of India's most remote corners, drawing its cadets from a state with a tiny population, two final selections — including an AIR 61 — is a serious result. It is also the clearest possible answer to a parent wondering whether a child from Nagaland can realistically reach the NDA from Punglwa: yes, and it happened this month.

## Life on a hill campus: what to weigh

Punglwa is not an easy school to reach, and you should weigh that honestly before you choose it. The campus sits in the foothills of the Pauna range, 17 km from Medziphema towards Peren and about 47 km from Dimapur — the nearest town with an airport and a railway station. For a family in Dimapur or Kohima, that is a manageable drive. For a family from outside the Northeast, it is a flight to Dimapur, then a road journey into the hills. Visiting your child will take planning, and the first term — when homesickness hits hardest — is when the distance feels longest.

What the distance buys is a self-contained world. The school is fully residential, with hostels built for up to 600 cadets, and it runs an Independent Company of the NCC with Army, Navy and Air Force wings as an integral part of school life. The days follow the standard Sainik School pattern — early reveille, morning PT, classes, games in the evening, prep before dinner — and the school's own history notes that it was built for a maximum strength of 600 boys, with girls now admitted alongside them since 2021-22.

If your child has grown up in the hills of the Northeast, the adjustment is mostly about the routine, not the place. If your child is coming from the plains, the weather, the food and the distance from home will all be new at once. That is not a reason to avoid Punglwa — the two cadets on the NDA list this month came through exactly that adjustment — but it is a reason to talk to your child about it before the form, not after the allotment.

## How to apply

Admission is through **AISSEE 2027 only** — there is no direct admission, no management quota, and no donation route. The form is open now on the official NTA portal, **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society**, and it closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. If you have not applied yet, read our [last-weekend checklist](/news/aissee-2027-4-days-left-last-weekend-submit-form-2026-11-28/) — today is the last full weekend before the deadline.

Eligibility is the same as everywhere: Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027 (born 1 April 2015 – 31 March 2017) and studying in Class 5; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 (born 1 April 2012 – 31 March 2014) and a passed Class 8. Girls are admitted in both classes — 12 seats in Class 6 and 4 in Class 9 this year. The application fee is ₹850 (General, OBC-NCL, Defence) or ₹700 (SC/ST), plus ₹500 if you use the late window of 3–6 December. After the exam come the answer key, the result, e-counselling on the AISSAC portal, and then the medical examination and document check.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Punglwa have for 2027-28?**
Punglwa has 120 Class 6 seats — 108 for boys (74 Nagaland domicile and 34 other states) and 12 for girls (6 Nagaland and 6 other states) — and 53 Class 9 seats (49 boys and 4 girls). Its 53 Class 9 seats are the second-largest Class 9 intake of any Sainik School in India, after Chhingchhip's 90. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Punglwa founded?**
It was inaugurated on 12 May 2007 by then Raksha Mantri A.K. Antony, after its foundation stone was laid on 16 February 2004 by Nagaland Chief Minister Neiphiu Rio. It is the 21st Sainik School in India, the first in Nagaland, and the third in the Northeast. The campus covers 300 acres in the foothills of the Pauna range, about 47 km from Dimapur.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Punglwa?**
Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8. There is no direct admission or management quota.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Punglwa?**
The school's own proposed fee document for 2026-27 puts a new Class 6 cadet's first year at Rs 1,93,516 (Rs 1,92,016 for SC/ST), including tuition of Rs 1,28,616 and diet charges of Rs 35,400. An older 2025-26 figure of Rs 1,76,624 is still quoted by some portals. Fees rise about 10 percent a year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure from the school before paying.

**Does Sainik School Punglwa admit girls?**
Yes — girls have been admitted since the 2021-22 academic session. For 2027-28 there are 12 girls' seats in Class 6 (6 Nagaland domicile and 6 other states) and 4 in Class 9.

**Has any Sainik School Punglwa cadet joined the NDA?**
Yes — in November 2026, two cadets made the final merit list of the NDA-157 course: Cadet Dev Dagar with All India Rank 61 and Cadet Anshik Yadav with All India Rank 125, as reported by Nagaland Post on 7 November 2026. Earlier in the same cycle, nine Punglwa cadets had cleared the NDA written examination.
