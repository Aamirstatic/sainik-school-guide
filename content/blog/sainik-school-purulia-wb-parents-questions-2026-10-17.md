West Bengal has exactly one established Sainik School — Sainik School Purulia. If you are a parent in Kolkata, Howrah, Siliguri or a village in Bankura, every one of your questions eventually funnels down to this single school and a single national exam: AISSEE. This is not another admission guide — for the full school profile, see our [West Bengal 2027 guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-west-bengal-guide-2027/). These are the ten questions WB parents actually ask me, answered straight.

## The language question

**"My child studies in Bengali medium. Will the exam be in Bengali?"**

Yes — for Class 6, and this surprises many parents. The AISSEE Class 6 paper is offered in **13 languages, including Bengali** (✅ CONFIRMED via the exam-pattern pages of two independent education portals). You pick the medium in the application form, and the choice is final — it cannot be changed on exam day. The Class 9 paper, by contrast, is English-only.

But read the second half of the answer carefully: Sainik Schools themselves are **English-medium, CBSE residential schools** — that is stated on the NTA's own AISSEE portal. So a Bengali-medium child can absolutely *write* the exam in Bengali, but will *study* in English from day one at Purulia. Start the English transition now — reading habits, not grammar drills — so Class 6 at Purulia doesn't feel like a foreign country. With 106 days to the exam, there is still time.

## The distance question

**"Purulia is far from us. How do children cope with hostel life?"**

Every Sainik School is fully residential — there is no day-scholar option. For a family in North Bengal, Purulia is genuinely a long journey; even from Kolkata it is a full day's travel. Two honest points. First, the school runs on a fixed annual calendar with official vacations — the rhythm becomes predictable, and children adapt faster than parents fear; homesickness peaks in the first term and fades. Second, don't romanticise the distance or dread it: visit once during an open interaction if the school offers one, and talk to parents of current cadets before you decide it is "too far". The decision should be the child's, made with eyes open.

**"Is it safe for my daughter?"**

Girls can apply to Purulia, and the Society's rule for Class 6 is **10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more** (✅ CONFIRMED, AISSAC SOP). Exact girls' seats for 2027 will be in the counselling seat matrix. These are military-run residential campuses with structured supervision — the concern I hear most is not safety but whether a girl will get enough seats to make the attempt worthwhile. She competes within the girls' pool; the 10% rule is a floor, not a suggestion.

## The money question

**"What does Purulia actually cost per year?"**

Education portals report Purulia's annual fee at roughly **Rs 1,65,130, rising about 10% a year** — treat this as *reported*, not official; confirm from the school's own prospectus at counselling time. On top of that come uniforms, books, and travel. Before the number scares you, know the relief valves: Defence Ministry scholarships for wards of serving and retired JCOs/NCOs, and West Bengal's merit-cum-means scholarships for eligible students. Our [fee-concession guide](/blog/sainik-school-fee-concession-income-slabs-how-to-apply-2026-10-16/) walks through the income slabs and how to apply — read it before you decide the school is unaffordable.

## The quota question

**"Is the 67% home-state quota real, and how much does it help us?"**

It is real, and it is the single biggest structural advantage a WB student holds anywhere in the Sainik School system (✅ CONFIRMED, AISSAC SOP). At Purulia, roughly two of every three seats are reserved for West Bengal domicile holders. A student from Bihar or Odisha can only contest the remaining third; your child contests both pools. The worked examples in our [home-state quota explainer](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-explained/) show how this plays out in practice.

The catch parents miss: the advantage only exists if the **domicile certificate** is in hand and correct. Apply now — most states take 10–30 days — and make sure the spelling matches the birth certificate exactly. The [state-wise domicile process guide](/blog/domicile-certificate-online-apply-state-wise-process-2026-10-11/) covers West Bengal's process.

## The exam questions

**"What is the paper actually like?"**

Class 6: **125 questions, 300 marks, 150 minutes** — Mathematics 50 questions (150 marks, half the paper), Language, Intelligence and General Knowledge 25 questions each. Class 9: **150 questions, 400 marks, 180 minutes**. **No negative marking** in either. Qualifying marks are 25% in each section plus 40% aggregate (not applicable to SC/ST candidates) — ✅ all CONFIRMED from the 2026 cycle, expected to repeat.

**"What if my child doesn't clear it?"**

Then Class 9 entry is a genuine second chance — same school, same exam system, two more years of preparation. Keep the child's current schooling strong rather than treating Class 6 as do-or-die; the pressure of a "last chance" narrative hurts scores more than it helps. And if neither works out, the preparation itself — maths discipline, English reading, general awareness — pays off in every board exam and competitive test that follows.

**"When should we have started? Is 106 days enough?"**

The best time was a year ago; the second-best time is today. 106 days is enough for a focused sprint *if* the syllabus is mostly covered and the remaining time goes to revision and timed mocks. If your child is starting from zero in October, be honest with yourselves: aim for a strong attempt now and keep Class 9 as the plan B, rather than burning the child out.

## The Purulia decision checklist

Answer these eight honestly, as a family, before the form opens:

| # | Question | What a "yes" means |
|---|----------|--------------------|
| 1 | Is your child okay living away from home for months? | The residential model will suit them |
| 2 | Is the domicile certificate ready or applied for? | The 67% quota advantage is secured |
| 3 | Has an English-reading habit started? | The medium transition won't shock them |
| 4 | Can the family afford ~Rs 1.6 lakh/year (reported), or a scholarship path? | Finances won't force a mid-course exit |
| 5 | Is the child in the 10–12 years age band on 31 Mar 2027? | Class 6 eligibility is clear |
| 6 | Are 2–3 hours of daily focused prep realistic for 106 days? | The sprint is sustainable |
| 7 | Does the child actually want this — not just the parents? | Motivation survives the first homesick month |
| 8 | Is there a Class 9 plan B the family accepts? | One exam doesn't decide the child's future |

Seven or eight yeses: apply with full energy. Four or fewer: fix the noes first — the school will still be there next year.

## FAQs

**Can a Bengali-medium student write the AISSEE in Bengali?**
Yes, for Class 6 — Bengali is among the 13 offered languages, chosen at application time and unchangeable later. Class 9 is English-only, and Purulia teaches in English medium.

**Is Sainik School Purulia good for girls?**
Girls can apply; Class 6 reserves 10% of vacancies or 10 seats (whichever is more) for girls. Exact 2027 seats come with the counselling matrix.

**How much does Sainik School Purulia cost?**
Around Rs 1,65,130/year per education portals (reported figure, ~10% annual rise) — confirm from the official prospectus. Defence and state scholarships can reduce it.

**Do WB students really get 67% of Purulia's seats?**
Yes — 67% of seats in the 33 established schools go to home-state domicile holders. The domicile certificate is what unlocks it.

**What if my child doesn't clear AISSEE for Class 6?**
Class 9 entry is a real second chance. Keep current schooling strong and treat the prep as an investment that pays off regardless.

**How many days are left for AISSEE 2027?**
106 days as of 17 October 2026. Exam: 31 January 2027 (tentative, CONFIRMED via NTA calendar); notification still awaited.
