## The trial is the tournament

Parents often picture a sports trial as a single afternoon: a child turns up, runs, jumps, and either makes the team or does not. In a Sainik School, it rarely works like that. The trial is the tournament itself — weeks of inter-house matches, races and meets in which every cadet already competes for their house, while the PT instructors and coaches watch.

That is why the house system matters so much to sport. A cadet does not wait to be invited to a trial; they are already in one from the moment house fixtures begin. The school team is then picked from the cadets who stood out in those house competitions, in each age group, for each game. One school's rule book puts it plainly: the school arranges inter-house tournaments specifically for the fair selection of school teams.

## The age groups, and why they matter

Most Sainik School sport is split into three broad groups — Sub-Junior, Junior and Senior — so that a Class 6 cadet is never judged against a Class 12 one. The scaling is visible in real events. At Sainik School Imphal's inter-house cross-country competition on 25 October 2026, all 555 cadets took part — 491 boys and 64 girls — with 3 km for Sub-Juniors, 6 km for Juniors and 11 km for Seniors, and Tagore House finishing as overall champions.

The same structure shows up in team games. At Sainik School Kunjpura, the 2026–27 inter-house football tournament ran separate Junior Boys', Senior Boys' and Girls' categories, with different houses winning each — Kurukshetra House took the Senior championship, Chillianwala House the Junior title, and Shakargarh House the Girls' championship. At Sainik School Kodagu's athletics meet, the overall championship went to Subroto House with 99 points, with separate best-athlete honours for senior, junior and girls' categories.

| Stage | What happens | Who is watching |
|---|---|---|
| Daily PT and games periods | Fitness, drills, practice matches, skill work | PT instructors and house staff |
| Inter-house tournaments | Every cadet competes for their house, by age group | Coaches, PT staff, house masters |
| School team selection | Strongest performers picked per game and age group | PT department, often with the Principal's approval |
| Inter-school fixtures | School team plays other Sainik Schools and cluster meets | Coaches and school leadership |

## What coaches actually look for

No school publishes a points table for selection, so any guide that claims an exact formula is inventing it. What the pattern across schools shows, as typical practice, is a blend of four things:

**1. Performance in house matches, not one performance.** A cadet who scores consistently across a tournament beats one who has a single brilliant game and disappears. Coaches remember the whole tournament.

**2. Fitness that survives the full fixture.** Cross-country, obstacle courses and athletics all reward stamina built over months of daily PT. Sainik School Balachadi's inter-house obstacle competition, for example, tests endurance, agility and teamwork across obstacles from the Burma Bridge to the eight-feet wall — the kind of event where fitness, not just ball skill, decides places.

**3. Discipline and availability.** A school team trains on a schedule and travels for fixtures. Cadets who turn up, follow instructions and keep their academic work in order are simply easier to pick — and to keep picking.

**4. The right age group, at the right time.** A strong Sub-Junior may not make a Senior team, and that is not a rejection. Teams are picked per group, and a cadet's window opens as they grow into the next group.

## The ladder above the school team

Making the school team is not the top of the ladder — it is the first rung above the house. From there, the path typically runs: house team, then school team picked from house competitions, then inter-Sainik School and cluster meets, and finally the All India Sainik Schools National Games. Not every cadet climbs all four rungs, and that is normal — the daily routine keeps every cadet active whether or not they wear a school-team jersey.

## What parents should — and should not — expect

**Do expect** your child to get a genuine chance. House competitions are built to include everyone, and a child who is keen, fit and regular at practice will be seen.

**Do not expect** a published trial date for every game, or a selection letter with reasons. Exact calendars vary by school, and selection is a coaching judgement, not an exam with a marksheet.

**Do not buy the shortcut story.** There is no verified rule that school-team membership earns marks in NDA selection, and no coach can promise it. What sport genuinely builds — stamina, composure under pressure, working in a team — is useful at SSB and in service life, but it is preparation, not a quota.

**Do ask the right questions** on a school visit or an open day: which games does the school field teams in, how often are inter-house fixtures held, and is there structured coaching or only supervision? The answers tell you more about your child's sporting life than any brochure photograph.

## A simple checklist for parents

- [ ] Encourage house games, not just one favourite sport — coaches notice versatility.
- [ ] Keep fitness steady through holidays; a long break costs more than a missed practice.
- [ ] Treat non-selection as a delay, not a verdict — many cadets make teams a year later.
- [ ] Watch for overtraining signals: persistent tiredness, falling grades, or lost enthusiasm.
- [ ] Ask the school, not social media, for the current term's fixtures calendar.

## FAQs

**How are Sainik School teams selected?**

Mainly through inter-house tournaments and regular PT and games periods, where PT instructors and coaches observe cadets over time and pick school teams from the strongest performers in each age group.

**Do all cadets get a chance to try out for a team?**

Yes, in practice. Inter-house competitions are open across houses and age groups, so every cadet competes for their house before any school team is chosen. The house tournament is itself the trial.

**What age groups do Sainik School sports use?**

Typically Sub-Junior, Junior and Senior groups, with distances and events scaled to age — for example, cross-country races of 3 km, 6 km and 11 km for the three groups at Sainik School Imphal in October 2026.

**Does being on a school team help with NDA selection later?**

There is no published rule that gives team membership marks in NDA selection. What it does build is fitness, stamina and team experience, which are genuinely useful at SSB, but it is not a shortcut to selection.

**What if my child is not picked for a team?**

It is not a verdict on their future. House games continue all year, fitness improves with the daily routine, and many cadets make teams in a later term or a later class as strength and skill develop.

**Are sports trials the same in every Sainik School?**

No. Each school runs its own calendar and its own house competitions, so exact trial dates and formats vary. The inter-house-first pattern described here is common across schools, but your child's school notice board is the final word.
