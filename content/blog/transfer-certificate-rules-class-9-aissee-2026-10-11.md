The Transfer Certificate is the most misunderstood document in the AISSEE Class 9 process — parents panic about it at the wrong time and forget it at the right time. Here's the honest truth: **nobody asks for your child's TC when you fill the AISSEE form. But everybody asks for it on admission day.** And admission day is the worst possible moment to discover a spelling mistake.

## What a Transfer Certificate actually is

A Transfer Certificate (TC) — sometimes called a School Leaving Certificate — is a formal document issued by the school your child is currently studying in. It certifies that the child was enrolled there, which class they completed, and that the school has no objection to them joining another school.

It is the formal proof that your child finished the previous class — and boards need that paper trail before registering anyone for Class 9.

## Why Sainik Schools insist on a TC for Class 9 entry

When your child gets a seat through AISSAC counselling, they transfer out of their current school mid-schooling — Class 8 done there, Class 9 beginning at the Sainik School. Documents are verified at the counselling/admission stage (confirmed in the AISSAC SOP), and the TC is what formally closes the loop on the old school.

For Class 6 entry, a TC is typically required too, when the child leaves their primary school. But the Class 9 case is genuinely stricter: the child's Class 9 board registration (CBSE or state board) needs the TC as the formal record of transfer. Without it, the Sainik School can't register your child for Class 9 with the board.

## What the TC must contain — and the mismatch problem

A valid TC for this purpose should come from a **recognised school** and confirm that the child studied in and **passed Class 8**. Sounds simple. But every year, parents hit the same wall: the name or date of birth on the TC doesn't match the AISSEE application or the birth certificate.

A parent in Kanpur once told us his son's TC said "Aarav," the birth certificate said "Aarav," and the AISSEE form said "Aarav" — one missing "h," three documents disagreeing. Admission day turned into a scramble for affidavits. Spelling and DOB mismatches are, in our experience, the number one cause of admission-day disputes — and they are entirely preventable.

Before filing the TC away, hold it next to the birth certificate and the AISSEE application and compare name, date of birth, and father's name line by line. If anything differs, get the issuing school to correct it **before** admission day. If the birth certificate itself needs correction, start early — see our [birth certificate correction guide](/blog/birth-certificate-correction-before-aissee-form-2026-10-10/).

## TC timeline: from selection to the school gate

Don't wait for the result to start thinking about documents:

1. **Result day (tentative: around the AISSEE result, expected March 2027):** Celebrate first. Then start a folder — physical or digital — and collect originals: birth certificate, domicile certificate, category certificate if applicable, and Class 8 marksheets.
2. **When Class 8 final results are declared:** Request the TC from your child's school in writing. Offices are slowest in the summer vacation — ask early.
3. **AISSAC counselling (online, at pesa.ncog.gov.in — no fee):** Have the original TC plus several attested photocopies ready. Document verification happens during admission.
4. **Admission day:** Carry the original TC. The school office verifies it and keeps a copy for the board registration file.
5. **If something is wrong:** Ask the allotted school's admission office what they will accept — a corrected TC, a school letter, or an affidavit. Their answer is the one that counts.

## Rules parents should know (and the honest unknowns)

- **You do not need the TC for the AISSEE application.** The application asks for your child's details, not the TC. Save the worry for later.
- **Request it early in the admission season.** Schools typically issue TCs after final results, and the process takes days, not minutes.
- **Keep the original safe and make attested photocopies.** The school keeps copies for its records; the original should stay with you.
- **Countersignature norms vary.** Some boards expect the TC to be countersigned by the district education officer — we can't confirm a universal rule, so ask both schools.
- **Never invent or alter a TC.** A forged or tampered document can end the admission — and invite legal trouble. Not worth it. Ever.

For unusual cases — homeschooling, an unrecognised institution, a gap year: don't guess. Call the admission office of your allotted Sainik School after counselling. Their instructions override everything on this page.

## FAQs

**Is a Transfer Certificate needed at the time of the AISSEE application?**

No. The TC is not required when you fill the AISSEE application form. It is required later, at counselling and admission, once your child is selected and allotted a Sainik School.

**What must the Transfer Certificate confirm for Class 9 entry?**

The TC should confirm the child studied in and passed Class 8 at a recognised school. Name and date of birth must match the AISSEE application and birth certificate exactly — mismatches are the most common cause of admission-day disputes.

**When should I request the TC from my child's current school?**

Ask early. Schools issue the TC only after Class 8 final results, and offices slow down in the summer vacation. Request it as soon as results are out; keep the original safe with attested photocopies.

**Is a TC also needed for Class 6 AISSEE entry?**

Yes, a TC is typically required when the child leaves the primary school too, but the Class 9 case is stricter because the child's Class 9 CBSE or state board registration needs the TC as the formal record of transfer.

**What if my child's name is spelled differently on the TC and the birth certificate?**

Fix it before admission day. Get the spelling corrected at the issuing school, or ask your allotted Sainik School what supporting document it accepts. Never arrive at admission with two conflicting names.

**Does a TC from an unrecognised school work for Sainik School admission?**

No. It should come from a recognised school — Sainik Schools verify admission documents at counselling. If in doubt, ask your allotted school directly.
