Ask ten parents about AISSEE and you will hear at least three confident statements that are simply wrong. Not lies — just old information, half-remembered from an older sibling's cycle, or something a well-meaning relative said at a family function. The problem is that parents act on these myths. They stop their child from attempting questions. They don't apply because they think girls can't. They panic when a deadline passes, not knowing a late window exists.

The NTA bulletin for AISSEE 2027 came out on 3 November 2026, and it settles most of these arguments in black and white. Here are the seven myths we hear most — and what the bulletin actually says.

## Myth 1: "Wrong answers lose marks"

This is the most expensive myth of all. **There is no negative marking in AISSEE 2027** — wrong answers and blanks both score zero, and nothing is deducted. Your child should attempt every single question, including the ones they are only half-sure about.

But — and this is the part the myth hides — there is a qualifying bar that makes guessing blindly a bad idea in a different way. Your child needs at least **25% in each section** and **40% in aggregate** to qualify (the rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates). A child who scores brilliantly in maths and 8 out of 50 in Language doesn't just lose marks. The child fails the paper. Read our full explainer on [why there is no negative marking — and what it really means for attempt strategy](/blog/aissee-negative-marking-honest-answer/).

## Myth 2: "Sainik Schools are only for boys"

No. **Girls can apply for Class 6 in all 33 Sainik Schools**, and for Class 9 where vacancies exist. In the 33 established schools, **10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more**, are reserved for girls in Class 6. For 2027, that works out to 351 girls' seats out of 3,160 Class 6 seats in the 33 schools. If anyone tells you not to apply because your daughter is a girl, they are working from a rulebook that expired years ago. Our [girls' quota guide](/blog/girls-quota-sainik-schools-explained-2026-10-22/) explains the numbers school by school.

## Myth 3: "The exam is online now"

It isn't. **AISSEE 2027 is a pen-and-paper test on an OMR sheet**, with multiple-choice questions, on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. Class 6 runs from 2:00 PM to 4:30 PM; Class 9 from 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM. That matters for preparation: your child needs practice bubbling answers on a real OMR sheet, managing a physical question booklet, and sitting still for two and a half to three hours in the afternoon. A child who has only ever clicked answers on a screen is practising for a different exam.

## Myth 4: "Miss 2 December and it's over"

Not quite. The regular window closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM** — but a **late window runs from 3 to 6 December** on payment of an additional **₹500 late fee**. After 6 December, there really is no further window, and the **correction window (7–9 December)** can fix details, not a missed application. So yes, missing the deadline costs you ₹500 and a few days of peace of mind — but it does not cost you the year. Don't let anyone tell you otherwise, and don't let the late window become your plan either: the portal is slowest in the last week.

## Myth 5: "You can change the exam medium later"

You can't. Class 6 candidates can choose **any of 13 languages** as their test medium, and Class 9 is **English only** — but the choice is **locked in once the form is submitted**. The question booklet comes only in that language, and the correction window cannot fix this one. Pick the language your child reads fastest in, not the one that sounds most impressive at a family gathering. Our [Class 6 Language section guide](/blog/aissee-class-6-language-section-strategy-50-marks/) explains why this choice is worth 50 marks.

## Myth 6: "You need 40% in every section"

Close, but wrong — and the difference matters. The rule is **25% in each section and 40% in aggregate**. A child who scores 30% in one section and 60% overall still qualifies; a child who scores 20% in one section and 70% overall does not. So the smart strategy isn't to push every section to 40%. It's to make sure no section falls below a quarter, and then let the strong sections carry the total. That is exactly how a balanced revision plan should be built.

## Myth 7: "A good AISSEE score means admission"

A good score means a place on the merit list. Admission needs more: a **medical examination** and **e-counselling** through AISSAC, where seats are allotted by rank, home-state quota (67% home state, 33% other states), category reservation and the school preferences you filled in the form. Children have lost seats at this stage over a failed medical or a carelessly filled preference list — after clearing the written paper. The exam is the door. It is not the house.

## One myth that isn't in the bulletin at all

"Your child needs coaching to clear AISSEE." The bulletin says nothing about coaching, because coaching is not a requirement — it is a choice. Plenty of children clear this exam with NCERT books, previous-year papers and a parent who keeps the routine steady. Honestly, the children who struggle most are rarely the ones without a coaching centre. They are the ones whose parents believed the wrong rules and planned around them.

So check the bulletin, not the WhatsApp group. The official portal is exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society, and our [AISSEE 2027 notification explainer](/news/aissee-2027-notification-out-applications-open-exam-jan-31-2026-11-03/) has the full date sheet in one place. If a claim about AISSEE 2027 doesn't match what you read there, treat it as a myth until proven otherwise.

## FAQs

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**

No. There is no negative marking anywhere in AISSEE 2027, so your child should attempt every question. But each section has a 25 percent qualifying minimum, so a weak section can still fail the paper even with a strong total.

**Can girls apply for AISSEE 2027?**

Yes. Girls can apply for Class 6 in all 33 Sainik Schools, and for Class 9 where vacancies exist. In the 33 schools, 10 percent of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, are for girls in Class 6.

**Is the AISSEE 2027 exam online or offline?**

Offline. AISSEE 2027 is a pen-and-paper test on an OMR sheet, with multiple-choice questions. Class 6 runs from 2:00 PM to 4:30 PM and Class 9 from 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM on Sunday, 31 January 2027.

**What happens if I miss the 2 December AISSEE deadline?**

You get one more window: 3 to 6 December 2026, on payment of an additional late fee of 500 rupees. After 6 December there is no further window, and the correction window runs from 7 to 9 December.

**Can I change the exam medium after submitting the AISSEE form?**

No. The medium chosen in the application form is locked in once the form is submitted. Class 6 candidates can choose any of 13 languages, and Class 9 is English only, so pick the language your child reads fastest in before you pay.

**What marks does my child need to qualify in AISSEE 2027?**

At least 25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate. The rule does not apply to SC and ST candidates. Clearing the written paper is only the first step — a medical examination and e-counselling follow.
