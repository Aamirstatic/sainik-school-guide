## The question I hear most from parents of daughters

"My daughter wants to join the armed forces. Can she go to a Sainik School — and does she even stand a chance, given how few seats there are for girls?"

The honest answer has two parts. Yes, she can — girls have been admitted to all 33 Sainik Schools since the 2021-22 session. And yes, the seats are genuinely limited: the reservation for girls is **10% of the total Class 6 vacancies, or 10 seats — whichever is more.** That is the rule, stated plainly. What it means in practice, how it came to exist, and why it is capped at 10% is worth understanding before you fill the AISSEE 2027 form.

## What the rule actually says (CONFIRMED)

The Sainik Schools Society's reservation policy, as published and as stated by the Defence Ministry in Parliament, holds that in **Class VI of each Sainik School, 10% of the total vacancies or 10 seats — whichever is more — is reserved for girls.** A parliamentary standing committee report quotes a ministry representative saying the same thing: 10% of Class VI seats, with a minimum of ten girls admitted every year.

A few things parents often get wrong about this: it applies to **Class 6 only** in the 33 Sainik Schools — not Class 9 intakes; and **there is no separate exam for girls** — your daughter writes the same AISSEE paper as the boys, with the same qualifying rule (25% per section, 40% aggregate).

## The real history: 2018 pilot to 2,000+ girl cadets

This did not happen overnight. The timeline matters, because it explains why the policy still feels "experimental" in the ministry's own words:

- **June 2018:** the Defence Ministry admitted **six girl cadets to Class 6** at Sainik School Chhingchhip in Mizoram as a pilot project. Around the same time, the Uttar Pradesh Sainik School in Lucknow — the country's oldest Sainik School, founded 1960 — admitted **15 girls to Class 9**.
- **Academic session 2020-21:** Raksha Mantri approved admission of girls (capped at 10% of authorised intake) at **five more schools** — Bijapur, Chandrapur, Ghorakhal, Kalikiri and Kodagu.
- **15 August 2021:** the Prime Minister announced that **all 33 Sainik Schools would admit girl cadets**. Admissions began across the board from the 2021-22 session.
- **2024-25:** the Defence Ministry told Parliament that the number of girl cadets across existing and new Sainik Schools had **crossed 2,000** — up from six girls in one Mizoram classroom in seven years.

## Why 10%? The answer sits in Dehradun, not the school

Here is the thing most articles on this topic miss: the 10% figure was not pulled out of thin air. Group Captain Madhu Sengar, the principal of Sainik School Kapurthala, put it bluntly to Education Times: **the 10% intake mirrors the NDA's own reservation for women.** The primary mission of a Sainik School is to prepare students for the National Defence Academy, and the NDA currently reserves roughly 10% of its seats for women. The feeder ratio matches the destination ratio — "even if a school has 100 seats, only 10 can go to girl students, regardless of how many apply or qualify."

For deeper context on what comes after, see our [complete guide for girls aiming at the NDA](/blog/girls-in-nda-complete-2027-guide-2026-10-08/) — the Sainik School quota is the entry gate, but the NDA path is where the real planning starts.

## What actually happens to girls inside the schools

The fear parents voice — usually the mother's — is whether a school built for boys treats girls as an afterthought. The people running these schools are reassuringly specific: **physical training is identical for girls** — running, swimming, horse riding, no diluted version — and Group Captain Sengar says girl cadets "usually settle in within three to four months, and many a times perform better than boys." Dropout rates among girls stay low; medical reasons, not disappointment, are the main cause.

The real bottlenecks are bricks and staff, not intent. Many campuses were built decades ago for boys, and separate dormitories had to be constructed — the ministry has sanctioned **₹3.3 crore per Sainik School** for girls' infrastructure. A teacher at Sainik School Purulia in West Bengal, where about 40 girls study up to Class X, named the second constraint plainly: a **shortage of female teachers** in institutions managing girls through adolescence.

## Will the cap ever rise?

The honest answer: **not this admission cycle.** The ministry describes the current 10% intake as experimental, and expansion hinges on two conditions — upgraded infrastructure (separate residential facilities, more female teachers and counsellors) and a full review after the first batch of girl cadets completes Class 12, which no school has reached yet under this model. The decision rests with the Defence Ministry.

## What parents of daughters should do now

1. **Eligibility is identical.** For AISSEE 2027 Class 6, your daughter needs the same thing a boy needs: born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017 (10–12 years on 31 March 2027), studying in or passed Class 5. (🔶 EXPECTED — projected from the 2026 pattern; final in the NTA bulletin.)
2. **Choose schools carefully in counselling.** The girls' seat count per school follows the 10%/10-seat formula, and the [home-state 67% quota](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-67-percent-explained-2026-10-17/) operates alongside it. Read the admission matrix in the bulletin before ranking your choices.
3. **Prepare exactly like the boys do.** Same paper, same cutoffs, same medicals. The only special preparation is logistical — the [girls' admission checklist](/blog/sainik-school-for-girls-2027/) covers documents and timelines for daughters.
4. **Do not wait for the cap to change.** It will not change before AISSEE 2027. The seats that exist are real and fillable today.

## FAQs

**What is the girls quota in Sainik Schools?**
In Class 6 of each Sainik School, 10 percent of the total vacancies or 10 seats — whichever is more — is reserved for girls. This was stated by the Ministry of Defence in Parliament and reflects the Sainik Schools Society's admission policy.

**Is the girls quota available in all 33 Sainik Schools?**
Yes. Following a phased rollout that began with a 2018 pilot in Mizoram, girls have been admitted to all 33 Sainik Schools since the 2021-22 academic session, and the 10 percent Class 6 reservation applies across them.

**Do girls write the same AISSEE paper as boys?**
Yes. Girls seeking Class 6 admission write the same AISSEE paper as boys — same syllabus, same age criteria, same 25 percent per-section and 40 percent aggregate qualifying marks.

**Why is the girls quota capped at 10 percent?**
The 10 percent figure mirrors the NDA, where roughly 10 percent of seats are reserved for women candidates. Since Sainik Schools exist primarily to prepare students for the NDA, the feeder ratio matches the destination ratio.

**Will the girls quota increase beyond 10 percent?**
The Defence Ministry calls the current intake experimental. Expansion depends on infrastructure upgrades — separate dormitories, more female teachers — and a review after the first full batch of girl cadets completes Class 12.
