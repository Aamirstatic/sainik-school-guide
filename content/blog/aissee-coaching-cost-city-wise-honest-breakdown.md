## The honest answer first: there is no city-wise price list

Search for "AISSEE coaching fees in Lucknow" or "in Dehradun" and you will find confident numbers. Ignore the confidence. No board, no ministry and no coaching association publishes city-wise AISSEE coaching prices, and institutes rarely publish a city average at all — they publish their own fee, for their own batch, in their own city. Any article that gives you a neat table of "Delhi: Rs. X, Lucknow: Rs. Y, Patna: Rs. Z" is either quoting one institute per city and calling it the city price, or guessing.

That is not a reason to avoid the question. It is a reason to ask it differently. The price of AISSEE coaching is not decided by the city on the board. It is decided by four things: the format (online, classroom, or residential), the duration (a 3-month crash course or a year-long batch), the batch size, and what is bundled in (hostel, mess, material, test series). Once you price those four things, the city mostly drops out — a residential programme in a small town can cost more than a classroom batch in a metro, because you are paying for a bed and three meals, not a pin code.

| Online AISSEE coaching, complete course (SainikSchoolCadet) | Rs. 20,000–40,000 |
| General coaching guide estimate (SD Campus) | Rs. 20,000–60,000 a year on average |

Read that table as a range, not a ranking. The cheapest row is not the worst and the dearest row is not the best — the Rs. 1,80,000 residential figure buys a year of boarding, food and supervision, while the Rs. 20,000 online figure buys tuition only, taught to a child sitting at home. They are different products, and comparing them on price alone is like comparing a bus ticket with a hotel stay because both involve travel.

Two patterns are still worth noting. First, online batches cluster at the bottom of the range — roughly Rs. 20,000–40,000 for a complete course — because the institute has no classroom rent or hostel cost to pass on. Second, the jump from classroom to residential is the biggest jump in the whole table: hostel and mess at Rs. 7,500–9,000 a month adds roughly Rs. 90,000–1,08,000 to a year, which is why residential totals reach Rs. 1,80,000 while the tuition underneath is often Rs. 35,000–45,000.

## The five things that actually move the price

**1. Format.** Online is cheapest, classroom sits in the middle, residential is dearest. If your child studies well at home and you can supervise the routine, online tuition plus home practice gives you most of the value at the lowest price. If the child needs a fixed routine and someone watching, classroom or residential earns its premium — but only if the batch is genuinely supervised.

**2. Duration.** A 90-day crash course (Rs. 20,000–25,000 in the published examples) looks cheap until you realise it covers three months of teaching. A year-long batch at Rs. 40,000–60,000 is more money in total but less per month of teaching. Compare fees per month of teaching, not per course, or the crash course will always look like a bargain it is not.

**3. Batch size.** This is the one factor no brochure advertises honestly. A batch of 15 children and a batch of 150 children can carry the same fee, but only in the first does the teacher know your child's weak subject. Ask the number directly, and ask how many children were in last year's batch, not this year's brochure.

**4. What is bundled.** Material, test series, doubt sessions and interview or medical guidance are sometimes included and sometimes charged separately. A Rs. 35,000 fee with a Rs. 10,000 test series on top is a Rs. 45,000 fee. Get the all-in figure in writing before you compare two institutes.

**5. The city premium — which is mostly a rent premium.** Institutes in metros pay more rent, and some of that reaches the fee. But the coaching hubs for AISSEE are not the metros: Lucknow, Dehradun, Jaipur and Patna appear again and again in published fee pages, and their classroom fees overlap heavily. Do not pay a "Delhi price" for a "Delhi brand" unless the batch size and mock schedule justify it — the AISSEE paper is the same paper wherever the classroom sits.

## The total cost, not just the tuition

Parents routinely under-budget by 30–50% because they price the tuition and forget the rest. Before you commit, add up the full year:

- **Tuition fee** — the headline number, Rs. 20,000–60,000 in most published examples.
- **Hostel and mess** — only for residential programmes, but the largest single extra: roughly Rs. 7,500–9,000 a month where published, or about Rs. 90,000–1,08,000 for a year.
- **Material and test series** — sometimes included, sometimes Rs. 3,000–10,000 extra; confirm in writing.
- **Travel** — weekly or monthly trips home for a residential child, plus the exam-day trip.
- **The AISSEE application fee itself** — small next to coaching, but part of the year's exam budget, and payable to NTA, not to the institute.
- **The opportunity cost nobody prices** — if the coaching fee eats into the money you are saving for Sainik School itself, read our guide to [the first-year costs parents miss](/blog/saving-for-sainik-school-fees-uniform-hidden-costs/) before you sign. A coaching fee that delays the school fee is a bad trade.

## When coaching is worth it — and when it is not

Coaching is worth paying for when it buys something your home cannot: a fixed daily routine, a teacher who spots that your child loses marks in Intelligence rather than Maths, and regular mock tests under exam timing. It is not worth paying for when it buys only attendance — a large batch, rare mocks, and no individual feedback. In that case, the cheaper route is often the better one: NCERT books, previous years' papers, and a disciplined home routine, which is exactly what our guide to [preparing without coaching](/blog/aissee-preparation-without-coaching/) lays out, plus a comparison of [online versus offline coaching](/blog/sainik-school-online-coaching-vs-offline/) if you are torn between formats.

A practical rule of thumb: visit two institutes, sit in on one class at each, and ask the same five questions at both — batch size, number of mocks, what happens when a child scores badly in a mock, what is included in the fee, and the refund policy if you leave after a month. The institute that answers all five plainly is usually the one worth the fee, whatever the fee is. The one that answers with a selection percentage and a discount deadline is selling, not teaching.

And one warning that matters more than any price table: no institute can guarantee selection, and any "100% selection" claim should end the conversation. AISSEE selection depends on the child's paper, the state merit list and medical fitness — none of which a fee can buy.

## A checklist before you pay

- [ ] Get the all-in fee in writing: tuition, material, test series, hostel, mess.
- [ ] Ask the batch size and the number of mock tests, not just the duration.
- [ ] Compare per month of teaching, not per course, between crash and long-term batches.
- [ ] Check the refund policy for leaving after one month.
- [ ] Confirm what happens after a bad mock — remedial classes, or silence?
- [ ] Keep the coaching budget separate from the Sainik School first-year savings.
- [ ] Take a free demo class before paying anything, wherever one is offered.

Coaching is a tool, not a ticket. Priced honestly — format first, city last, all-in figure in writing — it can be the best money a family spends in the preparation year. Priced on a brochure's promise, it is the easiest money to waste.

## FAQs

**What is the average cost of AISSEE coaching in India?**

Published institute fees cluster roughly between Rs. 20,000 and Rs. 60,000 a year for classroom or online tuition, with residential programmes reaching about Rs. 1,80,000. There is no official national average, so treat any single average figure as a rough guide, not a price.

**Is online AISSEE coaching cheaper than classroom coaching?**

Usually yes. Published online batches start around Rs. 20,000, while classroom tuition in the same institute often costs more, and residential coaching with hostel and mess costs several times more again.

**Does a higher AISSEE coaching fee mean a better result?**

No. The fee buys hours, batch size, mocks and hostel facilities, not selection. Ask for the batch size, the number of mock tests, and how weak topics are handled before judging a fee.

**What extra costs come on top of the AISSEE coaching fee?**

Hostel and mess charges, study material, test-series fees, travel, and the AISSEE application fee itself. Residential programmes bundle hostel and mess, but day programmes often charge material and test series separately.

**Can a child crack AISSEE without coaching?**

Yes. Many children prepare at home with NCERT books, previous papers and mock tests. Coaching helps mainly with routine, doubt-clearing and exam practice, not with secret syllabus.

**When is AISSEE coaching not worth the money?**

When the batch is so large that no one checks your child's work, when mocks are rare, or when the fee forces the family to cut into school fees or Sainik School savings. In those cases a smaller programme plus home practice is the better spend.
