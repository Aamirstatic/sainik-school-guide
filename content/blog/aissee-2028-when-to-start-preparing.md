## The honest answer first

If your child is in Class 4 or 5 right now and you are already thinking about AISSEE 2028, you are asking the right question at the right time. The answer most parents don't hear often enough: **you don't need to start "preparing for the exam" yet — you need to start building the foundations the exam will test.**

There is a difference, and it matters. Children who spend a calm year getting strong at reading, arithmetic, and thinking logically walk into the serious preparation phase with confidence. Children who are thrown into mock tests two years early usually burn out before the exam arrives. I've seen both — and the first group wins, every time.

## When exactly to begin

The AISSEE 2028 exam is expected in **January 2028** (EXPECTED — the pattern is a January exam every year; the 2027 exam is marked 31 January 2027 in NTA's official calendar). Working backwards from that:

- **Now to early 2027 (foundation phase):** no exam books, no pressure. Build reading speed, mental maths, tables up to 20, and daily reading in English.
- **Mid 2027 to December 2027 (preparation phase):** the real AISSEE preparation begins — NCERT-based study, previous years' papers, timed practice.
- **October 2027 (expected):** the notification and form window — this is when documents, photo specs and school preferences need to be ready, not started.

In short: start building foundations now if you haven't, but don't start "the AISSEE grind" until the child is roughly a year out.

## The quarter-by-quarter roadmap (2027 calendar year)

This is the original plan I give parents who ask me for a 2028 timeline. It assumes the exam lands in January 2028, following the usual pattern.

| Quarter | Focus | What a typical week looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Jan–Mar 2027 | Reading + arithmetic foundations | 30 min reading daily; tables to 20; Class 5 NCERT maths alongside school |
| Apr–Jun 2027 | Grammar + logical thinking | English grammar basics; puzzles and pattern questions 15 min/day |
| Jul–Sep 2027 | Syllabus coverage begins | Topic-wise study from NCERT; first previous-year paper as a diagnostic |
| Oct–Dec 2027 | Timed practice + revision | Weekly mock, error log, OMR bubbling practice; form window (expected) |

The logic behind the order: the Class 6 AISSEE paper gives **150 of 300 marks to Mathematics** and 50 each to language, intelligence, and GK. So maths fluency — not memorised formulas, but the ability to compute quickly and accurately — is the highest-return investment you can make early. Intelligence (reasoning) improves dramatically with daily 15-minute exposure; it is the easiest section to "grow" over a long runway.

## What early starters get wrong

**Mistake 1: Starting mock tests too early.** A Class 4 child scoring 40% on an AISSEE mock learns nothing except that the test is hard. Mocks are a measuring tool for the last 3–4 months, not a teaching tool for the first year.

**Mistake 2: Skipping NCERT for guidebooks.** The AISSEE Class 6 paper is built on the Class 5 NCERT syllabus. Our detailed guide on the [AISSEE application and preparation process](/blog/aissee-application-form-2027-guide/) covers how the exam connects to school learning — guidebooks are practice material, not the base.

**Mistake 3: Ignoring English reading.** The language section is only 50 marks, but weak reading slows down *every* section, including maths word problems. Twenty minutes of daily reading is the single cheapest improvement available.

**Mistake 4: Letting preparation eat childhood.** An 11-year-old who studies with dread performs worse than one who studies with curiosity. If you haven't read it, our piece on [how 11-year-olds handle exam pressure](/blog/how-11-year-olds-handle-exam-pressure/) is worth your evening — it applies double to early starters.

**Mistake 5: Assuming early = guaranteed.** Selection depends on exam-day performance and the merit list, not on how many months you prepared. Start early for *competence*, not for certainty.

## FAQs

**When should a child start preparing for AISSEE 2028?**
A relaxed, foundation-first start in early 2027 works well for most children — roughly a year before the expected January 2028 exam. Serious, exam-oriented preparation can wait until mid-2027.

**Is Class 4 too early to start AISSEE preparation?**
Not for the foundations. Reading habits, tables, mental maths and basic grammar built in Class 4 make the Class 5 preparation year far less stressful. What should wait is mock-test pressure.

**How many hours should a Class 5 student study for AISSEE?**
About 90 minutes to two hours of focused work daily is plenty, alongside school. Consistency beats long hours at this age.

**Does early preparation guarantee a Sainik School seat?**
No. Early preparation builds competence, but selection depends on exam-day performance, the number of vacancies, and merit. No amount of early study changes the competition.

**Which subjects matter most for AISSEE Class 6?**
Mathematics carries the highest weightage — 150 out of 300 marks in the Class 6 paper — followed by language, intelligence and general knowledge at 50 marks each.

**Should we join a coaching for AISSEE 2028 preparation?**
Coaching is optional. NCERT textbooks plus previous years' papers cover the syllabus honestly. Join a coaching only if your child needs the discipline of a structured batch, not out of panic.
