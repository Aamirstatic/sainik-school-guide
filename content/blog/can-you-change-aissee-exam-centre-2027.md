Here's a question that lands in our inbox every single year, usually in a panic, usually in January: "We picked the wrong exam city — can we change it now?"

The short answer is: probably, but only in December — and only if you act inside a three-day window. After that, the answer is no, full stop. So let's get the whole picture straight now, while the form is still open and a mistake costs you ten seconds instead of a year.

## How exam centres actually work in AISSEE 2027

When you fill the form, you don't pick one centre. You pick **four exam cities, in order of preference**, from the list in Appendix IV of the information bulletin. NTA then allots a centre based on your choices and on availability — the number of seats at each centre is limited by its capacity.

Two things parents rarely realise:

- **Your first choice is not guaranteed.** If your first-choice city fills up, NTA moves to your second, third or fourth choice. And if too few candidates pick a city at all, NTA may not open a centre there — which is why the order of all four cities matters, not just the first one.
- **The city is not the centre.** The advance city intimation slip on **11 January 2027** tells you the *city*. The exact school or building, with its address, appears only on the admit card, available from **25 January 2027**. So even after January, you are still waiting for the final detail. Our explainer on [what to check on the city slip and admit card](/news/aissee-2027-exam-city-slip-jan-11-admit-card-jan-25-what-to-check-2026-11-13/) lists every field to verify when they arrive.

## Can you change it? The one window that exists

The moment you pay the fee, your form locks. The bulletin says it plainly: after payment, the information provided cannot be edited, and no request for a change or amendment will be entertained.

There is exactly one exception: the **correction window, 7 to 9 December 2026** (up to 11:50 PM), for "correction in particulars" of the application form. 🔶 **EXPECTED:** in past AISSEE cycles, exam-city preference has typically been editable during the correction window, and our earlier guide to [what you can and cannot edit](/blog/aissee-correction-window-what-you-can-cannot-edit/) says the same. But the final list of editable fields for 2027 is set by NTA in the bulletin — so when the window opens, check the portal's editable-fields list first, and change the cities immediately if the option is there. Do not assume, and do not wait till 9 December evening.

After 9 December, there is no second window, no helpdesk exception, and no offline request that works. The bulletin is blunt about it: no change will be entertained.

## What you can and cannot change — the full picture

| Field | Before payment | Correction window (7–9 Dec) | After 9 Dec |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exam city preferences (4 cities) | Yes — edit freely | 🔶 Typically yes, per past cycles; confirm in the bulletin | No |
| Name, date of birth, category | Yes — edit freely | Yes, for permitted particulars | No |
| Photo, signature, certificates | Yes — re-upload | Yes, re-upload as specified | No |
| Question-paper medium | Yes — choose carefully | No — cannot be changed after submission | No |
| Mobile number and email | Yes | No — cannot be changed after payment | No |
| School preference list | Yes — edit freely | Yes, for permitted particulars | No |

The pattern is simple: the things that identify your child and decide their paper — medium, mobile, email — lock the moment you submit and pay. The things that are genuinely correctable get one three-day chance. Treat the form as final the day you pay, and treat the correction window as a safety net, not a plan.

## How to choose your four cities so you never need the window

Honestly, the best fix for a wrong exam city is never picking a wrong one. Here is the checklist we give parents before they touch the form:

1. **First choice: the nearest city with a realistic centre.** Not the city you *wish* had a centre — the nearest city on the bulletin's list that you can actually reach.
2. **Second and third: your backup routes.** Pick cities on different road or rail lines from home, so one cancelled train doesn't strand you.
3. **Fourth: the safe fallback.** A big city — a state capital or a major town — where a centre is almost certain to exist, even if it's a longer journey.
4. **Check the city code twice.** NTA states it is not responsible for problems caused by a wrongly selected city code. A mistyped code can send your child to a city you never intended. Read the code, read the city name next to it, and read them again.
5. **Think about 31 January, not November.** The exam is on a Sunday afternoon — Class 6 from 2:00 to 4:30 PM, Class 9 from 2:00 to 5:00 PM. A city that looks fine on a map can mean a 5 AM departure in January fog. If the journey is over three hours, plan to travel the previous evening.

One more thing worth doing while you're at it: check your [photo and signature specs](/blog/aissee-2027-photo-signature-specs-size-background-rejections/) before you pay. Photo and signature rejections are the most common reason forms get stuck, and unlike the exam city, a bad photo is something you can fix in an afternoon — if you catch it before payment locks everything.

## What if the allotted city is far from home?

It happens, even to careful parents — a first choice fills up, and the slip on 11 January names a city two districts away. At that point you cannot change it. What you *can* do is plan well:

- **Book travel the day the city slip arrives** (11 January), not when the admit card comes. Refundable tickets and hotel bookings cost little and remove the last-week panic.
- **Travel the previous evening.** A 2:00 PM exam with a morning journey in January fog is a risk you don't need.
- **Recheck the admit card on 25 January** for the exact centre address and reporting time — and if anything on it is wrong, call the NTA helpdesk (011-40759000 / 011-69227700, aissee@nta.ac.in) the same day.

A father once told us his son's centre was allotted 280 km from home because he had listed cities by "famous name" instead of by distance. The boy still wrote the exam — they drove the previous evening and stayed overnight — but the family spent the whole month before it worrying about the journey instead of the paper. The form gives you four choices for a reason. Use all four, in the right order, and the journey becomes a footnote instead of the story.

## The bottom line

- Pick four cities in the right order, check the codes twice, and pay only when you're sure.
- If you do slip up, the correction window (7–9 December) is your one chance — 🔶 **EXPECTED** to cover exam-city preference, per past cycles, but confirm against the bulletin's editable-fields list.
- After 9 December, nothing changes. The city slip comes 11 January, the admit card 25 January, and the exam is 31 January 2027.

And if you haven't submitted the form yet, do it this weekend — our [five reasons to submit now, not on 2 December](/news/aissee-2027-18-days-left-submit-this-weekend-5-reasons-2026-11-14/) explain why, and our timeline of [what happens after you submit](/news/aissee-2027-form-submitted-what-happens-next-4-dates-2026-11-14/) covers the four dates that follow. The form is paperwork. The exam is the real work — and the centre should be the last thing on your mind when January arrives.
