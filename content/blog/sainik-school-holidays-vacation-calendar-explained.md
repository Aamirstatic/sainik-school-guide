If you're reading this, you probably have one question that matters more than any other: *when do I actually get to see my child?*

The honest answer, up front: **Your child will come home for roughly two to three months a year.** The biggest chunk is the summer vacation — about six to seven weeks. Then there's a shorter winter or festival break — about three to four weeks. Those two stretches are your family time for the year. The rest of the year your child is in school, and you'll meet them on monthly visiting days — the [visiting-day system](/blog/how-often-can-parents-visit-sainik-school/) works very differently from a day school.

The catch is that **there is no single national vacation calendar.** Every date below is real, but each belongs to a specific school — by the end of this article you'll know where to find your own school's dates.

My cousin's son joined a Sainik School two years ago. Before his first summer vacation, the family counted the days for six weeks — his mother cooked enough food for an army platoon. That's what summer vacation feels like in a Sainik School family: not a holiday, a reunion.

## Why There Is No Single National Calendar

All Sainik Schools run under the Sainik Schools Society (Ministry of Defence), yet there is no uniform calendar — each school publishes its own, and the differences are real.

Three practical reasons: **weather** (southern schools break earlier for summer while northern ones keep going into June), **festivals** (southern autumn breaks cluster around October–November; northern winter breaks in December–January), and **exams** (board exams, AISSEE admissions, and local schedules decide when cadets can be released).

So treat a neighbour's "Sainik Schools get a 45-day summer break from 1 May" as one school's story, not a rule.

## The Pattern: What Vacations Actually Look Like

Strip away the date differences and nearly every Sainik School follows the same rhythm — two main breaks:

**Summer vacation — roughly 45 to 50 days.** The long one: every published calendar I've checked lands in this range — 45 days at Sainik School TVM, 47 days at KSRSS in 2026-27, 42 days at Nagrota in 2026-27. The *window* moves (late April in some schools, June into July in others), but the *length* is remarkably consistent.

**Winter or festival break — roughly three to four weeks.** Shorter, and timed very differently by region. In the 2026-27 calendars, Amaravathinagar's winter vacation starts 2 November 2026 (report back 22 November), while KSRSS runs 19 October to 16 November. Northern schools generally take this break in December–January instead. Festival holidays and a few national-holiday days come and go in between, but for most families these aren't homecoming windows.

One detail worth knowing: at least one school, Kalikiri, sends cadets home for winter vacation immediately after the Parent-Teacher Meeting. If your school does the same, your travel plans depend on the PTM schedule — don't book non-refundable tickets before dates are confirmed.

## Before Your Child Leaves for Vacation: A Parent Checklist

Vacation doesn't start when the child boards the train — it starts a week or two earlier, in your head:

- **Get the dates in writing.** The school website, the PTM notice, or the warden's message will carry the official departure and report-back dates. A screenshot counts.
- **Never assume an extension.** Kalikiri's published instructions state it plainly: "request for extension of leave will not be granted." Report-back dates are fixed, and late reporting can invite disciplinary trouble.
- **Attend the PTM if there is one.** At some schools cadets proceed on vacation right after the PTM — missing it can mean confusion about departure formalities.
- **Collect the holiday homework.** Kalikiri emails it to parents. Get your child started in the first week home — not the last three days before reporting back.
- **Sort out who picks up younger cadets.** Chittorgarh requires Classes VI to VIII to proceed on vacation only with a parent or guardian. Check your school's rule before a grandparent shows up at the gate alone.
- **Book return travel early.** Everyone reports back on the same day — trains and flights near the school fill up fast.
- **Plan real family time, and go easy on tuition.** Six weeks sounds long until it's gone. The break is doing its job by letting the child recover.
- **Watch for the post-vacation dip.** A child who doesn't want to go back — teary, quiet, clinging — is common in the first year, not proof the school is wrong for them. [Here's how parents can help with homesickness](/blog/homesickness-in-sainik-school-how-parents-can-help/).

## FAQs

**Is there one fixed vacation calendar for all Sainik Schools?**
No — each school publishes its own calendar. Published 2026-27 examples show summer vacations starting anywhere from mid-April (Amaravathinagar) to mid-June (Sujanpur Tira). Never plan travel on another school's dates.

**How long is the summer vacation in Sainik Schools?**
Roughly 45 to 50 days. Published examples: 47 days at Kranthiveera Sangolli Rayanna Sainik School (2026-27), 45 days at Sainik School TVM, and 42 days at Sainik School Nagrota (2026-27). The window moves between schools, but the length is fairly consistent.

**When is the winter vacation in Sainik Schools?**
It depends on the region. Some southern schools take it in October–November — for 2026-27, Amaravathinagar starts 2 November (report back 22 November) and KSRSS runs 19 October to 16 November — while northern schools typically take their winter break in December–January. Your school's calendar is the final word.

**Can vacation dates be extended if travel plans change?**
Generally, no. Sainik School Kalikiri's published instructions state that requests for extension of leave will not be granted, and report-back dates are fixed. Plan travel around the official dates, not the other way round.

**Do cadets get homework during vacations?**
At least some schools assign it — Kalikiri's documented practice is to email holiday homework to parents before cadets depart. Ask your school how it handles vacation assignments so your child isn't blindsided in the final week.

**Who can take the child home at the start of vacation?**
For younger classes, expect a strict pickup rule. Chittorgarh's published notice requires Classes VI to VIII cadets to proceed on vacation only with a parent or guardian. Confirm your school's rules in advance — don't find out at the gate.
