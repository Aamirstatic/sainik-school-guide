## Assam's only Sainik School — and its biggest Class 9 door

Here is the fact that shapes everything else in this guide: **Sainik School Goalpara is the only Sainik School in Assam.** A child from Guwahati, Dibrugarh, Silchar or Jorhat who wants a Sainik School education has exactly one in-state option — and that makes the home-state quota the whole story.

Under the Sainik Schools Society rules, **67 percent of seats go to children domiciled in the home state**. At Goalpara that means 81 of the 120 Class 6 seats are reserved for Assam children, competing only against each other. The remaining 39 seats are open to children from every other state in India. If your child holds an Assam domicile, this is one of the most favourable seat arithmetic of any Sainik School in the country.

And then there is the second fact, the one that makes Goalpara genuinely unusual this year: its **49 Class 9 seats are the largest Class 9 intake of any Sainik School in India**. Four schools have no Class 9 vacancies at all this year; most have between 5 and 30. Goalpara has 49. For a family with a child in Class 8, that changes the calculation completely — Class 9 entry here is a real plan, not a lottery ticket.

## Sixty-two years old, and the oldest name in the North-East

Goalpara was established on **12 November 1964**, under the Sainik Schools Society of the Ministry of Defence, with **Lt Col D. N. Sahani** as its founder principal — he served from 11 June 1964 to 6 January 1969. It began in a temporary campus in Goalpara town and moved to its permanent site at Mornai, near Rajapara village, in **1976**. It celebrated its Golden Jubilee on 12 November 2014, with a year-long programme run with its Old Boys Association.

That age matters. Goalpara is one of the older Sainik Schools, and it shows in the one number parents should weigh most heavily: **more than 200 of its former cadets — "SSGians" — serve as officers in the Armed Forces**, through the NDA, IMA, OTA, AFA and NAVAC, with around 500 more having joined through other entries such as CDS. A good number of alumni have also become IAS and IPS officers, doctors and engineers. For a school whose stated aim is to prepare cadets academically, physically and psychologically for the NDA, six decades of that record is the strongest evidence on the table.

The campus itself is part of the story. At **505 acres** on the southern bank of the Brahmaputra, in the shadow of the Garo Hills, it is one of the largest Sainik School campuses in the country — big enough that the school's own site calls it a "sprawling area" rather than listing facilities. (Wikipedia gives the figure as about 509 acres; the school's own site says 505, and we use the school's figure.) The nearest railway station is Guwahati, about 130 km away, and Assam State Transport buses run regularly from Goalpara town to the school.

## The seats: 120 in Class 6, 49 in Class 9

This is the part parents get wrong most often, because older admission guides quote different numbers — some still list 80 Class 6 seats, the 2026-27 figure. The AISSEE 2027 bulletin says **120**, and the bulletin is the only source that settles it. We read both appendices directly from the official NTA bulletin PDF for this guide, so these are the numbers to plan on. They are tentative — the bulletin says so — but they are the official tentative numbers.

| Girls | 7 (SC 1, ST 1, OBC 2, Defence 1, General 2) | 5 (SC 1, ST 1, OBC 1, Defence 1, General 1) | 12 |

| Class 9 (2027-28) | Home state (Assam) | Other states | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boys | 28 (SC 6, ST 2, OBC 6, Defence 4, General 10) | 16 (SC 2, ST 1, OBC 4, Defence 2, General 7) | 44 |
| Girls | 2 (SC 0, ST 0, OBC 1, Defence 0, General 1) | 3 (SC 1, ST 0, OBC 0, Defence 1, General 1) | 5 |
| **Total** | **30** | **19** | **49** |

Read that Class 9 table twice before deciding. Forty-nine seats is the biggest Class 9 intake in the country — ahead of Nalanda's 48 and Rewari's 45 — and 30 of them go to Assam children. A General-category Assam boy is competing for 10 seats in Class 9, which is a genuinely winnable number; at most schools that figure is two or three. Class 6, with 120 seats, is on the larger side too: only Tilaiya (140) and Amaravathinagar (122) take more. For context, all 33 Sainik Schools together have 3,160 Class 6 seats and 731 Class 9 seats in 2027-28. Goalpara alone holds about one in fifteen of the country's Class 9 seats.

The reservation math inside those seats follows the standard pattern: SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27%, with 25% of the remainder for defence wards — the same rules we explain in our [home-state quota guide](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-explained/).

## The fee: the cheapest in the system — and still honest about what we know

Here is a pleasant surprise. Among the 2026-27 fee sheets of all 33 Sainik Schools, **Goalpara's is the lowest: ₹1,77,560 for a new Class 6 cadet** (₹1,76,060 for SC/ST). The school's own fee document, dated 9 May 2026, breaks it down:

- Tuition fees: ₹1,28,615
- Dietary charges: ₹28,025
- Clothing charges (basic): ₹2,500
- Pocket money: ₹3,000
- Incidental charges: ₹1,300
- Misc charges: ₹11,120
- Security (refundable): ₹3,000 (₹1,500 for SC/ST)
- **Total, General: ₹1,77,560 · SC/ST: ₹1,76,060**

Old (continuing) cadets pay ₹1,74,560, with no security deposit. Careers360 lists an older figure of ₹1,49,214 — a different year, and the two cannot both be the 2027-28 fee. The school has not published a 2027-28 sheet yet, and tuition rises about 10 percent a year under Sainik Schools Society rules, so budget **around ₹1.95 lakh for the first year** and confirm the exact figure from the school before paying. Defence wards and SC/ST families should also ask the school directly about scholarships and fee concessions — the school's fee sheet lists different security money by category, and concession rules are applied at admission, not after.

## A day at Goalpara

We could not verify Goalpara's own published daily timetable, so what follows is the standard Sainik School day — the pattern every school in the system runs, with local timings varying slightly. Do not quote these hours as the school's own.

Reveille is early, around 5 AM, and the day starts with PT and drill. Then bath, breakfast and assembly, and a full morning of classes — seven or eight periods, English medium, CBSE syllabus, until about 2 PM. Afternoons are for lunch, rest and clubs; late afternoon is games and NCC, and this is where Goalpara's 27th NCC Company shows up in the routine rather than on a brochure. Evenings are prep: supervised study where the day's lessons are revised and homework is finished, because there is no going home to do it. Lights out is around 10–10:30 PM, and the next morning starts the same way.

For an 11-year-old joining Class 6, the shock is not the study — it is the routine. Waking yourself, making your bed, keeping your kit in order, eating on time, living with thirty other boys in a dormitory. Parents who prepare their child for the routine, not just the exam, have a far easier first term. Our [Assam guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-assam-complete-guide-2027/) covers the same ground for the state, and our [Imphal guide](/blog/sainik-school-imphal-complete-parents-guide-2027-2026-11-26/) covers the other old North-East school for comparison.

## How to apply

Admission is through **AISSEE 2027 only** — there is no direct admission, no management quota, and no donation route. The form is open now on the official NTA portal, **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society**, and it closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. Our advice, published this morning, is to submit by **today, Friday 27 November** — the weekend is your buffer, not your plan. The fee is ₹850 (General/OBC-NCL/defence) or ₹700 (SC/ST); the late window runs 3–6 December with an extra ₹500, and corrections can be made from 7–9 December.

- **Class 6:** age 10–12 on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017 — and currently in Class 5. Boys and girls both eligible.
- **Class 9:** age 13–15 on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014 — and must have passed Class 8 from a recognised school at the time of admission. English only.
- **The exam:** Sunday, 31 January 2027. Class 6 is 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks; Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks. No negative marking; 25% per section and 40% aggregate to qualify (not applicable to SC/ST).

After the exam comes the result, then AISSAC e-counselling — online, no fee, run by the Sainik Schools Society — where your school preferences and the home-state quota decide the allotment. Choose your preferences carefully: putting Goalpara first as an Assam-domiciled applicant is the single highest-value decision in the whole process, and our [AISSAC choice-filling guide](/blog/how-to-choose-school-preferences-aissac-counselling/) explains why the order matters more than most parents think.

## The honest questions to ask

Before you commit a year of your child's life to this school, ask these — of the school, of parents whose children study there, and of yourself:

1. **Is my child ready to live 18 km outside a small town, 130 km from Guwahati?** Distance from home is the hardest part of the first term, and it is worse when visits mean a full day's travel.
2. **Are we applying for Class 6 or Class 9?** If Class 9, Goalpara is genuinely the best Class 9 bet in the country — 49 seats — but keep a parallel plan anyway: RMS, JNVST, or next year's attempt.
3. **Can we afford roughly ₹1.8–1.95 lakh a year, every year, for seven years?** The first-year fee is not the cost. The cost is seven years of it, rising about 10 percent a year. Goalpara is the cheapest Sainik School, and it is still a serious commitment.
4. **Does my child actually want this?** A Sainik School is a wonderful education and a hard life in equal measure. A child who wants to be there thrives; a child who was pushed there counts the days. Ask them, and listen to the answer.

Goalpara is not the most famous Sainik School, and it does not pretend to be. What it is, is Assam's only one — with 120 Class 6 seats, the country's largest Class 9 intake, a 505-acre campus, a 62-year record of sending officers to the Armed Forces, and the lowest fee in the system. For an Assam family with a child in Class 5 or Class 8, that combination is genuinely hard to beat. The form closes on 2 December. Today is the day to fill it.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Goalpara have for 2027-28?**

Goalpara has 120 Class 6 seats — 108 for boys (74 Assam domicile and 34 other states) and 12 for girls (7 Assam and 5 other states) — and 49 Class 9 seats, the largest Class 9 intake of any Sainik School in India. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Goalpara founded?**

It was established on 12 November 1964 under the Sainik Schools Society, and moved to its permanent 505-acre campus near Mornai, about 18 km from Goalpara town, in 1976. Its founder principal was Lt Col D. N. Sahani.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Goalpara?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Goalpara?**

The school's own fee sheet for 2026-27 puts a new Class 6 cadet's first year at Rs 1,77,560 (Rs 1,76,060 for SC/ST) — the lowest 2026-27 fee among the 33 Sainik Schools. Careers360 lists an older figure of Rs 1,49,214. Tuition rises about 10 percent a year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure from the school before paying.

**Does Sainik School Goalpara admit girls?**

Yes — girls have been admitted since the 2021-22 academic session. For 2027-28 there are 12 girls' seats in Class 6 (7 Assam, 5 other states) and 5 in Class 9, with a dedicated girls' hostel, Chandraprabha.

**Is Sainik School Goalpara the only Sainik School in Assam?**

Yes. It is Assam's only Sainik School, which is why the 67 percent home-state quota matters so much here — 81 of the 120 Class 6 seats are reserved for Assam-domiciled children.
