## The maths problem is a time problem

Ask any parent whose child sat AISSEE last year what went wrong, and you will rarely hear "my child didn't know the maths." What you hear is: "he knew it, but he ran out of time."

Look, the numbers explain it. In Class 6, Maths is 50 questions worth 150 marks — half the paper — inside a 150-minute exam shared with three other sections. In Class 9, Maths is 50 questions worth 200 marks — again half the paper — inside 180 minutes. Speed in this one section decides the result more than any other skill your child can build.

And here's the good news: speed is trainable. It is not talent. It is a habit built by timed practice, and 80 days is plenty — if the practice is structured. This plan uses the 11 weeks left before 31 January 2027, and it assumes your child already knows the Class 5 (or Class 8) syllabus reasonably well. If the basics are shaky, fix those first; no drill plan saves a child who hasn't learned fractions yet.

## The three skills that actually make you faster

Speed in AISSEE Maths is not one thing. It's three:

**1. Instant recall.** Tables up to 20, squares up to 20, cubes up to 10, common fractions as decimals (1/8 = 0.125, 3/4 = 0.75). A child who pauses to work out 7 × 8 loses ten seconds a question. Ten seconds times fifty questions is eight minutes — gone.

**2. Question triage.** Not every question deserves the same time. The child who spends four minutes wrestling one hard mensuration problem while ten easy ones wait unread is losing marks, not gaining them. The rule we teach: first pass, solve everything you can in under 90 seconds; second pass, the medium ones; third pass, guess the rest. There is no negative marking, so nothing is ever left blank.

**3. OMR fluency.** Bubbling answers takes longer than children expect — roughly 5 to 8 minutes across a full paper if done carelessly, one question at a time. Practise bubbling in batches of ten, straight from the question paper, on a real OMR sheet. It sounds trivial. It saves real minutes on exam day.

## The 11-week drill plan

Do the daily drill every day, and the Sunday paper every week. The whole plan needs about 45 minutes a day — no more. Longer sessions don't make children faster; they make them tired.

| Weeks | Focus | Daily drill (25–30 min) | Sunday paper |
|-------|-------|------------------------|--------------|
| 1–3 | Recall + easy speed | 10-question timed sets (15 min limit); tables, squares and fraction-decimal flashcards for 10 min | One 50-question Maths-only set in 75 minutes |
| 4–6 | Triage habit | 15-question mixed sets (20 min limit); mark every question E (easy), M (medium) or H (hard) before solving | Full Class 6 paper (150 min) or Class 9 paper (180 min), with OMR sheet |
| 7–9 | Weak-topic attack | Timed sets drawn only from your child's two weakest topics, plus 10 min of mixed revision | Full paper; review every wrong answer the same evening |
| 10–11 | Exam simulation + taper | Alternate days: one timed Maths set, one timed mixed-section set; in the final days, light sets only (10 questions a day) and revise the formula sheet and the questions your child got wrong twice | Full paper under strict exam conditions — same start time, 2 PM, no phone in the room; one final full paper on Sunday 24 January, then rest |

One honest warning about the Sunday paper: the first two or three will feel bad. Your child will finish late, or rush, or leave questions. That is the point. The paper is where the speed is built — the weekday drills just keep the tools sharp.

## The four time-traps to kill now

**Trap 1: Re-reading the question three times.** Teach your child to underline the numbers and the actual ask on the first read. Most re-reading happens because the child didn't read properly once.

**Trap 2: Rough work everywhere.** One messy margin, numbers scattered, and the child can't find their own working. One rough-work column, top to bottom, question numbers marked. It sounds fussy; it saves minutes.

**Trap 3: The "one more minute" hard question.** Set a hard rule: no single question gets more than 3 minutes on the first pass. Circle it, move on, come back. The marks for that question are the same as the marks for the easy one waiting two pages later.

**Trap 4: Checking everything at the end.** There is no "end" with spare time in AISSEE. Check each answer as you bubble it. The last five minutes are for the circled questions, not for re-doing the whole paper.

## A small scene from last year

Last year, a parent called us in a panic in the last week of December. Her son was scoring well in untimed practice but finishing only 35 of the 50 Maths questions in mock tests. We told her to stop adding new chapters and do just one thing: ten-question timed sets, every evening, for three weeks, with a stopwatch on the table. By the third week he was finishing 46. He didn't learn new maths in those three weeks. He learned to move. That's what this plan does.

## FAQs

**How much time does a Class 6 student get per Maths question in AISSEE?**
The Class 6 paper is 150 minutes for 125 questions, and Maths is 50 questions for 150 marks. If you split time evenly, that is about 3 minutes per Maths question — but the smart split is closer to 2 minutes per easy question, saving time for the harder ones.

**How many days are left for AISSEE 2027?**
As of 12 November 2026, 80 days remain until the exam on 31 January 2027. Applications close on 2 December 2026.

**Should my child attempt every Maths question even if unsure?**
Yes. There is no negative marking in AISSEE 2027, so a wrong answer and a blank both score zero. Attempt everything — but guess last, after the questions you know are done.

**What is the best way to practise Maths speed for AISSEE?**
Short timed sets of 10 questions with a stopwatch, done daily, beat long untimed study. Add one full 150-minute paper every Sunday, and practise bubbling answers on an OMR sheet so the real exam has no surprises.

**Which Maths topics repeat most in AISSEE Class 6?**
Number systems, fractions and decimals, percentages, ratio and proportion, simple interest, mensuration (area and perimeter), and basic geometry appear year after year. Our guide to the 20 most repeated question types lists them with examples.

**How fast should my child be by exam day?**
A good target for Class 6 is finishing the 50 Maths questions in about 80 to 90 minutes with at least 5 minutes left for review. That leaves a comfortable buffer for the other three sections inside the 150-minute paper.
