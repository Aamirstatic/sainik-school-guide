## The first confusion: there are two study certificates, not one

Parents searching for the "AISSEE study certificate" usually find two different requirements and assume they are the same document. They are not. The certificate appears twice in the AISSEE process, at two different stages, for two different purposes:

| Stage | Who needs it | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Online application (now, before 2 December 2026) | Only students already studying at an approved New Sainik School | A school studying certificate, certified by the principal, uploaded as a PDF |
| Admission (after selection, at the allotted school) | Every selected candidate | A study certificate signed by the principal or headmaster of the previous school, submitted with the school leaving certificate and annual progress report |

If your child studies at an ordinary school and is applying to a regular Sainik School, you do not need to upload a study certificate with the AISSEE 2027 application form. If your child already studies at an approved New Sainik School, you do — and it must come from the principal. Getting this distinction wrong in either direction costs time: uploading a document nobody asked for, or missing the one document your category requires.

## What the application-stage certificate must contain

For New Sainik School students, the published document list describes the requirement plainly: a "certificate from the principal certifying that the applicant is studying in the approved new Sainik School", in PDF format, 50 KB to 300 KB.

In practice, when you go to the school office, ask for a certificate that states:

- The child's full name, spelled exactly as it appears on the birth certificate and on the AISSEE form
- The class the child is currently studying in
- The name and address of the school, and that it is an approved New Sainik School
- The academic session
- The principal's signature and the school seal

This is practical advice, not a prescribed official format — the NTA information bulletin is the final word on formats. But a certificate missing any of those five elements is the kind that gets questioned, because each element answers a question the verifier will ask: who is this child, where does he or she study, and who certifies it.

## What the admission-stage certificate must contain

After selection, the allotted Sainik School sends the successful candidate a list of documents for admission. On the published lists, that list includes a study certificate, a school leaving certificate (original), and the annual progress report, alongside the birth certificate, domicile certificate, category certificate where applicable, and the medical fitness report.

The study certificate at this stage is described as signed by the principal or headmaster of the previous school — Class 5 for a Class 6 admission, Class 8 for a Class 9 admission. The logic is simple: the receiving school wants the previous school to certify, in its own words, that this child studied there. A certificate signed only by a class teacher, or an unsigned printout from a school portal, does not carry the same weight. Get the principal's signature and the school seal on it.

## The five wording errors to avoid

These are the mistakes that turn a straightforward certificate into a verification problem:

1. **A name that does not match the form.** Even one letter different — "Aarav" on the form, "Arav" on the certificate — creates a mismatch the verifier must resolve. Check the spelling character by character against the birth certificate before leaving the school office.
2. **The wrong class or session.** A certificate that says Class 4 when the child is in Class 5, or that omits the academic session entirely, does not prove current enrolment. Make sure both are stated.
3. **No seal, no signature from the principal.** A certificate on school letterhead without the principal's signature and the school seal reads as an incomplete document. Both are part of what makes it a certificate rather than a note.
4. **A scanned copy nobody can read.** The upload format is PDF, 50 KB to 300 KB. A faded scan where the signature and seal are blobs will be treated as unreadable. Scan in colour, zoom in on the scan, and confirm the signature and seal are legible before uploading.
5. **Confusing it with the leaving certificate.** The study certificate proves current study; the school leaving certificate proves the child has left. Admission asks for both. Do not submit one in place of the other, and do not assume the school will issue the leaving certificate before the admission process asks for it — schools normally issue it only when the child actually leaves.

## A simple checklist before you upload or submit

- [ ] Child's name matches the birth certificate and the AISSEE form exactly
- [ ] Class and academic session are stated
- [ ] School name and address are complete
- [ ] Principal's or headmaster's signature is present
- [ ] School seal is present and legible
- [ ] Scan is in colour, legible, PDF, between 50 KB and 300 KB (for uploads)
- [ ] Original is kept safely — the allotted school will want originals at admission, not just scans

## Timing: do not leave this to December

AISSEE 2027 applications close on 2 December 2026, with a late-fee window from 3 to 6 December and a correction window from 7 to 9 December. School offices, meanwhile, get slower in December as term work piles up. If your child needs the application-stage certificate — that is, if he or she studies at an approved New Sainik School — get it this week, while the principal's office has time to issue it properly. If your child will need the admission-stage certificate later, you can wait until selection, but confirm now that the school knows how to issue one, so there is no scramble in April.

## FAQs

**Does every AISSEE applicant need a study certificate at application time?**

No. At the online application stage, a school studying certificate is mandatory only for students already studying at an approved New Sainik School, where it must be certified by the principal. For other applicants, the study certificate becomes relevant at the admission stage, after selection, when the allotted school asks for it along with the school leaving certificate and annual progress report.

**Who must sign the AISSEE study certificate?**

The principal or headmaster of the child's current school. The document lists for AISSEE admission specify a study certificate signed by the principal or headmaster of the previous school — Class 5 for Class 6 admission, Class 8 for Class 9 admission. A certificate signed by a class teacher alone, without the principal's signature and the school seal, is not the same document.

**What should the study certificate state?**

At minimum: the child's full name exactly as it appears on the birth certificate and the AISSEE form, the class the child is studying in, the school's name and address, and the academic session. Ask the school to add its seal and the principal's signature, and check the spelling of the child's name character by character before you leave the office.

**What file format and size does the AISSEE upload need?**

The published document lists for AISSEE applications put certificates in PDF format at 50 KB to 300 KB. Scan the signed certificate in colour, check that the signature and seal are legible in the scan, and confirm the file size before uploading — an unreadable scan is as useless as a missing one.

**Is the study certificate the same as the school leaving certificate?**

No. The study certificate proves the child is currently studying in a particular class at a particular school. The school leaving certificate (transfer certificate) proves the child has left that school. AISSEE admission asks for both: the study certificate and the school leaving certificate, plus the annual progress report.

**What if my child's name on the study certificate differs from the AISSEE form?**

Get it corrected at the school before you upload or submit it. Name mismatches between the study certificate, the birth certificate and the application form are among the commonest reasons a document is questioned at verification. Do not wait for the correction window — and note that for AISSEE 2027 the correction window runs 7 to 9 December 2026, after applications close on 2 December.
