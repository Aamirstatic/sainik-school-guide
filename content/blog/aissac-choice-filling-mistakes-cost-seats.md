Every year, children qualify in AISSEE with ranks that could have fetched a Sainik School seat — and their parents lose it at the choice-filling screen. AISSAC, the All India Sainik Schools Admission Counselling, is the online e-counselling through which qualified candidates are allotted schools, school-wise and category-wise, on merit, after the written result. The exam tests your child. Choice filling tests you.

Here are the seven mistakes I see parents repeat every cycle, and what to do instead.

![Parent and child planning school choices on a study calendar](/images/inbody/aissac-choice-filling-mistakes-cost-seats.webp)

## Mistake 1: Filling only 2–3 dream schools

The classic error. Parents list Sainik School Korukonda, Kunjpura or Ghorakhal and stop. AISSAC allots seats round by round, on rank, reservation and availability — a short list means your child can slip through every round with nowhere to land.

**Do this instead:** fill every school you would genuinely accept. A lower-preference school where your child actually gets in beats a dream school where they don't. You can always review the [40%/60% route for New Sainik Schools](/news/new-sainik-schools-40-60-route-explained-2026-09-29/) to see how many more options that adds to your list.

## Mistake 2: Ranking schools by prestige, not by real chances

Parents order choices by the school's reputation. Counselling doesn't care about reputation — it runs on your child's rank against the cutoffs of previous rounds. A famous school placed first where your child's rank is far below last year's closing rank is a wasted first choice; a strong but less famous school placed sixth might have been a lock.

**Do this instead:** before counselling opens, study last year's round-wise closing ranks for your category and gender, and order your list top-down from "dream but possible" to "near-certain". Ambition is fine in the first three slots — but anchor the rest in data.

## Mistake 3: Ignoring the home-state quota — the single biggest lever you have

This is the mistake with the largest mathematical cost. In the 33 established Sainik Schools, **67% of seats are reserved for candidates domiciled in the state where the school is located, and 33% for other states** (CONFIRMED — NTA information bulletin pattern, reported across education portals). Within each, SC 15%, ST 7.5% and OBC-NCL 27% reservations apply, plus 25% of the remaining seats for defence/ex-servicemen wards.

Read that again: a school in your own state gives your child a seat pool roughly twice as large as the same school in another state. Parents who rank five outside-state schools above the one in their own state are voluntarily competing in the smaller pool.

**Do this instead:** place at least your home-state old Sainik School(s) high in the list, and treat outside-state choices as bonus bets. And get your [domicile certificate](/blog/domicile-certificate-sainik-school-guide/) ready long before counselling — the quota you claim must match the certificate you can produce.

## Mistake 4: Pretending New Sainik Schools don't exist

Many parents fill only the 33 old schools and ignore the New Sainik Schools entirely, because they don't understand the admission routes. That leaves dozens of seats on the table. New Sainik Schools admit through two routes: roughly **40% open-merit seats** filled from AISSEE candidates, and a **60% route** for students studying in the approved school itself (CONFIRMED — AISSEE 2026 bulletin pattern).

**Do this instead:** treat well-rated New Sainik Schools in your region as genuine choices, not afterthoughts. Read our explainer on [how the 40/60 route works](/news/new-sainik-schools-40-60-route-explained-2026-09-29/) before you dismiss them.

## Mistake 5: Category or domicile mismatch at document verification

AISSAC runs on what your AISSEE form says — but the seat is confirmed only when original documents verify it. A child allotted a seat under the OBC-NCL quota who cannot produce a valid central-list OBC-NCL certificate, or under the home-state quota without a matching domicile certificate, loses the seat. Every year it happens.

**Do this instead:** audit your certificates now, during the notification wait, not in March. The [caste certificate format guide](/blog/caste-certificate-format-aissee-reservation-claims/) covers exactly which format NTA accepts, and the domicile guide covers the state-side paperwork. If a certificate won't be ready, don't claim that category or quota.

## Mistake 6: For girls — filling too few choices

Girls' seats are limited everywhere: a minimum of **10% of Class 6 seats or 10 seats, whichever is higher**, across all categories (CONFIRMED — NTA bulletin pattern). That minimum is real, but so is the competition. Parents of daughters often fill only the two or three schools they have heard admit girls.

**Do this instead:** fill every school with girls' seats where you would send your daughter. Read our [girls' quota breakdown](/news/girls-quota-sainik-schools-aissee-2027-2026-10-01/) to see how the quota actually works across categories.

## Mistake 7: Not locking choices, and skipping later rounds

Counselling has multiple rounds, and seats open up in later rounds when allotted candidates don't join or fail the medical. Parents who don't lock their choices, don't check round-2 and mop-up results, or assume "round 1 is the final answer" leave seats unclaimed.

**Do this instead:** lock your choices before the deadline, screenshot the confirmation, and check every round's result. Track the medical stage too — the allotment is provisional until the medical is cleared.

## FAQs

**What is AISSAC in AISSEE admissions?**
AISSAC is the online e-counselling through which AISSEE-qualified candidates are allotted seats in Sainik Schools and New Sainik Schools, school-wise and category-wise, on merit. It runs after the AISSEE result.

**How many school choices should I fill in AISSAC?**
As many as you would genuinely accept, ordered by your real chances. A 2–3 school list of dream schools is the most common way a qualifying rank goes to waste.

**Does my home state help in AISSAC counselling?**
Yes — decisively. In the 33 established schools, 67% of seats are reserved for home-state domiciled candidates versus 33% for other states, so home-state schools offer roughly double the seat pool.

**Should I include New Sainik Schools in my AISSAC choices?**
Yes. They admit through a 40% open-merit route via AISSEE plus a 60% route for students of the approved school, which meaningfully expands your options.

**Can I correct a wrong category or domicile during AISSAC?**
Counselling runs on your AISSEE form data, verified against original certificates. A mismatch at verification can cancel a seat — fix certificates before the form closes, not during counselling.

**Do girls have separate choices in AISSAC?**
Girls compete for a minimum of 10% of Class 6 seats (or 10 seats, whichever is higher) across all categories. Fill every school with girls' seats rather than only the famous names.
