Here's a sentence no parent expects to hear in February: "Your child scored 201 out of 300 — and did not qualify."

It sounds impossible. It is also exactly how AISSEE works: the exam has **two** pass marks, and most families only ever hear about the second. With 74 days left until the exam on 31 January 2027, this is the rule to understand now — while there is still time to fix the weak section.

## The rule, in one paragraph

To qualify in AISSEE 2027 for a Sainik School, a child must score **at least 25% in every section** AND **at least 40% in aggregate**. Miss either one and the child does not qualify, whatever the total. The rule does not apply to SC and ST candidates. The total is not the pass mark — each section is its own small exam, and the weakest section decides everything.

## The exact minimums — Class 6 and Class 9

| Class | Section | Section total | 25% pass mark | Minimum correct answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class 6 | Language | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 6 | Mathematics | 150 | 37.5 | **13** (39 marks) |
| Class 6 | Intelligence | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 6 | General Knowledge | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 6 | **Aggregate** | **300** | **120** | — |
| Class 9 | Mathematics | 200 | 50 | **13** (52 marks) |
| Class 9 | Intelligence | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 9 | English | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 9 | General Science | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 9 | Social Science | 50 | 12.5 | **7** (14 marks) |
| Class 9 | **Aggregate** | **400** | **160** | — |

Two things jump out. First, the minimums look small — seven correct answers in a 25-question section. That is precisely why parents underestimate them. Second, Maths carries half the paper, so it swallows most of the preparation time — and that is exactly how the trap is set.

## Three worked examples that show the trap

**Example 1 — the maths star who fails.** A Class 6 child scores Maths 135 out of 150, Language 30, Intelligence 26, and GK 10. Total: **201 out of 300 — 67%**. GK is 10 out of 50, below the 12.5 pass mark. **Result: not qualified.** Ten marks in GK erased a near-perfect maths score.

**Example 2 — the balanced 150 that passes.** Another Class 6 child scores Maths 72, Language 26, Intelligence 26, GK 26. Total: **150 out of 300 — 50%**. Every section clears 25%, aggregate clears 40%. **Result: qualified.** Fifty-one marks fewer than Example 1 — and this child goes through to the merit list while the maths star does not.

**Example 3 — the near-miss that hurts most.** A Class 6 child scores Maths 147, Language 12, Intelligence 40, GK 40. Total: **239 out of 300 — nearly 80%**. Language is 12 out of 50 — six correct answers, one short of the seven needed. **Result: not qualified.** One question. That is the margin this rule works on. The Class 9 version is identical: 328 out of 400 (82%) still fails if English is one question short of its minimum.

## Why this rule exists — and why it punishes lopsided preparation

Honestly, the rule is fair. A Sainik School wants a cadet who can cope with every subject in a residential school, not a child brilliant at one thing and blank at another. The 25% line is a floor, not a target — it only catches children who have effectively ignored a section. Nobody fails it because the section was hard; they fail it because it was never practised — almost always GK for Class 6 children who live inside maths workbooks, and English or Social Science for Class 9 children who do the same.

## What to aim for instead: safe targets

Qualifying is not the same as getting a seat — the merit list is where the real competition happens, and cutoffs vary by state and category. But if the 25% line is the floor, here is a sensible target for the next 74 days: **60% in every section**.

| Class | Safe target per section | Safe total |
|---|---|---|
| Class 6 | Language 30, Maths 90, Intelligence 30, GK 30 | **180 / 300** |
| Class 9 | Maths 120, English 30, Intelligence 30, Science 30, Social Science 30 | **240 / 400** |

These are our editorial targets, not NTA cutoffs. A child at 60% in every section has cleared the qualifying rule with room to spare, and is in realistic contention for a seat in most states.

## The 74-day fix: weakest section first

If a mock test shows one section below 25%, that section becomes the first job of every study day — not the last, when the child is tired:

1. **Diagnose this week.** One full-length timed mock, marked section by section. Write down the section scores, not just the total.
2. **Give the weakest section 20 minutes a day, every day.** For GK, 10 facts a day; for Language or English, one comprehension passage and ten grammar questions; for Intelligence, one pattern or series set.
3. **Re-test the weak section every Sunday.** A 25-question section test takes half an hour. Watch the number climb past 25%, then 40%, then 60%.

One more thing: **there is no negative marking** in AISSEE 2027. A guessed answer can only add marks; a blank adds nothing. Attempt every question — a single guessed correct answer in Language is, quite literally, the difference between qualifying and not in Example 3.

## What qualifying does not do

Clearing the 25% and 40% lines only makes your child eligible for the merit list. The seat is decided afterwards — by merit position, reservation category, domicile and the 67% home-state quota, medical fitness, and the AISSAC counselling rounds. One caution: qualifying rules can differ by school route and category, so if your child is applying to an approved New Sainik School, check the exact rule for that route in the bulletin.

The point is not to frighten anyone. Seven correct answers per section is a low bar. The danger is only for the child who prepared brilliantly in three sections and never opened the fourth. Seventy-four days is more than enough to make sure that child is not yours.

## FAQs

**What are the qualifying marks in AISSEE 2027?**

At least 25% in each section and 40% in aggregate, for Sainik Schools. The rule does not apply to SC and ST candidates. Check the bulletin for the exact rule on your child's school route.

**Can my child fail AISSEE 2027 even with a high total score?**

Yes. If any one section falls below 25%, the child does not qualify — no matter how high the total is. A Class 6 child scoring 201 out of 300 with only 10 out of 50 in GK would not qualify.

**How many questions must a Class 6 child get right in each section to qualify?**

At minimum, 7 correct in Language, 13 in Maths, 7 in Intelligence and 7 in GK. That is the bare pass line — aim much higher.

**Does the 25% rule apply to New Sainik Schools as well?**

The bulletin summary states the rule for Sainik Schools, and notes that qualifying rules differ by school route and category. Check the exact rule for your child's route in the bulletin.

**If my child meets the qualifying marks, is admission guaranteed?**

No. Qualifying only makes your child eligible for the merit list. The seat then depends on merit position, reservation, domicile and the home-state quota, medical fitness, and counselling.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**

No. The bulletin states there is no negative marking for a wrong response, so your child should attempt every question.
