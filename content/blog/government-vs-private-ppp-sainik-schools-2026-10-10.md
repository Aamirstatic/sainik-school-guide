## Why this question keeps coming up

Two parents ask me the same thing every admission season: "There is a Sainik School near our city that is run by a private trust — is it the real thing?" The honest answer is that there are now two genuinely different kinds of institutions sharing the Sainik School name, and the difference matters for your pocket, your child's daily routine, and your expectations. Here are the seven differences that actually matter.

## The 7 differences that matter

### 1. Who owns and runs them

The **33 original Sainik Schools** — from Kazhakootam, the first under the Ministry of Defence's scheme, to Korukonda, Kunjpura and the rest — are fully government institutions run by the Sainik Schools Society under the Ministry of Defence. **New Sainik Schools** (officially "New Sainik Schools in partnership mode," colloquially PPP Sainik Schools) are private schools, NGOs, trusts or state government schools that have been *affiliated* with the Sainik Schools Society under the scheme the Union Cabinet approved in October 2021. They stay their own institutions; the Society gives them the framework and oversight, not ownership.

### 2. Why the second type exists at all

The government was explicit about the motive: Sainik School-style education — discipline, leadership, NDA preparation — should reach more students than 33 campuses can hold, without the Centre building and staffing every new campus itself. The target was 100 new schools. It is an access play, not a replacement.

### 3. The fee gap — and the safety net

Original schools are highly subsidised because the government pays the bills. New Sainik Schools charge whatever the partner school charges, which is often meaningfully higher. The safety net: the Ministry of Defence provides financial support on a merit-cum-means basis — up to **50% of the fee**, subject to a ceiling of **Rs 40,000 per student per year**, for up to **50% of the class strength** (maximum 50 students per class). Translation for parents: do not assume the fee you see is the fee you will pay — but do not assume you are in the subsidised half either. Ask the school in writing how many merit-cum-means seats it had last year.

### 4. Oversight and how a school gets approved

Original schools are built and inspected by the government itself. New Sainik Schools go through a gauntlet: an online application, a Documents Scrutiny Committee of five serving uniformed officers, then a physical inspection by a School Evaluation Committee headed by the District Magistrate alongside the principals of the local Navodaya/Kendriya Vidyalaya and the nearest Sainik School, and finally an Approval Committee with the Sainik Schools Society's honorary secretary and a Brigadier-rank officer. It is serious scrutiny — but a school can still clear paperwork and deliver a weaker on-ground experience, which is why difference 7 matters.

### 5. Curriculum: same spine, different body

New Sainik Schools must deliver the **Academic-Plus** curriculum prescribed under the Sainik School framework — the discipline, physical training and value-education layer — but they keep their own regular board curriculum (CBSE or otherwise) underneath it. The original 33 are CBSE residential schools built from the ground up around one routine. If you are choosing between a new PPP school and an original school, the question is not "which syllabus" but "which daily life."

### 6. Admission: one exam, both doors

Here is what surprises many families: admission to both types goes through the **same AISSEE**, conducted by NTA. The AISSEE 2026 bulletin added three new partnership schools to the participating list — Srri SPK Public Senior Secondary School (Namakkal, Tamil Nadu), Vadem Nagar Higher Secondary School (Vasco-Goa), and Yogeshwari Sainik School, Ambajogai (Maharashtra). During counselling you rank your school preferences across both categories. One exam, but the school list changes year to year — never assume last year's list.

### 7. The thing nobody puts in brochures: campus culture

The original schools are residential institutions with decades of alumni — the Ghorakhal campus alone claims over 800 NDA entrants. A brand-new PPP school affiliated two years ago simply cannot have that depth of military-preparation culture yet. Some partner schools are excellent; the point is that the name on the gate is a beginning, not a guarantee. Visit. Talk to current parents. Ask: how many of your students attempted NDA/UPSC-NDA last year, and how many cleared written?

## Which should you pick? A parent's decision checklist

- [ ] **Check the AISSEE bulletin first:** is this school even on this year's participating list?
- [ ] **Get the fee in writing:** total annual cost including hostel and mess, not just tuition.
- [ ] **Ask about merit-cum-means seats:** how many, who got them last year, what is the income ceiling?
- [ ] **Visit unannounced if you can:** the gap between brochure and hostel corridor is where the truth lives.
- [ ] **Ask for NDA-attempt data:** cleared written? how many? over how many years?
- [ ] **Rank both types in counselling:** do not restrict yourself to one category out of pride — a good PPP school beats a great name on paper.

## FAQs

**Are PPP or New Sainik Schools recognised the same as government Sainik Schools?**
Yes, for admission purposes. New Sainik Schools function under the aegis of the Sainik Schools Society and admit students through the same AISSEE. They are distinct institutions from the 33 original government-run schools.

**Are fees higher in New (PPP) Sainik Schools?**
Usually, yes. The 33 original schools are fully government-funded and highly subsidised. PPP schools set their own fee structures, though the Ministry of Defence provides merit-cum-means support of up to 50 percent of the fee (capped at Rs 40,000 per student per year) for up to half the class strength.

**Is the AISSEE syllabus the same for both types of Sainik Schools?**
Yes. Admission to both happens through the same AISSEE, and the Academic-Plus Sainik School curriculum framework applies to New Sainik Schools too, alongside their regular board curriculum.

**How many New Sainik Schools are operational now?**
The Union Cabinet approved a target of 100 New Sainik Schools in October 2021. As of mid-2026 reporting, 86 had been approved across three rounds and 79 were operational, with a fourth approval round in process. Check the latest AISSEE information bulletin for the current list.

**Can I list both types of schools in my AISSEE choices?**
Yes. The AISSEE counselling lets you express preferences across participating schools, including New Sainik Schools. Check the participating-schools list in the current year's information bulletin before assuming a particular school is included.

**Do New Sainik Schools have the same military-style training?**
They follow the Sainik School framework of discipline, physical training and the Academic-Plus curriculum, but the depth of the residential and military-preparation environment varies by school. Visit the campus and ask specifically about NDA-track preparation before deciding.
