## The section everyone leaves for last

Ask a Class 9 aspirant what they are studying this week and you will hear about Maths. Always Maths — and fair enough, it carries 200 of the 400 marks. But here is the thing: Science is 50 marks, and it is the section most children prepare last, which is exactly why it decides ranks. Two children can both score 140 in Maths. The one who also picked up 40 in Science walks into a Sainik School. The one who scored 22 does not.

The good news is that Science is the most honest section on the paper. The syllabus is the NCERT Class 8 Science textbook — all of it, nothing exotic. The questions are mostly direct: a fact from the book, a diagram you have seen, a definition in different words. A child who has genuinely read the book twice scores well. A child who only skimmed guidebooks does not.

## The paper, in numbers

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Science questions | 25 (2 marks each) |
| Science marks | 50 of 400 |
| Whole paper | 150 questions, 400 marks, 180 minutes |
| Medium (Class 9) | English only |
| Negative marking | None — attempt every question ✅ **CONFIRMED** |
| Qualifying marks | 25% per section (12.5 of 50) + 40% aggregate ✅ **CONFIRMED** |

That qualifying rule is worth spelling out, because parents keep asking. Twelve and a half marks out of 50 means at least **7 correct answers out of 25** — each question is worth 2 marks, so 6 correct is 12 marks and falls short. Seven is the floor. Aim for 18 or more, and Science stops being a risk and starts being an advantage.

## The chapter list — all of Class 8 Science

The bulletin sets the paper at Class 8 level, and the chapter list below follows the NCERT Class 8 Science book. I have grouped it the way a child should actually study it — by branch, not by the order in the book.

| Branch | Chapters to cover |
|---|---|
| **Biology** | Crop Production and Management; Microorganisms: Friend and Foe; Conservation of Plants and Animals; Cell — Structure and Functions; Reproduction in Animals; Reaching the Age of Adolescence |
| **Chemistry** | Synthetic Fibres and Plastics; Materials: Metals and Non-Metals; Coal and Petroleum; Combustion and Flame; Chemical Effects of Electric Current; Pollution of Air and Water |
| **Physics** | Force and Pressure; Friction; Sound; Light; Some Natural Phenomena; Stars and the Solar System |

If that looks like a lot, it is — but it is also finite. Eighteen chapters, one book, 75 days left until 31 January. That is roughly four chapters a week, with revision time built in.

## Priority order: where the marks actually come from

Honestly, most guidebooks overcomplicate this. Every chapter can produce a question, but some chapters produce them every year, and some produce one question in three years. Study in this order:

1. **Cell structure, microorganisms, metals and non-metals, combustion and flame** — the four chapters that recur most. Cell diagrams, the friend-and-foe table for microbes, metal vs non-metal properties, and the combustion triangle: these are near-guaranteed questions.
2. **Force and pressure, friction, sound, light** — the physics core. Numerical-free, mostly conceptual: why friction helps you walk, how sound travels, reflection and refraction in plain words.
3. **Crop production, reproduction in animals, chemical effects of electric current, coal and petroleum** — solid, factual chapters. Learn the tables and the processes step by step.
4. **Conservation, pollution, adolescence, stars and the solar system, some natural phenomena** — lighter chapters, mostly direct recall. Read them properly once and revise from short notes.

One mistake to avoid: do not study Physics, Chemistry and Biology as three separate subjects with three separate notebooks. The paper mixes them — question 3 can be Biology, question 4 Chemistry. Practise mixed sets from the start, so the switching feels normal on exam day.

## The 10-week plan (75 days to 31 January)

You have about ten and a half weeks. Here is how to use them without burning out a child who still has school every day:

| Weeks | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–4** (17 Nov – 14 Dec) | Read the whole NCERT Class 8 Science book once, two chapters a week, with the diagrams. Make one page of notes per chapter — definitions, one table, one diagram. Do the textbook exercises. |
| **Weeks 5–7** (15 Dec – 4 Jan) | Second pass, faster: notes only, plus previous-year AISSEE Science questions chapter by chapter. Mark every question you got wrong and why — fact forgotten, concept confused, or silly slip. |
| **Weeks 8–9** (5 – 18 Jan) | Mixed 25-question Science sets, timed at 20 minutes. One full 150-question paper every Sunday, phone in another room. |
| **Week 10** (19 – 30 Jan) | Notes only. No new chapters. Re-read the error list from weeks 5–7 twice. Sleep properly — a tired child forgets the one fact that was worth 2 marks. |

Thirty minutes a day is enough for Science if it is genuinely thirty minutes — book open, phone away. An hour of distracted flipping is worth less than half an hour of real reading.

## How to attempt Science on exam day

Science should take **20 to 25 minutes** of the 180. Here is the order that works:

1. **First pass (12 minutes):** answer every question you know straight away. Most Science questions are direct recall — if you have read the book, the answer is there in seconds. Do not stop to think on a question you do not know.
2. **Second pass (6 minutes):** come back to the ones you skipped. Eliminate two wrong options, then pick the most likely. There is no negative marking, so **never leave a question blank** — a guess is worth 2 marks, a blank is worth zero.
3. **Last check (2 minutes):** make sure every one of the 25 has an answer marked on the OMR sheet, and that the question numbers match.

And a small practical point: Science is in English only for Class 9, so if your child reads Science in Hindi at school, start reading the English NCERT now. The concepts are the same; the words are not, and exam day is a bad time to meet a word for the first time.

## The three mistakes that cost the most marks

**Reading guidebooks before NCERT.** Guides summarise; the paper asks what the book actually says. Read the book first, use the guide only for practice questions.

**Skipping the diagrams.** Cell structure, the human ear, the reflection of light, the nitrogen cycle — the paper loves diagrams, and a child who has drawn them once remembers them. Copy each important diagram by hand at least once.

**Treating Science as a "later" subject.** It is 50 marks, the same as English, Intelligence and Social Science. A child who starts Science in January has three weeks for 18 chapters. A child who starts this week has ten. That gap is the rank.

## FAQs

**How many marks is the Science section in AISSEE Class 9?**

25 questions, 2 marks each, for 50 marks out of 400. The paper is 150 questions in 180 minutes, and there is no negative marking.

**Which book should a Class 9 candidate use for AISSEE Science?**

The NCERT Class 8 Science textbook. The Class 9 paper is set at Class 8 level, and the chapter list in the bulletin follows the Class 8 book — read it cover to cover before touching any guidebook.

**How many Science questions must a Class 9 candidate get right to qualify?**

The qualifying rule is 25 percent in each section, which is 12.5 marks out of 50 — so at least 7 of the 25 questions must be correct, since each question carries 2 marks.

**How much time should a Class 9 candidate spend on Science in AISSEE?**

About 20 to 25 minutes of the 180-minute paper. Science questions are mostly direct recall from NCERT, so a prepared child answers fast — and the saved time goes to Mathematics, which carries 200 marks.

**Is there negative marking in the AISSEE Class 9 Science section?**

No. The AISSEE 2027 bulletin states there is no negative marking for a wrong response, so every question should be attempted.

**Which Science chapters matter most for AISSEE Class 9?**

Cell structure, microorganisms, metals and non-metals, combustion and flame, force and pressure, friction, sound and light recur most often. But no chapter can be skipped entirely — 25 questions spread across the whole Class 8 book.
