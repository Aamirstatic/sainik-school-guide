# Sainik School Reservation 2027: SC/ST/OBC & Defence Quota Rules, Documents and Category-Wise Seats

If your child belongs to the SC, ST or OBC category, or if you come from a defence background, this is probably the article you need most. And here's the honest answer to the question keeping you up at night: **yes, sainik school reservation exists, and yes, it genuinely improves your child's chances** — but it works differently from the reservation system in most government colleges.

There are two layers: a **home-state quota** and a **category quota**, and they apply together. Get your category wrong on the form, though, and you could lose the benefit entirely — or face trouble at document verification. So read this carefully before you fill anything.

## First, the 2027 situation — straight talk

- ✅ **CONFIRMED (AISSAC SOP):** The reservation structure — 67% home-state / 33% other-state, SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27%, plus a provision for defence wards.
- 🔶 **EXPECTED:** The AISSEE 2027 bulletin hasn't been released yet (as of 26 September 2026), so these rules follow the 2026 cycle's official SOP. The structure is unlikely to change, but the bulletin is the final word — cross-check when it arrives, expected around October 2026.

The AISSEE exam is set for ✅ **31 January 2027 (tentative)** — that's in NTA's official exam calendar published 16 September 2026. The application window is 🔶 expected around October–November 2026, going by the pattern of the last three cycles.

## Layer 1: The 67/33 home-state rule (most parents miss this)

This is the part almost nobody explains well. In the 33 established Sainik Schools, seats are split:

- **67% of seats go to students from the school's home state**
- **33% go to students from all other states and UTs combined**

So if your child applies to the Sainik School in *your own state*, two-thirds of the seats are reserved for children of that state. That alone changes the competition picture dramatically — for most seats, your child competes only with candidates from your state, not the whole country.

Here's the thing, though: **the category quotas apply on top of this split.** An SC candidate from the home state competes for the SC portion of the 67% home-state pool. It sounds complicated, but in practice the AISSAC counselling software handles the allocation automatically. You just need to claim the right category.

## Layer 2: Category quotas — the actual numbers

The official percentages, confirmed in the AISSAC SOP:

| Category | Reservation |
|---|---|
| Scheduled Castes (SC) | **15%** |
| Scheduled Tribes (ST) | **7.5%** |
| Other Backward Classes — Non-Creamy Layer (OBC-NCL) | **27%** |
| Defence wards | **25% of the remainder** |

Let me make this concrete with a simple illustration. Say a school has 100 seats:

1. Home-state pool: 67 seats. Other-states pool: 33 seats.
2. Inside the home-state pool, roughly: ~10 seats for SC, ~5 for ST, ~18 for OBC-NCL.
3. Defence wards get 25% of what remains after the SC/ST/OBC allocation — their own protected slice.

(That's an illustration of how the layers stack, not any school's real seat count — actual numbers vary by school and come out school-wise during counselling.)

A small real-life scene, because this is exactly where parents slip up: last year a father called in a panic — his son is OBC, but he'd ticked "General" on the AISSEE form because a neighbour told him "OBC doesn't matter in Sainik Schools." It does matter. A lot. The boy scored decently but missed the General cutoff by a few marks, while the OBC cutoff for his state was well below his score. And the category can't be changed after the form is submitted. **Check your category field twice before hitting submit.** Thirty seconds of care, and it's irreversible.

## The defence wards provision

Children of serving personnel and ex-servicemen get special consideration — 25% of the remaining seats (after the SC/ST/OBC allocation) are set aside for defence wards.

What you'll need: a **service certificate** (for serving personnel) or a **discharge certificate / PPO** proving ex-serviceman status, from the competent authority. Don't leave this for the last week — records offices take their own sweet time, and an incorrectly formatted certificate is the single most common reason defence-category claims get stuck at verification. Get the correct format from your records office or the Zila Sainik Welfare Office early.

One fee detail worth knowing: defence wards pay the same expected application fee as General/OBC candidates (🔶 ₹850 expected for 2027, based on 2026 rates — not confirmed yet), not the reduced SC/ST rate.

## Girls quota — yes, it's separate

For Class 6 admission in the 33 established schools, **10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, are reserved for girls** — a ✅ confirmed SOP rule. So a girl from the SC category benefits from both provisions at once. If you have a daughter applying, read our [guide for girls](/blog/sainik-school-for-girls-2027/) alongside this one.

## One more advantage nobody talks about: qualifying marks

Here's a genuine, confirmed benefit from the 2026 bulletin (expected to repeat in 2027): the qualifying criteria — 25% marks in each section and 40% aggregate — **do not apply to SC/ST candidates**. Everyone else must clear those sectional cutoffs; SC/ST candidates are placed on the merit list within their category instead. That's a real, documented edge, and most coaching centres never mention it.

## Documents you'll need — start collecting them now

| Document | Who needs it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Caste certificate (SC/ST/OBC-NCL) | Category candidates | In the child's name, from the competent state authority |
| OBC Non-Creamy Layer certificate | OBC candidates | Must be valid for the admission year — check the issue date |
| Domicile certificate | Everyone claiming home-state quota | Proves eligibility for the 67% pool |
| Service / ex-serviceman certificate | Defence wards | Correct format from records office or Zila Sainik Welfare Office |
| Birth certificate | Everyone | Must match the [age criteria](/blog/sainik-school-age-limit-2027-detailed-guide/) |

**My honest advice:** start gathering these *now*, in September–October, well before the application window opens (🔶 expected Oct–Nov 2026). Government offices slow down exactly when you need them most. A missing or expired caste certificate at document verification can cost your child a confirmed seat — and yes, that happens every single year.

## How your category works in AISSAC counselling

After the AISSEE, counselling runs through **AISSAC** — the Sainik Schools Society's online system at `pesa.ncog.gov.in`. It's ✅ online, free of charge, and runs for roughly six rounds.

The system automatically applies your claimed category and domicile to place you in the right merit pools. You don't pick pools yourself — but you *do* pick your school preferences, and that's where strategy matters. Read our [counselling guide](/blog/sainik-school-counselling-2027/) before locking preferences: choosing a school in your home state versus another state decides which side of the 67/33 split you land on.

## Four mistakes that cost seats every year

1. **Wrong category on the form** — can't be corrected later. Verify before submitting.
2. **Expired OBC-NCL certificate** — the non-creamy-layer certificate has a validity window; an old one gets rejected at verification.
3. **Domicile mismatch** — claiming home-state quota for a state where you don't hold a valid domicile.
4. **Assuming new PPP schools follow identical rules** — the 67/33 structure applies to the 33 established schools; newer schools under the PPP model may have different admission mechanics. The bulletin will clarify.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does OBC reservation apply in Sainik Schools?**
Yes. OBC-NCL candidates get 27% reservation in the 33 established Sainik Schools, confirmed in the AISSAC SOP. Make sure your certificate is the Non-Creamy Layer variant and currently valid — creamy-layer candidates are counted as General.

**Is there a defence quota in Sainik Schools for 2027?**
Yes — 25% of the remaining seats (after SC/ST/OBC allocation) are provisioned for wards of serving and ex-service personnel. You'll need a service certificate or PPO as proof.

**Do SC/ST students need 40% aggregate to qualify in AISSEE?**
No — this is confirmed. The 25%-per-section and 40%-aggregate qualifying criteria from the 2026 bulletin do not apply to SC/ST candidates. They compete on the category merit list instead.

**What is the 67/33 rule in Sainik School admission?**
67% of seats in each of the 33 established Sainik Schools are reserved for students domiciled in the school's home state; 33% are open to students from other states and UTs. Category quotas apply within these pools.

**Can my daughter benefit from both the girls quota and SC/ST reservation?**
Yes. The girls quota (10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, for Class 6) and the category reservation are separate provisions — a girl from a reserved category benefits from both.

**When will the official 2027 reservation details be confirmed?**
When NTA releases the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, expected around October 2026. The structure here follows the official 2026 SOP and is unlikely to change, but the bulletin is the final authority.
