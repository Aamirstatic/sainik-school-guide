> **Last verified: 7 October 2026.** The rhythm below is a representative composite of weekend routines widely reported across Sainik Schools — ✅ **timings vary from school to school**, and a principal's or housemaster's word on your child's campus always wins over any general guide. What does *not* vary: weekends are structured, residential, and nothing like a school-going day-scholar's Saturday–Sunday.

## Forget Your Own Idea of a Weekend

For a day-scholar, the weekend means the bag gets dumped on Friday evening and reappears on Monday morning. In a Sainik School, the first lesson the weekend teaches is that **structure does not take Sundays off** — it just changes its shape. Saturday is, in most schools, a near-normal working day. Sunday is genuinely lighter, but it is not free. If your child is joining through [AISSEE 2027](/blog/first-day-at-sainik-school-what-to-expect-2026-10-07/), knowing what the weekend actually feels like is part of knowing what they are signing up for — and the first [few weeks are the hardest adjustment](/blog/how-strict-is-discipline-in-sainik-school/).

## Saturday: The Sixth School Day (Almost)

Most Sainik Schools run academic classes on Saturday, though the day usually ends earlier than a Monday–Friday timetable. The morning keeps its military skeleton — wake-up, physical training, breakfast, assembly — and the middle of the day is classes. Where Saturday differs is the afternoon: with the regular classes done, the evening often belongs to **games, house competitions, and preparation periods**.

This is a useful thing for parents to understand. In day schools, extracurriculars fight for time against homework. In a Sainik School, Saturday is deliberately designed as the day the extracurricular calendar breathes: inter-house matches, athletics practice, club activities, and the drill or parade practice that some schools schedule on Saturdays rather than crowding it into weekdays. Your child is not "losing" their weekend to school. The school has simply stopped treating sports and hobbies as optional extras.

## Sunday: The Lighter Day That Still Has a Shape

Sunday is the weekly holiday — no regular classes — but it is a mistake to picture it as a day of sleeping in and wandering around. The wake-up call comes a little later, and then the day has its own rhythm:

| Time block | Typical Sunday rhythm |
|---|---|
| Early morning | Later wake-up, followed by a lighter PT session or free games |
| Forenoon | Personal time — laundry, room upkeep, letter/phone contact with home |
| Midday | Lunch, rest hour (many houses observe a quiet rest period) |
| Afternoon | Organised games, athletics, hobby clubs, hobby master sessions |
| Evening | Preparation period, roll call, dinner |

Two blocks deserve your attention. The **personal time in the forenoon** is when the unglamorous skills of boarding life happen: washing clothes, polishing shoes, keeping the bed and locker inspection-ready. Nobody teaches this at home in quite the same way, and most parents are surprised — pleasantly — at what their child learns to do without being asked.

The **evening preparation period** is why Sunday does not feel like a lost day. After a genuinely relaxed afternoon, the school asks cadets to spend an hour or two in quiet study. It is less about new learning and more about not entering Monday with a cold start.

## The Parts Parents Ask About Most

**Can we visit on weekends?** Most schools fix one visiting day — often one Sunday a month — and casual drop-ins are generally not encouraged. The reasoning is practical: a steady stream of visitors through the weekend unsettles the house routine and is unfair to children whose families live far away. Read your school's visitor rules carefully; turning up unannounced is the classic avoidable mistake.

**Do they come home on weekends?** No. Sainik Schools are fully residential. Cadets go home during term breaks and vacations, not on Saturdays and Sundays. This is worth discussing honestly as a family before admission — homesickness is real, it is normal, and it passes, but pretending it won't exist helps no one. (For a candid read on the harder parts of boarding life, see our [parent's guide to bullying and boarding schools](/blog/bullying-boarding-schools-parents-guide-2026-10-06/).)

**Do they talk to home?** Most schools give scheduled letter-writing time on Sundays, and many now permit regulated phone calls on a fixed weekly slot. The Sunday phone call, where it exists, becomes a genuine family ritual — short, precious, and eagerly awaited.

**What about Saturday night?** Houses often allow a slightly later lights-out, and Saturday evenings carry a relaxed flavour — house films, group activities, or a long games session followed by dinner. It is the week's natural pressure valve.

## Why the Weekend Is the Best Part of the System

Strip away the romance, and the logic of the Sainik School weekend is straightforward. A cadet's week is high-discipline: early wake-ups, drill, classes, supervised prep, roll calls. The weekend is not the absence of that discipline — it is the demonstration that the discipline can flex. Saturday keeps the skeleton; Sunday keeps the outline. The message, repeated every week for seven years, is that structure is not a prison you escape on weekends; it is a habit you carry.

Parents who have been through it usually say the same thing: the child who dreaded the routine in April is the one defending it by December. Give the first term the space to work.

## A Parent's Weekend Checklist (Before the First Term)

- **Ask the school about its visiting day** at admission time and put the dates on your calendar — don't plan trips outside them.
- **Teach basic self-care before joining:** laundry basics, shoe polishing, keeping a bed. Sunday forenoon will be easier.
- **Set the Sunday call expectation early:** one scheduled call beats constant anxiety about phone access.
- **Resist the rescue instinct in month one.** Homesick calls home in the first two weeks are normal; pulling a child out before the routine takes hold usually restarts the clock.
- **Watch for the genuine red flags, not the routine ones:** persistent distress after six weeks, sleep trouble, or falling appetite deserve a calm conversation with the housemaster.

## FAQs

**Do Sainik Schools have classes on Saturday?**
Yes, most Sainik Schools hold regular classes on Saturday mornings. Sunday is the weekly holiday, though it still has fixed routines like games, letter writing and preparation time.

**Do cadets get to go home on weekends?**
No. Sainik Schools are full residential boarding schools, so cadets stay on campus through weekends. They go home during term breaks and the long vacations.

**What do Sainik School students do on Sundays?**
Sundays are lighter: no regular classes. The day usually includes games, hobbies, letter or phone time with family, and preparation periods, plus mandatory routines like roll call.

**Can parents visit on weekends?**
Most schools designate one visiting day or a fixed visiting Sunday each month. Casual weekend visits are generally not allowed; check the school's visitor rules before travelling.

**Is there really a roll call every day in a Sainik School?**
Yes. Muster or roll call is part of the daily routine, including weekends — it is a small ritual that keeps the whole house accounted for and teaches punctuality.

**Do weekends include physical training and drill?**
Generally yes, in lighter form than weekdays. Saturdays often carry the normal PT and drill schedule, while Sundays lean toward games, athletics and hobbies.
