Nagaland has produced some of India's finest soldiers — the Naga Regiment, the Assam Rifles, officers in every service. And yet, for a state with that kind of military tradition, Nagaland has exactly one Sainik School. One. And here is the part that should stop every parent in Kohima, Dimapur and Mokokchung mid-scroll: the Chief Minister said this week that **10 to 20 of its Nagaland-quota seats go vacant every year**.

If your child is in Class 5 or Class 8 in Nagaland right now, this guide is the complete picture — the school, the quota math that works in your favour, the NDA record, and the ten days you have left to apply.

## The one school: Punglwa

| | **Sainik School Punglwa** |
|---|---|
| Established | 2007 — Nagaland's only Sainik School |
| Location | Punglwa, Peren district, Nagaland |
| Classes | VI to XII, residential, CBSE, English medium |
| Nearest town | Dimapur, about 50 km away |
| Nearest airport / railway | Dimapur |
| Admission | Only through the AISSEE (NTA) — no other route |

Punglwa was founded in 2007 to give Naga youth a structured military-oriented education in a region that had none. It sits in Peren district, close to the Myanmar border — remote by mainland standards, but reachable: Dimapur, with its airport and railway station, is about 50 km away. Full profile: [Sainik School Punglwa](/schools/sainik-school-punglwa/).

A word on distance, because every Nagaland parent asks it. Yes, Punglwa is residential, and your child will live away from home from Class 6 — that is the deal at every Sainik School. What is different here is that the school is *in* your state, not a thousand kilometres away. For most Naga families, Punglwa is the closest residential military school there is.

## The quota math — and why it favours you

This is the section that matters most.

- **67% of seats** at Punglwa are reserved for **Nagaland-domicile** candidates. Your child competes in this pool.
- **33% of seats** go to **other-state** candidates — children from Assam, Manipur, Arunachal and beyond.
- Within the seats, the reservation structure follows the Sainik Schools SOP: **SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27%**, and **25% of the remainder** for defence wards. Nagaland is an ST-majority state, so the ST reservation is directly relevant to most local applicants.
- **Girls:** at least **10% of Class 6 seats** (or 10 seats, whichever is higher) are reserved for girls. The Chief Minister specifically said the school should give greater opportunities to Nagaland's girls.

Now the vacancy problem. Chief Minister Neiphiu Rio said on 21 November 2026 that around **10 to 20 or more Nagaland-quota seats remain vacant every year** before being filled by students from outside the state. He attributed this partly to parents' concerns over the **cost of education**, and called for greater awareness about the opportunities at the school.

Think about what that means in plain terms. The home-state quota exists to protect local children. When local children do not apply, the protection does not wait — the seats go to other-state applicants. Every vacant Nagaland seat is a child who never filled the form, or a family that assumed the school was unaffordable without checking. Read our [home-state quota explainer](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-explained/) for the full 67/33 mechanism, and our [girls' quota guide](/blog/girls-quota-sainik-schools-explained-2026-10-22/) if your daughter is applying.

## The NDA record: small school, big ranks

Punglwa does not have the scale of Kunjpura or Satara. What it has, this year, is proof that size is not the point. Two Punglwa cadets made the **NDA-157 merit list**: Cadet **Dev Dagar at AIR 61** and Cadet **Anshik Yadav at AIR 125**. For a school with a small annual NDA pipeline, two ranks inside the top 150 is a serious return — and it is exactly the kind of result the Chief Minister's revamp push is trying to build on.

On 21 November, Rio pledged a personal contribution of **₹50 lakh** to the Old Boys' Association of Sainik Schools Nagaland Chapter, announced a state-provided **prefabricated multipurpose building** for the association's complex, and proposed a **committee to study financial assistance and entrance-exam coaching** for deserving students from economically weaker families. Our full report on the announcement is here: [Nagaland CM pledges ₹50 lakh to revamp Sainik School Punglwa](/news/nagaland-cm-50-lakh-sainik-school-punglwa-revamp-2026-11-22/).

Be honest with yourself about the committee, though. It is a **proposal**, not a scheme. No scholarship, fee waiver or coaching programme exists yet. If someone offers you a Punglwa seat or a fee concession for money, walk away — admission is only through the AISSEE.

## The neighbourhood: what is changing around Nagaland

Nagaland's parents should know what is happening next door. On **17 November 2026**, Assam's Chief Minister laid the foundation stone for Assam's **second Sainik School at Langvoku in Karbi Anglong** — a **₹336-crore** campus for **640 cadets**, to be completed in about 30 months. A second Assam school will, in time, absorb some of the other-state applicants who compete for Punglwa's 33% pool — but that campus is years away from its first admission. For AISSEE 2027, the picture is unchanged: Punglwa is the only Sainik School in Nagaland, and its Nagaland quota is yours to claim. ## Your timeline: 10 days to apply

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM** | AISSEE 2027 form closes (Class 6 and Class 9) |
| 3–6 December 2026 | Late-fee window — extra ₹500 on top of the regular fee |
| 7–9 December 2026 | Correction window — fix permitted details |
| 11 January 2027 | Exam city announced |
| 25 January 2027 | Admit cards available |
| **31 January 2027 (Sunday)** | **AISSEE 2027 exam** |

The fee is **₹850** for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and **₹700** for SC/ST. Class 6 is a 150-minute, 125-question, 300-mark paper; Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks, in English only. There is **no negative marking**, and the qualifying rule is **25% in every section and 40% in aggregate** (not applicable to SC/ST). Age limits: Class 6, born **1 April 2015–31 March 2017** and studying in Class 5; Class 9, born **1 April 2012–31 March 2014** and studying in Class 8.

Do not wait until 2 December — the portal is always busiest on the last day, and the late-fee window costs you ₹500 for the same form. Submit this week, while it is calm. Our [10-week countdown calendar](/blog/aissee-2027-10-week-countdown-calendar-23-november-to-exam-2026-11-21/) takes you from the form to exam day, week by week.

## What to do this week: a Nagaland parent's checklist

1. **Check the date of birth** against the birth certificate — Class 6: 1 April 2015–31 March 2017; Class 9: 1 April 2012–31 March 2014. A wrong DOB is the commonest reason a form gets rejected.
2. **Get the domicile certificate ready.** Your child's Nagaland domicile is what puts them in the 67% pool. If it is not in hand, apply now.
3. **Photograph and signature** to NTA specs — white background, recent photo, correct file size. A rejected photo is a rejected form.
4. **Fill the form at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society** and pay the fee (₹850 / ₹700) by 2 December, 11:50 PM.
5. **Save the confirmation page and application number.** You will need them for the admit card in January.
6. **Start the prep, not just the form.** Ten days to the deadline is also ten weeks to the exam — a child who starts serious revision now, with one full mock paper every Sunday, is a different candidate by 31 January.

One last thing. The Chief Minister is a Sainik School alumnus himself — Purulia, class monitor, the whole discipline-and-hostel-life story he told on Saturday. He said the greatest contribution of Sainik Schools was not the academics but the values drilled in during those formative years. Whether or not you buy the romance, the practical point stands: Nagaland's only Sainik School has seats going spare, the quota is built for your child, and the form is open for ten more days.
