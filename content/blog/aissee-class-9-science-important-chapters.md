Class 9 Science in AISSEE is worth **50 marks** — 25 questions, 2 marks each, inside a 180-minute, 400-mark paper. **CONFIRMED (2026 pattern):** the section is set from the **Class 8 NCERT science syllabus**, the whole Class 9 paper is in **English only**, and there is **no negative marking**.

That last fact changes everything. Science is not a section to "play safe" in — it is a section to prepare properly and then attempt fully. The students who score here do one thing differently: they stop memorising and start understanding.

## What the syllabus actually covers

The Class 9 Science section draws on the complete Class 8 NCERT science book — physics, chemistry and biology together. Roughly, the book splits like this:

| Theme | Class 8 NCERT chapters |
|-------|----------------------|
| Physics | Force and Pressure; Friction; Sound; Chemical Effects of Electric Current; Light; Stars and the Solar System; Some Natural Phenomena |
| Chemistry | Synthetic Fibres and Plastics; Materials: Metals and Non-Metals; Coal and Petroleum; Combustion and Flame |
| Biology | Crop Production and Management; Microorganisms: Friend and Foe; Conservation of Plants and Animals; Cell Structure and Functions; Reproduction in Animals; Reaching the Age of Adolescence; Pollution of Air and Water |

Eighteen chapters sounds like a lot. It is not, once you stop treating all chapters equally. Here is a priority order that reflects how application-based questions — not just definitions — actually appear in AISSEE.

## Priority tiers: where to spend your time

### Tier 1 — Build these first (concept chapters)

These chapters are the foundation of the most application-based questions: numericals, "why does this happen" reasoning, and diagram-based MCQs.

- **Force and Pressure, Friction, Sound** — the physics core. If you can explain *why* friction both helps and hinders, you own this cluster.
- **Chemical Effects of Electric Current** — small chapter, big payoff. Electroplating, LED vs bulb, tester circuits — all question-ready.
- **Cell Structure and Functions** — the biology anchor. Every diagram question in the section traces back to this chapter.
- **Microorganisms: Friend and Foe** — nitrogen cycle, pasteurisation, vaccination logic. Highly question-dense.

### Tier 2 — Cover thoroughly (content chapters)

- **Coal and Petroleum, Combustion and Flame** — fuels, ignition temperature, flame zones. Pair them together; they test as one block.
- **Reproduction in Animals** — definitions plus a few diagrams (amoeba binary fission, frog life cycle).
- **Light** — laws of reflection, human eye, Braille. Draw the ray diagrams yourself once; that is the entire trick.
- **Stars and the Solar System** — phases of the moon, constellations, planets. Memory-heavy but scoring.
- **Crop Production and Management, Pollution of Air and Water, Conservation of Plants and Animals** — the environment cluster. NCERT-line questions; read the book, not a guidebook, for these.

### Tier 3 — Finish and revise (lighter chapters)

- **Synthetic Fibres and Plastics, Some Natural Phenomena, Reaching the Age of Adolescence** — smaller, mostly definition-based. Do not skip them (a question from here is free marks), but do not spend a week on them either.

## The 4-week chapter plan

| Week | Focus | Target |
|------|-------|--------|
| 1 | Tier 1 physics cluster: force, friction, sound, electric current | One-page concept notes per chapter + chapter-end questions |
| 2 | Tier 1 biology + Tier 2 fuels: cell, microorganisms, coal/petroleum, combustion | Redraw all labelled diagrams once from memory |
| 3 | Tier 2 remainder: reproduction, light, solar system, environment cluster | Timed sets: 25 questions in 30 minutes |
| 4 | Tier 3 chapters + full-section mock tests | Error log review; re-attempt every wrong question |

Work the plan in **45-minute blocks** — one chapter concept, then its questions. Science rewards this rhythm more than marathon reading sessions.

## How to actually study a science chapter

1. **Read the NCERT page once**, pencil in hand, underlining only causes and definitions.
2. **Close the book and redraw** every labelled diagram from memory. In AISSEE Science, the diagram *is* the question half the time.
3. **Answer the chapter-end exercises** without looking back. Anything you cannot answer becomes your revision list.
4. **Convert facts into "why" questions.** "Light travels in straight lines" is a fact; "then why does a coin in water look raised?" is an AISSEE question.
5. **Keep an error log.** Every wrong MCQ gets one line: chapter, concept missed, correct logic. Review it every Sunday — the same mistakes repeat otherwise.

## The traps that cost marks

- **Units and conversions.** Force in newtons, pressure in pascals — a full quarter of avoidable physics errors are unit errors.
- **Diagram laziness.** Students who "recognise" a diagram but never drew it get the labelling questions wrong.
- **Skipping the environment cluster.** "Easy" chapters feel safe to ignore and then cost 4–6 marks. They never take more than a day each.
- **English terminology.** Since the Class 9 paper is English-only, learn terms like *electroplating*, *pupation*, *constellation* in English even if your school taught in another medium.

## The revision checklist (use in January)

- [ ] All 18 chapters read once from NCERT, not a digest
- [ ] Every labelled diagram drawn once from memory
- [ ] Chapter-end exercises attempted for every chapter
- [ ] Error log reviewed — no concept appears twice
- [ ] At least 4 timed Science sections (25 Q, 30 min) completed
- [ ] Key definitions recited aloud without the book
- [ ] "Why" questions answered for every Tier 1 chapter

Science in AISSEE is not about genius — it is about coverage done properly. Fifty marks sit there for any student willing to read one book carefully and draw its diagrams. ## FAQs

**What is the syllabus for AISSEE Class 9 Science?**

Class 9 Science in AISSEE follows the Class 8 NCERT science syllabus — physics, chemistry and biology topics taught in Class 8. The section carries 50 marks with 25 multiple-choice questions, in English, with no negative marking.

**How many marks does Science carry in AISSEE Class 9?**

Science carries 50 marks out of 400 in the Class 9 paper: 25 questions of 2 marks each. The paper runs 180 minutes for 150 questions in total.

**Is AISSEE Class 9 Science only in English?**

Yes. Unlike the Class 6 paper, which is offered in 13 languages, the entire Class 9 paper including Science is conducted in English only.

**How should I revise Science in the last month before AISSEE?**

Work through your NCERT chapter-end questions, re-draw every labelled diagram once, and solve previous AISSEE Science sections under timed conditions. Diagrams and definitions are the most repeated question types.

**Which chapters need the most attention for AISSEE Class 9 Science?**

Start with the physics cluster — force, friction, sound and electric current — plus cell biology and micro-organisms. These foundations feed the most application-based questions, though the 2027 chapter-wise break-up will only be known after the exam.
