## One exam, two very different schools

When parents fill the AISSEE 2027 form, they face a choice that did not exist a few years ago. The form now covers **33 established Sainik Schools** and **80 approved New Sainik Schools for Class 6 and 40 for Class 9** — and the two kinds of school are not the same thing with a different name. The exam is the same. The date is the same. Almost everything else — who runs the school, what it costs, who gets the seats, what daily life looks like — is different in ways that matter more than the name on the gate.

Here is the honest comparison, and then the part nobody writes: which one actually suits your child.

## The comparison, side by side

| | 33 existing Sainik Schools | Approved New Sainik Schools |
|---|---|---|
| **Who runs it** | Sainik Schools Society, under the Ministry of Defence | Partner schools approved into the system — mostly existing private/CBSE schools |
| **History** | The first opened in 1961; decades of alumni, NDA record and tradition | Newly approved, mostly in the last few years; the expansion was announced in March 2026 |
| **Classes offered** | Class 6 and Class 9 | 80 schools offer Class 6; 40 offer Class 9 |
| **Admission** | AISSEE 2027, 31 January 2027 | Same AISSEE 2027 — one form, one exam |
| **Boarding** | Fully residential — every child lives on campus | Differs by school — some residential, some not; check the individual school |
| **Fees** | Society fee structure, with scholarships and fee concessions | Set by the partner school; varies widely — check before applying |
| **Home-state quota** | 67% home-state / 33% other-states (SOP) | Not the same rule by default — check the bulletin and the school |
| **Reservation** | SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27%; 25% of the remainder for defence wards | As per the bulletin and the school's own rules |
| **Qualifying marks** | 25% per subject + 40% aggregate; SC/ST exempt from the minimum | 25% + 40% applies to **all** categories, including SC/ST |
| **Girls admission** | Girls eligible; 10% of seats or 10 seats, whichever is more (Class 6) | Girls eligible for Class 6; Class 9 subject to seat availability |
| **Daily life** | Full military-school routine: morning PT, parade, mess, prep, games | Follows the Sainik School pattern — but the depth of it depends on the school |

Two rows in that table deserve a second look, because they are the ones parents get wrong.

**First, the qualifying marks.** In the 33 old schools, SC/ST candidates are exempt from the 25%-per-subject and 40%-aggregate minimum. In the New Sainik Schools, the minimum applies to everyone, including SC/ST. That is straight from the 2027 bulletin, and it changes the target score for SC/ST families choosing between the two lists.

**Second, the quota.** The 67% home-state split is a rule of the 33 Society schools. For a New Sainik School, the bulletin and the school govern the seats. Do not assume your home-state advantage travels with you — check the bulletin list for the school you actually want.

## What the old schools give you that the new ones can't (yet)

Let's be fair to both sides. The 33 established schools have something no new school can buy: sixty years of it. The alumni network that opens doors in the services, the civil services and business. A fully residential routine — morning physical training, parade, mess, evening prep, games — that has produced officers for decades. Teachers and housemasters who have run this system for years. And a peer group selected from the whole country through one of the toughest Class 6 exams in India.

That is also why they are hard to get into. Thirty-three schools, limited seats, the whole country competing. A child who clears AISSEE for an old school has beaten serious odds, and the school knows it.

## What the new schools give you that the old ones can't

The new schools solve a different problem: distance. There are 80 of them for Class 6, spread across far more districts than the 33 ever reached. For many families, a New Sainik School is the first Sainik School within practical reach of home — no 800-kilometre move for an 11-year-old, no once-a-term visit. And because they are existing schools brought into the system, many of them have modern classrooms and facilities that older campuses are still catching up on.

But — and this is the honest part — the quality varies. A New Sainik School is a partner school, not a Society school. Its boarding arrangements, its discipline, its sports and its fees are its own. Some will be excellent. Some will be an ordinary private school with a Sainik School board on the gate. The bulletin approves them; it does not make them identical.

## So which one should your child apply for?

Stop thinking of it as old versus new. Think of it as three questions:

**1. What does your child actually want?** If the dream is the full residential military-school life — the uniform, the parade ground, the house system — the 33 old schools are the real thing, and the competition is the price. If your child would be miserable away from home for months at a stretch, a nearby new school with a gentler routine is not a compromise. It is the right school.

**2. What can you check before you apply?** For an old school, the facts are public: fees, routine, alumni, results. For a new school, you have homework. Visit it. Ask: is it residential or day? What are the fees, all-in, for a year? Who runs the physical training? How many AISSEE-qualified children joined last year, and where did they come from? A school that cannot answer those questions is telling you something.

**3. What does the merit list say?** You do not choose one or the other on the form — you list your preferences, and the merit list decides. Put the schools you genuinely want at the top, in the order you would actually send your child to them. A new school you have visited and liked, ranked above an old school you have never seen, is a better preference order than the reverse.

One last thing. The form closes on **2 December 2026**. The exam is on **31 January 2027**. Neither the old schools nor the new ones will wait for a family still deciding in the last week of November. Pick your list this week, check the schools on it, and then get back to the one thing that decides everything either way: the preparation.

## FAQs

**How many old and new Sainik Schools are there for AISSEE 2027?**

There are 33 existing Sainik Schools, plus 80 approved New Sainik Schools offering Class 6 and 40 offering Class 9, for the 2027-28 academic year, as per the NTA public notice of 3 November 2026.

**Is the AISSEE exam the same for old and new Sainik Schools?**

Yes. Admission to both kinds of school is through the same AISSEE 2027, on 31 January 2027, in pen-and-paper OMR mode with no negative marking. One form, one exam, one merit process.

**Do new Sainik Schools have the same 67 percent home-state quota?**

The 67 percent home-state and 33 percent other-states split is the rule for the 33 existing Sainik Schools under the Sainik Schools Society. For New Sainik Schools, check the Information Bulletin and the individual school's admission rules — do not assume the same split applies.

**Are fees the same in old and new Sainik Schools?**

No. Fees in the 33 established schools follow the Sainik Schools Society structure with scholarships and concessions. New Sainik Schools are partner schools and set their own fees, which vary from school to school — always check the individual school before applying.

**Can girls apply to new Sainik Schools?**

Yes. Boys and girls are both eligible for Class 6 in the existing schools and the approved New Sainik Schools. For Class 9, girls are eligible subject to availability of seats.

**Which should I choose — an old Sainik School or a new one?**

Choose the old schools if you want the classic fully residential military-school life and your child can compete for the limited seats. Choose a new school if you want a Sainik School education closer to home, with a school whose boarding arrangements, fees and daily routine you have checked yourself first.
