Here's a sentence I hear constantly: "My child knows all the formulas but still leaves 10–15 maths questions unattempted." **AISSEE maths speed** is not about being a genius calculator — it's about having a plan for 150 (or 180) minutes that survives a real question paper. Time budget, triage habit, 4 weeks of timed drills. A child with that plan finishes the paper; a child with only "knowledge" doesn't.

## Maths is half the paper — act like it

This is where every speed plan starts. ✅ **CONFIRMED** (AISSEE 2026 bulletin; 🔶 **EXPECTED** to repeat for 2027):

**Class 6:** 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks — **Maths: 50 questions × 3 marks = 150 marks**, exactly half. Language, Intelligence and GK carry 25 questions / 50 marks each.

**Class 9:** 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks — **Maths: 50 questions × 4 marks = 200 marks**, again half. English, Intelligence, General Science and Social Science carry 25 questions / 50 marks each.

A Class 6 child who answers maths slowly but correctly still loses if the last 10 questions go unattempted — that's 30 marks, *sixty percent* of the entire Language section. Speed in maths is the highest-leverage skill in this exam.

One more fact shapes everything: ✅ **there is no negative marking in AISSEE** (CONFIRMED per NTA pattern). Our [negative marking explainer](/blog/aissee-negative-marking-honest-answer/) says it plainly — every unattempted question is marks your child hands back to the paper.

## Your per-section time budget (Class 6)

Most children treat the paper as one 150-minute maths session. Wrong. Split it into time blocks before the booklet opens — treat each like a mini-exam. Adjust ±5 minutes to your child's strengths, but never steal from the review block:

| Block | Section | Questions | Minutes |
|-------|---------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Maths — Pass 1 (triage, see below) | 50 | 55 |
| 2 | Language | 25 | 20 |
| 3 | Intelligence | 25 | 20 |
| 4 | GK | 25 | 20 |
| 5 | Maths — Pass 2 (mop-up) | starred | 25 |
| 6 | Review + OMR check | all | 10 |
| | **Total** | **125** | **150** | |

**For Class 9** (180 min, 150 questions): 105 minutes for maths (65 + 40 mop-up), 15 minutes per other section, 15 minutes review. Each maths question is worth 4 marks there, so the extra investment pays double.

Most parents flinch at 55 minutes for 50 questions. That's exactly why we drill — the triage method below is what makes a minute per question possible.

## The triage method: three passes, zero stuck time

Speed doesn't come from solving faster. It comes from *not solving the hard ones yet*. The three-pass rule:

**Pass 1 — The harvest (55 min).** Solve instantly anything you can do in under a minute; star the rest and move on — no "just one more step." Target: 30–35 solved, 10–15 starred.

**Pass 2 — The other three sections.** Fresh brain, faster sections — and it gives the subconscious time to work on the starred maths questions. You'd be amazed how many "hard" questions crack on the second look.

**Pass 3 — The mop-up (25 min).** Return to the starred questions. Anything still stuck gets an educated guess — no negative marking.

Parents, drill one skill: *walking away*. Sit with your child for one timed set; the moment they spend 4 minutes on one question, tap the table. Repeated for 4 weeks, that tap becomes their internal alarm. For the bigger picture, see our [AISSEE paper attempt strategy](/blog/aissee-paper-attempt-strategy/).

## The 14-day speed-drill checklist

Drills beat lectures. Print this, stick it on the fridge, and tick each day off:

- [ ] **Day 1:** Diagnostic — one full maths section, timed, no triage. Count attempted vs. correct. This is your baseline number.
- [ ] **Day 2:** The 90-second rule — 15 questions, hard stop at 90 seconds each. Star and move on.
- [ ] **Day 3:** Topic sprint — 20 questions, only percentages + profit-loss, 25 minutes.
- [ ] **Day 4:** Batch bubbling drill — solve 20 questions, bubble in batches of 10. Record total OMR time.
- [ ] **Day 5:** Topic sprint — 20 questions, only fractions + decimals + LCM/HCF, 25 minutes.
- [ ] **Day 6:** Rest day (light formula revision only).
- [ ] **Day 7:** Mini-mock — full 50-question maths section, three passes, strict budget. Compare with Day 1.
- [ ] **Day 8:** Error-log day — re-solve every Day 7 mistake. Log the error *type* (concept / calculation / silly / time).
- [ ] **Day 9:** Topic sprint — 20 questions, mensuration + area/perimeter, 25 minutes.
- [ ] **Day 10:** Distraction drill — a 20-question set in a noisy room. Exam halls are never silent; concentration must be portable.
- [ ] **Day 11:** Topic sprint — 20 questions, speed-distance-time + averages + ratio, 25 minutes.
- [ ] **Day 12:** Full paper under the exact budget table above. [Analyse it with the error-log method](/blog/aissee-mock-test-analysis-error-log-method/).
- [ ] **Day 13:** Only your weak spots — re-drill the 2 topics with the worst Day 12 accuracy.
- [ ] **Day 14:** Confidence mock — another full maths section. Most children beat Day 1 by 8–12 attempted questions.

## OMR bubbling: the hidden time thief

Nobody practices bubbling. Everybody should — in our own mock sessions, children lost 6–12 minutes to the OMR sheet. Here's the protocol:

1. **Bubble in batches of 10**, not one-by-one and not all at the end. Batch bubbling keeps your place and limits misalignment risk.
2. **Verify against the booklet.** Your star marks must match the OMR — after each batch, glance-check the last bubble.
3. **Keep the last 10 minutes sacred.** No new solving in the final 10 minutes, only review and bubbling. A rushed final row is where wrong-row errors live.
4. **Practise on real OMR sheets** at least 5 times before the exam. The physical feel matters.

One last rule: never leave a question unattempted. With no negative marking, an educated guess in the final two minutes is pure expected value — 3 marks each for Class 6, zero cost.

## FAQs

**My child solves maths correctly but attempts only 35 of 50 questions — is that a knowledge problem or a speed problem?**

A speed problem, and a very common one. If most attempted questions are right, the concepts are fine — what's missing is a time budget and a triage habit. Start with timed sets of 15 questions in 25 minutes plus the three-pass method above; most children add 8–10 attempted questions in three weeks.

**Should Class 9 students give maths more time since each question carries 4 marks?**

Yes, proportionally — 100 to 105 minutes on the 50 maths questions is sensible. But not at the cost of the 25 percent per-section qualifying mark in every other section. Maths deserves priority, never a monopoly.

**Should my child bubble the OMR sheet after each question or at the end of the section?**

Bubble in batches — after every 10 questions, stop and mark those 10. One-by-one bubbling breaks concentration; end-only bubbling risks a rushed, misaligned final five minutes. Batches also give a built-in checkpoint against skipped-question misalignment.

**When should my child give up on a hard maths question and move on?**

The 90-second rule works well: if a Class 6 maths question has not yielded a clear path by 90 seconds (120 seconds for Class 9), mark it with a star in the question booklet and move on. Come back in the mop-up round — questions often crack on a second look when the pressure is off.

**Is mental maths safe, or should my child still write down steps in the exam?**

A hybrid is safest. Single-step calculations go mentally in drills; in the real paper, write the one-line setup (formula with numbers plugged in) before solving mentally. That line is your error-checking safety net, and it costs five seconds.
