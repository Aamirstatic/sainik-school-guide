## Two days that explain what a Sainik School is for

If you want to understand a Sainik School in a single visit, do not come on an ordinary Tuesday. Come on one of its two big ceremonial days. Annual Day shows you what the school achieved in a year. The Passing Out Parade shows you what it produces at the end of it all — young men and women walking out of the gates as something visibly different from the children who walked in.

For first-time parents, both days can be confusing. There is drill, there are speeches, there are prizes, there are tears, and nobody hands you a programme that explains which is which. Here is the honest version of what happens, in the order it usually happens, so you can enjoy the day instead of whispering questions to the parent next to you.

## Annual Day: the school reports to its parents

Annual Day is the school's yearly report card, delivered in person. It is usually held towards the end of the academic session, and it is the one day when the whole school — cadets, teachers, staff, and families — gathers for celebration rather than routine.

A typical Annual Day runs roughly like this:

| Part of the day | What you will see |
|---|---|
| Assembly and welcome | Cadets in formation, the principal's welcome, and the chief guest's arrival |
| Annual report | The principal reads out the year's achievements — results, sports, NDA selections, new facilities |
| Prize distribution | Academic toppers, sports champions, and best houses collect trophies, often to loud house cheers |
| Cultural programme | Cadets perform plays, dances, music, and skits they have rehearsed for weeks |
| Closing and tea | Informal time when parents meet teachers and housemasters, and children finally get a hug |

The part parents remember most is rarely the speeches. It is the cultural programme, because that is where you see something the daily routine hides: the same disciplined cadet who marches in straight lines at dawn is also the boy who held a note on stage or made a hall laugh in a skit. That range — discipline and personality in the same child — is the whole point of the system, and Annual Day is where it becomes visible.

A practical note for first-timers: arrive early. Seating for parents is usually arranged by house or by class, and the best view of the stage goes to families who arrive before the ceremony starts. Bring water, and if the event is outdoors, a hat — these programmes run long, and Indian afternoons do not forgive anyone.

## The Passing Out Parade: the day the school says goodbye

If Annual Day is a celebration, the Passing Out Parade is something quieter and heavier. It is the formal farewell for the outgoing batch — usually the Class 12 cadets — and it is the most military-flavoured event in the school calendar.

The parade typically follows a shape that has barely changed in decades:

1. **The parade forms up.** The outgoing batch marches onto the ground in full ceremonial order, led by the School Captain and the cadet appointments — the same young leaders described in our guide to [cadet leadership ranks](/blog/sainik-school-cadet-leadership-ranks-house-captain-school-captain/).
2. **Inspection and march past.** The chief guest — often a senior serving officer — inspects the parade, and the cadets march past in slow and quick time. This is the moment parents reach for their cameras, and the moment cadets have rehearsed the longest.
3. **The handing over.** Appointments are formally handed to the next batch. The outgoing School Captain passes the baton, literally or symbolically, to a junior. It is a small gesture that carries the whole idea of the school: nothing here belongs to one batch; everything is held in trust for the next.
4. **The farewell.** The outgoing cadets march out through the gates — or past a guard of honour formed by their juniors — while the school watches. Parents of the passing-out batch are usually seated prominently for this, and yes, this is the part where people cry.

For the cadets, the parade is the end of six or seven years of a life that started with homesickness in Class 6 and ends with them leading a parade ground. For parents, it is the first time many of them see the full distance their child has travelled. The [morning routine of PT, assembly, and the first bell](/blog/sainik-school-morning-routine-pt-assembly-first-bell/) that once felt like a shock to a new cadet has, by this day, become something the cadet performs without thinking.

## What first-time parents should know before they go

**Invitations come from the school, not from other parents.** Each school issues its own notice with dates, reporting times, and entry rules. If you have not received one, contact the school office rather than relying on another parent's message — dates shift, and ceremonies are sometimes combined or rescheduled around exams.

**The parade ground has rules, and they are not optional.** Stay in the designated seating, do not step onto the ground for a photograph, and follow the marshals' instructions even when the ceremony pauses. Cadets are performing a drill they have rehearsed for weeks; a parent wandering into the frame is not a funny interruption, it is a break in a ceremony that matters to hundreds of families.

**Your child will be busy, not free.** Do not expect a long family afternoon after either event. Cadets usually have duties before and after the ceremony, and the passing-out batch has its own farewell schedule. The hug you get may be brief — take it, and let the rest of the day belong to the school.

**Photographs are welcome from your seat, within the school's rules.** Some schools restrict photography during specific moments, such as the march past. When in doubt, ask a teacher or marshal rather than assuming. The school's official photographer usually covers the key moments, and those photographs are often shared with parents afterwards.

**If your child is not passing out, the parade still matters.** Younger cadets watch the outgoing batch leave, and that sight does more for their motivation than any speech. It gives them a picture of where the road leads. Parents of Class 6 and 7 cadets sometimes feel like spectators at someone else's farewell — but their children are learning what six years of this education looks like, and that is worth the visit.

## The difference between the two days, in one line each

Annual Day answers the question: what did this school do this year? The Passing Out Parade answers a different one: what does this school do to a child over six years? One is a celebration of a year. The other is a farewell to a childhood spent in uniform. Go to both if you can — and if you can only make one, make it the parade of the batch your child will one day join.

## A note on what the parade is not

It is worth clearing up one common misunderstanding, because it confuses many first-time families. The Passing Out Parade of a Sainik School is not a military commissioning parade. Graduates are not officers when they march out; they are school-leavers. Many will go on to attempt the NDA and other defence entries, and the discipline of the parade ground genuinely helps them there, but the ceremony itself confers no rank and no posting. What it confers is simpler and, in its own way, larger: a public acknowledgement that a child entered this school and is leaving it as someone the school is proud to send into the world.

## FAQs

**What is the Passing Out Parade at a Sainik School?**

The Passing Out Parade is the formal farewell ceremony for the outgoing senior batch, usually Class 12 cadets. It features a ceremonial parade, the handing over of leadership appointments to juniors, and a farewell from the school, marking the end of the cadets' school journey.

**Are parents invited to the Annual Day and Passing Out Parade?**

Yes, parents of cadets are generally invited to both events, and parents of the passing-out batch are the guests of honour at the parade in most schools. Exact invitations, seating, and entry rules are set by each school, so follow the school's own notice for the year.

**What should parents wear to a Sainik School Annual Day or parade?**

Smart, comfortable clothing works best. You will be outdoors for much of a parade, often standing or sitting in the sun, so comfortable shoes matter more than formal ones. Avoid anything that would distract from the ceremony.

**Can parents take photos during the parade?**

Usually yes from the designated seating areas, but rules vary by school and by event. Some moments, such as the march past or the farewell, may have restrictions. Follow the school's instructions on the day and never step onto the parade ground.

**How is Annual Day different from the Passing Out Parade?**

Annual Day celebrates the whole school year, with prize distribution, cultural performances, and reports of achievements. The Passing Out Parade is specifically about the outgoing batch leaving the school, and it is far more military in character, with drill, marching, and formal farewells.

**Does the Passing Out Parade mean my child is joining the armed forces?**

No. Passing out of a Sainik School is a school farewell, not a military commissioning. Graduates go on to many paths, including NDA, other colleges, and civilian careers. The parade honours their school years, whatever they choose next.
