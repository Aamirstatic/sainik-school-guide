Here is the honest truth about the GK section: it is the only part of the AISSEE Class 6 paper where a child can genuinely gain 15 to 20 marks in eleven weeks without being "good at studies" in the usual sense. Maths needs years of practice. Language needs reading habits. GK needs a notebook, twenty minutes a day, and a parent who asks three questions at dinner.

And it matters more than most families think. GK is 25 questions for 50 marks — one-sixth of the whole paper. And because a child must score at least 25% in *every* section to qualify, a neglected GK section can sink an otherwise strong paper. The good news: 78 days is plenty, if you start this week and stop treating GK as "we'll do it in January."

## What GK actually covers

The section is not one subject — it is five small piles of facts. Past AISSEE papers keep returning to the same piles, so that is where the plan aims:

- **Static GK:** national symbols, states and capitals, rivers and mountains, national parks and wildlife sanctuaries, famous monuments, important days and dates.
- **Defence and the armed forces:** the three services, their chiefs' ranks, gallantry awards, and famous regiments and battles from history. For a Sainik School paper, this pile is heavier than in other exams — our guide to [defence, sports and awards questions that repeat](/blog/aissee-class-6-gk-defence-sports-awards-repeat/) goes deeper here.
- **Sports:** major tournaments, trophies, famous players and recent winners. One or two questions every year come from the sporting year just gone by.
- **Awards and personalities:** Bharat Ratna, Padma awards, Nobel Prizes, and the people in the news for the right reasons.
- **Current affairs:** the last 6 to 12 months — big national events, new schemes, elections, space and science news. Our piece on [balancing static GK with current affairs](/blog/aissee-gk-static-gk-current-affairs-balance/) explains why neither pile works alone.

## The 11-week plan

Seventy-eight days, eleven Sundays, one section. Work in weekly blocks — each week has one theme, one revision day, and one 25-question self-test on Sunday.

| Week | Dates | Theme | What to finish |
|------|-------|-------|----------------|
| 1–2 | 14–30 Nov | Static GK foundations | States and capitals, national symbols, rivers, mountains, important days |
| 3–4 | 1–14 Dec | India in detail | National parks, monuments, dance forms, folk arts, famous personalities |
| 5–6 | 15–28 Dec | Defence + sports | Three services, ranks, gallantry awards; trophies, tournaments, sportspersons of 2026 |
| 7–8 | 29 Dec–11 Jan | Current affairs | News from mid-2026 to January 2027: schemes, elections, space, science, awards |
| 9 | 12–18 Jan | Science + misc | Everyday science, inventions, famous books and authors, miscellaneous facts |
| 10 | 19–25 Jan | Full revision | Re-read the whole notebook; two timed 25-question GK tests; admit card arrives 25 Jan |
| 11 | 26–31 Jan | Light touch only | One quick read of the notebook; no new topics; exam on Sunday, 31 January |

Two rules for the whole plan. First, **no new topics in the last week** — week 11 is for remembering, not learning. Second, **the notebook is the plan**: every fact your child learns goes into one GK notebook, in their own handwriting, and revision means re-reading that notebook, not re-reading a book.

## The daily 20-minute routine

GK fails when it becomes a Sunday-only subject. It works as a daily habit, small enough to survive school days:

1. **10 minutes — one theme.** One page from the week's theme. Read it, close the book, write down what you remember.
2. **5 minutes — yesterday's page.** Re-read yesterday's page and cover-and-recall it. This is the step most children skip, and it is the step that makes facts stick.
3. **5 minutes — the news.** Read the headlines together. One question at dinner: "What happened today that a GK paper might ask about?" If nothing happened, ask about yesterday instead.

Twenty minutes. That is shorter than one episode of a cartoon, and over 78 days it adds up to more than 25 hours of GK — enough to cover every pile above twice.

## Three mistakes to avoid

**Mistake 1: Buying five GK books.** One compendium plus NCERT is enough. Five books means the child reads a little of each and finishes none. Depth in one book beats breadth across five.

**Mistake 2: Only studying current affairs.** Current affairs feels urgent and modern, so families over-weight it. In past papers, static GK has been at least half the section. The current-affairs-only child walks in knowing the news and missing the capitals.

**Mistake 3: Treating GK as a "last month" subject.** January is for mocks, revision and the admit card — not for learning 28 states and their capitals for the first time. The families who finish GK by mid-January walk into the exam calm. The families who start in January walk in guessing.

## How GK fits the rest of the paper

GK is the section to finish fast inside the hall. The paper is 150 minutes for 125 questions; Maths alone needs the biggest share. Plan roughly 20 to 25 minutes for GK: read the question, recall the answer, mark it, move on. A question you don't know costs you two marks at most — and with no negative marking, a guess on an unknown question costs nothing. Never leave a GK question blank, and never let one stuck question steal time from Maths.

If your child is following a full-paper schedule, slot this GK plan into it — our [final 79-days, 11-week study plan](/blog/aissee-2027-final-79-days-11-week-study-plan-school-days/) shows how the sections share the week, and our [mock-test calendar](/blog/aissee-2027-mock-test-calendar-final-80-days/) tells you when to test GK under timed conditions. Test it every Sunday: 25 questions, 25 minutes, marked honestly. A Sunday test that shows 14 out of 25 in week 3 and 20 out of 25 in week 8 is the clearest proof the plan is working.

Start tonight. One page, ten minutes, three questions at dinner. By 31 January, that is a section your child walks into without fear — and in a paper where every section must be passed, "without fear" is worth marks all by itself.

## FAQs

**How many GK questions are there in AISSEE Class 6?**

25 questions worth 50 marks — 2 marks each. The Class 6 paper is 125 questions for 300 marks in 150 minutes, and GK is one of four sections alongside Maths, Language and Intelligence.

**Is GK the easiest AISSEE section to improve in 11 weeks?**

For most children, yes. Maths and Language need months of skill-building, but GK is largely memory and awareness — a steady 20 minutes a day for 11 weeks can add real marks. It is also the section where a blank costs you nothing, since there is no negative marking.

**How much current affairs should my child study for AISSEE 2027?**

Focus on the last 6 to 12 months before the exam — roughly mid-2026 to January 2027 — plus the big recurring events of the year. Past papers mix current affairs with static GK, so current affairs alone is never enough.

**Does my child need a separate GK book for AISSEE?**

Not necessarily. The Class 6 NCERT EVS and social science books cover most of the static base. Add one Class 6-level GK compendium for facts and a habit of reading the news headlines together for 10 minutes a day.

**How much time should my child spend on GK inside the 150-minute paper?**

Aim for about 20 to 25 minutes. GK questions are mostly one-line answers — read, recall, mark, move on. Do not let one stuck question eat the time Maths needs.

**Can GK alone decide whether my child qualifies?**

It can decide it the other way round. A child needs at least 25 percent in every section and 40 percent overall to qualify, so a weak GK section can fail an otherwise strong paper. GK is too big — 50 marks — to leave to chance.
