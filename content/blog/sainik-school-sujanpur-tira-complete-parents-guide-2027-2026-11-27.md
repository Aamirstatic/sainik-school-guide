## Himachal's only Sainik School — and one of the smallest doors in the country

Here is the fact that shapes everything else in this guide: **Sainik School Sujanpur Tira is Himachal Pradesh's only Sainik School.** A child from Shimla, Dharamshala, Mandi or Solan who wants a Sainik School education has exactly one in-state option — and that makes the home-state quota the whole story.

Under the Sainik Schools Society rules, **67 percent of seats go to children domiciled in the home state**. At Sujanpur Tira that means 40 of the 59 Class 6 seats are reserved for Himachal children, competing only against each other. The remaining 19 seats are open to children from every other state in India.

And then there is the second fact, the one that should change how you plan: Sujanpur Tira's **59 Class 6 seats are the second-smallest intake of any Sainik School in India** — only Jhansi, with 50, takes fewer. This is not a school where a decent score is enough. A Himachal boy in the General category is competing for 14 seats. An other-state boy in the General category is competing for 6. The arithmetic is honest, and it is brutal, and it is the reason this guide starts with the numbers rather than the brochure.

## Forty-eight years old, on the ground where kings once held court

Sujanpur Tira was inaugurated on **2 November 1978** by the then President of India, **Shri Neelam Sanjeeva Reddy** — the 18th school in the national chain of Sainik Schools, set up to serve the people of Himachal Pradesh. The first batch of **50 students** had arrived a fortnight earlier, on **14 October 1978**, when the school was three buildings — one hostel (today's Chenab House), one mess and one school building — and the first principal, **Wing Commander H. S. Mehta**, gave the excited boys a personal tour of a real helicopter's cockpit.

The setting is part of the school's character. Sujanpur Tira is a historic town on the banks of the Beas, famous for its Chaugan — the great open ground the school sits on — and for the Narbadeshwar temple with its Kangra-style miniature paintings. The campus is small by Sainik School standards at **28 acres including the Chaugan**, but it is packed: five football grounds, two hockey fields, a handball ground, an athletics track, four basketball courts, a lawn tennis court, a squash court, a swimming pool and four badminton courts, with the Dhauladhar ranges as the backdrop. The main academic building is the Sudhir Block, facing the rectangular ground; the cadets' mess has two blocks named after Field Marshals Cariappa and Manekshaw.

Getting there takes planning, and parents should know this before admission day. The school is **24 km from Hamirpur**, the district headquarters. The nearest broad-gauge railhead is **Una, 99 km away**, and Kangra Airport at Gaggal is **75 km away**. For most families, the journey is by road — which is also why the home-state quota matters: this is a school built for Himachal's children, and the journey home for the holidays is a Himachal journey.

One more thing about Hamirpur district that changed this year: it now hosts **two** Sainik Schools. Woods Park Sainik School, a newer school under the PPP model, sits in the same district — and it is the school that 57 tribal students from Arunachal Pradesh won seats at this week through the Army's Mission Sainik School programme, as we [reported on 26 November](/news/army-mission-sainik-school-57-arunachal-tribal-students-woods-park-2026-11-26/). Both schools admit through the same AISSEE, but they are different institutions with different seat matrices — do not confuse them when you fill in your school preferences.

## The seats: 59 in Class 6, 6 in Class 9

This is the part parents get wrong most often, because older admission guides quote different numbers — some still list 64 Class 6 seats, the 2026-27 figure. The AISSEE 2027 bulletin says **59**, and the bulletin is the only source that settles it. We read both appendices directly from the official NTA bulletin PDF for this guide, so these are the numbers to plan on. They are tentative — the bulletin says so — but they are the official tentative numbers.

| Class 6 (2027-28) | Home state (Himachal Pradesh) | Other states | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boys | 35 (SC 5, ST 2, OBC 10, Defence 4, General 14) | 14 (SC 2, ST 0, OBC 4, Defence 2, General 6) | 49 |
| Girls | 5 (SC 1, ST 1, OBC 1, Defence 1, General 1) | 5 (SC 1, ST 1, OBC 1, Defence 1, General 1) | 10 |
| **Total** | **40** | **19** | **59** |

| Class 9 (2027-28) | Home state (Himachal Pradesh) | Other states | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Boys | 4 (SC 1, ST 0, OBC 1, Defence 1, General 1) | 2 (SC 0, ST 0, OBC 1, Defence 0, General 1) | 6 |
| Girls | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Total** | **4** | **2** | **6** |

Read that Class 9 table twice before deciding. Six seats, all for boys, and a General-category Himachal boy is competing for exactly **one** seat. Class 9 entry at Sujanpur Tira is a lottery ticket, not a plan — if your child is in Class 8, apply here by all means, but put your real hope in the Class 6 route for a younger sibling, or in a school with a larger Class 9 intake. Our [Himachal guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-himachal-pradesh-complete-guide-2027/) covers the same ground for the state, and our [home-state quota guide](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-explained/) explains why 40 of these 59 seats are the ones that matter.

For context, all 33 Sainik Schools together have 3,160 Class 6 seats in 2027-28. Sujanpur Tira holds fewer than one in fifty of them. The reservation math inside those seats follows the standard pattern: SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27%, with 25% of the remainder for defence wards — and 10% of Class 6 seats, or 10 seats, whichever is more, for girls. Unfilled girls' seats go to boys of the same category.

## The fee: what the school's own sheet says

The school's own fee document for 2026-27 puts a new Class 6 cadet's first year at **₹1,92,755** (₹1,91,255 for SC/ST). The breakdown, as the school published it:

| --- | --- |
| Tuition fee (₹1,16,923 + 10%) | ₹1,28,615 |
| Dietary charges (₹158 × 295 days) | ₹46,610 |
| Clothing charges | ₹6,250 |
| Pocket money | ₹3,000 |
| Incidental charges | ₹1,500 |
| Smart class fee | ₹1,350 |
| Caution money (refundable) | ₹3,000 (₹1,500 SC/ST) |
| SPACS | ₹1,650 |
| Misc receipts + Maths Olympiad | ₹780 |
| **Total, General** | **₹1,92,755** |
| **Total, SC/ST** | **₹1,91,255** |

A new Class 9 cadet pays slightly more — ₹1,93,075 (₹1,91,575 SC/ST) — and old (continuing) cadets pay ₹1,86,005, with no caution money and lower clothing charges. Careers360 lists an older figure of ₹1,48,519 — a different year, and the two cannot both be the 2027-28 fee. The school has not published a 2027-28 sheet yet, and tuition rises about 10 percent a year under Sainik Schools Society rules, so budget **around ₹2.1 lakh for the first year** and confirm the exact figure from the school before paying. The fee is paid by PNB challan, demand draft to the Principal, or the school's online gateway — and the school's May 2026 notice asked parents to pay by 31 May, so the pattern is one annual payment, not instalments.

## A day at Sujanpur Tira

We could not verify Sujanpur Tira's own published daily timetable, so what follows is the standard Sainik School day — the pattern every school in the system runs, with local timings varying slightly. Do not quote these hours as the school's own.

Reveille is early, around 5 AM, and the day starts with PT and drill — on the Chaugan, in the mountain air, which is a different proposition from PT on a plains campus. Then bath, breakfast and assembly, and a full morning of classes — seven or eight periods, English medium, CBSE syllabus, until about 2 PM. Afternoons are for lunch, rest and clubs; late afternoon is games and NCC, and this is where the school's independent NCC unit shows up in the routine rather than on a brochure — NCC is compulsory from Class VI to XI here. Evenings are prep: supervised study where the day's lessons are revised and homework is finished, because there is no going home to do it. Lights out is around 10–10:30 PM, and the next morning starts the same way.

For an 11-year-old joining Class 6, the shock is not the study — it is the routine. Waking yourself, making your bed, keeping your kit in order, eating on time, living with five other houses of boys in dormitories named after rivers. Parents who prepare their child for the routine, not just the exam, have a far easier first term. The six houses — Beas, Satluj, Ravi, Chenab, Yamuna and Jhelum — are the child's family inside the school, and the house competitions are where most of the growing up happens.

## How to apply

Admission is through **AISSEE 2027 only** — there is no direct admission, no management quota, and no donation route. The form is open now on the official NTA portal, **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society**, and it closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. With five days left, the weekend is your buffer, not your plan: submit by Sunday, and keep Monday and Tuesday for fixing whatever the portal throws back at you. The fee is ₹850 (General/OBC-NCL/defence) or ₹700 (SC/ST); the late window runs 3–6 December with an extra ₹500, and corrections can be made from 7–9 December.

- **Class 6:** age 10–12 on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017 — and currently in Class 5. Boys and girls both eligible.
- **Class 9:** age 13–15 on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014 — and must have passed Class 8 from a recognised school at the time of admission. English only.
- **The exam:** Sunday, 31 January 2027. Class 6 is 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks; Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks. No negative marking; 25% per section and 40% aggregate to qualify (not applicable to SC/ST).

After the exam comes the result, then AISSAC e-counselling — online, no fee, run by the Sainik Schools Society — where your school preferences and the home-state quota decide the allotment. Choose your preferences carefully: putting Sujanpur Tira first as a Himachal-domiciled applicant is the single highest-value decision in the whole process, and our [AISSAC choice-filling guide](/blog/how-to-choose-school-preferences-aissac-counselling/) explains why the order matters more than most parents think.

## The honest questions to ask

Before you commit a year of your child's life to this school, ask these — of the school, of parents whose children study there, and of yourself:

1. **Can my child win one of 14 General-category home-state seats?** That is the real question at Sujanpur Tira. If the mock-test scores say no, the honest move is to keep Sujanpur Tira on the preference list and build the preparation around a school with a bigger intake as well.
2. **Are we applying for Class 6 or Class 9?** If Class 9, six seats for the whole country is not a plan — it is a hope. Apply, but keep a parallel plan: RMS, JNVST, or next year's attempt.
3. **Can we afford roughly ₹1.9–2.1 lakh a year, every year, for seven years?** The first-year fee is not the cost. The cost is seven years of it, rising about 10 percent a year. Ask the school directly about scholarships and fee concessions for defence wards and SC/ST families before you commit.
4. **Does my child actually want this?** A Sainik School is a wonderful education and a hard life in equal measure — and a small school on a hill in Hamirpur is a long way from home for a child who is not sure. A child who wants to be there thrives; a child who was pushed there counts the days. Ask them, and listen to the answer.

Sujanpur Tira is not the biggest Sainik School, and it does not pretend to be. What it is, is Himachal's only one — 48 years old, on a historic Chaugan beside the Beas, with six river-named houses, an NCC unit that runs from Class 6, and a fee sheet that is honest about what a year costs. For a Himachal family with a child in Class 5, those 40 home-state seats are the best odds the state offers. The form closes on 2 December. This weekend is the time to fill it.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Sujanpur Tira have for 2027-28?**

Sujanpur Tira has 59 Class 6 seats — 49 for boys (35 Himachal Pradesh domicile and 14 other states) and 10 for girls (5 Himachal Pradesh and 5 other states) — and just 6 Class 9 seats, all for boys. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Sujanpur Tira founded?**

It was inaugurated on 2 November 1978 by the then President of India, Shri Neelam Sanjeeva Reddy — the 18th Sainik School in the national chain. The first batch of 50 students had arrived a fortnight earlier, on 14 October 1978, under the first principal, Wing Commander H. S. Mehta.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Sujanpur Tira?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Sujanpur Tira?**

The school's own fee document for 2026-27 puts a new Class 6 cadet's first year at Rs 1,92,755 (Rs 1,91,255 for SC/ST), including tuition of Rs 1,28,615 and dietary charges of Rs 46,610. Careers360 lists an older figure of Rs 1,48,519. Tuition rises about 10 percent a year, so budget around Rs 2.1 lakh for 2027-28 and confirm the exact figure from the school before paying.

**Does Sainik School Sujanpur Tira admit girls?**

Yes — there are 10 girls' seats in Class 6 for 2027-28 (5 for Himachal Pradesh girls and 5 for other states). There are no girls' seats in Class 9 this year; the 6 Class 9 seats are all for boys.

**Is Sainik School Sujanpur Tira the only Sainik School in Himachal Pradesh?**

It is the state's only school under the Sainik Schools Society — which is why the 67 percent home-state quota matters so much here: 40 of the 59 Class 6 seats are reserved for Himachal Pradesh children. Hamirpur district also hosts a newer PPP Sainik School, Woods Park, which admits through the same AISSEE.
