## The number nobody tells you first: it's not one bill

When parents ask about Sainik School fees, they usually get a single annual figure — somewhere around ₹1 lakh to ₹1.9 lakh a year, according to fee guides. That number is real, but it hides the part that actually causes trouble: almost nobody pays it in one go, and the schedule you pay on matters as much as the total.

Here's the thing — the school your child joins will hand you a payment schedule, not just a total. Some schools split the year into two instalments. Some bill quarterly. Some issue a statement of account twice a year and expect payment by fixed dates. Miss the schedule, and the consequences range from a surcharge to, in the strictest schools, your child being struck off the rolls. This guide walks through the models schools actually use, with real published examples, so you can plan the money before admission day rather than scrambling after it.

## The three payment models schools use

| Model | How it works | Published example |
|---|---|---|
| Two instalments (half-yearly) | Pay part at admission/term start, the balance mid-year | Sainik School Kodagu: half-yearly instalments; Sainik School Kittur: 1st and 2nd instalment |
| Lump sum or two instalments | Parent's choice: full year at once, or 70% first + balance later | Sainik School Imphal prospectus |
| Quarterly in advance | Fees paid at least quarterly, before the quarter begins | Sainik School Chittorgarh withdrawal policy |

No model is "the Sainik School system." Each school's prospectus sets its own. That is the single most important sentence in this article: **read your school's prospectus, not a general guide, before you plan.**

## Worked examples: what the schedules actually look like

### Sainik School Kittur (2026–27, new admission)

Kittur publishes one of the clearest fee tables available, and it shows why first year costs more than later years:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| School, diet, tuition, admission, insurance, caution money, uniform, pocket money, deposits, kit and clothing | ₹2,36,900 total for a new admission |
| 1st instalment (online, SBI Collect only) | ₹1,46,900 |
| 2nd instalment (online) | ₹90,000 |
| Fees for existing students (later years, approximate) | ₹1,71,700 |

Notice the gap: a first-year parent pays about ₹65,000 more than a returning parent, because admission fee, caution money, uniform and kit are one-time charges. If you are budgeting for Class 6 entry, budget the first-year figure, not the "annual fee" you saw quoted for older students.

### Sainik School Imphal: the 70% rule and the 5% sting

Imphal's prospectus offers a choice: pay the whole fee in one lump sum, or pay in two instalments — **70% of the total at the beginning, and the balance plus 5% of the second instalment by December**. Imphal also runs a six-monthly billing system: statements of account are issued in May and December, and parents must pay dues by **15 June and 15 December** each year.

That 5% matters. On a second instalment of, say, ₹60,000, the surcharge is ₹3,000 — the price of choosing instalments over lump sum. It is not a penalty for late payment; it is the built-in cost of splitting. Know which one you are paying before you choose.

### Sainik School Kodagu: the simple half-yearly split

Kodagu's prospectus is refreshingly plain: parents may pay fees in half-yearly instalments, at ₹40,030 at the beginning of each term (the figure in the prospectus extract; confirm the current session's figure with the school). The academic year is divided into two equal terms for fee purposes, and fees are charged for the full term even if a cadet joins late in a term — though a diet-charge rebate applies if admission is 10 or more days late.

### Sainik School Chittorgarh: quarterly, and strict

Chittorgarh's policy is the one to take most seriously. All fees are payable in advance and must be paid **at least quarterly, before the beginning of the quarter**. If dues are not received by the first day of the month they fall due, a reminder goes out; if the amount is still unpaid by the 15th of the month, the cadet can be struck off the rolls and sent home at the parent's cost, with the amount deducted from caution money. The transfer certificate is not issued until all dues are cleared and any scholarship money enjoyed is refunded.

That is not a scare story — it is the published policy. The lesson is not that schools are harsh; it is that the fee calendar deserves the same seriousness as the exam calendar.

## What the annual total actually includes

Across schools, the annual fee is a bundle, not a single charge. Typical components include tuition, diet (mess) charges, hostel and boarding, uniform and kit, pocket money, health insurance, caution money (refundable deposit), and small deposits such as a co-operative society deposit or alumni fee. Kittur's table lists all of these separately; other schools bundle them differently.

Two components deserve a second look. **Caution money** is a deposit, not a fee — but it is also the first thing adjusted against unpaid dues, as Chittorgarh's policy shows. **Pocket money and incidental charges** (Kodagu asks ₹1,500 each at the start of the year in its prospectus) are small, but they are charged on top of the headline fee, so a budget built only on "tuition + mess" will come up short.

## The payment calendar parents should build

You cannot copy another school's dates, but you can copy the structure. Build your calendar the week admission is confirmed:

1. **Admission week:** pay the first instalment plus all one-time charges (admission fee, caution money, uniform, kit). Confirm the payment method — Kittur takes SBI Collect online only; Imphal's older prospectus names demand drafts and states cash is not accepted. Methods change; the current prospectus wins.
2. **Mid-year (November–December):** the second instalment falls due. Imphal's deadline is December, with the 5% surcharge on the second instalment; Kittur's second instalment is the balance of the year's total.
3. **Before each term or quarter:** if your school bills quarterly (Chittorgarh model) or half-yearly (Kodagu model), set a reminder a week before each due date, not on it. Bank failures on the due date are still late payments.
4. **Scholarship season:** if your child receives a state scholarship or fee concession, track it separately. Scholarships reduce the bill, but the instalment dates usually do not move — if the scholarship arrives late, ask the school office in writing how that instalment is treated.
5. **Withdrawal planning:** if there is any chance of withdrawal, check the notice rule now, not later. Kodagu charges fees up to the end of the month after withdrawal, and parents withdrawing after 30 September pay for the full academic session. Chittorgarh requires two months' written notice before the next term, or caution money is forfeited.

## The mistakes that cost real money

- **Budgeting the returning-student fee for a first-year admission.** First year includes one-time charges; Kittur's gap is about ₹65,000.
- **Choosing instalments without reading the surcharge.** Imphal's 5% on the second instalment is a real cost, not a footnote.
- **Assuming a grace period exists.** Some schools send reminders; Chittorgarh's policy escalates to striking off within weeks. Never assume.
- **Paying by the wrong method.** A payment sent by a method the school does not accept is not a payment. Confirm the method every year.
- **Forgetting the small charges.** Pocket money, insurance, deposits and kit add thousands on top of the headline figure.
- **Mixing up the exam fee and the school fee.** The AISSEE application fee (₹850 General/OBC/Defence, ₹700 SC/ST in the 2026 cycle) is paid to NTA at application time. The school fee is a separate, much larger annual cost paid to the school after admission. They are not the same bill, and paying one does not cover the other.

## A note on fees and concessions

If the annual total looks out of reach, do not rule the school out before checking concessions. State governments offer full or partial fee waivers based on family income, and defence wards and low-income families receive the most help — we covered the income slabs and application process in our [fee concession guide](/blog/sainik-school-fee-concession-income-slabs-how-to-apply-2026-10-16/) and the broader [fee comparison across schools](/blog/sainik-school-fee-comparison-all-schools/). Apply for the concession early: a concession approved after the first instalment date does not automatically move that date.

## FAQs

**Can Sainik School fees be paid in instalments?**

Yes, in most schools. The common models are two half-yearly instalments or quarterly payments in advance, and some schools also allow a single lump-sum payment. The exact schedule is set by each school's prospectus, so confirm it with your child's school.

**What happens if I pay a Sainik School fee instalment late?**

It depends on the school. Some charge a surcharge on the delayed instalment, others send a reminder and then strike the cadet off the rolls if dues remain unpaid past the grace date. Never assume a grace period exists — check the prospectus wording for your school.

**Are Sainik School fees the same every year?**

No. First-year fees are higher because they include one-time charges such as admission fee, caution money, uniform and kit. Later years drop those one-time items. Fees also rise over time under Sainik Schools Society rules.

**Do scholarships change the payment schedule?**

Scholarships reduce what you actually pay, and state scholarship amounts are adjusted against the fee bill, but the instalment dates themselves usually stay the same. If a scholarship is delayed, ask the school office in writing how the instalment is treated in the meantime.

**How are Sainik School fees paid — cash, cheque, or online?**

Payment methods vary by school and have changed over time. Some schools accept only demand drafts or online payment through a portal such as SBI Collect, and some state plainly that cash is not accepted. Use only the method your school's current prospectus names.

**Is the AISSEE application fee the same as the school fee?**

No. The AISSEE application fee is a small one-time exam fee paid to NTA when applying. The school fee is the annual boarding and tuition cost paid to the school after admission, and it is many times larger.
