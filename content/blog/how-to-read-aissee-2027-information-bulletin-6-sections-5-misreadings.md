## The document nobody reads — and everybody quotes

Here's the thing. Ask ten parents in an AISSEE WhatsApp group where they got a date or a rule, and nine will say "someone posted it." Almost nobody has actually opened the Information Bulletin. That is how a wrong age cut-off or a made-up fee travels faster than the truth — and how a perfectly eligible child ends up with a rejected form.

The bulletin is not a long read. It is a government document, yes, and the language is dry, but the whole thing that matters fits in six sections. Read those six, in this order, and you know more than most coaching centres. Skip them, and you are trusting strangers with your child's admission.

Last year, a parent nearly abandoned his son's form because a video had told him the Class 6 age limit was "10 to 12 years on the exam date" — his son turns 12 in February 2027. The bulletin counts age on **31 March 2027**, and his son was safely inside the window. One sentence in the right document saved a year. That is what this guide is for.

## The bulletin map — six sections, in the order to read them

| # | Section | What you actually need from it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Important dates | The full calendar — apply, late fee, correction, city slip, admit card, exam |
| 2 | Eligibility & age | The date-of-birth windows for Class 6 and Class 9, and the Class 8 pass rule |
| 3 | Exam scheme & pattern | Questions, marks, timing, medium — and no negative marking |
| 4 | List of schools & seats | The 33 Sainik Schools plus the 80 (Class 6) / 40 (Class 9) New Sainik Schools, with tentative seats |
| 5 | Reservation & domicile | The 67/33 home-state split, category quotas, defence-ward share |
| 6 | Documents & how to apply | Upload sizes, certificates, fee, and the one-form rule |

Read section 1 first, because it holds the only hard deadline in the cycle. Then section 2 — if your child fails the age window, nothing else matters.

## Section 1: the dates — write these on the wall

| Event | Date |
|---|---|
| Online application | 3 November – 2 December 2026 (up to 11:50 PM) |
| Last date for fee payment | 2 December 2026 (up to 11:50 PM) |
| Late application (₹500 late fee) | 3 – 6 December 2026 |
| Correction window | 7 – 9 December 2026 |
| Exam city intimation slip | 11 January 2027 |
| Admit card | 25 January 2027 |
| **Exam date** | **Sunday, 31 January 2027** |
| Class 6 timing | 2:00 PM – 4:30 PM (150 minutes) |
| Class 9 timing | 2:00 PM – 5:00 PM (180 minutes) |

Two things parents miss here. First, the late-fee window is real but narrow — 3 to 6 December, ₹500 extra, and then the door closes. Second, the correction window (7–9 December) is for correcting particulars, not for fixing a fundamentally wrong form. Our [post-submission checklist](/news/aissee-2027-form-submitted-5-things-to-check-confirmation-page-2026-11-17/) explains what to verify the moment you submit.

## Section 2: the age window — read the dates, not the ages

The bulletin gives age as a **date-of-birth window**, and that is the only thing that counts:

- **Class 6:** born **1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017** (10 to 12 years on 31 March 2027)
- **Class 9:** born **1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014** (13 to 15 years on 31 March 2027), and the child must have passed Class 8 at the time of admission

Girls can apply for Class 6 in all Sainik Schools, and for Class 9 subject to availability of vacancies. Our [age-window explainer](/news/aissee-2027-16-days-left-age-window-birthday-decides-class-2026-11-16/) works through the boundary birthdays — 31 March and 1 April — in detail, because those two dates decide more forms than any other rule in the bulletin.

## Section 3: the pattern — know what each question is worth

| Class 6 | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Language | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| Mathematics | 50 (3 marks each) | 150 |
| Intelligence | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| General Knowledge | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| **Total** | **125** | **300** |

| Class 9 | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 50 (4 marks each) | 200 |
| Intelligence | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| English | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| General Science | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| Social Science | 25 (2 marks each) | 50 |
| **Total** | **150** | **400** |

The paper is pen-and-paper on an OMR sheet, multiple choice, with **no negative marking** — so every question should be attempted. The qualifying rule is **25% in each section and 40% in aggregate** (it does not apply to SC/ST candidates). Read that carefully: a child can score 250 out of 300 and still fail if one section falls below 25%.

## Sections 4 and 5: schools, seats and quotas

Across the 33 Sainik Schools there are **3,160 tentative Class 6 seats and 732 Class 9 seats** for 2027-28. The New Sainik Schools add 80 schools for Class 6 and 40 for Class 9 — but they sit in a **separate list** in the bulletin, and you must check a school against the right list before choosing it. Our [new Sainik Schools explainer](/news/aissee-2027-new-sainik-schools-80-class-6-40-class-9-explained-2026-11-06/) walks through the difference.

Reservation, in one line: **67% of seats for children domiciled in the school's state, 33% for other states**; within those, **15% SC, 7.5% ST, 27% OBC-NCL** (central list), and **25% of the remaining seats for wards of defence personnel and ex-servicemen**. The word that matters is *domiciled* — it is the domicile certificate that decides the home-state quota, not where the child studies today.

## Section 6: documents, fee and the one-form rule

- **Fee:** ₹850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards; ₹700 for SC/ST. Bank charges are extra, and the fee is **not refundable**.
- **Photo:** JPG, 10 KB to 200 KB. **Signature and left-hand thumb impression:** JPG, 10 KB to 50 KB each. **Certificates:** PDF, 50 KB to 300 KB.
- **One form per child.** If two forms are paid for the same class, NTA keeps the latest one. A second form buys nothing.
- The form is complete **only when the confirmation page is generated** — paying the fee is not enough. Download it and keep it.

Our [upload-mistakes guide](/news/aissee-2027-15-days-left-5-upload-mistakes-that-reject-forms-2026-11-17/) covers the file-size traps in detail, because a photo at the wrong size is still the commonest reason a form gets stuck.

## The 5 misreadings that cost forms

| # | The misreading | What the bulletin actually says |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "My child is 12, so Class 6 is fine" | Age is counted on **31 March 2027**, not today and not on exam day. Check the birth-date window, not the current age. |
| 2 | "Class 9 can pick Hindi medium" | Class 9 is **English only**. Class 6 offers 13 languages — and the medium **cannot be changed** after submission. |
| 3 | "I'll submit a second form to fix the first" | One form per child. NTA keeps the **latest paid form**; a second form fixes nothing and can only confuse the record. |
| 4 | "I'll pick an exam city near the school I want" | The exam city has **no link** to the school. Choose four cities for convenience — allotment depends on merit and domicile, not the city. |
| 5 | "The fee is refundable if we withdraw" | It is not. ₹850/₹700 plus bank charges, **non-refundable** — so check everything before paying, not after. |

## How to use the bulletin from here

Print the dates table, or write it on the wall where your child studies. Before you pay, sit with the form and the bulletin side by side and check five things: the birth-date window, the medium, the four exam cities, the document file sizes, and the school list your child actually belongs in. Fifteen minutes with the real document beats fifteen videos from strangers.

And one last thing — if a coaching centre or an agent quotes you a date, a fee or a rule that is not in the bulletin, ask them to show you where it is written. If they can't, it isn't real.

## FAQs

**Where is the official AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin?**

On the official NTA portal, exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society. It was released on 3 November 2026 along with the public notice. Any other site's copy is a mirror — check the dates and rules against the portal.

**What is the age limit for AISSEE 2027 Class 6 and Class 9?**

Age is counted on 31 March 2027. Class 6: born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017 (10 to 12 years). Class 9: born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014 (13 to 15 years), and the child must have passed Class 8 at the time of admission.

**Can the exam medium be changed after submitting the AISSEE 2027 form?**

No. The bulletin states the medium cannot be changed after the form is submitted. Class 6 candidates choose from 13 languages; Class 9 is English only.

**What happens if two AISSEE 2027 forms are submitted for the same child?**

Only one form per child is allowed. If two forms are paid for the same class, NTA keeps the latest one. A second form does not give a second chance — it can only create confusion.

**Does the exam city chosen in the AISSEE form affect school allotment?**

No. You choose any four exam cities, and the exam city has no link to the school you want. What decides the home-state quota is the domicile certificate, not the exam city.

**What are the qualifying marks in AISSEE 2027?**

At least 25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate, for Sainik Schools. The rule does not apply to SC and ST candidates.
