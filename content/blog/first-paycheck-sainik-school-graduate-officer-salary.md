Gross and in-hand figures are estimates that vary with the current dearness allowance rate and posting city — they are labelled as such, not presented as fixed pay slips.

## The question every Sainik School family eventually asks

At some point — usually over dinner, usually when a relative asks what the point of all this discipline is — a Sainik School parent hears the question: "But what will he actually earn?" It is a fair question, and it deserves a real answer, not a motivational poster. So here it is, with the numbers as they stand under the 7th Pay Commission, which is the pay structure in force today.

The short version: a Sainik School graduate who clears the NDA written exam, the SSB interview and four years of training is commissioned as a Lieutenant (or the Navy/Air Force equivalent) on basic pay of **Rs 56,100 a month**, plus **Rs 15,500 a month** of Military Service Pay. Add dearness allowance and the standard allowances, and the gross package lands at roughly **Rs 1 lakh a month** for a 21- or 22-year-old. The rest of this article shows how that number is built, how it grows, and what it does not include.

## The road to the first paycheck

Before the first salary comes a long road, and the pay story starts earlier than commissioning:

| Stage | What happens | Pay |
|---|---|---|
| Class 6 or 9 | Admission to Sainik School via AISSEE | School fees apply (separate from this article) |
| Class 12 | NDA written exam (UPSC), then SSB interview, then medical | No pay |
| NDA, Khadakwasla (3 years) | Tri-service training | Cadet stipend Rs 56,100/month |
| Service academy (1 year) | IMA Dehradun / INA Ezhimala / AFA Hyderabad | Stipend continues at Rs 56,100/month |
| Commissioning | Lieutenant (Army), Sub Lieutenant (Navy), Flying Officer (Air Force) | Full officer pay begins |

Two things about the stipend matter. First, the UPSC NDA notification fixes it at Rs 56,100 a month — the same figure as a Lieutenant's starting basic pay. Second, it is a stipend, not a salary: Military Service Pay and most regular allowances do not apply during training, and messing and other deductions come out of it. Some secondary websites quote an older, lower stipend figure; the UPSC notification figure is the one to trust.

## The first paycheck, line by line

A newly commissioned Lieutenant's monthly package is built from fixed components and variable ones:

| Component | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Basic pay (Level 10) | Rs 56,100 | 7th CPC, same for Army, Navy and Air Force |
| Military Service Pay (MSP) | Rs 15,500 | Officers only; fixed, not linked to inflation |
| Subtotal before allowances | Rs 71,600 | The figure every other allowance builds on |
| Dearness Allowance (DA) | Varies | Revised twice a year; calculated on basic pay |
| Transport Allowance | Rs 3,600–7,200 | Depends on the posting city's classification |
| House Rent Allowance | 24% / 16% / 8% of basic | Only when government accommodation is not provided |
| **Approximate gross** | **~Rs 1,00,000–1,10,000** | Varies by posting city and current DA rate |

That gross figure is the honest centre of the range published by 2026 pay guides. Some guides quote up to Rs 1.25 lakh for metro postings with higher HRA; others quote Rs 85,000–1,00,000 in hand after deductions. The spread is real, not a contradiction — it reflects different cities, different DA snapshots and whether accommodation is provided in kind.

## The parts that never appear on the pay slip

A civilian salary comparison that stops at the pay slip understates the officer's package badly. These are the components families forget to count:

- **Free accommodation or HRA.** Officers posted to cantonments are typically allotted government quarters — a 2BHK or 3BHK depending on family status — for a nominal licence fee. Where quarters are unavailable, HRA is paid instead. Either way, the officer is not paying market rent for most of their career.
- **Ration money.** Officers in peace stations receive ration money, roughly Rs 18,000–22,000 a month in recent guides, which is not taxed as salary. In field areas, rations are issued in kind instead.
- **Medical cover.** Free medical treatment for the officer and dependents through military hospitals, and ECHS cover after retirement for the officer and spouse for life.
- **CSD canteen access.** Subsidised prices on groceries, electronics and fuel — guides estimate a saving of roughly Rs 3,000–5,000 a month for a typical household.
- **Leave and LTC.** Sixty days of earned leave a year (against 30 for most civilian central government staff), plus Leave Travel Concession for the family.

Add the accommodation and ration value to the cash package, and the effective monthly value of a Lieutenant's first posting is closer to Rs 1.25–1.40 lakh — before counting the pension, which for post-2004 officers runs through the National Pension System alongside One Rank One Pension.

## The pay ladder: where the first paycheck leads

| Rank | Pay Level | Entry basic pay | Typical time to reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lieutenant | 10 | Rs 56,100 | On commissioning |
| Captain | 10B | Rs 61,300 | ~2 years (time-based) |
| Major | 11 | Rs 69,400 | ~6 years (time-based) |
| Lieutenant Colonel | 12A | Rs 1,21,200 | ~13 years (selection-based) |
| Colonel | 13 | Rs 1,30,600 | ~15–26 years (selection-based) |
| Brigadier | 13A | Rs 1,39,600 | Highly selective |

The jump that matters most is Major to Lieutenant Colonel: basic pay nearly doubles, from Rs 69,400 to Rs 1,21,200, and every allowance calculated on basic pay jumps with it. But that promotion is selection-based — roughly 60–70 percent of eligible Majors make it, according to 2026 career guides — so it is the first point where the career stops being automatic and starts being competitive. Families should know that now, not be surprised by it in year 13.

## Three honest limits on these numbers

**First, nobody is promised this paycheck.** The chain from AISSEE to commissioning has a filter at every stage — the NDA written exam, the SSB, the medical, and four years of training. Sainik School improves a child's odds; it does not guarantee them. Any article that presents officer pay as a certain return on school fees is selling something.

**Second, these are 7th Pay Commission figures.** An 8th Pay Commission will eventually revise them upward, and you will see speculative 8th CPC figures online (often around 1.9 times the current matrix). Until a new matrix is notified, those are estimates. Do not plan a household budget on them.

**Third, posting changes everything.** A Lieutenant in a high-altitude field posting earns field and high-altitude allowances that a peace-station Lieutenant does not, and a Siachen posting carries its own allowance of Rs 31,500 a month. The first paycheck is not one number — it is a range, and the posting decides where in the range a given officer lands.

## The bottom line for the dinner table

A Sainik School graduate who becomes an officer starts at Rs 71,600 a month before allowances and roughly Rs 1 lakh a month gross with them, at an age when most graduates are still looking for their first job — plus accommodation, ration, medical cover and a pension system that civilian employers rarely match. That is a genuinely strong first paycheck. It is also the reward at the end of a decade-long funnel that starts with AISSEE, and the families who get there are the ones who treated each stage — the entrance exam, the school years, the NDA attempt — as its own goal, not as a guaranteed escalator.

## FAQs

**What is the starting salary of a newly commissioned Lieutenant?**

Basic pay of Rs 56,100 a month at Pay Level 10 of the 7th Pay Commission, plus Military Service Pay of Rs 15,500 a month, giving Rs 71,600 before dearness allowance and other allowances. With allowances, the gross package is roughly Rs 1 lakh a month, varying by posting city.

**Do NDA cadets get paid during training?**

Yes. During training at the service academies, cadets receive a fixed stipend of Rs 56,100 a month, as stated in the UPSC NDA notification. Military Service Pay and most regular allowances do not apply during training, so the in-hand amount is lower than a commissioned officer's.

**What is Military Service Pay?**

Military Service Pay is a fixed Rs 15,500 a month paid to commissioned officers of the armed forces, on top of basic pay, in recognition of the unique demands of military service. It is not paid during cadet training.

**How fast does an officer's pay rise after commissioning?**

Captain comes at about 2 years and Major at about 6 years, both largely time-based. The big jump is to Lieutenant Colonel at about 13 years, where basic pay rises from Rs 69,400 to Rs 1,21,200 — but that promotion is selection-based, not automatic.

**Is the officer's package better than a civilian graduate's first job?**

In cash terms, a Lieutenant's roughly Rs 1 lakh a month gross beats most fresh-graduate salaries in India. The bigger gap is the non-cash package: free accommodation or HRA, ration money, free medical cover for the family, and CSD canteen access, which civilian jobs rarely match.

**Will the 8th Pay Commission change these figures?**

Yes, eventually. The figures in this article are 7th Pay Commission figures, which are the ones in force today. An 8th Pay Commission revision would raise them, but no new pay matrix has been notified yet, so any 8th CPC number you see online is an estimate, not a fact.
