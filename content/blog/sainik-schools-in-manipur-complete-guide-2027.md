## Manipur's one Sainik School — and why that changes the strategy

Manipur has one school under the Sainik Schools Society: **Sainik School Imphal, on Pukhao Road, about 8 km from Imphal**. It is a fully residential, CBSE-affiliated, English-medium school, and it admits to Classes 6 and 9 through one exam only — the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE), conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA). There is no school-level test, no walk-in admission, and no separate Manipur entrance exam for this school.

That single-school fact shapes everything for Manipur parents. The 67% home-state pool (explained below) means a Manipur-domicile child is competing first against other Manipur children for most of Imphal's seats, not against the whole country — but the competition for that one school is concentrated too. And Imphal is not a new school finding its feet: it was established on **7 October 1971**, making it one of the older Sainik Schools in the country, with more than five decades of cadets behind it.

If you are reading this in the AISSEE 2027 window, the immediate point is simple: applications opened on 3 November 2026 and close on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**, with a late-fee window from 3 to 6 December. The exam is on **31 January 2027**. Everything in this guide is built around that calendar.

Here is the thing most Manipur parents already sense but rarely hear said plainly: this school sits in a state that produces a remarkable share of India's footballers and athletes, and the cadet record reflects it. Two of Imphal's named alumni are footballers — Nongdamba Naorem, who was in India's squad for the FIFA U-17 World Cup in 2017, and Thangjam Singh, who has played in the Indian Super League. That sporting culture is real, and it shows up in the school's daily life: physical training and games are not an afterthought here. But do not mistake the school for a sports academy. The NDA is still the destination the school is built around, and the written exam is the gate. A child who is fast on the field but weak in Class 6 Maths will not clear AISSEE — Maths alone is half the marks.

## The 67% Manipur quota — and what it means for your child

Under the standard Sainik School reservation pattern, **67% of seats are reserved for candidates from the state or Union Territory where the school is located** — here, Manipur — and the remaining 33% are open to candidates from other states and Union Territories. Within those pools, the standard pattern reserves 25% of seats for children of service personnel including ex-servicemen, 15% for Scheduled Castes and 7.5% for Scheduled Tribes. In Class 6, 10% of vacancies (or 10 seats, whichever is more) are reserved for girl cadets across the 33 schools.

That home-state pool is the single biggest advantage a Manipur-domicile child holds in this exam, and it is decided by one document: the domicile certificate. If your child's domicile certificate is not ready, or the name on it does not match the birth certificate and the school records exactly, fix that this week. Our [AISSEE form-mistakes guide](/news/aissee-2027-form-mistakes-to-avoid-before-you-submit-2026-11-04/) lists the errors parents keep making, and the domicile mismatch is near the top. Girls can apply for both classes too — the 2027 notification covers boys and girls — so if you have a daughter in Class 5 or Class 8, she is in this race as well.

The application fee is **Rs. 850** for General, OBC-NCL and wards of defence personnel and ex-servicemen, and **Rs. 700** for SC/ST candidates. The exam is pen-and-paper OMR, multiple-choice, with **no negative marking** — a wrong answer costs nothing, so your child should attempt every question.

The exam pattern is the same one parents have prepared against for years. Class 6 runs 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks — Maths 50 questions for 150 marks, Language 25 for 50, Intelligence 25 for 50, and General Knowledge 25 for 50. Class 9 runs 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks — Maths 50 for 200, and English, Intelligence, General Science and Social Science 25 questions for 50 marks each, in English only. The qualifying bar is 25% in each section and 40% aggregate (not applicable to SC/ST candidates).

## What Manipur parents should do this week

**1. Apply, don't wait.** The form is open now at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society. The 2 December deadline looks far away; the families who miss it are the ones who planned to apply "next week" four times in a row. Apply in the first week, then use the correction window only if you genuinely need it.

**2. Get the documents in one sitting.** Birth certificate, domicile certificate, caste certificate if applicable, the child's photograph and signature in the format the bulletin specifies, and the study certificate from the current school. Photograph and signature rejections are the most avoidable reason a form gets stuck — and the [full AISSEE notification explainer](/news/aissee-2027-notification-out-applications-open-exam-jan-31-2026-11-03/) walks through the whole schedule.

**3. Put Imphal first — and list backups honestly.** In counselling you will rank schools. If Imphal is your first choice, put it first. But list the other schools you would genuinely accept, because an empty second preference is a wasted seat if the Imphal cut-off lands above your child's score. The nearest alternatives for most Manipur families are in Assam and Nagaland — our [Assam guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-assam-complete-guide-2027/) covers Sainik School Goalpara the same way.

**4. Plan the exam city now.** Manipur exam cities are listed in the bulletin; the city is announced on 11 January. If your family is outside Imphal — in Churachandpur, Thoubal, Ukhrul or the hill districts — check which cities the bulletin lists and whether travel is realistic on a January morning. Admit cards come on 25 January — six days before the exam — so there is no time to fix a travel problem then.

**5. Study to the pattern, not to the syllabus.** For Class 6, Maths is half the marks. One full 150-minute paper every Sunday morning, phone in another room, is worth more than three extra hours of chapter reading. For Class 9, Maths is again half the marks, and Social Science is where most children leak marks on dates and concepts they half-remember.

## How Imphal compares with its neighbours

Manipur parents often ask whether they should also consider schools in neighbouring states. The honest answer: the 67% home-state quota makes Imphal your best statistical bet by a wide margin, and the same logic works in reverse for children of Assam and Nagaland — their home schools are their best bet. Our [state-wise directory of all Sainik Schools](/blog/sainik-school-state-wise-complete-directory-2027/) lists every school if you want to compare.

The other comparison that matters is with the New Sainik Schools. The 2027 notification covers Class 6 in 80 approved New Sainik Schools and Class 9 in 40 of them. Whether any of those are in Manipur is a bulletin question — check the school list in the AISSEE Information Bulletin rather than trusting a directory. And one warning that applies to every parent in the North-East: a private school calling itself "Sainik School Manipur" on a website is not the same as Sainik School Imphal. The real school is the one on Pukhao Road, run by the Sainik Schools Society under the Ministry of Defence, and the only way in is AISSEE. If a website is selling direct admission, walk away.
