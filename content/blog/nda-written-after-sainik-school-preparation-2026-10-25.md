## The question every parent asks at admission time

Here is the thing most Sainik School brochures will not say out loud: a seat in Class 6 is not a ticket to the National Defence Academy. It is six years of preparation for one exam, taken in Class 12, against students from every school in India. The school does a great deal of the work — but the exam still has to be cleared by the cadet, on the day, in the hall.

So what does the school actually do for those six years? More than most parents realise, and less than the brochures imply. Let's walk through it honestly.

## First, what the target looks like

The NDA written exam, run by UPSC twice a year, has two papers. Mathematics carries 300 marks; the General Ability Test (GAT) carries 600. GAT itself splits into English, physics, chemistry, general science, history, geography and current events. Only those who clear the written stage are called for the SSB interview, which tests personality, leadership and fitness over five days.

That structure is the whole reason the Sainik School routine looks the way it does. Every element of the school day maps onto some part of that exam — and once you see the mapping, the routine stops looking like tradition and starts looking like a plan.

## How the school day maps onto the NDA papers

| NDA written section | What the school does about it, year after year |
|---|---|
| Mathematics (300 marks) | Maths is the spine of the academic day from Class 6 onward, and in Classes 11–12 the coaching shifts to NDA-level topics and timed problem-solving, not just board syllabus |
| GAT English | Daily English use — assemblies, debates, dramatics, written work — plus targeted work on comprehension, vocabulary and grammar in the senior classes |
| GAT Physics & Chemistry | Science labs and senior-secondary science teaching, with NDA-style objective practice layered on top of the board syllabus in Classes 11–12 |
| GAT History & Geography | Humanities teaching across the middle and senior school, reinforced by quizzes, current-affairs discussions and house competitions |
| GAT Current Events | Newspapers, morning assembly briefings and general-knowledge sessions are part of the routine, not an optional extra |
| SSB interview | Drill, sports, adventure activities, leadership roles (house captain, school appointments) and group tasks that build exactly the qualities assessors look for |
| Physical fitness | Morning PT, games every evening, cross-country runs and obstacle courses — the stamina base that the SSB's physical tasks and the academy's training demand |

Notice what is missing from that table: there is no magic subject called "NDA". The preparation is the routine itself, sharpened in the last two years.

## The last two years are where it sharpens

Classes 11 and 12 are when the general preparation turns specific. Most schools run dedicated NDA coaching alongside the board syllabus — extra classes, mock tests on the UPSC pattern, previous-year papers solved under time pressure, and interview grooming that includes group discussions and personal-interview practice.

This is also when the routine's hidden advantage shows up. A day scholar preparing for the NDA has to find time for fitness, current affairs and mock tests around school and tuition. A cadet's day already contains all of them. The preparation is not an extra load bolted onto the day; it is the day.

And the results, when they come, are real. In October 2026 alone, 45 cadets from Sainik School Kunjpura cleared the NDA written exam — 32 of them from the current Class 12 batch — and 44 cadets from Sainik School Ghorakhal did the same. Those are written-stage clears, not final selections, and that distinction matters. But as evidence that the routine works, they are hard to argue with.

## What the school cannot do for your child

Honesty demands the other side too. Three things stay firmly on the cadet:

**The written exam is competitive, not qualifying.** Clearing it means beating a national cutoff, not just passing. A cadet who coasts through school on the strength of the routine will not make it; the toppers are the ones who treat mock tests seriously.

**The SSB is a personality test, not a knowledge test.** You cannot mug your way through it. The school builds the raw material — confidence, teamwork, communication — but the interview still exposes anyone who has only memorised answers.

**Not every cadet wants the NDA, and that is fine.** Some discover in Class 11 that their future is engineering, medicine or something else entirely. A good school does not punish that; the same six years of maths, English and discipline feed every other competitive exam too. Parents who treat "not NDA" as failure are measuring the wrong thing.

## A parent's checklist for the NDA years

If your child is in Class 10 or 11 at a Sainik School and the NDA is the goal, here is what actually helps, in order:

1. **Protect the maths.** It is one-third of the written marks and the section where practice compounds fastest. One full timed paper every week in Class 12 beats ten chapters re-read.
2. **Read the newspaper daily.** GAT current events rewards a habit, not a crash course in the last month.
3. **Take every mock test seriously.** Treat the school's mock schedule as the real exam calendar — same timing, same pressure, same review afterwards.
4. **Keep the body in the game.** PT and games are not a distraction from study; they are preparation for the SSB and for academy life. Cadets who skip them arrive unfit.
5. **Take leadership roles.** House captain, club secretary, team captain — these are not resume lines, they are the SSB's raw material.
6. **Have a Plan B ready.** Other officer entries, engineering, and the wider competitive-exam world all reward the same preparation. A cadet with a Plan B attempts the NDA with less fear, and usually performs better.

## The bottom line

A Sainik School does not hand your child the NDA. What it hands them is six years in which every part of the day points at the same target — strong academics, daily fitness, current affairs as a habit, and leadership practice built into school life. In Classes 11 and 12 that general preparation sharpens into specific NDA coaching, and the cadets who take it seriously have a genuine, proven route through the written exam and into the SSB.

The school builds the archer. The cadet still has to loose the arrow.

## FAQs

**Does joining a Sainik School guarantee selection in the NDA?**

No. A Sainik School gives six years of structured preparation, but the cadet still has to clear the UPSC NDA written exam and the SSB interview on merit, competing with students from every kind of school in India.

**When can a Sainik School cadet first attempt the NDA exam?**

In Class 12, once they meet the UPSC age and education conditions for that NDA cycle. Most cadets attempt NDA-I and NDA-II during their Class 12 year, and schools plan their coaching calendar around those two attempts.

**What does the NDA written exam contain?**

Two papers: Mathematics (300 marks) and the General Ability Test (600 marks), which covers English, physics, chemistry, general science, history, geography and current events. The SSB interview follows only for those who clear the written stage.

**Is NDA coaching inside Sainik Schools enough, or do cadets need outside coaching?**

Most schools run their own NDA-focused coaching in Classes 11 and 12, and many cadets clear the written exam on that alone. Outside coaching is a personal choice, not a requirement — the school routine already covers the syllabus, timed practice and interview grooming.

**What if a cadet does not clear the NDA?**

Nothing is lost. The same preparation — strong maths, English, general knowledge and physical fitness — feeds directly into other officer entries, engineering and other competitive exams, and the discipline carries into any career.
