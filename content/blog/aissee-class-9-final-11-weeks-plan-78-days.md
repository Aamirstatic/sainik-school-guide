Most Class 9 preparation advice you will find online is really Class 6 advice with the numbers changed. That is a mistake, because Class 9 is a different exam. It is longer (180 minutes, not 150), heavier (400 marks, not 300), and — here is the part that decides ranks — **Maths alone is worth 200 of those 400 marks**. Half the paper. A child who treats all five subjects equally is preparing for the wrong exam.

You have 78 days left. Eleven weeks. Here is how to use them.

## First, see the paper the way it is

| Subject | Questions | Marks | Share of paper |
|---------|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Maths | 50 | 200 | **50%** |
| English | 25 | 50 | 12.5% |
| Intelligence | 25 | 50 | 12.5% |
| General Science | 25 | 50 | 12.5% |
| Social Science | 25 | 50 | 12.5% |

And the qualifying bar: at least 25% in every subject (50 marks in Maths, 12–13 in each of the others) and 40% overall — 160 marks out of 400. There is no negative marking, so every question attempted is a free shot. But qualifying is only the entry ticket. There are just **732 Class 9 seats** in the 33 schools, so the real fight is the merit rank, and the merit rank is mostly decided in Maths.

## The 11-week plan

The rule is simple: **Maths every day, one other subject per day, mocks on Sundays from week 6.** School days get 2 to 2.5 hours; Sundays get 4 to 5.

| Weeks | Focus | What "done" looks like |
|-------|-------|------------------------|
| 1–2 (15–28 Nov) | Maths foundation: number system, algebra basics, geometry theorems | All Class 8 NCERT Maths chapters revised once; 30 mixed problems a day |
| 3–4 (29 Nov–12 Dec) | Maths depth + Science | Algebra and geometry problem sets finished; Science chapters (physics, chemistry, biology) revised with diagrams |
| 5 (13–19 Dec) | English + Intelligence | Comprehension and grammar rules drilled; 10 non-verbal reasoning sets done — see our [Class 9 English reading comprehension strategy](/blog/aissee-class-9-english-reading-comprehension-strategy/) |
| 6–7 (20 Dec–2 Jan) | Social Science + first mocks | History, civics and geography timelines finished; first two full 180-minute mocks taken and analysed |
| 8–9 (3–16 Jan) | Weak-area repair | Mock analysis decides the syllabus now — the two weakest subjects get double slots |
| 10 (17–23 Jan) | Full revision, no new chapters | Every formula sheet rewritten from memory; one mock on Sunday, one mid-week |
| 11 (24–30 Jan) | Light revision + exam readiness | Only revision and OMR practice; admit card printed, centre route checked, sleep fixed |

Two things to notice. First, the form deadline (2 December, 11:50 PM) falls in week 3 — get the form submitted early in that week so it does not eat a study weekend. Second, nothing new is studied after 16 January. The last two weeks are for turning what your child already knows into marks.

## Maths: the subject that decides the rank

Maths is 50 questions for 200 marks, which means each Maths question is worth 4 marks while every other question is worth 2. A child who gets 40 of 50 Maths questions right has 160 marks — the entire qualifying total — from one subject alone. That is the whole game.

Spend the daily Maths hour like this: 20 minutes revising one concept (say, linear equations or triangles), 30 minutes solving mixed problems from that concept, 10 minutes writing down every mistake in a notebook. That mistake notebook becomes the revision material for weeks 8 and 9. Our guide to [Class 9 Maths — where algebra meets geometry](/blog/aissee-class-9-maths-where-algebra-meets-geometry-2026-10-23/) breaks down which chapters carry the most weight.

And honestly — skip the expensive crash course. At this stage, a solved previous-year paper teaches more than a new chapter of theory. If a concept is still weak in week 8, learn it from one solved example, not from a whole new book.

## The other four subjects: steady, not equal

**Science (50 marks):** Class 8 NCERT science, chapter by chapter, with diagrams. Our [Class 9 Science important chapters](/blog/aissee-class-9-science-important-chapters/) lists the high-weight areas. One hour, three days a week, is enough if it is regular.

**Social Science (50 marks):** dates and facts feel endless, so study it as stories and maps, not lists. Our [Class 9 Social Science guide](/blog/aissee-class-9-social-science-dates-or-concepts/) and the [map-work tips](/blog/map-work-tips-aissee-class-9-social-science/) show how to turn map questions into easy marks.

**English (50 marks):** the paper is in English only, so weak English hurts twice — in the English section and in understanding every other question. Daily: one comprehension passage, ten grammar questions. Reading comprehension is a skill, not a chapter — it only improves by doing it.

**Intelligence (50 marks):** the most scoreable section for most children, because it needs no memorisation. Series, coding, odd-one-out, non-verbal reasoning — practise in short bursts, and time yourself. Speed matters here more than depth.

## Sundays are mock days (from week 6)

From 20 December, every Sunday is a full 180-minute paper, 2:00 to 5:00 PM — the same slot as the real exam, so your child's body clock learns the timing. Phone in another room, OMR sheet, no breaks. Then analyse it the same evening: which subject leaked marks, which questions were guessed, which mistakes repeat. Our guide on [how to analyse your mock tests](/blog/how-to-analyse-your-mock-test-aissee-2027/) shows the exact method — an unanalysed mock is just three hours spent.

One warning: never judge your child by a single mock score. Mocks are diagnostic tools, not verdicts. A bad mock in week 7 that reveals a weak chapter is worth more than a good mock that hides one.

## The last week: protect the marks you have

From 24 January, the job changes. No new chapters, no new books, no new test series. Re-read the mistake notebook. Rewrite the formula sheets from memory. Practise filling an OMR sheet cleanly — a wrongly darkened circle costs the same as a wrong answer. Print the admit card (available from 25 January), check the centre route, and fix the sleep schedule so your child is alert at 2 PM, not drowsy. The exam is in the afternoon, and children who study only at night often fade in the second hour.

## FAQs

**What is the AISSEE Class 9 exam pattern for 2027?**
180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks in total. Maths carries 50 questions worth 200 marks, while English, Intelligence, Science and Social Science carry 25 questions worth 50 marks each. The paper is in English only, offline OMR mode, with no negative marking.

**How many marks do I need to qualify in AISSEE Class 9?**
At least 25 percent in each subject and 40 percent overall — that is 100 marks out of 400 in total, with 50 in Maths and 12 to 13 marks in each 50-mark subject. There is no minimum for SC and ST candidates in the 33 Sainik Schools. Qualifying is only the first step; seats go by merit rank.

**How much time should a Class 9 student study daily for AISSEE 2027?**
About 2 to 2.5 hours on school days and 4 to 5 hours on Sundays for the next 11 weeks. The plan above fits around school — the heaviest subject, Maths, gets a daily slot because it is half the paper.

**Is Maths really half of the AISSEE Class 9 paper?**
Yes. Maths is 200 of the 400 marks. A child who scores well in Maths can carry an average performance in the other four subjects; a child who neglects Maths cannot make it up elsewhere, because the other four subjects together are worth the same as Maths alone.

**When should I start taking full mock tests for AISSEE Class 9?**
From week 6 of this plan — one full 180-minute paper every Sunday, under exam conditions, phone in another room. Before that, take chapter-wise and section-wise tests. Analyse every mock the same day; an unanalysed mock is just three hours spent.

**Can I prepare for AISSEE Class 9 in 78 days from zero?**
It is hard but not impossible if the Class 8 base is solid, because the Class 9 paper tests Class 8 level concepts. Follow the Maths-first order in this plan, skip low-weight chapters ruthlessly, and take a mock every week from week 6 to find your weak spots fast.
