## The choice every defence-minded family faces

Walk through any coaching market in Patna, Lucknow or Kota and you will see the banners: "NDA Foundation — Class 6 onwards". And walk through any parents' WhatsApp group for AISSEE 2027 and you will see the other question: is it worth sending a 10-year-old to a residential school hundreds of kilometres away?

Both paths claim the same destination — a commission in the armed forces. But they are completely different journeys, and families routinely pick one for the wrong reasons: because a neighbour did it, or because a brochure made the other option sound effortless.

Let us compare them honestly, on the dimensions that actually matter.

## The two paths, side by side

| Dimension | Sainik School (Classes 6–12) | Day school + NDA coaching |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily environment** | Fully residential; military routine — PT at dawn, study hours, house system | Home life with school by day, coaching in the evening or weekends |
| **Entry point** | AISSEE at Class 6 or 9 — one competitive exam at age 10–15 | No entry exam; you simply enrol (coaching institutes accept almost everyone) |
| **Discipline training** | Built into the institution: routine, seniors, mess discipline, drill | Depends entirely on the child and the parents; coaching teaches subjects, not habits |
| **Academics** | CBSE curriculum, generally strong, especially maths and science | Depends on the day school chosen; can be excellent or ordinary |
| **Physical fitness** | Daily PT, games, obstacle-type training woven into the week | Almost entirely on the family — coaching rarely trains the body |
| **Peer group** | 600+ boys and girls who all chose a defence dream; powerful motivation | Mixed group; the defence dream may be shared by only a few |
| **Cost shape** | Annual school fee (varies by school) covering boarding, food, education | School fee plus coaching fee, both paid separately — coaching institutes in big cities typically advertise annual fees in the low lakhs |
| **Emotional cost** | Homesickness is real, especially in Class 6; no going home every evening | The child stays home; the family absorbs the pressure of a second "school" |
| **NDA outcome** | Prepares and grooms for NDA; still requires clearing UPSC written + SSB | Directly targets the UPSC NDA written; SSB preparation is a separate purchase |

## What the brochures don't tell you about coaching

The foundation-coaching industry has one structural incentive: enrolment. A "Class 6 NDA foundation" course is mostly a study-skills and exam-temperament product dressed in military marketing. There is nothing wrong with that — study skills matter — but understand what you are buying.

Ask three questions before paying for any coaching:

1. **Can they show verifiable past selections?** Names, roll numbers or verifiable details — not "500+ selections" on a flex board.
2. **Is the faculty stable?** Foundation batches often get the newest teachers while the star faculty handles the Class 11–12 NDA batches.
3. **Does your child actually want the extra hours?** A 12-year-old doing school till 3 PM and coaching till 8 PM, six days a week, burns out by Class 9. I have watched it happen. The defence dream survives on motivation, and exhaustion kills motivation first.

## What nobody tells you about Sainik School either

It is not a gentler option. Sending a 10-year-old to a hostel is a family decision, not just an admission decision. The first term is hard — most children cry, most parents feel guilty, and most of both get through it. What changes a child is not the uniform or the drill; it is the peer group. When every child around you is studying for the same dream, studying stops feeling like punishment.

But a Sainik School is also not a guarantee machine. The school prepares you for NDA; you still have to clear the UPSC NDA written examination after Class 12 and then the five-day SSB interview, which tests personality — leadership, teamwork, presence of mind — not where you studied. Plenty of Sainik School students do not make it to NDA, and plenty of day-school students do. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

## Who should pick which path?

**Pick Sainik School if:** your child is self-driven enough to live away from home, you value the all-round military environment (not just exam prep), and you are comfortable with residential schooling from a young age. It is the strongest *foundation* of the two options.

**Pick day school + coaching if:** your child is not ready for hostel life, your local day school is academically strong, and the family can enforce the discipline that a Sainik School would otherwise provide — study routine, physical fitness, limited distractions. It is the more *flexible* option.

**Consider both, sequentially:** a surprising number of successful NDA candidates do neither at Class 6 — they join a good day school, crack AISSEE at Class 9 for a Sainik School (two extra years of maturity make the hostel transition much easier), and take targeted NDA coaching in Classes 11–12. The Class 9 entry route exists for exactly this reason.

## The honest bottom line

If the goal is the NDA, the real question is not "school or coaching". It is: *who provides the discipline?* A Sainik School provides it institutionally. Day school plus coaching requires the family to provide it. The option where discipline is guaranteed — whichever that is for your family — is the option that works.

## FAQs

**Is Sainik School better than NDA coaching for a defence career?**
They serve different stages. A Sainik School gives a residential, discipline-first environment for Classes 6 to 12. NDA coaching after Class 10 sharpens exam technique for the NDA written test. Many successful NDA candidates never attended a Sainik School, and many Sainik School students need coaching too.

**Can a day-school student clear NDA without a Sainik School?**
Yes. The NDA written exam is conducted by UPSC and tests Class 11-12 level mathematics plus general ability. Day-school students clear it every year with disciplined preparation and coaching. The SSB interview tests personality, not schooling background.

**Which option costs more: Sainik School or day school plus coaching?**
Both cost money, in different shapes. Sainik Schools charge annual fees that vary by school and include boarding; NDA coaching institutes in big cities typically advertise annual fees in the low lakhs. Always compare the specific school's published fee against the coaching institute's published fee rather than relying on rules of thumb.

**Is NDA foundation coaching from Class 6 worth it?**
Foundation coaching that runs alongside school mostly helps with study habits and exam temperament. It does not guarantee a Sainik School seat or an NDA selection. Buy it only from institutes that publish past results with verifiable details, and only if your child enjoys the extra hours.

**Does a Sainik School guarantee entry into NDA?**
No. A Sainik School prepares and grooms candidates for NDA, but admission to NDA still requires clearing the UPSC NDA written exam and the SSB interview independently. Think of it as the best training ground, not a ticket.
