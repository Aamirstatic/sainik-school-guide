Of all the documents in the AISSEE 2027 file, the domicile certificate is the one most families apply for last — and it is the one that takes the longest to get. The notification is still awaited, the exam is 112 days away, and a tehsil office that takes three weeks to verify a residence claim will not hurry because your counselling date is near. If your child is claiming the home-state quota, this certificate is not paperwork. It is the quota itself.

## Why the domicile certificate matters so much

Sainik Schools are all-India institutions, but the seats are not. Under the seat matrix, **67% of seats are reserved for candidates domiciled in the state where the school is located**, and only 33% go to other-state candidates on national merit. A child competing under the home-state quota is effectively competing in a smaller, more winnable pool.

The certificate is verified twice: once when the AISSEE form asks for the state of eligibility, and again — hard — during [AISSAC counselling document verification](/blog/aissac-choice-filling-mistakes-cost-seats/), when a mismatched or missing domicile can turn an allotted seat into a lost one. For state-specific exams the bar is even higher: UP Sainik School requires valid Uttar Pradesh domicile for **both the child and the father** — applications without it are rejected outright.

## What the issuing authority wants to see

A domicile certificate is issued by your state's revenue authority — the tehsildar, Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM), or district collector's office, depending on the state. Whatever the office, they are answering one question: *has this family genuinely lived in this state for the required period?* Keep these ready before you apply:

- **Proof of residence:** electricity bill, water bill, ration card, registered rent agreement, or house tax receipt — ideally in a parent's name at the current address
- **Proof of residence duration:** most states expect continuous residence; old bills, voter list entries, or the parent's old domicile help establish this
- **Child's birth certificate** — name spellings must match school records (our [birth certificate correction guide](/blog/birth-certificate-correction-before-aissee-form-2026-10-10/) covers the common fixes)
- **Parent's identity proof:** Aadhaar card is the usual standard
- **Photographs:** passport-size, recent
- **Application form:** filled online on the state portal or offline at the tehsil/CSC (Common Service Centre)

One rule above all: **name spellings and the address must be identical across the domicile, birth certificate, and school records.** A "Kumar" that becomes "Kumarr" on one document, or a house number written differently, is the most common cause of verification trouble.

## State-wise: where to apply (general guide)

Every state runs its own citizen-services portal. The table below gives the starting point; the exact service name varies ("Residence Certificate", "Domicile Certificate", "Niwas Praman Patra") but the flow is the same everywhere: register → fill the form → upload documents → track → download/print the digitally signed certificate.

| State | Usual portal / office route | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Uttar Pradesh | e-Sathi (UP citizen portal) or tehsil; required for UP Sainik School and UP home-state quota | 15–30 days |
| Maharashtra | MahaOnline / SETU or tehsildar office | 15–21 days |
| Bihar | RTPS (Right to Public Services) portal | 15–30 days |
| Madhya Pradesh | e-District / MPOnline | 15–30 days |
| Karnataka | Seva Sindhu portal | 15–21 days |
| Rajasthan | e-Mitra kiosk / tehsildar | 15–30 days |
| Telangana / Andhra Pradesh | MeeSeva centres | 7–15 days |
| Other states | State e-district portal or SDM/tehsildar office | 15–30 days |

Timelines are typical ranges, not guarantees — October to December is peak season for these offices, so treat the upper end as realistic. **Start this week.** If the certificate is not in hand when the AISSEE form opens, at minimum have the application reference number and the uploaded-document set ready.

## The 6 mistakes that get domicile applications stuck

1. **Applying too late.** Three weeks of processing plus one round of objection/correction eats the entire application window. October is the month; November is gambling.
2. **Address mismatch.** The residence proof address must match the address on the form. Families who recently moved are the highest-risk group — keep old address proofs until the certificate is issued.
3. **Father's domicile missing (UP-specific).** For UP Sainik School, the father's UP domicile is a separate requirement. One family, two certificates.
4. **Wrong certificate type.** Some states issue separate "permanent residence" and "domicile" certificates. AISSEE counselling needs the domicile/residence certificate that establishes state of eligibility — check which name your state uses.
5. **Expired or undated certificates.** A domicile certificate issued years ago for a different purpose is usually still valid, but if it carries no clear issue date or has visible damage, get a fresh one rather than arguing at verification.
6. **Digitally signed copy not downloaded.** Many states now issue e-signed certificates. Download and save the digitally signed PDF — a photocopy of a photocopy raises eyebrows.

## What to do this week: the checklist

- [ ] Identify your issuing authority (tehsildar/SDM office of your district) and your state's citizen portal
- [ ] Assemble: residence proof, residence-duration proof, birth certificate, parent's Aadhaar, photographs
- [ ] Cross-check name spellings and addresses across all documents — fix birth-certificate errors first
- [ ] Submit the domicile application online (or at the tehsil/CSC) and save the reference/acknowledgement number
- [ ] Mark a follow-up date 15 days out; if the status hasn't moved, visit the office in person
- [ ] When issued, verify the spelling of names and the address on the certificate itself — correct now, not at counselling

The domicile certificate is boring, slow, and absolutely decisive. It is also the document you can fully control while waiting for the notification. Families who finish it in October spend November on the form itself — which is exactly where your attention should be.

## FAQs

**Is a domicile certificate mandatory for AISSEE?**
Yes, effectively. It decides your home-state quota — 67% of seats go to home-state candidates — and it is checked during AISSAC counselling. Without it, you cannot claim the home-state quota.

**How long does it take to get a domicile certificate?**
Typically 15 to 30 days, depending on the state and the tehsil office's workload. Apply now, while the AISSEE 2027 notification is still awaited, so the certificate is ready before the application window opens.

**Can I apply for a domicile certificate online?**
In most states, yes — through the state's citizen services portal (for example, e-district/e-Sathi/RTPS style portals). You apply online, upload documents, and collect the digitally signed certificate or visit the tehsildar/SDM office as the state requires.

**Whose domicile certificate is needed for AISSEE — the child's or the parent's?**
The child's domicile of the state being claimed as home state is what the quota rests on. Keep the father's/mother's domicile too — some state-specific exams, like UP Sainik School, explicitly require the father's domicile as well.

**What if the address on the domicile differs from other documents?**
That is one of the most common rejection triggers at document verification. The address and spelling of names on the domicile certificate must match the birth certificate and school records exactly.
