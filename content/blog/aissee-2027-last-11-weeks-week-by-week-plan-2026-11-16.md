## Where you stand today: 76 days, and two deadlines, not one

Here is the thing most families get wrong about the next 11 weeks: they count down to only one date. There are two. The **form closes on Wednesday, 2 December 2026** — 16 days from today — and the **exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027** — 76 days away. The form deadline sits inside week 3 of this plan, and everything before it has to share time with the form. Everything after it belongs to the exam alone.

The paper itself has not changed. Class 6 sits a **150-minute paper, 125 questions, 300 marks** — Maths 50 questions for 150 marks, Language 25 for 50, Intelligence 25 for 50, GK 25 for 50 — from **2:00 to 4:30 PM**. Class 9 sits a **180-minute paper, 150 questions, 400 marks** — Maths 50 for 200, and English, Intelligence, Science and Social Science 25 questions for 50 marks each — from **2:00 to 5:00 PM**. Both papers are pen-and-paper OMR, multiple choice, with no negative marking — so every question gets attempted, and the real skill is not knowledge alone but finishing the paper inside the clock. That is what these 11 weeks are for.

## Phase 1 — Weeks 1 to 3: finish the form, find the weak spots

This week has two jobs, and the form comes first. Fill it, pay the fee — **Rs. 850** for General, OBC (NCL) and Defence wards, **Rs. 700** for SC/ST — and download the confirmation page. [Our news piece today explains exactly what to do if the payment fails](/news/aissee-2027-16-days-left-fee-payment-rules-confirmed-2026-11-16/). Aim to be done by **30 November**, not 2 December — the portal is slowest when everyone rushes in on the last day. If you have not filled the form yet, [our day-by-day plan to 2 December](/blog/aissee-2027-17-days-left-day-by-day-plan-to-2-december/) breaks the remaining days into small jobs.

The second job this week is a **baseline mock** — one full paper, on an OMR sheet, timed from 2:00 PM, the real exam slot. Do not judge the score. Judge the pattern: which section ran out of time, which section bled marks on silly errors, which section your child avoided. That list is your revision plan for the next seven weeks. Honestly, most families skip this step and revise everything equally — which means they revise their child's strongest subject the most. The baseline mock stops that.

Week 2 belongs to JNVST if your child is appearing for it — the exam is on **Saturday, 28 November**, and it comes first, so it gets the priority. The AISSEE form still has to be submitted by 2 December, so keep the form work in week 1 and let week 2 be JNVST revision. Week 3 is the form's last week: submit by 30 November, use the late window (3–6 December, plus Rs. 500) only as a safety net, and fix anything in the correction window from 7 to 9 December.

## Phase 2 — Weeks 4 to 7: mocks and the error log

From 7 December onwards, the routine is simple and non-negotiable: **one full paper every Sunday, timed 2:00 PM, on an OMR sheet, reviewed the same evening**. A mock without review is just a confidence test. Every wrong answer goes into one notebook with the reason — "rushed", "formula forgotten", "misread the question", "guessed" — not just the correct answer. By week 7, that notebook is the most valuable book your child owns, because it contains only their own mistakes.

Between Sundays, work section by section, not chapter by chapter. Two hours a day is enough for a Class 6 child if it is focused: one hour on the weakest section from the baseline mock, one hour rotating through the others. Class 9 children can stretch to two and a half hours, with Maths getting the biggest share — it carries 200 of the 400 marks. For the section-wise approach, [our Class 9 English strategy](/blog/aissee-class-9-english-section-strategy-50-marks/) and [Class 6 Language strategy](/blog/aissee-class-6-language-section-strategy-50-marks/) show how one section at a time actually works, and [today's paper-attempt plan](/blog/aissee-2027-paper-attempt-strategy-150-180-minute-plan/) sets the time budget for the whole paper.

December is crowded — RIMCEE on 6 December, RMS CET on 13 December, UP Sainik School on 20 December, three Sundays in a row — so if your child is sitting any of those, treat that Sunday as their exam, not a mock day, and keep the AISSEE routine light that week. The plan bends; it does not break.

## Phase 3 — Weeks 8 to 11: the city slip, the admit card, the taper

Week 9 brings the **city intimation slip on Monday, 11 January** — it tells you the exam city, not the centre. Check it the day it comes out, and start planning travel if the city is not your first choice. Week 11 brings the **admit card on Monday, 25 January** — check the name, photograph, centre address and the language of the question booklet the same day, because the centre cannot be changed later.

The last 10 days are a taper, not a sprint. No new topics, no new books, no new mock papers after 24 January. Revise the error notebook, re-read the formula sheet, glance at the admit card and the centre's route, and sleep early. [Our taper plan from October](/blog/aissee-2027-last-10-days-taper-plan-2026-10-18/) still holds — the only thing that has changed since then is that the dates are now confirmed, not expected.

## Two rules that make the plan work

First, **never skip the Sunday review**. A child who writes ten mocks and reviews none of them will repeat the same ten mistakes on 31 January. A child who writes six mocks and reviews all six will not.

Second, **protect the last week**. I have seen parents panic in the final days and push a new guidebook or a new crash course on a tired child. It never adds marks. It only adds anxiety. The work is done by 24 January. The last week is for rest, revision and arriving at the centre calm.

Eleven weeks is a long time for an 11-year-old and a short time for a parent watching the calendar. The plan above is deliberately boring — same Sundays, same routine, same notebook. Boring is what works. Start this week with the form and the baseline mock, and the rest of the plan takes care of itself.

## FAQs

**How many days are left for the AISSEE 2027 exam?**

From 16 November 2026, there are 76 days to the exam on Sunday, 31 January 2027. The application form closes much sooner — on 2 December 2026, just 16 days away.

**How many mock tests should my child take before AISSEE 2027?**

One full-length paper every Sunday from now until the exam — about 10 papers — plus one baseline mock this week. Sit each paper on an OMR sheet in the real 2 PM slot and review it the same evening.

**What is the AISSEE 2027 paper pattern?**

Class 6 is a 150-minute paper of 125 questions for 300 marks: Maths 50 questions for 150 marks, Language 25 for 50, Intelligence 25 for 50 and GK 25 for 50. Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions for 400 marks: Maths 50 for 200, and English, Intelligence, Science and Social Science 25 questions for 50 marks each.

**What score does my child need to qualify in AISSEE 2027?**

Under the 2026 bulletin pattern, expected to repeat in 2027, a candidate needs at least 25 percent in each section and 40 percent overall. The rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates.

**My child is also appearing for JNVST on 28 November. How do we manage both?**

Give JNVST the priority in weeks 1 and 2, since it comes first. The AISSEE form still has to be submitted by 2 December, so fill it in week 1 and pay the fee early. From week 3 onwards, AISSEE takes over completely.
