## One school, one hill, one serious reputation

Uttarakhand has exactly one Sainik School, and it does not need company to make its case. **Sainik School Ghorakhal**, near Bhowali in Nainital district, was established on **21 March 1966** on a 500-acre estate once owned by the Nawab of Rampur, at an altitude of about **1,800 metres** in the Outer Lesser Himalayas. Its motto is *Buddhiryasya Balam Tasya* — wisdom brings strength. It is a CBSE-affiliated, co-educational, fully residential school for Classes 6 to 12, and its principal is Group Captain Madhu Senger.

If you are a Uttarakhand parent, this is your home-state school. If you are from another state, Ghorakhal is still on your list — but you will be competing for the smaller, all-India share of seats, which is exactly why the quota section below matters more than any brochure line.

## Ghorakhal's NDA record is the headline

The single strongest reason parents put Ghorakhal first is its officer pipeline. In the **NDA (II) 2026 written examination**, **44 Ghorakhal cadets qualified** — 30 from the current Class XII batch and 14 from the March 2026 batch, as reported by SSBCrack in October 2026. That is not a one-year spike: the school is reported to hold the national record for winning the **Raksha Mantri Trophy ten times since 2000**, the trophy awarded to the Sainik School sending the most cadets to the NDA.

Read that record the right way. It does not guarantee your child a commission — the SSB interview, medicals and merit list still stand between any cadet and Khadakwasla. What it does prove is that the school's academic coaching, physical training and SSB preparation work together year after year, which is precisely what a parent is buying into.

## Admission: AISSEE, and only AISSEE

There is no management quota, no donation route and no direct admission to Ghorakhal. Entry is to **Class 6 and Class 9 only**, through the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE), conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA) for the Sainik Schools Society. After the written exam come e-counselling through AISSAC, document verification and a medical fitness test.

The age rules follow the standard AISSEE pattern used in previous cycles: **10–12 years as on 31 March 2027 for Class 6** (born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017) and **13–15 years for Class 9** (born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014, studying in Class 8). Treat these as EXPECTED for 2027 until the official bulletin confirms them — the notification was still awaited as of checks reported on 24 October 2026, with the exam expected in January 2027. Our [AISSEE 2027 notification guide](/blog/aissee-2027-notification-guide/) tracks the watch in detail.

## The 67% Uttarakhand quota — and what it means for your child

Under the Sainik School reservation system, **67% of seats are reserved for candidates domiciled in the home state** — here, Uttarakhand — and the remaining 33% are open to candidates from other states. Within the seats, the usual reservations apply: 15% for Scheduled Castes, 7.5% for Scheduled Tribes, 27% for OBC (Non-Creamy Layer), and 25% of the balance for children of defence personnel, with 10% of seats for girls.

For a Uttarakhand-domiciled child, that 67% share is a genuine advantage — the competition pool is the state's own applicants, not the whole country. For an out-of-state child, the honest picture is the reverse: you are chasing roughly a third of the seats against national competition, so your target score has to be higher. Our full explainer, [the 67% home-state quota, explained](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-67-percent-explained-2026-10-17/), works through the arithmetic with examples.

One practical consequence: domicile paperwork matters as much as marks. A Uttarakhand domicile certificate that is expired, mismatched with the birth certificate or issued in a parent's name instead of the child's can cost a home-state seat. Sort it now, while the notification is still awaited.

## Houses, campus life and the hill adjustment

Ghorakhal runs a house system with ten houses: the senior houses are Shiwalik, Kesari, Garhwal, Kumaon and Singh, and the junior houses are Dhruv, Luv, Kush, Abhimanyu and Bharat. Houses are where a cadet's daily life actually happens — dormitory, competitions, cross-country runs, discipline and friendships — and inter-house rivalry is one of the school's oldest traditions.

Then there is the hill itself, and parents should not romanticise it. The campus sits about 4 km from Bhowali and 7 km from Bhimtal, at 1,800 metres. Winters are properly cold, mornings start early, and the routine is fully residential from day one. A child from the plains who has never lived away from home will adjust — thousands have — but the adjustment is real: cold, altitude, homesickness and a regimented day, all at once. If you can, visit in winter before you fill the preference list, and speak to parents of current cadets about the first term, not just the prospectus.

## Fees: what is reported, and what is not verified

This is the section where honesty costs us a clean number — and that is the point. Published secondary figures for Ghorakhal's annual fee **conflict sharply**: one school directory quotes about Rs 1,15,000, another about Rs 1,70,000, a third a range of roughly Rs 1,70,600–1,74,100, and a coaching guide about Rs 1,80,000 including hostel, food and other charges. None of these is a verified 2027 figure from the school or the NTA bulletin, so this guide will not pick one and call it the fee.

Budget on the upper end of that reported range, ask the school directly for the current fee schedule and the scholarship position for your category, and compare with our [all-schools fee comparison](/blog/sainik-school-fee-comparison-all-schools/) — but treat every secondary fee figure, including ours, as a starting question, not an answer.
