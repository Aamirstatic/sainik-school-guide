Parents obsess over the syllabus and barely glance at one field in the application form: the exam city preference. That one dropdown decides whether your child walks to the centre on exam morning or spends the night before in a hotel bed in an unfamiliar city. For an 11-year-old sitting a career-defining paper, that difference is not minor.

AISSEE 2027 is expected on 31 January 2027 (per the NTA calendar), with the notification and form expected in October 2026. Before you fill the form, understand exactly how NTA allots cities — and how to choose so you almost never need the travel plan at the end of this article.

## How NTA allots exam cities

The mechanics are the same across recent NTA cycles, including AISSEE:

1. **You list preferred cities in priority order.** The form asks you to rank a few exam cities — most to least preferred. Your first preference is the one NTA tries hardest to honour.
2. **Allotment is preference-plus-capacity.** NTA assigns each candidate to a city based on their stated preferences and the seats available in that city. Popular cities fill up; late applicants are more likely to be pushed to a lower preference.
3. **The admit card is the final word.** Your allotted city, centre name, address and reporting time appear only on the admit card. The city intimation slip that NTA usually releases a week or two before the exam gives an earlier heads-up — use it to start travel planning.
4. **Changes are rare after submission.** Exam city is typically among the fields editable during the correction window, *if* the bulletin offers one for 2027. Do not count on this; get the choice right the first time.

One important nuance: NTA allots the *city*, and the *centre* within that city is allocated separately. Two children from the same town can end up at centres 20 km apart — check the exact address, not just the city.

## The decision matrix: home town vs nearby city vs metro

| Factor | Home town | Nearby district city | Metro |
|---|---|---|---|
| Travel fatigue | None — sleep in own bed | 1–3 hours, manageable | Long journey, possible overnight stay |
| Familiarity | Knows routes, food, water | Mostly familiar | Unfamiliar; logistics add stress |
| Centre reliability | Usually fine, but smaller centres can be basic | Good balance | Best facilities, strictest frisking queues |
| Seat competition | May fill early in big towns | Often the sweet spot | High demand, fills fastest |
| Best for | Most families (default) | Families in small towns without centres | Families already living in the metro |

The rule of thumb: **pick the city where your child will be calmest at 9 AM.** A tired, anxious child in a "better" city scores less than a rested child at an ordinary centre. The paper is identical everywhere — AISSEE is a pen-and-paper OMR exam with the same question paper across centres, in the child's chosen exam language (the 2027 bulletin is expected to retain the multilingual option we [explained here](/news/aissee-2027-class-6-exam-language-13-languages-explainer-2026-10-23/)).

## When a metro or a different city makes sense

- **Your town has no centre.** Some districts are not on the city list; then the nearest listed city is your de-facto first preference.
- **You are relocating.** If the family moves cities between the form and the exam, pick the city you will be in during January.
- **Repeated past experience.** If a centre in your town had chaos last year — late starts, poor seating — a district city is worth the drive. Ask neighbours and coaching groups about last year's experience.
- **Boarding-school candidates.** If the child studies in a different town (hostel, relatives), prefer that city over the home town — the child's comfort zone is where they sleep now, not where the family house is.

## The 10-point city-choice checklist

Run through this before submitting the form:

1. **Check the official city list first.** Only cities in the bulletin's list are selectable; do not assume last year's list carries over unchanged.
2. **Put the most realistic city first.** "Realistic" beats "ideal". Your first preference should be the city NTA is most likely to allot *and* the one easiest for you to reach.
3. **Rank all preferences, don't leave blanks.** An unfilled lower preference can push you to a random city if the top ones fill.
4. **Avoid two cities in opposite directions.** If preferences 1 and 2 are 300 km apart, you cannot pre-book anything — keep your list geographically tight.
5. **Consider January weather.** North Indian cities can see dense fog in late January; fog delays trains and morning traffic. A centre 50 km away can become a 3-hour journey. This alone has made many families prefer the nearest city.
6. **Submit the form early.** City allotment is partly first-come capacity-based. Filing in the first week of the window — not the last — improves your odds of getting preference one.
7. **Match the child's routine.** If the exam starts in the morning, pick a city where the child can reach the centre by 8:30 AM without waking at 4 AM.
8. **Check school calendar clashes.** If the child has a school exam or event the same weekend, the nearer city leaves recovery time.
9. **Note the reporting time buffer.** NTA gates typically close before the paper starts; plan to arrive 45–60 minutes early, not "on time".
10. **Save a screenshot of your preferences.** If the city slip later shows something odd, your screenshot is your evidence during the correction window.

## The travel-day plan (for when the centre is outstation)

Even with perfect planning, NTA may allot a lower preference. So have this ready:

- **When the city slip drops** (expected a week or two before the exam): if the city is not your first preference, book transport and a room near the centre immediately. Do not wait for the admit card.
- **Travel the day before.** Reach by evening, locate the centre physically — drive or walk past it — so the morning route is known.
- **Pack the exam kit the night before:** printed admit card (two copies), one valid photo ID, two black/blue ball pens (AISSEE is OMR — check the bulletin's pen colour instruction), a transparent water bottle, and a simple watch if allowed (check the bulletin; smartwatches are not).
- **Eat and sleep normally.** New-city food experiments the night before an exam are a classic self-inflicted wound. Eat what the child eats at home.
- **Morning:** reach 45–60 minutes early, find the room, use the washroom, sit. The first 15 minutes of calm are worth more than 15 minutes of last-minute revision.

## What this costs you — and what it doesn't

Getting the city right costs nothing and buys sleep, calm and punctuality. Getting it wrong costs a hotel bill, a tired child and a morning of logistics — on the most important school morning of their year. The city preference field takes thirty seconds to fill and months to regret. Spend ten minutes on it when the [AISSEE 2027 form opens](/news/aissee-2027-notification-not-out-oct-22-checks-day-13-2026-10-23/) — it is the cheapest performance boost in the entire admission cycle.

## FAQs

**How does NTA allot exam cities for AISSEE 2027?**
Candidates list preferred exam cities in priority order in the application form. NTA allots a centre based on those preferences and seat availability in each city; the final allotted city is shown on the admit card. Allotment rules for the 2027 cycle will be confirmed in the information bulletin, which is expected soon.

**When is the AISSEE 2027 exam expected?**
The exam is expected on 31 January 2027, based on the NTA examination calendar reported by multiple education portals. The AISSEE 2027 information bulletin and notification are expected to be released in October 2026.

**What if I get an exam city far from home?**
Plan travel immediately when the admit card arrives: reach the city a day before, stay near the centre, carry a printed admit card and a valid photo ID, and report at the time printed on the admit card. Choosing a realistic first preference in the form is the best way to avoid this situation.

**Can I change my exam city after submitting the AISSEE form?**
In recent NTA cycles, exam city was among the fields that could be edited during the application correction window, if one was offered. The correction-window rules for AISSEE 2027 will be stated in the information bulletin when it releases.

**Should I pick a metro or my home town as first preference?**
Home town first is the safer default for most families: no travel fatigue, familiar food and a calm morning. A metro or district centre makes sense only when your home town's centres have limited capacity or unreliable logistics. Pick the city where the child will sleep best the night before.
