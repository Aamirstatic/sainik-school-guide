## Winter is the season that tests a cadet

Summer at a Sainik School is uncomfortable. Winter is a test of character — and parents feel it most, because this is the season their child is far from home, waking up in the dark, and living in a dormitory where one cough travels down a whole row of beds in a week.

The good news is that Sainik Schools are built for this. These are residential schools with a fixed routine, a mess, an infirmary and house staff who have run many winters before your child arrived. Winter does not break the routine; it changes its texture. Here is what actually changes, and what you can do about each part.

## The uniform: layers, not bulk

The winter uniform is where parents make the most expensive mistakes. The rule to remember is simple: **the school decides what is worn, and the list is usually shorter than parents expect.**

Most schools issue or specify winter uniform items — a jersey, blazer or jacket, and sometimes a cap — and allow warm inner layers underneath. What typically matters:

- **Fit first.** A jersey that fit in September may be tight over a shirt and vest in December. Check the fit before the winter term, not during it.
- **Colours and patterns are regulated.** A brightly coloured jacket from home may not be permitted on parade or in class, even if it is warmer. Buy to the school's list.
- **Socks and footwear matter more than jackets.** Cold mornings mean damp ground. Two pairs of warm socks in rotation, and footwear that grips wet ground, prevent more sick days than any extra sweater.
- **Name everything.** Winter clothing is the most commonly lost property in a hostel, because every cadet owns near-identical items. Name tags or stitched initials save a term of arguments.

Do not send a trunk full of woollens "just in case". Unauthorised items are stored away, and an overstuffed trunk is one more thing for a young cadet to manage.

## The routine: same structure, colder mornings

The daily structure of a Sainik School — early rising, physical training, classes, games, prep and lights-out — does not pause for winter. What shifts is the experience of it:

- **Mornings are the hardest hour.** Waking in darkness and stepping out for PT in cold air is the moment most new cadets struggle. It gets easier within two weeks as the body adapts — the first fortnight is the one to encourage them through, not rescue them from.
- **Timings may shift slightly.** Schools adjust for light and for fog, especially in northern schools where winter mornings can be genuinely unsafe for outdoor activity. These adjustments come through school orders, not rumour — if your child reports a timing change, it will also be in the school's circular.
- **Games continue, with conditions.** Winter is actually a good season for outdoor sport — cool air, firm ground — but wet or foggy mornings can move games indoors or delay them. Kit that stays dry matters: a wet tracksuit after games is a cold waiting to happen.
- **Study hours feel longer.** Shorter days and earlier darkness push more of a cadet's free time indoors, into prep rooms and libraries. For exam-year cadets, winter is quietly the most productive term of the year — if they protect their sleep.

## Health: the hostel cold season

This is the section parents should read twice. In a day school, a cold stays in one house. In a hostel, it moves through a dormitory. Colds, coughs, sore throats and stomach upsets cluster in winter for three boring reasons: shared rooms, shared dining, and cold air drying out throats and noses.

What actually helps:

- **Report early.** The infirmary exists for the first sneeze, not the third day of fever. Cadets who hide illness to avoid missing PT or class end up missing a week instead of a day.
- **Sleep is medicine.** Winter prep tempts cadets to cut sleep. A tired cadet catches everything going around; a rested one shrugs most of it off.
- **Warm food, regular meals.** Skipping breakfast before morning PT in winter is the single worst habit a cadet can pick up. The mess menu is planned for the season — encourage your child to eat it rather than live on tuck-shop snacks.
- **Hand hygiene and ventilation.** Simple, unglamorous, and the reason some dormitories stay healthy while others do not. Fresh air in a dormitory, even briefly, beats a sealed, stuffy room.
- **Medicines go through the infirmary.** Do not stock a trunk with antibiotics or strong cough syrups for self-medication. If your child has a regular medicine, send it with a written note to the medical staff.

One thing winter does not do: it does not excuse a cadet from the routine. Schools expect attendance at PT, classes and games through winter, with medical exemption only on the infirmary's advice. Parents who write "he catches cold easily, please excuse him" letters without a medical basis put their child in an awkward position with the house staff.

## The parent's winter checklist

| Check | Do it when | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Winter uniform fit and condition | Before the winter term starts | Tight or torn items cannot be fixed mid-term easily |
| Two sets of warm socks, dry footwear | With the uniform check | Damp feet are the fastest route to a cold |
| Health card and vaccination record up to date | Before sending the child back | The infirmary needs current information, not last year's |
| Current phone number with the school | Now | Winter emergency calls and travel-disruption messages need a working number |
| A short, encouraging letter for the first cold week | First week of the cold spell | Morale in the first fortnight decides how the whole winter feels |
| Travel plan for the winter break | Early, not in the last week | Fog and train delays are a winter fact of life in the north |

## What winter gives back

It is easy to write winter as pure hardship, and that would be unfair to the cadets. Winter is also the season of early-morning camaraderie — the shared joke about the cold, the hot tea after PT, the games played in crisp air, and the quiet prep-room hours that build exam results. Senior cadets remember winter terms fondly precisely because they were hard together.

Parents can help most by treating winter as normal, not as an emergency. Ask about the routine, not just the temperature. Ask what your child ate, whether they slept, and whether they reported to the infirmary when they felt ill. And when the first homesick winter phone call comes — and for a new cadet, it usually does — listen, encourage, and remind them that the whole dormitory is feeling exactly the same cold morning.

## FAQs

**Do Sainik Schools change their daily routine in winter?**

The structure stays the same, but timings and emphasis shift: morning activities move with the light and the cold, and schools typically allow warmer layers and adjust outdoor schedules in severe cold. Exact changes are set by each school, so check your school's winter circular.

**What extra clothing do cadets need for winter?**

School-issued winter uniform items plus warm inner layers, woollen caps or mufflers where permitted, sturdy warm footwear for mornings, and a second set of dry socks. Buy only items the school allows, in the colours it specifies.

**Do cadets fall ill more often in winter?**

Colds, coughs and throat infections spread faster in hostels in winter because cadets live, eat and study together indoors. Early reporting to the infirmary, warm food, sleep and hand hygiene matter more than any medicine bought in advance.

**Should parents send extra food or medicines for winter?**

Send what the school permits and nothing more. Home food that breaks mess rules gets confiscated, and medicines should go through the school infirmary with a note, not sit in a trunk for self-medication.

**Is morning PT still held in winter?**

Yes, physical training remains part of the routine through winter, though schools adjust for fog, extreme cold or poor visibility. A cadet who skips PT to stay warm loses fitness exactly when the body needs it most.

**What should parents check before the winter term?**

Uniform fit and condition, footwear, the health card and any pending vaccinations, and whether the school has your current phone number for winter emergency calls.
