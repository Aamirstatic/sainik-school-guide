## Why this matters before the exam

Most parents first hear about the answer key in February, in a panic, with a two-day window closing. That is the worst possible time to learn the rules. The honest truth is this: in a paper with no negative marking, a single wrong answer in the official key can cost your child real marks — 3 marks per Maths question in Class 6, 4 marks per Maths question in Class 9 — and the only moment you can do anything about it is the short objection window after the provisional key comes out. Read this now, file it away, and when February comes you will move fast while everyone else is still figuring out the portal.

## What the provisional answer key actually is

After the exam, NTA publishes two things on the AISSEE portal: the **provisional answer key** (NTA's official answers for every question, separately for Class 6 and Class 9, for each paper set) and your child's **scanned OMR response sheet** — the record of what the machine read from the answer sheet. Both are downloaded by logging in with the application number and password.

The key word is *provisional*. It is NTA's first draft of the answers, and NTA knows it can contain errors. That is exactly why the objection window exists. After the window closes, subject experts review every challenge, revise the key where a challenge is valid, and publish a **final answer key**. The result is calculated from the final key — not the provisional one.

## How to challenge: the step-by-step process

This is the 2026 process, which the 2027 notice is expected to repeat:

1. **Download both documents the day they appear.** The provisional key for your child's paper set, and the OMR response sheet. Save both as PDFs — the download link expires with the window.
2. **Score the paper yourself first.** Match the key against the OMR sheet question by question. This gives you your child's expected score — and it is also how you spot the two different kinds of error (below).
3. **Separate the two kinds of problem.** A *wrong answer in the key* is challenged at **Rs 200 per question**. A *wrongly recorded response* — the OMR sheet shows an answer your child did not mark — is challenged separately at **Rs 100 per response**. They are different challenges with different fees.
4. **For each key challenge, prepare proof.** A challenge without evidence goes nowhere. The strongest proof is a page from an NCERT textbook or a standard reference book showing the correct answer. A screenshot of a coaching institute's key is weak. Your child's memory of the question is not proof.
5. **File online and pay per question.** On the portal, select the question, state what you believe the correct answer is, upload the proof, and pay the fee — debit card, credit card or net banking. No offline or postal challenges are accepted, and no challenge is accepted without the fee.
6. **Keep the receipts.** Save the payment confirmation and the challenge reference number. The fee is **non-refundable**, even if your challenge is accepted and the key is corrected.

## What makes a challenge succeed — and what wastes Rs 200

Honestly, most challenges fail, and most of them fail for avoidable reasons. Here is the difference:

**Worth challenging:** the key's answer contradicts an NCERT textbook; the question has two correct options and the key picked the other one; the key's answer is a calculation error you can show step by step; the question itself is ambiguous and the key's reading is the weaker one. These are the challenges subject experts accept.

**Not worth challenging:** your child *feels* the answer should be different; a coaching centre's answer key disagrees with NTA's (coaching keys are often wrong themselves); the question was hard and your child guessed wrong; you want to challenge ten questions "just in case". Each of those costs Rs 200, none of it comes back, and a scattergun challenge list does not make any single challenge stronger. Challenge two or three questions with solid proof, not fifteen on hope.

One more thing parents miss: a successful challenge helps *every* candidate who attempted that question, not just your child. If the key is corrected, everyone's score is recalculated from the final key. So the fee buys a correction to the paper, not a private favour.

## The OMR challenge: when the machine misread the sheet

This one is rarer but worth knowing about. Sometimes the scanned OMR sheet shows a different answer from the one your child actually bubbled — a faint bubble, a smudge, a half-erased mark. In 2026, NTA allowed this to be challenged separately at **Rs 100 per response**. If your child's score looks inexplicably low in one section, compare the OMR sheet against what your child remembers marking, question by question, before assuming the preparation failed. A single misread bubble in Maths is 3 or 4 marks — the difference between a seat and a waiting list in a tight year.

## What happens after you challenge

NTA's subject experts review every challenge. If yours is accepted, the provisional key is revised and the **final answer key** is published. The result is then prepared from the final key. In 2026, the result followed in late February, about four to six weeks after the exam. If your challenge is rejected, the fee is not refunded and there is no appeal — the final key is final. That is why the proof matters more than the number of questions you challenge.

## FAQs

**When will the AISSEE 2027 answer key be released?**
NTA has not announced a date. The 2027 bulletin says the display of OMR sheets, question papers and answer keys will be announced later on the website. In 2026 the provisional key came about three and a half weeks after the exam, so mid-February 2027 is the pattern to expect, not a promise.

**How much does it cost to challenge an AISSEE answer?**
In 2026 it was Rs 200 per question challenged, and Rs 100 per recorded response challenged on the OMR sheet. The fee is non-refundable, even if your challenge is accepted. The 2027 fee will be confirmed in the 2027 answer-key notice.

**How do I challenge the AISSEE answer key?**
Online only, on the NTA AISSEE portal, by logging in with the application number and password. You select the question, state the correct answer, upload proof, and pay the fee per question. No offline or postal challenges are accepted.

**Can I challenge my OMR sheet if the recorded answer is wrong?**
Yes. In 2026 NTA allowed a separate challenge to the recorded response at Rs 100 per response. This is for cases where the OMR sheet shows a different answer from the one your child actually marked.

**Does a successful objection change my child's result?**
Yes, if NTA's subject experts accept it. The provisional key is revised, a final answer key is published, and the result is calculated from the final key. That is why the objection window matters — after it closes, the key is final.

**Should I challenge every answer my child got wrong?**
No. Challenge only the questions where you have solid proof the key is wrong, such as an NCERT textbook page or a standard reference. Random challenges cost Rs 200 each, are not refunded, and do not change anything.
