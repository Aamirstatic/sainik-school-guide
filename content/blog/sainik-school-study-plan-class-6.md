# AISSEE Class 6 Study Plan: A Realistic 4-Month Week-by-Week Plan (26 Sept – 31 Jan 2027)

Today is 26 September. The exam is on 31 January. That's roughly four months — 127 days, if you want the exact number.

Is it enough? Honestly — yes. Four months is plenty *with* a plan, and barely enough *without* one. We've seen children start in October and clear it, and children who "prepared" for a year without any structure and didn't. The difference was never talent. It was always the plan.

So here's one. An AISSEE class 6 study plan built for a real child who goes to school, has homework, gets distracted, and has parents who worry. Not a fantasy timetable — a workable one.

## Know the Battlefield First: The Exam Pattern

Half of preparation is just understanding the paper. Here's the Class 6 pattern:

| Subject | Questions | Marks | Share of paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 50 | 150 | **50%** |
| Language (English/Hindi) | 25 | 50 | ~17% |
| Intelligence (Reasoning) | 25 | 50 | ~17% |
| General Knowledge | 25 | 50 | ~17% |
| **Total** | **125** | **300** | 150 minutes |

- ⏱️ **150 minutes** for 125 questions = about **1.2 minutes per question**
- ❌ **No negative marking** (✅ CONFIRMED — 2026 pattern) — so every question gets attempted, no exceptions
- 📝 OMR-based MCQs, offline pen-paper mode (✅ CONFIRMED — 2026 pattern)
- ✅ **Qualifying marks:** 25% in each section + 40% aggregate — not applicable to SC/ST candidates (2026 bulletin)

Read that table once more. **Mathematics alone is half the paper — 150 out of 300 marks.** A child who is strong in Maths is already halfway to selection. That's not an opinion; it's arithmetic. Your study time should reflect it: Maths gets roughly half your daily preparation hours. For the full topic breakdown, see our [Class 6 and 9 syllabus guide](/blog/aissee-2027-syllabus-class-6-class-9/).

## The 4-Month Plan: Week by Week

Eighteen weeks, three phases. Each week has one clear focus — print this out or copy it into a notebook and tick weeks off. Children love ticking things off. Use that.

### Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–6: 26 Sept – 6 Nov)

Build the base. No timers yet, no pressure. Just understanding.

| Week | Dates | Focus | Key task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 26 Sep – 2 Oct | Maths: number system, place value | Finish one chapter fully + 30 practice sums |
| 2 | 3 – 9 Oct | Maths: fractions & decimals | 50 mixed problems; note every mistake in a copy |
| 3 | 10 – 16 Oct | Language: grammar basics (tenses, articles) | 10 new words daily + 1 short passage daily |
| 4 | 17 – 23 Oct | Intelligence: series & coding-decoding | 15 reasoning questions daily |
| 5 | 24 – 30 Oct | GK: states, capitals, national symbols | 10 facts daily; make a wall chart |
| 6 | 31 Oct – 6 Nov | Maths: geometry basics + revision of Weeks 1–2 | First self-test: 50 Maths questions in 60 min |

Daily time in Phase 1: **~2 hours** (1 hr Maths, 30 min Language/Intelligence on alternate days, 30 min GK).

### Phase 2 — Speed (Weeks 7–12: 7 Nov – 18 Dec)

Now the base exists. Time to get fast. Every practice session gets a timer from here on.

| Week | Dates | Focus | Key task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | 7 – 13 Nov | Maths: percentages, ratio, averages | 30 timed questions daily |
| 8 | 14 – 20 Nov | Language: comprehension speed | One passage in under 5 minutes, daily |
| 9 | 21 – 27 Nov | Intelligence: mixed sets, odd-one-out | 25 questions daily, timed |
| 10 | 28 Nov – 4 Dec | GK: science basics + 2026 current affairs | Revise wall chart; add monthly events |
| 11 | 5 – 11 Dec | **First full mock test** (150 min, OMR-style) | Analyse every wrong answer — this analysis IS the study |
| 12 | 12 – 18 Dec | Fix Phase 2 weak topics | Re-do all mock mistakes; second full mock |

Daily time in Phase 2: **~2.5 hours**, plus one longer weekend session.

### Phase 3 — Exam Mode (Weeks 13–18: 19 Dec – 31 Jan)

Stop learning new things. Start performing. From here, it's mocks, revision, and mistake registers.

| Week | Dates | Focus | Key task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 19 – 25 Dec | 2 full mocks | Formula sheet + short notes only for revision |
| 14 | 26 Dec – 1 Jan | 2 full mocks | GK rapid revision; no new current affairs |
| 15 | 2 – 8 Jan | 2 full mocks | OMR practice — bubbling errors cost real marks |
| 16 | 9 – 15 Jan | 2 full mocks | ⏰ Admit card expected mid-January — download from exams.nta.ac.in (🔶 EXPECTED) |
| 17 | 16 – 22 Jan | 1 mock + light revision | Sleep schedule fixed; exam-day documents ready |
| 18 | 23 – 31 Jan | **No new topics.** Light revision only. | Exam on **Sunday, 31 January** — reach early, stay calm |

Two rules for Phase 3: **never start a new topic** in the last two weeks, and **analyse every mock for a full hour** afterwards. The analysis is where the marks come from.

## A Daily Schedule That Actually Fits Around School

| When | What | How long |
|---|---|---|
| Morning (before/after school) | Maths practice | 60 min |
| Evening | Language / Intelligence (alternate days) | 45 min |
| Night | GK facts revision | 30 min |
| Sunday | Mock test or topic test + mistake register | 2.5–3 hrs |

Total: **about 2–2.5 hours on weekdays.** More than that and a 10–11-year-old burns out — and a burnt-out child in December helps nobody. Consistency beats intensity. Every single time.

## Mock Tests: The Most Underrated Weapon

Here's something we've noticed year after year: children who attempt **at least 10 full mock tests** before the exam perform noticeably better. Not because the questions repeat — but because three things happen:

1. **Time management becomes instinct.** 1.2 minutes per question stops feeling rushed when you've done it ten times.
2. **OMR mistakes disappear.** Wrong bubbling, skipped rows, double-marking — these cost real marks, and only practice fixes them.
3. **The fear goes away.** The tenth "exam" feels routine. The first real one feels like the eleventh.

Your first mock score will probably disappoint you. That's normal — it's supposed to. A mock is a diagnostic tool, not a verdict. What matters is the mistake register: every wrong answer written down, the concept revised, and never repeated. ## For Parents: 5 Things That Actually Matter

1. **Don't shout about marks.** Pressure shrinks performance in children; it never grows it. Praise effort and consistency, not scores.
2. **Fix screen time, don't ban it.** A total ban breeds resentment and secret phone use. Fixed hours work better.
3. **Guard their health.** The exam is in peak winter — a fever in the last week can undo four months of work. Warm clothes, proper sleep, simple food.
4. **Watch for the notifications.** Application window 🔶 expected October–November 2026; admit card 🔶 expected mid-January 2027 at exams.nta.ac.in. Fill the category and domicile details carefully — see the [quota guide](/blog/sainik-school-sc-st-defence-quota-2027/) before you submit.
5. **Book recommendations matter less than you think.** One good book per subject, finished twice, beats five books started once. Our [best books list](/blog/best-books-aissee-2027/) keeps it minimal on purpose.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q1. Is the Class 5 syllabus enough for AISSEE Class 6?**
The paper is set at roughly Class 5 level, but with more depth and trickier framing. NCERT Class 5 done thoroughly, plus the easier chapters of Class 6, is the ideal combination. Check the [syllabus guide](/blog/aissee-2027-syllabus-class-6-class-9/) for the topic list.

**Q2. Is coaching necessary?**
No. A clear plan, regular mocks, and disciplined revision work from home. Coaching helps mainly with routine and peer pressure — if your child studies sincerely at home, you don't need it.

**Q3. English or Hindi for the Language section — which to choose?**
Whichever your child is stronger in. Both carry exactly 50 marks, and the choice doesn't affect anything else.

**Q4. What score is safe for selection?**
Cut-offs change every year by school, category, and gender. As a working target, aim for **200+/300** in mocks — that's our suggestion, not an official figure. The only official bar is the qualifying rule: 25% per section and 40% aggregate (✅ CONFIRMED — 2026 bulletin; SC/ST exempt).

**Q5. How should we handle GK?**
Split it: static GK (states, capitals, rivers, national symbols, basic science) plus 2026 current affairs. A little every day from October — GK crammed in January doesn't stick.

**Q6. What should we do the day before the exam?**
Nothing new. Keep documents ready (admit card, photograph, ID), visit the centre if it's unfamiliar, eat light, sleep early. Read our [exam-day tips](/blog/aissee-exam-day-tips-2027/) together that evening.
