## The certificate is not the claim — the chain behind it is

Every year, defence families make the same understandable mistake on the AISSEE form. They tick the defence category, upload whatever service paper is nearest to hand — a discharge book, an old identity card, a photocopy of a certificate from years ago — and assume the claim is made. Then, at document verification, someone asks a simple question: who certified this, and does it match the child on this form? And the claim wobbles.

The reason is that AISSEE does not ask for one vague thing called "a defence certificate". It asks for a chain: a document that proves service, a document that proves the relationship to the child, and an authority that has certified both. Break any link and the claim is not proved, however genuine the service behind it is.

This guide walks through that chain for AISSEE 2027, whose applications are open now and close on 2 December 2026. If your child is a ward of a serving member or an ex-serviceman, read this before you submit — not after.

## Step 1: know which document you actually need

The document lists published for AISSEE applications draw a clean line, and it is the first thing to get right:

| Your situation | The core document |
|---|---|
| Ward of serving defence personnel | Service certificate from the unit or Record Office |
| Ward of an ex-serviceman | Pension Payment Order (PPO) |
| Defence-quota certification, where asked | Certificate signed by the Secretary, Kendriya Sainik Board, or the Secretary, Rajya/Zila Sainik Board, or the Officer-in-Charge of the concerned Record Office |

A PPO is not the same as a discharge certificate, and a discharge certificate is not the same as an ex-serviceman identity card. They are related papers that prove different things. The PPO proves pensionable service and is the document the AISSEE lists name for ex-servicemen. The service certificate proves current service for serving personnel. If you hold one but the list asks for the other, do not quietly substitute — get the position clarified through the Zila Sainik Board (see Step 2) and carry both papers to verification.

## Step 2: get it certified by the right authority

For most ex-serviceman families, the practical route runs through the district. The Zila Sainik Welfare Office (ZSWO) — also called the Zila Sainik Board — exists in each district precisely to help veterans with services including the issue of ex-serviceman certificates. The standard defence-quota certificate format names three certifying authorities: the Secretary of the Kendriya Sainik Board in Delhi, the Secretary of the Rajya or Zila Sainik Board, and, for serving personnel, the Officer-in-Charge of the concerned Record Office.

In practice:

1. **Visit the Zila Sainik Welfare Office in your district** with the originals, not just photocopies. If you do not know where it is, the District Administration or the Rajya Sainik Board can point you to it.
2. **Carry the discharge certificate or retirement order**, which proves service and retirement or discharge, plus the PPO and the child's birth certificate, which proves the relationship.
3. **Let the office verify the service record.** Verification means cross-checking your details against service records, sometimes with the Defence Ministry or military authorities. Reports put it at days to several weeks depending on workload — which is why starting this week matters.
4. **Check the finished certificate before you leave the office.** It should carry the correct names, service number and rank, the ex-serviceman or widow identity card number where applicable, the round stamp of the office, and the signature of the Secretary or District Sainik Welfare Officer. A certificate without the stamp and signature is a piece of paper, not a certification.

## Step 3: the five mismatches that get claims questioned

These are the mismatches to hunt for while you still have time to fix them:

**1. Name spelling across documents.** The child's name and the parent's name must read the same way on the PPO or service certificate, the birth certificate, and the AISSEE form. A missing middle name, a changed spelling after marriage, or a transliteration difference between Hindi and English records is enough to trigger a query. Fix it at the source — the Zila Sainik Board with originals — rather than hoping the verifier overlooks it.

**2. Service number or rank errors.** A single wrong digit in a service number breaks the link between the certificate and the service record. Read it off the PPO, digit by digit, against the form.

**3. The wrong certificate for the wrong status.** Serving and retired are different claims with different papers. A serving certificate uploaded for a retired parent, or a PPO uploaded while the parent is still serving, invites a rejection that has nothing to do with whether the service is real.

**4. An uncertified photocopy.** A scanned photocopy of an old certificate, without the certifying authority's signature and stamp, is the weakest form of proof. If the only copy you hold is uncertified, take it to the Zila Sainik Board and get a certified version.

**5. A claim made late, in the correction window.** AISSEE 2027 allows corrections from 7 to 9 December 2026, but that window is for correcting particulars on a submitted form — it is not a safe plan for obtaining a certificate you do not yet have. Treat the correction window as a safety net for typos, not as the timetable for your verification. The honest rule: certificate in hand before 2 December, form submitted with it, correction window kept in reserve.

## Step 4: the upload and verification checklist

Tick each item against the original document, not from memory:

- [ ] PPO (ex-serviceman) or service certificate (serving personnel) — original seen, scanned copy legible
- [ ] Certificate certified by the Zila/Rajya Sainik Board Secretary or Record Office, with stamp and signature
- [ ] Child's birth certificate — names match the PPO/service certificate exactly
- [ ] AISSEE form — defence category selected, and the names on the form match the certificate
- [ ] File in the format and size the NTA bulletin prescribes (document lists report PDF, 50–300 KB for certificates — confirm against the current bulletin before uploading)
- [ ] Originals plus self-attested photocopies set aside for the verification stage at admission
- [ ] Fee paid in the correct band — wards of defence personnel are reported in the Rs 850 band for AISSEE 2027, against Rs 700 for SC/ST candidates; confirm on the official portal before paying

Keep the whole set together in one folder — PPO, certified certificate, birth certificate, fee receipt and the AISSEE confirmation page. At verification, the question is never "did you serve?" It is "can you prove, with certified papers that match this child, that this claim is yours?"

## What this certificate does not do

Two honest limits. First, a defence certificate proves eligibility for the defence category; it does not by itself decide a seat, which depends on the merit list, the school's seats, and the admission process after the exam on 31 January 2027. Second, this guide cannot state a seat percentage or quota figure for AISSEE 2027 from the sources checked — the bulletin is the place to confirm any such figure, and any article that quotes one without citing the bulletin should be treated with suspicion. ## FAQs

**What document proves my child is an ex-serviceman's ward for AISSEE?**

For an ex-serviceman's ward, the Pension Payment Order (PPO) is the core proof, supported by a defence-category certificate where the school or NTA asks for one. For a serving personnel ward, it is a service certificate from the Record Office or unit.

**Who verifies an ex-serviceman ward certificate?**

Certification for a defence quota claim is given by the Secretary of the Kendriya Sainik Board, the Secretary of the Rajya or Zila Sainik Board, or the Officer-in-Charge of the concerned Record Office for serving personnel. The Zila Sainik Welfare Office in your district is the usual first stop.

**Can I upload a discharge book instead of a PPO for AISSEE?**

Do not assume so. The document lists for AISSEE ask for the service certificate for serving personnel and the PPO for ex-servicemen. If you only have a discharge certificate, get the position confirmed through the Zila Sainik Board and carry both documents to verification.

**What if the name on the PPO does not match the AISSEE form?**

Fix it before you submit, not at verification. A spelling mismatch between the PPO, the birth certificate and the application form is one of the commonest reasons a claim is questioned. Take the mismatch to the Zila Sainik Board with the original documents and get it corrected or formally certified.

**Is there time to get a certificate verified before the AISSEE 2027 deadline?**

Yes, but start this week. AISSEE 2027 applications close on 2 December 2026, and Zila Sainik Board verification can take days to weeks depending on workload. Do not plan to sort it out in the correction window of 7 to 9 December.

**Does the defence category change the AISSEE fee?**

The fee categories reported for AISSEE 2027 put wards of defence personnel in the same fee band as General and OBC-NCL candidates, at Rs 850, against Rs 700 for SC and ST candidates. Confirm the fee on the official NTA portal before paying.
