"The 2027 exam hasn't even happened — why prepare for 2028?" Because **the early starter wins.** Here's something nearly every AISSEE topper has in common: they started 12 to 15 months out. Cracking it in 3 months is possible. Cracking it in 15 months is *comfortable*. That's a big difference — ask anyone who's done it the hard way.

If your child is in Class 4 or Class 7 right now, this guide is for you.

Picture a Class 4 classroom in Patna. A boy finishes his sums early and sits there, bored. That evening, his mother hands him a puzzle book instead of her phone. Nothing dramatic — just twenty minutes a day. Fifteen months later, that boredom has turned into the fastest mental maths in his class. That's the whole game, really.

## The 15-month roadmap

The biggest advantage of starting early: you build **understanding, not rote learning**. No panic, no crash courses, no burnout.

| Phase | When | Focus |
|-------|------|-------|
| **Phase 1 — Foundation** (months 1–5) | Now – Feb 2027 | NCERT Class 5/8 books in depth, 1.5–2 hrs a day |
| **Phase 2 — Concept + practice** (months 6–10) | Mar – Jul 2027 | Reasoning practice, maths drills, 2–3 hrs a day |
| **Phase 3 — Papers + mocks** (months 11–13) | Aug – Oct 2027 | Previous years' papers, one mock a week, fix weak topics |
| **Phase 4 — Intensive** (months 14–15) | Nov 2027 – Jan 2028 | 3 mocks a week, revision, fill the application form |

## Phase 1: the five months that decide everything

Honestly, Phase 1 is where the race is won. Get this right and everything after feels manageable.

1. **Read NCERT like a storybook.** Class 5 books (for Class 6 aspirants) or Class 8 books (for Class 9 aspirants) — Maths, Science/EVS, English. There's no hurry, so understand each concept fully before moving on. This depth is what three-month crash courses can never replicate.
2. **Build a reading habit.** Twenty minutes of English reading daily — newspaper, storybook, anything with sentences. Comprehension and vocabulary are built here, quietly, over months.
3. **Mental maths.** Tables up to 20, basic calculations without pen and paper. Fifteen months of this becomes a genuine superpower in a 150-minute paper.
4. **A current-affairs diary.** Three big news items a day, jotted in a notebook. By exam time you'll hold 400+ days of notes — and the GK section will feel like revision, not study.

## What NOT to do (early starters' classic mistakes)

- **Don't rush into coaching.** The first five months belong to self-study and NCERT. Coaching pays off most in Phase 3, when there's something solid to sharpen.
- **Don't buy ten books.** Three or four good ones beat a shelf of untouched guides. Start with our [best books list](/blog/best-books-aissee-2027/).
- **Don't burn the child out.** Fifteen months is a marathon. Two consistent hours a day beats an eight-hour Sunday every single time. Your child's interest in studying is your most valuable asset — protect it.

## FAQs

**Q1. My child is in Class 4 now — is this the right time to start?**
Yes. For Class 6 entry in 2028, the projected window is birth between 1 April 2016 and 31 March 2018 — which is roughly today's Class 4. Cross-check with the [age-limit guide](/blog/sainik-school-age-limit-2027-detailed-guide/) shifted one year forward.

**Q2. Could the syllabus change by 2028?**
The pattern has been stable for three years; a major change is 🔶 unlikely. But always confirm against the official bulletin when it releases.

**Q3. Should we start mock tests now?**
No. Give the first 8–10 months to foundation. Start mocks in Phase 3 (around August 2027).

**Q4. What about girls — will the 2028 rules differ?**
🔶 Expect the same framework as 2027: in the established schools, 10% of vacancies or 10 seats (whichever is more) reserved for girls. See the [girls' admission guide](/blog/sainik-school-for-girls-2027/).

**Q5. How many hours a day is enough, starting this early?**
Just 1.5–2 hours in Phase 1. Consistency beats hours — increase gradually as the exam approaches.

**Q6. Is Class 9 entry harder than Class 6?**
Different, not harder. Class 9 has fewer seats and an English-only paper, but also thinner competition per seat in many states. The [Class 9 guide](/blog/sainik-school-class-9-admission-2027/) gives you the 2027-cycle picture.
