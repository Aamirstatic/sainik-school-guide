Ask a parent what their child is revising for AISSEE 2027 and you'll hear the same answer every time: maths. Then maybe GK. The Language section almost never comes up — and that is exactly why it decides so many results.

Look, here's the thing. The Class 6 paper is 300 marks. Mathematics is 150 of them, and everyone knows it. But Language is 50 marks — 25 questions, 2 marks each — the same weight as Intelligence and the same as General Knowledge. One-sixth of the paper. And unlike maths, it doesn't need a formula sheet or a coaching class. It needs a habit your child probably already has, or can build in four weeks.

## The section, in numbers

| Section | Questions | Marks | Share of paper |
|---------|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Mathematics | 50 | 150 | 50% |
| Language | **25** | **50** | **~17%** |
| Intelligence | 25 | 50 | ~17% |
| General Knowledge | 25 | 50 | ~17% |
| **Total** | **125** | **300** | **100%** |

The exam runs 150 minutes, pen-and-paper on an OMR sheet, with **no negative marking** — so every Language question your child attempts is a free shot at 2 marks. But there is a catch that makes this section more dangerous than it looks: your child must score at least **25% in each section** and **40% in aggregate** to qualify (the rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates). A child who scores 140 in maths and 8 out of 50 in Language doesn't just lose 42 marks. The child fails the paper.

Last year, a parent called us in a panic because her son's mock scores were "good" — 190 out of 300 — and yet the coaching centre had marked him as not qualified. The maths was fine. The Language score was 10 out of 50. One section, one blind spot, and the whole paper was gone. That's the story of this section.

## The one decision you can't undo

Before a single question is answered, the Language section asks your family one question — in the application form. Class 6 candidates can choose any of **13 languages** as their test medium, including English and Hindi. Class 9, for comparison, is English only.

**The medium you choose in the form is locked in.** It cannot be changed after submission, and the question booklet on 31 January comes only in that language. The correction window (7–9 December) cannot fix this one.

So how do you choose? Honestly, most parents overthink it. The rule is simple: pick the language your child **reads fastest in**, not the language that sounds most impressive. If your child reads Hindi storybooks fluently but stumbles through English paragraphs, choose Hindi. The comprehension passage doesn't care about prestige — it cares about speed. Your child has roughly a minute per question across the whole paper, and a passage read twice in a weak language eats the time maths needs.

Check the medium before you pay the fee on or before 2 December. It is a two-second check that saves a three-hour regret.

## What the section actually tests

The bulletin doesn't publish a question-by-question syllabus for Language, but the section is built the same way every year, and the pattern is consistent: **reading comprehension, grammar and vocabulary** in the chosen medium.

- **Comprehension (roughly a third of the section):** a short passage, then questions whose answers sit inside the passage. The trap is the option that sounds right but isn't in the text. The fix is boring and it works: read the questions first, then read the passage looking for them.
- **Grammar:** the everyday rules — tenses, articles, prepositions, sentence correction. Not the obscure corners of a grammar book. The mistakes that cost marks are the ones children make while writing fast, not the ones they can't explain.
- **Vocabulary:** synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutions. This is where the daily reading habit pays directly, because a child who has met a word in a story remembers it; a child who met it in a list forgets it by exam day.

Notice what isn't on that list: essay writing, handwriting, speaking. The section tests reading, not writing. That changes how you prepare for it.

## The four-week plan

There are 11 weeks to the exam, but Language doesn't need 11 weeks. It needs four honest ones, and then maintenance.

| Week | Daily work (20 minutes) | Why it works |
|------|-------------------------|--------------|
| 1 | Read one short story or passage in the exam medium, out loud, then answer 5 questions on it | Builds reading speed — the real bottleneck |
| 2 | Add 10 minutes of grammar: one topic a day (tenses, articles, prepositions), 10 questions each | Fixes the fast, careless errors |
| 3 | Two comprehension passages a day, timed at 8 minutes each, plus 5 new words a day from the passages | Trains the "questions first, passage second" method under time pressure |
| 4 | One full 25-question Language paper, timed at 25 minutes, every other day; review every wrong answer the same day | Turns the section into a habit, not a hope |

After week 4, keep it alive with one passage a day and one timed paper a week. That's it. Twenty minutes a day, and the section that quietly fails children becomes the section that quietly banks 40-plus marks.

Two rules for the whole plan. First, **everything in the exam medium** — if the medium is Hindi, the passages are Hindi. Practising comprehension in English when the exam is in Hindi is practising the wrong skill. Second, **never skip the review**. A wrong answer you don't understand is a wrong answer you'll repeat on 31 January.

## On exam day: 25 minutes, then move on

The Language section is the fastest section in the paper when the reading habit is there — and the slowest when it isn't. Give it **20 to 25 minutes** of the 150, no more. Read the questions before the passage, answer from the text, and if a vocabulary question won't come, mark your best guess and move on. There is no negative marking, so a guess costs nothing and a blank costs 2 marks.

Then hand the saved minutes to Mathematics, where they are worth more. That's the real trade this section offers: prepare it well and it pays you back in time, not just marks.

## Your Language-section checklist

- [ ] Medium chosen in the form is the language your child reads fastest in — checked before the fee is paid
- [ ] One passage a day, in the exam medium, every day from this week
- [ ] Grammar: one topic a day, 10 questions, for two weeks
- [ ] A 25-question timed Language paper every other day in the final two weeks
- [ ] Every wrong answer reviewed the same day it happens
- [ ] On exam day: questions first, passage second, 25 minutes maximum, attempt all 25

Fifty marks. One-sixth of the paper. Twenty minutes a day. The children who clear AISSEE aren't the ones who studied the hardest subjects the longest — they're the ones who left no section unattended. Don't let Language be the one yours leaves behind.

## FAQs

**How many marks is the Language section in AISSEE Class 6?**
50 marks out of 300 — 25 questions worth 2 marks each. That is one-sixth of the whole paper, the same weight as Intelligence and General Knowledge, and half the weight of Mathematics.

**Which languages can my child choose for the AISSEE Class 6 exam?**
Class 6 candidates can choose any of 13 languages as their test medium, including English and Hindi. The medium is chosen in the application form and cannot be changed after the form is submitted, so pick the language your child reads fastest in, not the one that sounds most impressive.

**Is there negative marking in the AISSEE Language section?**
No. There is no negative marking anywhere in AISSEE 2027, so your child should attempt all 25 Language questions. But each section has a 25 percent qualifying minimum, so a weak Language score can still fail the paper even with a strong total.

**What does the AISSEE Class 6 Language section actually test?**
Reading comprehension, grammar and vocabulary in the chosen medium. The questions reward children who read regularly and can find the answer inside a passage, not children who memorise rules. A short daily reading habit beats a grammar guide in the last month.

**How much time should my child give the Language section on exam day?**
About 20 to 25 minutes of the 150-minute paper. It is the fastest section to finish when the reading habit is there, and the extra minutes it saves are exactly what the Mathematics section needs.

**Can my child change the exam medium after submitting the AISSEE form?**
No. The medium chosen in the application form is locked in once the form is submitted, and the question booklet comes only in that language. Check the medium before you pay the fee — the correction window cannot fix this one.
