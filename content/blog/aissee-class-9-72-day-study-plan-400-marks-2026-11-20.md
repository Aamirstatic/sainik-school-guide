## Class 9 is not Class 6 with harder sums

Most Class 9 preparation fails in the same quiet way: families prepare for it like Class 6, only a little harder. But the Class 9 paper is a different exam, not a bigger one. It is 150 questions for **400 marks in 180 minutes**, entirely in English, and Maths alone is worth half of it. A child who studies all five sections equally is studying the paper wrong.

Here is the paper, in one table. Everything in this plan hangs off it.

| Class 9 section | Questions | Marks each | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 50 | 4 | **200** |
| Intelligence | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| English | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| General Science | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| Social Science | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| **Total** | **150** | — | **400** |

Two numbers deserve a second look. First, Maths: 200 marks — every other section combined is also 200. Second, the qualifying floor: 25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate. That means 12.5 marks out of 50 in each small section — at least **7 correct answers out of 25** in Intelligence, English, Science and SST — and 100 marks overall.

From today, Friday 20 November, to the exam on Sunday 31 January, there are **72 days — ten weeks and two days**. The form closes on 2 December, which falls inside week 2, so the plan treats that as an admin week: form first, study second, no guilt about it.

## The 10-week plan

| Week | Dates | The job | Sunday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20–26 Nov | **Diagnose.** One full 180-minute paper under real conditions; build the error log; list the five weakest Maths chapters. | Mock 1 (diagnostic), 22 Nov |
| 2 | 27 Nov–3 Dec | **Foundations + form.** Maths: number system and algebra basics. English: one passage a day. File the AISSEE form before 2 Dec. | Mock 2, 29 Nov |
| 3 | 4–10 Dec | **Maths: geometry and mensuration.** Science: physics — motion, force, light. Intelligence drills, 15 min daily. | Mock 3, 6 Dec |
| 4 | 11–17 Dec | **Maths: arithmetic and speed.** SST: history timelines + map work (rivers, states, capitals). English grammar sets. | Mock 4, 13 Dec |
| 5 | 18–24 Dec | **Science: chemistry and biology.** Maths: algebra problem sets. First timed section tests — 25 questions in 25 minutes. | Mock 5, 20 Dec |
| 6 | 25–31 Dec | **Winter-break push.** Two study blocks a day; finish the syllabus. Every unfinished chapter gets a date, not a wish. | Mock 6, 27 Dec |
| 7 | 1–7 Jan | **Mocks take over.** Full papers matter more than new chapters now. Error log review every evening. | Mock 7, 3 Jan |
| 8 | 8–14 Jan | **Weak-area repair.** The error log decides the timetable — the three worst chapters get the most hours. City slip out 11 Jan. | Mock 8, 10 Jan |
| 9 | 15–21 Jan | **Speed week.** Section-wise timing: Maths 80 minutes, the rest 20 minutes each, 20 minutes buffer. No new topics. | Mock 9, 17 Jan |
| 10 | 22–30 Jan | **Taper.** Formula sheets, error log, one light mock. Admit card out 25 Jan — download and print it. Rest on 30 Jan. | Mock 10 (light), 24 Jan |
| Exam | **31 Jan** | **Exam day, 2:00–5:00 PM.** Reach early, carry the admit card and a photo ID, attempt everything. | — |

A note on the Sundays, because they are the spine of the plan: there are ten Sundays between now and the exam, and each one carries a full paper in the real 2:00 to 5:00 PM slot. A mock taken at 10 in the morning teaches your child to be sharp at 10 in the morning. The exam is at 2 in the afternoon, when most children are at their sleepiest — so practise at 2 in the afternoon, phone in another room, OMR sheet, no breaks. By 31 January, the real paper should feel like the eleventh Sunday, not the first.

## The Maths-first rule, in hours

If your child studies two hours a day, the split should look roughly like this: **one hour for Maths, and one hour shared between the other four sections** — rotating daily, so Intelligence, English, Science and SST each get attention twice a week. On Sundays, the mock replaces the split.

Why so much Maths? Because each Maths question is worth 4 marks, and the section is 50 questions long. A child who improves from 25 to 35 correct in Maths gains 40 marks — more than an entire small section is worth. But the rule has a hard limit: Maths never eats the small sections' time. Seven correct answers per small section is the minimum that keeps the paper alive, and a section ignored for three weeks can easily fall below it. The error log keeps the balance honest: if Intelligence is stuck at 5 correct, it gets an extra 15 minutes a day until it is not.

## Three mistakes that cost Class 9 children the most

**Studying new chapters in January.** Weeks 7 to 10 are for mocks and repair, not discovery. A chapter first met on 15 January will be half-learned on 31 January — and it will have stolen hours from the chapters your child already nearly knows. The syllabus must be finished by 31 December. That is what week 6 is for.

**Treating the mock as a score, not a diagnosis.** A mock's number is the least useful thing about it. What matters is the error log: which questions were wrong, and why — concept missing, silly slip, time pressure, or misread question. After three mocks, the log will tell you exactly where the next 40 marks are hiding.

**Forgetting the form while planning the study.** The form closes on 2 December at 11:50 PM, with a late-fee window to 6 December and corrections from 7 to 9 December. None of that helps if the photo is the wrong size or the signature is rejected — [the photo and signature specs](/blog/aissee-2027-photo-signature-specs-size-background-rejections/) and [the 25 percent rule, worked with examples](/blog/aissee-2027-qualifying-marks-25-percent-rule-worked-examples/) are worth one evening each in week 2. Administrative marks are the cheapest marks in this exam, and the only ones lost without ever opening a book.

## The last week, hour by hour

The taper is where plans usually fall apart, so here it is plainly. Friday 29 January: formula sheets and the error log only. Saturday 30 January: rest — a short revision of the formula sheet in the morning, then the admit card, ID, pens and a watch packed and checked, and an early night. Sunday 31 January: reach the centre early, read every question, and attempt all 150. There is no negative marking, so a blank scores exactly what a wrong answer scores — zero — and a guess sometimes scores 2 or 4.

Seventy-two days is a long time to a 14-year-old and a short time to a parent watching the calendar. The plan asks for two honest hours a day and one honest Sunday a week. That is all. Start with the diagnostic mock this Sunday, and let the error log — not anxiety — decide what happens next.

## FAQs

**How many days are left for the AISSEE 2027 Class 9 exam?**

72 days from 20 November 2026 to the exam on Sunday, 31 January 2027. The Class 9 paper runs from 2:00 to 5:00 PM — 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks, in English only, on an OMR sheet with no negative marking.

**How is the AISSEE Class 9 paper different from Class 6?**

Class 9 is 150 questions for 400 marks in 180 minutes, while Class 6 is 125 questions for 300 marks in 150 minutes. Class 9 has five sections — Maths 200 marks, and Intelligence, English, General Science and Social Science at 50 marks each — and the whole paper is in English only.

**How much time should a Class 9 student give to Maths?**

About half of all study time. Maths is 50 questions for 200 marks — exactly half the paper — and it is the section where most children lose the most marks. The other four sections share the other half, so they get one focused session each per week, not equal time with Maths.

**What is the minimum score needed to qualify in AISSEE Class 9?**

At least 25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate — 100 marks out of 400 overall, and 12.5 marks out of 50 in each of the four smaller sections, which means at least 7 correct answers per section since each question carries 2 marks. The 25 and 40 percent conditions do not apply to SC and ST candidates in the 33 established schools, but they do apply to everyone in New Sainik Schools.

**When should my Class 9 child start full-length mock tests?**

The first full 180-minute mock should be the diagnostic in week 1, on Sunday 22 November. After that, one full paper every Sunday in the real 2:00 to 5:00 PM slot, with the answers reviewed the same evening. By exam day that is ten full papers.

**My child studies in Hindi medium. Can they still crack the Class 9 paper?**

Yes, but the English-only paper needs deliberate work, not hope. Read one English passage aloud every day, learn five new words with their use in a sentence, and solve Maths word problems in English from the start. Our 72-day Hindi-medium bridge explains the routine in detail.
