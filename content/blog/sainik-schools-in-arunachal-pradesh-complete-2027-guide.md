## One school, one shot — and a quota that works for you

Arunachal Pradesh has exactly one Sainik School. One. And that is precisely why this guide is short on choices and long on the maths that matters: **Sainik School East Siang**, at Niglok village near Pasighat, is the only established Sainik School in the state — and for an Arunachal-domicile child, the home-state quota makes it the most winnable Sainik School seat in the country.

If your child is in Class 5 or Class 8 in Itanagar, Pasighat, Tawang, Ziro or any border district right now, this is the complete picture — the school, the seats, the quota, the coaching pipeline the Army built for your districts, and the nine days you have left to apply.

## The one school: East Siang

|  | **Sainik School East Siang** |
|---|---|
| Location | Niglok village, Ruksin, East Siang district (Pasighat HQ) |
| Raised | **27 August 2018** — the 28th Sainik School of India |
| Campus | 100 acres near the banks of the Sille River; nearest airport Pasighat, 35 km |
| Motto | Education, Character, Nationalism |
| Classes | VI–XII, fully residential, CBSE, English medium |
| Intake | Boys and girls, Class 6 and Class 9, through AISSEE |
| First session | 27 August 2018, with 60 students |
| Official website | sainikschooleastsiang.com |

The school is young — eight years old — and that youth cuts both ways. On one side, there is no sixty-year alumni network and no decades-deep NDA record to point at: its first Class 6 batch, admitted in 2018, reached Class 12 only in 2024–25, so its NDA story is only just beginning. On the other side, your child would join a school still being shaped — small batches, a new campus, and a culture where a cadet's batch genuinely leaves a mark.

Chief Minister Pema Khandu inaugurated the school in 2018, thanking the Prime Minister and the then Defence Minister for granting permission for three Sainik Schools in the state — the locations of the other two were to be decided after consultation. As of the AISSEE 2027 bulletin, East Siang remains the state's only school among the 33 established ones. If a new Sainik School is ever approved for Arunachal under the PPP model, it will appear in the bulletin's new-schools list — check that list in the form itself before you rank your choices.

## The seats, and why the home-state quota is your edge

The AISSEE 2027 bulletin lists East Siang's tentative intake as **85 seats in Class 6 and 7 in Class 9**. NTA calls these figures tentative, so treat them as the planning numbers, not a promise — but the quota maths on top of them is the standing policy, and it is generous to Arunachal families:

| Rule | What it means at East Siang |
|---|---|
| 67% home-state / 33% other states | Roughly 57 of the 85 Class 6 seats go to Arunachal-domicile children first |
| Girls (Class 6) | 10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more — so 10 girls' seats |
| SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27% | Applied within the home-state and other-state pools |
| Defence wards | 25% of the remainder, after the category reservations |

Read that first row twice. In most states, the home-state quota is a small advantage. In Arunachal, with one school and a small applicant pool from a sparsely populated state, it is the whole game: more than two-thirds of the seats are reserved for children domiciled in Arunachal Pradesh before any out-of-state candidate is considered. The 7 Class 9 seats are few — lateral entry is brutally competitive everywhere — but the Class 6 route, with 85 seats and the quota behind it, is genuinely winnable for a prepared child.

Girls should note the Class 6 girls' seats specifically: 10 seats, because the rule takes the higher of 10% of vacancies or 10 seats. A girl from Arunachal applying to East Siang is competing for a protected pool, not against the whole field.

## The Sela Warriors: the Army is coaching your districts

Here is the part of the Arunachal story most parents outside the state never hear. The Indian Army runs a coaching programme for tribal youth from Arunachal's border districts — **Mission Sainik School**, locally called **Sela Warriors** after the Sela Pass in Tawang, run by the Gajraj Corps. On 28 October 2026, the Chief Minister's Office publicly commended it, putting two numbers on record: **59 students have secured Sainik School admissions through the programme, and 75 aspirants are preparing for AISSEE 2027**.

This is not the first time the model has worked. In May 2024, the Army's Spear Corps picked 33 students from remote hamlets — including Sarli in Kurung Kumey district — and prepared them with 88 theory classes, 18 rounds of testing and counselling. The Hindu reported in September 2025 that **32 of the 33 qualified at the national level**, and that Milli Yabi of Sarli became the first to secure final admission — to Sainik School East Siang, the school this website is named after.

The practical lesson is simple: structured preparation plus help with the paperwork beats talent alone. If your child is in a remote or border district, ask your school, your district education office or your nearest Army unit whether a Mission Sainik School batch runs near you — and if one does, join it. Our full report on the Sela Warriors numbers is here: [Arunachal's 'Sela Warriors': 59 admissions, 75 preparing for AISSEE 2027](/news/arunachal-mission-sainik-school-sela-warriors-59-admissions-2026-10-28/).

## The exam, the dates, and your 9-day checklist

The exam is the same national paper for every state. Class 6: **150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks** — Maths 50 questions for 150 marks, Language, Intelligence and GK 25 questions for 50 marks each. Class 9: **180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks** — Maths 50 for 200, English, Intelligence, Science and Social Science 25 for 50 each, and the Class 9 paper is **English only**. There is **no negative marking**, and a child must score **25% in each section and 40% in aggregate** to qualify (not applicable to SC/ST).

Your timeline, from tonight:

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM** | AISSEE 2027 form closes (Class 6 and Class 9) |
| 3–6 December 2026 | Late-fee window — extra ₹500 on top of the regular fee |
| 7–9 December 2026 | Correction window — fix permitted details, one time only |
| 11 January 2027 | Exam city announced |
| 25 January 2027 | Admit card available |
| **31 January 2027 (Sunday)** | **AISSEE 2027 exam** |

The fee is **₹850** for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and **₹700** for SC/ST. Apply only at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society**.

Three things to do before you submit. First, **rank East Siang first** in your school choices — it is your home-state school, and the quota is the strongest card you hold. Our [choice-filling guide](/blog/aissee-2027-form-which-sainik-schools-to-list-rank-order/) explains the ranking logic, including the 40/60 rule at the new PPP schools, in full. Second, **check the certificates now** — domicile, caste and birth-certificate fixes need a government office visit and working days, and this is the last full working week before the deadline. Third, **do not plan on the late window**: the extra ₹500 is annoying, but the real risk is the last-night server rush on 2 December.

Arunachal's parents should also know what is happening next door. On 21 November, Nagaland's Chief Minister said **10 to 20 of Sainik School Punglwa's Nagaland-quota seats go vacant every year** — our [Nagaland guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-nagaland-complete-2027-guide/) explains why. Vacant home-state seats eventually go to out-of-state candidates. The quota only helps the families who actually apply.

East Siang is eight years old, one school for a whole state, and 85 Class 6 seats with a 67% home-state claim on them. For an Arunachal child who prepares, that is not a long shot. It is the best-structured opportunity in the state's education system — and the form closes in nine days.

## FAQs

**How many Sainik Schools are in Arunachal Pradesh?**

One established Sainik School: Sainik School East Siang at Niglok village, near Pasighat, raised on 27 August 2018 as the 28th Sainik School of India. It admits boys and girls to Class 6 and Class 9 through AISSEE.

**How many seats does Sainik School East Siang have for AISSEE 2027?**

Tentatively 85 seats in Class 6 and 7 seats in Class 9, from the AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin's appendix. NTA calls these figures tentative, and the home-state quota gives Arunachal-domicile children the first claim on most of them.

**Do Arunachal Pradesh students get a home-state quota at East Siang?**

Yes. Under the standing AISSEE policy for the 33 established schools, 67 percent of seats are reserved for home-state candidates and 33 percent for other states. Within each, SC gets 15 percent, ST 7.5 percent and OBC-NCL 27 percent, and 25 percent of the remainder goes to defence wards.

**What is the age limit for AISSEE 2027 Class 6 at East Siang?**

Children must be 10 to 12 years old as on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017 — and studying in Class 5. For Class 9, the age is 13 to 15 years as on 31 March 2027, born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014, and the child must have passed Class 8 from a recognised school.

**What is the AISSEE 2027 fee, and when does the form close?**

Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and Rs 700 for SC and ST candidates. The form closes on Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM, with a late window from 3 to 6 December costing an extra Rs 500, and a one-time correction window from 7 to 9 December. The exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027.

**What is the Sela Warriors programme, and can it help my child?**

Sela Warriors is the Indian Army Gajraj Corps' Mission Sainik School coaching programme for tribal youth from Arunachal's border districts. According to the Chief Minister's Office, 59 students have secured Sainik School admissions through it and 75 aspirants are preparing for AISSEE 2027. Ask your school, district education office or nearest Army unit whether a batch runs near you.
