Here is the number that should decide your child's whole revision plan: in the AISSEE Class 6 paper, maths is **50 questions for 150 marks — exactly half the paper**. The other three sections combined are worth the same as maths alone. And the good news is that most of that maths lives in one book your child already owns: the Class 5 NCERT Maths book, *Math-Magic*.

You don't need a new guidebook to start. You need a plan for the book you have, in the right order, over the 78 days left to the exam on 31 January 2027.

## Why Class 5 NCERT is the right base — and where it stops

AISSEE Class 6 maths is set at Class 5 level, with a few topics stretching into Class 6. That is why *Math-Magic* works so well as the base: fractions, decimals, area and perimeter, multiples and factors, patterns, data handling — the spine of the AISSEE maths section is all in there.

But be honest with yourself about the gap. A few topics in the AISSEE syllabus go beyond Class 5 NCERT: **percentage, profit and loss, simple interest, average, ratio, and time–speed–distance**. NCERT alone will not teach those. Your child needs a Class 6 NCERT book or a decent guidebook for that short list — and our [Class 6 maths formula cheat sheet](/blog/aissee-class-6-maths-formula-cheat-sheet/) covers the formulas for exactly those topics in one place.

## The 14 chapters, sorted by priority

Don't revise the book front to back. Some chapters carry far more AISSEE weight than others. Work in this order:

| Chapter | What it teaches | Priority | Why it matters for AISSEE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4. Parts and Wholes | Fractions | ⭐ Must-master | Fractions appear in many forms — compare, add, subtract, word problems |
| 10. Tenths and Hundredths | Decimals | ⭐ Must-master | Decimal–fraction conversion is a favourite AISSEE question type |
| 13. Ways to Multiply and Divide | Multiplication & division methods | ⭐ Must-master | Speed and accuracy here decide the whole paper's pace |
| 6. Be My Multiple, I'll be Your Factor | Multiples, factors, LCM & HCF | ⭐ Must-master | LCM/HCF questions are quick marks if the method is automatic |
| 11. Area and its Boundary | Area & perimeter | ⭐ Must-master | Area–perimeter of rectangles, squares and triangles, again and again |
| 2. Shapes and Angles | Angles & shapes | Important | Angle types and basic geometry feed the geometry questions |
| 3. How Many Squares? | Counting squares, area sense | Important | Builds the visual sense behind area questions |
| 7. Can You See the Pattern? | Number patterns | Important | Pattern questions are easy marks once the habit is built |
| 12. Smart Charts | Data handling, charts | Important | Bar charts and tables — straightforward marks |
| 14. How Big? How Heavy? | Measurement & units | Important | Unit conversion trips up more children than the sums do |
| 1. The Fish Tale | Number sense, big numbers | Light touch | A revision chapter — skim it, don't camp on it |
| 5. Does it Look the Same? | Symmetry | Light touch | One or two questions at most; a quick pass is enough |
| 8. Mapping Your Way | Directions & maps | Light touch | Useful, but low weight — do the exercises once |
| 9. Boxes and Sketches | 3D shapes & nets | Light touch | Interesting, rarely heavy in the paper |

Five must-master chapters, five important ones, four light touches. That order is the whole trick: if your child runs out of time in January, the must-master five should already be finished.

## The 11-week revision plan

78 days is 11 weeks. Here is how the chapters fit into them:

| Weeks | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1–2 (15–28 Nov) | Foundations. Chapters 4 and 10 — fractions and decimals — plus the fraction–decimal–percentage conversions from our formula sheet. 20 sums a day. |
| 3–4 (29 Nov–12 Dec) | Chapters 6 and 13 — multiples, factors, LCM/HCF, and multiplication–division methods. Start a formula notebook. |
| 5–6 (13–26 Dec) | Chapter 11 — area and perimeter — plus Chapters 2 and 3 for geometry sense. First two timed section tests (25 maths questions, 40 minutes each). |
| 7–8 (27 Dec–9 Jan) | Chapters 7, 12 and 14 — patterns, charts, measurement — plus the beyond-NCERT list: percentage, profit and loss, simple interest, average, ratio, time–speed–distance. |
| 9–10 (10–23 Jan) | Mixed practice only. Previous year papers and sample papers, timed. One full 150-minute paper every Sunday at 2 PM — the same time as the real exam. |
| 11 (24–30 Jan) | Taper. No new chapters. Re-do the error log, re-read the formula notebook, one last easy paper mid-week. Rest the day before the exam. |

## Five habits that make the revision stick

**1. Keep an error log.** Every wrong sum goes in a notebook with the correct method written beside it. In week 11, this notebook — not the textbook — is what your child revises. Last year, a parent told us her son's error log had the same fraction mistake three times before it finally stopped appearing. That's the log doing its job.

**2. Twenty sums a day, every day.** Not fifty on Sunday and none on Monday. Maths is a daily habit, and twenty focused sums beat a hundred rushed ones. Honestly, most guidebooks overcomplicate this: the child who does twenty sums daily for eleven weeks will beat the child who buys five books and opens none.

**3. Practise at 2 PM.** The exam runs from 2:00 to 4:30 PM. If your child only ever practises at 10 in the morning, the afternoon paper will feel strange — heavier lunch, sleepier mind. One full paper every Sunday at 2 PM fixes that for free.

**4. One formula notebook.** Area of a triangle, HCF × LCM = product of two numbers, percentage change — every formula on one page each, in your child's own handwriting. Memory works better for things you have written yourself.

**5. Teach it back.** After each chapter, ask your child to explain one question to you as if you were the student. If they can teach it, they know it. If they can't, that chapter isn't finished yet — and it's better to find out now than on 31 January.

## FAQs

**Is NCERT Class 5 Maths enough for the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
Mostly, but not entirely. The Class 5 NCERT book Math-Magic covers the core of the AISSEE Class 6 maths section — fractions, decimals, area and perimeter, multiples and factors, patterns and data handling. A few topics in the AISSEE syllabus, like percentage, profit and loss, simple interest, average, ratio and time-speed-distance, go beyond Class 5 NCERT and need a Class 6 book or a guidebook.

**How many marks is maths in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
Maths is 50 questions for 150 marks — exactly half of the paper's 300 marks. The full paper is 125 questions in 150 minutes, with no negative marking, so every maths question is worth 3 marks.

**Which NCERT Class 5 Maths chapters matter most for AISSEE?**
Parts and Wholes (fractions), Tenths and Hundredths (decimals), Ways to Multiply and Divide, Be My Multiple I'll be Your Factor (LCM and HCF), and Area and its Boundary (area and perimeter) matter most. Together they cover the topics that carry the heaviest weight in the AISSEE Class 6 maths section.

**How many weeks does it take to revise Class 5 Maths for AISSEE?**
About 11 weeks at a steady pace — roughly one chapter every four to five days in the first six weeks, then mixed practice and full papers in the last five weeks. With 78 days left to the 31 January 2027 exam, starting now fits that plan exactly.

**Should my child solve NCERT exercises or previous year papers for AISSEE maths?**
Both, in that order. NCERT exercises build the concept; previous year papers and sample papers teach speed and the AISSEE question style. In the last five weeks, shift most of the time to timed papers — one full 150-minute paper every Sunday at 2 PM, the same time as the real exam.
