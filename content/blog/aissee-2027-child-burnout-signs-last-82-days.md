## The child who studies the most is not always the one who is fine

Last week, a mother told us her son had started crying over a maths worksheet. Not a hard one — a worksheet he had solved easily a month ago. He had been studying six hours a day after school, plus Sundays. His mock scores had fallen for three weeks straight. She thought he needed more practice. He needed the opposite.

With 80 days left to the AISSEE 2027 exam on 31 January, this is the point in the season where the danger shifts. In September, the risk was starting too late. Now the risk is pushing too hard. A 10- or 11-year-old cannot run a 11-week sprint at full speed without paying for it — and the payment usually shows up as falling scores, tears, and a child who quietly stops caring.

Burnout in a child rarely looks like laziness. It looks like effort that stops working. Here is how to spot it early, and what to actually do about it.

## 7 signs your child is burning out

Check this list honestly. One sign on a bad day means nothing. Three or more, lasting over two weeks, means the plan needs changing — not the child.

| # | Sign | What it looks like at home |
|---|------|----------------------------|
| 1 | Scores fall while hours rise | More study, worse mocks, week after week |
| 2 | Small mistakes cause tears or anger | Crying over one wrong answer; throwing the pencil down |
| 3 | Sleep goes wrong | Can't fall asleep, wakes early, or sleeps far more than usual |
| 4 | Study avoidance | Suddenly "forgets" homework, drags feet, hides books |
| 5 | Body complaints | Headaches, stomach aches, tiredness with no fever |
| 6 | The spark goes out | Stops talking about Sainik School; says "I don't care" |
| 7 | Everything feels personal | Takes every correction as criticism; snaps at siblings |

The sixth sign is the one parents miss most. A child who says "I don't care about the exam" usually cares too much and has run out of energy to show it. Don't argue with the sentence. Fix the energy.

## What actually causes it (it is rarely the syllabus)

Honestly, most burnout in AISSEE preparation is not caused by maths or English. It is caused by three things that pile up quietly:

**No recovery built in.** Seven days a week, no rest day, no play. Adults cannot work like that for 11 weeks; children definitely cannot. The brain consolidates learning during rest and sleep, not during the sixth hour of the same chapter.

**Sleep traded for study.** The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 9 to 12 hours of sleep per 24 hours for children aged 6 to 12. When a child sleeps seven hours to fit in two more hours of revision, the revision is mostly wasted — tired children retain less and make the same mistakes again the next day.

**The score becomes the child.** When every dinner conversation is about marks, the child starts believing that a bad mock means they are a bad child. That is when avoidance starts: if I don't try, I can't fail. It looks like laziness. It is self-protection.

## The recovery plan: one week to get your child back

If you recognise three or more signs, don't wait for the next mock to confirm it. Do this for one week:

| Day | What changes |
|-----|--------------|
| Day 1–2 | Cut study to half. No new chapters, only revision of easy topics. Bedtime fixed, no screens after 8 pm. |
| Day 3 | One full hour of play or sport, no guilt attached. Ask the child what they want to do — and let them choose. |
| Day 4–5 | Rebuild to 70% of the old load. One mock section only, not a full paper. Praise effort, not the score. |
| Day 6 | Sunday mock at 2 PM — the real exam time — then a genuinely free evening. |
| Day 7 | Sit with your child for ten minutes. Ask: "What part of study feels worst right now?" Then fix that one thing first. |

Most children bounce back within a week. If scores are still falling after two weeks of a lighter load, the problem may be a gap in understanding rather than effort — a weak chapter, not a weak child. That is a teaching problem, and it is fixable: go back to basics in that one subject instead of adding more hours across all four.

## A weekly rhythm that prevents burnout

Prevention is cheaper than recovery. For the remaining 80 days, build the week like this:

- **Six study days, one real rest day.** Sunday can be the mock day, but the evening after the mock belongs to the child — not to analysing every wrong answer.
- **Sleep is non-negotiable.** 9 to 12 hours for a Class 6 child. A 9:30 pm bedtime with a 6:30 am wake-up is 9 hours. Protect it the way you protect the exam date.
- **Play is not a reward for finishing study.** It is part of the plan. An hour of cricket or cycling keeps a child sharper than an hour of extra sums done half-asleep.
- **One honest conversation a week.** Not "how much did you score?" but "what was hard this week?" The first question measures the child. The second one helps them.
- **Mock tests at 2 PM.** The exam starts at 2:00 PM on 31 January 2027 — an afternoon exam. Practising in the morning and performing in the afternoon is a needless handicap. ## What to say — and what not to say

Words land harder in the last two months than at any other time. Two sentences to keep, two to drop:

**Say this:** "I am proud of how hard you are working, whatever the score." And: "If this paper goes badly, we will figure out the next step together." The second sentence is not giving up. It is removing the fear that makes children freeze.

**Stop saying this:** "Your cousin scored 280, why can't you?" Comparison is the fastest route to a child who stops trying. And stop saying: "Only 80 days left, no time to waste." Your child knows the countdown. What they need from you is calm, not a second clock.

## When to get help

Most burnout fixes itself with rest and a lighter week. Get professional help — your child's doctor, or the school counsellor — if you see persistent sleep problems, refusal to eat or go to school, frequent crying, or talk about being worthless or a failure. Don't wait for the exam to pass. No entrance exam is worth a child's health, and a child who feels safe performs better than a child who feels cornered.

## The honest ending

AISSEE 2027 matters. The form closes on 2 December, the exam is on 31 January, and 80 days is enough time to prepare well. But here is the thing parents forget in the final stretch: the exam tests what your child knows on one afternoon. Burnout decides whether any of that knowledge shows up at all.

A rested child who studied four focused hours beats an exhausted child who studied seven. That is not soft advice. It is how memory works. Protect the child first, and the score usually follows.

## FAQs

**How do I know if my child is burning out before AISSEE 2027?**

Watch for falling mock scores despite more study hours, tears or anger over small mistakes, trouble sleeping, avoiding study altogether, and complaints of headaches or stomach aches. One bad day is normal; a pattern over two weeks is a warning sign.

**How many hours should a Class 6 child sleep during AISSEE preparation?**

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 9 to 12 hours of sleep per 24 hours for children aged 6 to 12. Cutting sleep to add study hours backfires — tired children retain less and make more mistakes.

**Should I reduce my child's study hours if they seem stressed?**

Yes, briefly. Cut the load for three to four days, protect sleep and one hour of play, then rebuild gradually. A short recovery usually restores scores faster than grinding through exhaustion.

**Does taking a break before AISSEE hurt the exam result?**

No. A planned rest day each week and a lighter week after every mock test keeps a child sharp for 31 January 2027. Children who study seven days a week for months usually peak too early and fade before exam day.

**When should I seek professional help for my child's exam stress?**

If your child has persistent sleep problems, refuses to eat or go to school, cries often, or talks about being worthless or a failure, speak to your child's doctor or a school counsellor. Do not wait for the exam to pass.

**How many days are left for AISSEE 2027?**

As of 12 November 2026, there are 80 days left. The exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027, starting at 2:00 PM for both Class 6 and Class 9.
