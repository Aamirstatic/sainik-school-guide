Every Sainik School parent eventually faces the same fork in the road: after Class 10 or 12, does my child aim for the **NDA** — the officer's road — or enter through the **Agniveer** route under the Agnipath scheme? The timing is right to ask honestly. The **first batch of Agniveers, enrolled in 2023, completes its four-year tenure between November 2026 and January 2027** — Navy and Air Force batches first, then the Army's sizeable batches. For the first time, we are not discussing the scheme in theory. We are watching it land.

This is not a "which is better" article, because the two routes are not competing for the same student. They differ in entry level, commitment, pay and risk. Here is the honest comparison, then who each suits.

## The two routes at a glance

| | **Agniveer (Agnipath)** | **NDA** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Short-service soldier entry (personnel below officer rank) | Officer entry through UPSC |
| Entry after | Class 10 (GD) or Class 12 (Technical/Clerk) | Class 12 |
| Age | 17.5 to 21 (first batches had a one-time relaxation to 23) | 16.5 to 19.5, unmarried |
| Selection | Written test + physical + medical; no SSB | UPSC written (Maths 300 + GAT 600) + 5-day SSB + medicals |
| Training | About 6 months, then unit service | 3 years at NDA Khadakwasla + 1 year at service academy |
| Service length | 4 years, then exit — or retention | Full career till superannuation |
| Rank structure | Agniveer, then regular soldier if retained | Commissioned as Lieutenant, rises to General |
| Pay | ~Rs. 21,000 in hand (year 1) to ~Rs. 28,000 (year 4); Seva Nidhi ~Rs. 11.71 lakh tax-free on exit | Officer pay scales + allowances from commissioning |
| Pension | None for the 4-year term | Full pensionary benefits for regular officers |
| Best for | Early government salary + discipline + funded exit | A lifetime defence career as an officer |

## Agniveer: what four years really look like

The Agnipath scheme, launched in June 2022, takes in young men and women aged 17.5 to 21 for a four-year engagement including roughly six months of training. The monthly package starts at Rs. 30,000 and rises to Rs. 40,000 by year four; after the 30% Seva Nidhi contribution, the **in-hand salary is about Rs. 21,000 in year one, rising to about Rs. 28,000 in year four**, with allowances like hard-area and ration money on top.

At the end of four years, the fork appears again:

- **Up to 25% of each batch** can be selected into the regular cadre on merit and service need — a full 15-year engagement with regular pay scales, allowances and pensionary benefits. The government has said there is no current move to change this ratio.
- The rest exit with a **tax-free Seva Nidhi payout of roughly Rs. 11.71 lakh** (own contributions plus matching government funds, with interest), a skill certificate, and — this is the part many parents miss — **real reservation backing**: 10% of vacancies in the CAPFs, Assam Rifles and the Indian Coast Guard are reserved for ex-Agniveers, and several states have added police quotas. Uttar Pradesh notified a **20% horizontal reservation** for former Agniveers in police constable, PAC and firemen recruitment; Uttarakhand notified a **10% horizontal reservation** in uniformed Group-C posts (police, fire, forest, excise) in September 2025.

And states are building the bridge: Uttarakhand set up an **Agniveer Cell** (announced July 17) to map returning Agniveers' skills against government, CAPF, PSU and private-sector vacancies, with bridge training where skills don't match. The cell estimates about 1,200 Uttarakhand Agniveers return in the first two Army batches.

Be clear-eyed about the trade: **no pension, no gratuity for the four-year term** — by design, not an oversight. The Seva Nidhi corpus stays locked until exit. And if you are retained, the fine print: you receive only *your own* contribution plus interest; the government's matching half goes back to the exchequer. A fair exchange for a full military career — but know it before you plan around the full figure.

## NDA: the officer's road, unchanged and unforgiving

The National Defence Academy entry is the route Sainik Schools were founded to feed. UPSC conducts it **twice a year**, takes unmarried candidates aged **16.5 to 19.5**, and needs Class 12 (PCM for the Air Force and Navy wings). The written paper is 900 marks, the SSB is another 900, and then comes medical examination. Selection means three years at Khadakwasla plus a year at the service academy, and commissioning as a **Lieutenant** — the start of an officer's career with full pay, allowances and pensionary benefits till superannuation.

Girls have been eligible for NDA **since 2021**, which matters for Sainik School families with daughters watching the [girls' NDA pathway](/blog/girls-in-nda-complete-2027-guide-2026-10-08/).

What hasn't changed: the competition is brutal, the written paper rewards genuine Maths and GAT strength, and the SSB rewards personality built over years — exactly what six or seven years of Sainik School routine builds. If your child wants the olive green for life, this is still the premier road. Other officer doors exist after Class 12 too — the [Technical Entry Scheme](/blog/tes-entry-after-class-12-sainik-school-advantage-2026-10-21/) needs no written exam — so NDA is not the only one.

## Who fits whom: five honest questions

Sit with your child and answer these plainly:1. **How is the written-exam appetite?** NDA means beating lakhs in a UPSC paper plus the SSB. If your child loves academics and competition, NDA fits. If written exams are the weak point but physical standards and discipline are strengths, Agniveer GD plays to them.
2. **What is the risk tolerance?** Agniveer is a four-year term with a defined exit — income at 17-18, a funded lump sum at 21-22, then a second career with reservation backing. NDA is all-in for a career; failing it repeatedly costs the narrow age window.
3. **How strong is Class 12 PCM?** NDA's Air Force/Navy wings need PCM; TES needs 60% in PCM plus JEE Main. Strong PCM students have three officer doors (NDA, [TES](/blog/tes-entry-after-class-12-sainik-school-advantage-2026-10-21/), and later CDS/AFCAT).
4. **What does the family need first?** If the family needs an earning member quickly, Agniveer delivers a government salary at 17-18. If it can fund 3-4 more years of preparation, the officer routes pay far more over a lifetime.
5. **What does the child actually want?** The question parents skip. Some cadets want to *serve as soldiers* — in the unit, now. Some want to *lead as officers*. Both are honourable. Don't force one dream onto the other temperament.

**The Sainik School edge, honestly stated:** no published data breaks defence selections down by school type, so nobody can honestly quote you a "Sainik School selection rate." What is structural, not statistical: daily PT, drill, group tasks, regimented living and English-medium academics map directly onto the physical standards of Agniveer selection *and* the SSB's group-testing — whichever road your child takes.

## Decision timeline: when each door opens

- **After Class 10 (age 16+):** Agniveer General Duty becomes an option at 17.5. NDA is not yet open.
- **Class 11-12 (16.5-19.5):** the NDA window. This is also when TES (Class 12, PCM) opens. Two or three genuine NDA attempts are realistic here.
- **After Class 12 (17.5-21):** Agniveer (GD, Technical, Clerk) remains open — a real option even after NDA attempts are exhausted.
- **Age 21+:** the Agniveer window closes; officer entries (CDS after graduation, AFCAT) take over.

**The practical strategy most counsellors suggest:** NDA as the first target from Class 11, Agniveer as a parallel or fallback option after Class 12 — not a consolation prize, but a different career with its own dignity and economics.

## FAQs

**Can a Sainik School student try both Agniveer and NDA?**
Yes, if the age and qualification windows overlap. A Class 12 student can appear for NDA (16.5 to 19.5 years) and also apply as an Agniveer (17.5 to 21 years) as separate entries. Many cadets keep NDA first and Agniveer as a parallel option.

**What really happens to the Agniveers who exit after four years?**
Up to 25% of a batch are retained in the regular cadre. The rest exit with a tax-free Seva Nidhi payout of roughly Rs. 11.71 lakh with interest, a skill certificate, and 10% reservation in CAPFs, Assam Rifles and the Coast Guard, plus state-level police quotas.

**Which route pays more in the long run?**
An NDA officer's lifetime earnings are far higher: full officer pay scales, allowances, and a career running to superannuation. An Agniveer earns about Rs. 21,000 in hand in year one rising to Rs. 28,000 in year four, then exits with the Seva Nidhi lump sum and no pension. The officer route pays more over a career; the Agniveer route pays a government salary immediately after Class 10 or 12.

**Can an Agniveer become an officer later?**
Yes, through the normal officer entries. An ex-Agniveer within the age limit and holding the required qualification can apply for NDA (if under 19.5), CDS, AFCAT or the Technical Entry Scheme, like any other candidate.
