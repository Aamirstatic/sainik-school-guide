## The last free stretch — and it is shorter than it looks

Here is the thing most families realise too late. Between now and 31 January, there is exactly one stretch of days with no school, no homework and no morning bell: the winter break, in the last week of December. Everything after it is January — school, revision, admit-card downloads, and the exam itself, all stacked on top of each other.

Seventy-four days sounds like a lot. It is not. Take away school days, take away the December exams your child may also be sitting — RIMCEE on 6 December, RMS CET on 13 December, UP Sainik School on 20 December — and what is left is a handful of genuinely free days. The winter break is the biggest handful. Use it like a revision camp, not like a holiday, and not like a punishment either. Somewhere in between, with a plan written down before the break begins.

Last year, a parent told us their child "studied all winter break" and still lost marks in the same section in January. When we asked what the child had actually done, the answer was: the same chapters, in the same order, from the same book — the comfortable ones. That is the trap this plan is built to avoid. The break is not for doing more of what your child already does well. It is for fixing the one thing that keeps costing marks.

## The 10-day sprint

Assume ten break days. If your child's break is seven days, cut Days 3–4 and Day 9 — the structure still works. The rule for every study day: two focused sessions, morning and afternoon, with the 2:00 PM slot kept for papers, because that is when the real exam happens.

| Day | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Full mock paper, 2:00 PM, OMR sheet, phone away | Sets the baseline — and the error log for the whole break |
| Day 2 | Error-log day: every wrong answer rewritten with the correct method | The mock only helps if the mistakes get fixed |
| Day 3 | Weak section #1 — the section that lost the most marks | Repair, don't repeat: new questions, not re-reading notes |
| Day 4 | Weak section #1, continued + 30 minutes of the strongest section | Keeps the strong section warm while the weak one gets fixed |
| Day 5 | Light day: revision of formulas, vocabulary and GK facts only | A breather that still counts — no new material |
| Day 6 | Weak section #2 + OMR bubbling practice on a rough sheet | Speed and clean bubbling are worth marks by themselves |
| Day 7 | Full rest day — family, play, sleep | Rest is part of the plan, not a reward for finishing it |
| Day 8 | Second full mock paper, 2:00 PM, same conditions as Day 1 | The only honest measure of whether the break worked |
| Day 9 | Compare the two mocks: what improved, what is still leaking marks | This comparison decides the January plan |
| Day 10 | Light revision + pack the exam-day bag mentally: admit card, pencils, timing | Ends the break calm, not cramming |

Two things to notice. First, there are only two full mocks — not four, not five. More mocks in ten days just produce more scores to worry about and no time to fix anything. Second, Day 7 is a full rest day, in the middle, on purpose. A child who studies ten days straight does not arrive in January sharper. They arrive tired, and tired children make silly mistakes — the wrong bubble, the skipped question, the misread instruction. The exam on 31 January is 150 minutes for Class 6 and 180 minutes for Class 9. Stamina matters, and stamina is built by rest as much as by practice.

## What to revise — and what to stop doing

The error log decides the revision, not the textbook's chapter order. Here is how to read it. After each mock, sort every lost mark into three piles: **didn't know**, **knew but got it wrong**, and **ran out of time**. The first pile is the only one that needs new study. The second pile needs practice, not theory — the child knows the method and still slips, which is a concentration problem, not a knowledge problem. The third pile needs speed work: timed sets of ten questions, not more chapters.

And stop doing three things during the break. Stop starting new books — one book per section, finished properly, beats three books half-read. Stop comparing your child's mock scores with anyone else's — the only comparison that matters is Day 1 versus Day 8. And stop studying past 9 PM. The exam is at 2 in the afternoon, and a child who is used to studying late at night is not used to thinking sharply at 2 PM.

## The December calendar around the break

The break does not sit in isolation. December is the busiest month of this whole admission season, and the break only works if the rest of December is already handled:

- **28 November** — JNVST Class 6 exam (for children who applied; admit cards are already out).
- **2 December, 11:50 PM** — AISSEE 2027 regular form window closes. Late window 3–6 December (+₹500), correction window 7–9 December.
- **6 December** — RIMCEE written exam (Class 8, RIMC Dehradun).
- **13 December** — RMS CET written exam (Class 6 and 9, Rashtriya Military Schools).
- **20 December** — UP Sainik School entrance exam (Class 6 and 9, Lucknow and Gorakhpur).
- **Last week of December** — the winter break: the sprint above.
- **11 January 2027** — AISSEE exam city announced; **25 January** — admit cards; **31 January** — the exam.

Notice what that calendar means: after 20 December, nothing competes with AISSEE. The break is the first clean run at the exam, and January is the second. Families who waste the break on unstructured "study all day" arrive in January with the same weak section they had in November. Families who run the sprint arrive in January knowing exactly what to fix — and with a rested child.

## One last thing before the break starts

Write the plan down, on paper, with your child, before the break begins. Not a vague "we'll study in the holidays" — the actual days, the actual sections, the actual mock dates. Children follow written plans; they ignore intentions. And agree the rest days in advance, so Day 7 does not turn into a negotiation. A plan that includes rest is a plan a child will actually keep. A plan that doesn't is a plan that collapses on Day 3 — and then the whole break becomes a guilt trip, which helps nobody and teaches nothing.

The exam is 74 days away. The break is the last free stretch. Use it well, and January becomes revision, not rescue.

## FAQs

**How many days are left for AISSEE 2027?**

74 days from 18 November 2026. The exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027 — Class 6 from 2:00 to 4:30 PM and Class 9 from 2:00 to 5:00 PM. The winter break in the last week of December is the only school-free stretch left before the exam.

**Should my child study every day of the winter break?**

No. Plan for about six focused study days out of ten, with two full rest days and two light days. A child who studies ten days straight arrives in January exhausted, and exhaustion is what costs marks on exam day, not a missed chapter.

**How many mock tests should my child take during the winter break?**

Two full papers, on Day 1 and Day 8, in the exact exam window — 2:00 PM onwards, OMR sheet in hand, phone in another room. The point is not the score; it is the error log you build from each paper and the weak section you repair in between.

**What should we revise first in the winter break?**

Whatever the error log says, not whatever feels comfortable. If the last mock lost 20 marks in Maths, the break is a Maths break. The sections your child already scores well in need only one light revision each — protect the strong sections, repair the weak one.

**My child also has the RIMCEE or RMS CET exam in December. How does the break fit?**

RIMCEE is on 6 December and RMS CET on 13 December, both before the break, so the break belongs entirely to AISSEE. The UP Sainik School exam is on 20 December, also before the break. Nothing else competes with AISSEE after 20 December.

**Is the AISSEE 2027 form still open during the winter break?**

No. The regular window closes on 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM, the late window runs 3 to 6 December with an extra Rs. 500, and the correction window is 7 to 9 December. By the winter break, the form is done — which is exactly why the break is for revision, not paperwork.
