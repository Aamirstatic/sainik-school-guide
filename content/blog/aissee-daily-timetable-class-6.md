Ask ten parents how many hours a Class 6 child should study for AISSEE, and you'll get ten anxious numbers between two and eight. The honest answer: **three focused hours a day, done consistently for four months, beats six distracted hours done for three weeks and then abandoned.**

This article gives you one concrete, realistic daily timetable — not a fantasy schedule that requires your child to wake at 4 AM and quit playing. It assumes a normal school day, homework, and that your child is 10–12 years old and needs a childhood too.

## Know what the three hours are for

The AISSEE Class 6 paper is 150 minutes for 300 marks:

| Section | Questions × Marks | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 50 × 3 | 150 |
| Intelligence | 25 × 2 | 50 |
| Language | 25 × 2 | 50 |
| General Knowledge | 25 × 2 | 50 |

Maths is half the paper. Intelligence and Language are learnable skills, not talents. GK is a slow drip, not a flood. Your daily three hours should reflect that weight: **roughly 75 minutes Maths, 45 minutes Language, 40 minutes Intelligence, 20 minutes GK.** Adjust ±15 minutes to your child's weak spots — but never let one section swallow the whole evening.

## The daily timetable (weekdays)

Assume school ends around 3:30 PM and bedtime is 9:30 PM.

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 3:30 – 4:30 PM | Rest, play, snack | Non-negotiable. A tired 11-year-old cannot learn fractions. |
| 4:30 – 5:00 PM | School homework (fast finish) | Do it first so it doesn't haunt the evening. |
| 5:00 – 6:15 PM | **Slot 1: Maths** (75 min) | One topic + 15–20 practice questions, timed at the end. |
| 6:15 – 6:30 PM | Break | Walk, stretch, drink water. |
| 6:30 – 7:15 PM | **Slot 2: Language** (45 min) | Alternate: comprehension one day, grammar the next. |
| 7:15 – 7:45 PM | Dinner | |
| 7:45 – 8:25 PM | **Slot 3: Intelligence** (40 min) | Puzzles, patterns, coding-decoding — treat it like a game, not a subject. |
| 8:25 – 8:45 PM | **Slot 4: GK** (20 min) | Current-affairs diary: 5 facts written in own words. |
| 8:45 – 9:00 PM | Quick recap | Child explains one thing learned today, aloud, to you. |
| 9:00 – 9:30 PM | Wind down, bed | |

That's 3 hours of real study (5:00–9:00 with breaks), not 3 hours of sitting-at-a-desk. On lighter homework days, move the recap to include one extra mock section.

## The subject rotation (what changes each day)

Doing the same thing daily is how timetables die. Rotate inside the slots:

- **Maths days:** Mon (fractions/percentages), Tue (geometry & mensuration), Wed (word problems), Thu (ratio, averages), Fri (mixed revision of the week). For speed drills specifically, our [AISSEE maths speed guide](/blog/aissee-maths-speed-improvement-guide/) breaks down the triage system.
- **Language days:** alternate between reading comprehension practice and grammar topics (tenses, voice, prepositions, vocabulary). Read one English newspaper paragraph aloud daily — it quietly builds both comprehension and GK.
- **Intelligence days:** Mon/Wed/Fri — non-verbal (figures, series); Tue/Thu — verbal (analogy, coding, blood relations).
- **GK:** this is a *collection* habit. Maintain a small diary: 5 facts a day — a state capital, a sports winner, a national scheme, one science fact, one "who is who". Review the diary every Sunday. 100 days × 5 facts = 500 facts, which is more GK than most aspirants ever attempt.

## Sundays: the mock-test day

One full mock every Sunday from **October onwards** (there are roughly 17 Sundays between now and 31 January 2027). Rules:

1. Same time window as the real exam — build the body clock early.
2. OMR-style bubbling on paper, not answers ticked in the book.
3. 150 minutes, no phone, no "just one hint".
4. Error log after: every wrong answer rewritten with the correct method. The error log, not the score, is the real product of a mock.

If your child is not ready for full mocks yet, start with **one section per Sunday** and go full-length from mid-November. And remember — AISSEE has no negative marking, so every mock should be practised with an attempt-everything strategy (see our [negative marking guide](/blog/aissee-negative-marking-honest-answer/)).

## The four mistakes that break timetables

1. **Studying in the gaps between coaching, tuition and Olympiad classes.** If the week is already full, AISSEE prep needs a *dedicated* three hours, not the leftovers.
2. **Letting Maths eat the evening.** Maths is 50% of marks but children spend 80% of time on it because it's "serious". Protect the other sections — 25% in each section is mandatory to qualify.
3. **No rest days built in.** A 10-year-old running a 7-day grind will quietly quit by December. One genuinely free evening a week keeps the other six honest.
4. **Parents policing instead of partnering.** The 15-minute aloud recap at 8:45 PM works because the child *teaches* you. Teaching cements memory; being quizzed builds resentment.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many hours should a Class 6 student study daily for AISSEE?**
About 3 focused hours a day, five to six days a week, plus one mock on Sunday. Consistency over four months matters far more than marathon sessions.

**Should my child skip school homework to focus on AISSEE?**
No. School homework first, AISSEE prep second. The school syllabus overlaps heavily with the AISSEE paper anyway — homework done properly is hidden prep.

**When should we start full-length mock tests?**
October 2026 at the latest — roughly four months before the tentative 31 January 2027 exam. Start with one section per Sunday if full papers feel too early.

**Is the timetable different for Class 9 aspirants?**
The principle is the same (weight study time by marks), but the Class 9 paper has five sections and 400 marks with 50 Maths questions worth 4 marks each. Maths weightage is even heavier there — roughly half the paper's marks in 50 questions.

**What if my child can't finish the timetable every day?**
A missed day is fine; a missed *week* is the problem. On tight days, do just Slot 1 (Maths, 75 min) and the 20-minute GK diary. Protect the habit, not the hours.

**Does GK really need daily study?**
Not daily *study* — daily *collection*. 20 minutes of writing 5 facts in a diary beats a monthly GK cram session. The diary review every Sunday is where the marks come from.
