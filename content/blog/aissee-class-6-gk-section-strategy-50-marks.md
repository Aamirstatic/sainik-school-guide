## The section nobody plans for

Ask a parent what their child is studying for AISSEE and you will hear about Maths. Then English. Then reasoning. GK almost never comes up — and that is exactly why it matters so much.

Look at the paper honestly. Maths is 150 marks, yes, and it deserves half of every study day. But Language, Intelligence and GK are 50 marks each. GK carries the same weight as the other two, and it is the section children prepare for least. A child who scores 40 in GK and one who scores 20 has a 20-mark gap — the same as getting seven extra Maths questions right. And here is the sharper point: a child must clear **25% in every section** to qualify. A GK collapse can fail the whole paper even if Maths went well.

Last year, a parent told us his son had "finished the syllabus" in December — and then admitted the boy had never once opened a GK book. They spent January in a panic, cramming awards and capitals. Do not be that family. GK is the cheapest 50 marks on the paper, and it is won in 20 minutes a day, not in a January sprint.

## The paper at a glance

| Section | Questions | Marks each | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Language | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| Mathematics | 50 | 3 | 150 |
| Intelligence | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| **General Knowledge** | **25** | **2** | **50** |
| **Total** | **125** | — | **300** |

The Class 6 paper runs **150 minutes, 2:00 PM to 4:30 PM on Sunday, 31 January 2027**, in pen-and-paper OMR mode with **no negative marking**. The whole paper, GK included, comes in the medium you chose on the form — one of 13 languages — and that choice is now locked.

The qualifying bar is worth doing the maths on. 25% of 50 marks is 12.5, so **7 correct answers (14 marks) is the minimum that clears the GK section**. The aggregate bar is 40% of 300, or 120 marks. Both bars apply together — and neither applies to SC/ST candidates. That 7-question floor is the first target: secure it, then build upward.

## What actually comes in GK

The official syllabus (Appendix II of the bulletin) lists the GK topics plainly. No surprises, no secret chapters — just these:

| Topic bucket | What it covers | How to study it |
|---|---|---|
| India and its symbols | National flag, emblem, anthem, animal, bird, flower, states and capitals | One page of facts, revised weekly — this is the highest-yield bucket |
| Awards and personalities | Major national awards, famous Indians, firsts in India | Learn 2 awards or personalities a day; link each to one fact |
| Defence | The three services, ranks, famous regiments, military honours | Keep it simple: services, chiefs' titles, and what each force does |
| Sports | Major games, trophies, famous players, Olympics and Asian Games basics | One sport a day — the game, its trophy, one famous player |
| Organisations | UNO, UNICEF, WHO and other bodies children meet in textbooks | Name, headquarters, one line on what it does |
| Science and environment | Solar system, water cycle, energy, farming, food, health, everyday science | This is Class 5 science revision, not new learning — revise, don't re-study |
| Art and culture | Dances, festivals, monuments, folk arts | Pair each state with one dance and one festival |

Notice what is *not* on the list: there is no deep current-affairs section. A light awareness of the year's big events helps, but [chasing daily news is the lowest-return use of GK time](/blog/aissee-gk-static-gk-current-affairs-balance-plan/) — static GK from the Class 5 level is where the marks are.

## The 75-day GK plan

Seventy-five days is generous for a 50-mark section — if you start now and stay regular. Here is how to split it.

| Phase | Dates | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Build** | 17 Nov – 14 Dec (4 weeks) | Cover one topic bucket per week from the table above. 20 minutes a day, every day. Keep a small GK notebook: one page per topic. |
| **Practise** | 15 Dec – 11 Jan (4 weeks) | 25-question GK sets under 20 minutes, twice a week. Review every wrong answer the same evening and add it to the notebook. |
| **Sharpen** | 12 – 30 Jan (final 3 weeks) | Re-read the notebook only — no new topics. One GK set every other day. In the last week, revise symbols, awards and capitals daily; they are the most forgettable facts. |

That is the whole plan. It fits inside [the 11-week study plan](/blog/aissee-2027-final-79-days-11-week-study-plan-school-days/) without disturbing Maths, which still gets half of every day.

## The 20-minute daily routine

GK fails when it is treated as a weekend subject. It works as a habit. This is the routine we give our own students:

1. **10 minutes — read one topic.** One page, one bucket. Symbols on Monday, an award on Tuesday, a sport on Wednesday. Small and finished beats big and abandoned.
2. **10 minutes — answer 10 to 15 questions on it.** From a workbook, a previous paper, or questions a parent makes up at dinner. The answering matters more than the reading — GK is a recall subject.
3. **Sunday — one 25-question timed set.** Twenty minutes, OMR-style bubbling if you have a practice sheet. It builds the same exam habit [the Maths section strategy](/blog/aissee-class-6-maths-section-strategy-150-marks/) recommends for the rest of the paper.

And one rule for parents: **never test GK as a quiz show.** "Quick, who wrote the national anthem?" at the dinner table turns a friendly subject into a feared one. Ask one question, accept a wrong answer calmly, and tell the child the fact with a smile. GK learned under pressure is GK forgotten by January.

## Three mistakes that cost GK marks

- **Studying GK only from January.** By then Maths revision is eating every hour, and GK gets whatever is left — which is nothing. The 20 minutes has to start this week.
- **Memorising lists without links.** A list of 30 awards memorised in one sitting is gone in three days. Two awards a day, each linked to one person and one year, stays.
- **Skipping questions on exam day.** There is no negative marking, so a blank GK answer and a wrong one score exactly the same: zero. Guess. Eliminate two options, pick the best of the rest, and move on. [The Language section](/blog/aissee-class-6-language-section-strategy-50-marks/) and [the Intelligence section](/blog/aissee-class-6-intelligence-section-strategy-50-marks/) play by the same rule — attempt everything.

## FAQs

**How many marks is GK in AISSEE Class 6?**

25 questions worth 2 marks each — 50 marks out of the 300-mark Class 6 paper. That is one-sixth of the total, the same weight as Language and Intelligence.

**What is the qualifying mark for the GK section in AISSEE Class 6?**

Candidates must score at least 25% in each section and 40% in aggregate to qualify. On the 50-mark GK section, 25% is 12.5 marks, so 7 correct answers (14 marks) is the minimum that clears the section bar. The rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates.

**What topics come in AISSEE Class 6 General Knowledge?**

The official syllabus (Appendix II of the NTA bulletin) lists India and its symbols, art and culture, awards and personalities, defence, sports, organisations, solar system, environment, water cycle, energy, farming, food, health and everyday science.

**Does AISSEE Class 6 GK include current affairs?**

Mostly it is static GK from the Class 5 level — symbols, awards, sports, defence and everyday science. A light touch of recent major events helps, but chasing daily news is the lowest-return use of GK time.

**How much time should a child spend on GK every day?**

About 20 minutes a day is enough if it is regular: 10 minutes reading one topic and 10 minutes answering 10 to 15 questions on it. GK rewards steady, small doses far more than weekend cramming.

**Is there negative marking in the AISSEE GK section?**

No. There is no negative marking anywhere in AISSEE 2027, so every GK question should be attempted — even a guess costs nothing.
