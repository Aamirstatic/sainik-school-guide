## The short answer: yes — but only in one three-day window

You picked an exam city in a hurry, or your transfer came through, or you realised the city you chose is a five-hour bus ride from home. Can you change it?

Yes. But there is exactly one chance, and it lasts three days: the **correction window, 7 to 9 December 2026 (up to 11:50 PM)**. Log in to the NTA portal at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society, change your exam city preferences, save, and download the updated confirmation page. After 9 December, the door closes. NTA does not process correction requests by email or phone after the deadline — and no amount of calling the helpline changes that.

Last year, a parent called us in a panic in mid-January because the admit card showed a centre 280 km away. The form had been filled by a cyber-cafe operator who had picked the first city on the list. There was nothing anyone could do by then. The correction window had closed a month earlier, and the family spent the night before the exam in a hotel in a strange town. That is the mistake this article exists to prevent.

## How exam cities actually work

When you fill the AISSEE form, you choose **four exam cities, in order of preference**, from the list in the Information Bulletin. That is all you choose. You do not pick a centre, a school building, or a hall — NTA does that.

Three things about this choice catch parents out every year:

1. **NTA tries, but does not promise.** The bulletin says NTA will try to give you one of your four cities. It can also allot a different city. Your first choice is a preference, not a guarantee.
2. **A city with too few candidates may not open at all.** If hardly anyone picks a particular city, NTA may not set up a centre there, and those candidates get moved elsewhere. Picking an obscure city as your first choice is a gamble, not a clever shortcut.
3. **The city is not the centre.** The exam city is announced on **11 January 2027**. The exact centre — name, address, room — appears only on the admit card, available from **25 January 2027**. So you will know the city six weeks before the exam, and the building six days before it. Plan travel around the city; plan the final morning around the admit card.

And here is the part that surprises people: the exam city has **nothing to do with school allotment**. Which Sainik School your child can get depends on domicile state and the school preferences in the form — not on where they sit the paper. Changing your exam city does not change your home-state quota, and it does not change your school choices. It only changes where your child writes the exam.

## What you can and cannot change in the correction window

The correction window is not a free re-do of the whole form. It is a short list of editable fields. Here is the honest table, based on the 2027 bulletin as reported by two independent sources:

| Field | Can you change it? |
|---|---|
| Exam city preferences | ✅ **Yes** — this is the main thing the window is for |
| Category / sub-category (General, OBC-NCL, SC, ST, Defence) | ✅ Yes |
| Class applied for (Class 6 or Class 9) | ✅ Yes |
| Photograph, signature or thumb impression (if flagged by NTA) | ✅ Yes, re-upload |
| Child's name | ❌ **No** |
| Date of birth | ❌ **No** |
| Gender | ❌ **No** |
| Father's / mother's name | ❌ **No** (one source notes a parent's name may be editable in some cases — do not rely on it) |
| Mobile number and email ID | ❌ **No** — after payment, these are locked |

One honest complication: the sources disagree on the **medium of the question paper**. One bulletin-based guide lists medium among the editable fields; another states plainly that the medium cannot be changed once chosen. Because two sources conflict, treat the medium as **unchangeable** — choose it carefully when you first fill the form, and treat any chance to change it later as a bonus, not a plan. For Class 9, the paper is English only in any case; for Class 6, the paper is available in 13 languages, and the choice matters.

## The correction-window checklist (7–9 December)

Do this in the window, in this order, and you will not be the parent calling in January:

1. **Log in on 7 December, not 9 December.** Portals slow down on the last day. If the site hangs on the 9th, nobody will extend the window for you.
2. **Change the city preferences first.** Put the city you can actually reach — by train or bus, in winter, with a child — as choice one. Then the next-best. Do not leave a faraway city in the list "just in case".
3. **Re-check the unchangeable fields while you are there.** Name spelling, date of birth, category. If one of these is wrong, the correction window will not fix it — you need to know that now, not on admit-card day, so you can contact NTA through the official channels with time to spare.
4. **Re-upload the photo and signature if NTA flagged them.** A photo that is too dark, too old, or the wrong size can hold up the admit card. The window is your chance to replace it.
5. **Download the updated confirmation page and save it.** Screenshot it too. This is your proof of what you changed, and you will need the application number on it all the way through counselling.
6. **Tell your child the plan.** If the city changed, the travel plan changed. A child who knows the journey in advance walks into the exam calmer than one who finds out the night before.

## The dates that decide all of this

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM** | Application window closes (late window 3–6 December with ₹500 late fee) |
| **7–9 December 2026** | ✅ **Correction window — your only chance to change the exam city** |
| **11 January 2027** | Exam city announced |
| **25 January 2027** | Admit card download opens — exact centre on the card |
| **31 January 2027, 2:00 PM** | Exam day (Class 6: 150 minutes; Class 9: 180 minutes) |
| **1:30 PM on exam day** | Last entry permitted at the centre — latecomers are not admitted |

Note the gap between 9 December and 11 January: a full month in which nothing can be changed. That is why the correction window is not a formality. It is the last edit you will ever make to this form.

## If the centre is still far away

Suppose you did everything right and the allotted centre is still a long way from home. It happens — NTA allots by availability, not by your address. Then the job changes from "change the centre" to "manage the journey":

- **Book travel and stay as soon as the admit card arrives on 25 January.** Do not wait for the last week; exam towns fill up.
- **Visit the centre the day before, if you can.** A child who has seen the gate, the road and the building sleeps better than one navigating an unknown town at 1 PM on exam day.
- **Reach by 1:30 PM at the latest.** The centre opens two hours before the exam, and entry after 1:30 PM is not permitted. Aim for 1:00 PM. A missed entry is not rescheduled.
- **Carry the admit card, the self-declaration with photo and thumb impression, a valid photo ID, and one passport-size photo** for the attendance sheet. Pens are provided inside the hall — do not carry your own stationery.

The exam itself has no negative marking, so your child should attempt every question. But none of that matters if they are not in the room. The centre is the first question of the exam, and it is answered in December, not January.
