## Nine days. Not for learning — for finishing.

The JNVST 2027 Class 6 exam is on **Saturday, 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM**. That is nine days from today. If you are reading this with a knot in your stomach, here is the honest truth about the last nine days: they are not for learning new chapters. Nothing your child crams in the final week will outweigh the damage of a tired, panicked child walking into the hall on the 28th.

What the last nine days *are* for is three things: fixing the weakest section, rehearsing the paper at the exact exam time, and getting the logistics right so nothing goes wrong on the morning. This plan does exactly that, day by day.

## The paper your child is walking into

Before the plan, the paper — because the plan only makes sense once you see where the marks sit. The JNVST Class 6 paper has **80 questions for 100 marks in 2 hours**, with **no negative marking** and **1.25 marks per correct answer**. Three sections:

| Section | Questions | Marks | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — Mental Ability (20) + Environmental Studies (20) | 40 | 50 | 60 minutes |
| II — Arithmetic | 20 | 25 | 30 minutes |
| III — Language Test | 20 | 25 | 30 minutes |
| **Total** | **80** | **100** | **2 hours** |

Here is the detail parents miss, and it is the single most important fact in this article: **each section has its own qualifying minimum**. Your child must score at least **14 in Section I, 7 in Arithmetic and 7 in Language**. A child who scores 90 out of 100 but gets 5 in Language does not qualify. The total is not the target — clearing all three floors is. And the new EVS section matters more than most families realise: it is half of Section I, 20 questions on the world around your child — the environment, everyday science, the neighbourhood — and it is the section where a Class 5 child's general awareness either pays off or quietly costs 25 marks.

Seating is by **11:00 AM**, half an hour before the paper starts, and latecomers are not allowed in. A bell rings every 30 minutes, and nobody can leave before 1:30 PM. Divyang candidates get 40 minutes extra.

## The 9-day plan: 19 to 27 November

| Day | Date | Focus | Mocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 — Repair | Thu 19 Nov | Admit card check + weakest section drill (2 hours on the lowest-scoring section only) | None — fix, don't test |
| Day 2 — Repair | Fri 20 Nov | Second-weakest section drill + EVS rapid revision (everyday science, environment, maps) | 1 short section test |
| Day 3 — Repair | Sat 21 Nov | Arithmetic speed drill: 20 questions in 25 minutes, then error review | 1 timed Arithmetic set |
| Day 4 — Simulate | Sun 22 Nov | Full paper, 11:30 AM–1:30 PM, hall conditions, phone away | 1 full paper |
| Day 5 — Repair | Mon 23 Nov | Analyse Sunday's paper question by question; re-do every wrong answer | None |
| Day 6 — Simulate | Tue 24 Nov | Full paper, 11:30 AM–1:30 PM; practise the Language section last, when tired | 1 full paper |
| Day 7 — Lighten | Wed 25 Nov | Formula sheet + error notebook only; Mental Ability patterns, no new material | None |
| Day 8 — Settle | Thu 26 Nov | One short mixed set in the morning; pack the exam bag; early dinner, early sleep | None |
| Day 9 — Rest | Fri 27 Nov | No study after lunch. Admit card, ID, photos, pens laid out. Sleep by 9 PM | None |

Three rules hold the plan together. **First, the mocks happen at 11:30 AM**, the exact exam slot — the brain learns the rhythm of the paper, and the child discovers now, not on the 28th, that the Language section feels harder in the second hour. **Second, every wrong answer gets re-done the same evening**, while the mistake is still warm; a mock without analysis is just a confidence test. **Third, the last two days are deliberately light.** A child who arrives fresh with 70 marks of preparation beats a child who arrives exhausted with 90 marks of preparation. Really.

## The admit card: do this today, not on the 27th

The admit cards have been out since **28 October 2026** — if you have not downloaded your child's yet, do it before reading further. Go to the NVS admission portal linked from **navodaya.gov.in**, log in with the **registration number and the child's date of birth**, and print two copies. Then check four things against the application: the name spelling, the date of birth, the photograph, and the exam centre. If anything is wrong, contact NVS now — the 27th is too late to fix a wrong centre.

On exam morning, your child carries: the printed admit card, a photo ID (the child's Aadhaar card or the parent's residence certificate uploaded with the form), two passport-size photographs, and two black ballpoint pens. Reach the centre by **10:15–10:30 AM**; the child must be seated by 11:00 AM. Give a proper breakfast — nobody leaves the hall before 1:30 PM, and a hungry child loses concentration in the second hour exactly when the Language section needs it most.

## What to do with the nine days if your child also sits AISSEE

Many children sitting JNVST on 28 November will sit [AISSEE 2027 on 31 January](/news/aissee-2027-14-days-left-apply-2-december-deadline-2026-11-18/) as well — and the AISSEE form closes on 2 December, four days after the JNVST exam. Do not let the form steal the final week: if the AISSEE application is not submitted yet, finish it this week, in the evenings, and keep the mornings for JNVST. After 28 November, the AISSEE preparation resumes with a full two months in hand. We covered how to choose between the two schools if your child clears both in [our Navodaya vs Sainik School choice guide](/blog/navodaya-vs-sainik-school-which-to-choose-after-clearing-both/), and how families juggle all four December–January exams in [our four-exams guide](/blog/juggling-jnvst-rimc-rms-cet-aissee-2027-four-exams-one-child/).

One last thing. The JNVST result is expected by the end of March 2027 — that is the pattern, not an announced date. Between 28 November and March, your child has the AISSEE to prepare for, and you have a long wait. The nine days in front of you are the only part of this you can still control. Use them the way the plan says: repair, simulate, settle — and then let the child walk in fresh on Saturday morning.

## FAQs

**When is the JNVST 2027 Class 6 exam?**
Saturday, 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM, in a single phase for all Navodaya Vidyalayas. Your child must be seated in the exam room by 11:00 AM — latecomers are not allowed in.

**What is the JNVST 2027 Class 6 paper pattern?**
80 objective questions for 100 marks in 2 hours, with no negative marking. Section I combines Mental Ability (20 questions) and the new Environmental Studies section (20 questions) for 50 marks in 60 minutes. Arithmetic has 20 questions for 25 marks in 30 minutes, and the Language Test has 20 questions for 25 marks in 30 minutes. Each correct answer is worth 1.25 marks.

**Is there a minimum score in each section of JNVST 2027?**
Yes. A child must score at least 14 marks in Section I (Mental Ability plus EVS), 7 in Arithmetic and 7 in Language to qualify. Clearing the total is not enough — a weak section can fail an otherwise good paper, so the last nine days must protect the weakest section first.

**Is the JNVST 2027 admit card out?**
Yes. NVS released the Class 6 admit cards on 28 October 2026 on the NVS admission portal linked from navodaya.gov.in. Download it with the registration number and the child's date of birth, print two copies, and check the name, date of birth, photograph and exam centre against the application.

**What should my child carry to the JNVST exam centre?**
The printed admit card, a photo ID (the child's Aadhaar card or the parent's residence certificate uploaded with the form), two passport-size photographs and two black ballpoint pens. Reach the centre by 10:15 to 10:30 AM. Nobody can leave the hall before the paper ends at 1:30 PM.

**When will the JNVST 2027 result come?**
The result is expected by the end of March 2027, following the previous year's pattern — that date is not announced yet. If your child also sits AISSEE 2027 on 31 January, the two results arrive months apart, so plan the choice between the schools only when both are in hand.
