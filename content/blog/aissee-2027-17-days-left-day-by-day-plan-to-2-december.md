Seventeen days. That is what stands between tonight and the AISSEE 2027 form closing on 2 December at 11:50 PM. It sounds short, and it is — but only if you treat the form and the preparation as one giant job sitting on your chest. Split it up, and 17 days is plenty. Here is the split that actually works: three days for the form, eleven days of one-section-a-day revision, and three days of mocks and buffer.

A quick story first. Last year, a parent called us in a panic on the last evening of the window. The form was half-filled, the photo was the wrong size, and the payment page kept timing out on a slow connection. Everything got submitted in the end — but the child spent the whole of December revising with a knot in his stomach, because the family had spent the final week fighting a form instead of studying. The lesson is simple: the form is a three-day job. Give it three days, and the other fourteen belong to your child.

## The 17 days, at a glance

| Block | Dates | The job |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Days 1–3** | 15–17 Nov | Finish the form completely — documents, fee, confirmation page |
| **Days 4–8** | 18–22 Nov | One section a day: Maths, Language, Intelligence, GK, then weak-section repeat |
| **Days 9–14** | 23–28 Nov | Second pass: mistakes notebook, one full paper on Sunday 23 Nov, timed section drills |
| **Days 15–17** | 29 Nov–2 Dec | Final full paper, light revision, buffer for anything that slipped |

## Days 1–3: finish the form (15–17 November)

Do this first, before another page of revision. Sit together one evening — this is the family-discussion slot, use it — and get the whole form done in one sitting if you can.

- **Photo:** recent passport-size photograph, JPG, 10 KB to 200 KB. Use the same photo later on the admit card.
- **Signature and left-hand thumb impression:** JPG, 10 KB to 50 KB each.
- **Birth certificate:** issued by the competent authority, with the name and date of birth matching school records. Certificates upload as PDF, 50 KB to 300 KB.
- **Domicile certificate:** this decides the home-state quota, so check it twice.
- **Caste certificate (if SC, ST or OBC-NCL):** OBC must be non-creamy layer and in the central list.
- **Service certificate** if you are claiming the defence or ex-servicemen category, in the format the bulletin asks for.

Then the choices that cannot be undone casually. Pick the **medium of the question paper** carefully — Class 6 offers English, Hindi and regional languages, Class 9 is English only, and the medium cannot be changed after submission. Choose **four exam cities in order of preference**; NTA tries to give one of them but can allot another city, and if too few candidates choose a city, NTA may not open a centre there. Fill the name, parents' names and date of birth exactly as in the birth certificate and school records.

Pay the fee — **₹850** for General, OBC (NCL) and wards of Defence personnel and ex-servicemen, **₹700** for SC and ST — and download the **confirmation page**. The form is not complete until the fee is paid and the confirmation page is generated; money deducted from your bank account alone is not proof of payment. Only one application per candidate is allowed. If more than one form is submitted for the same class, all of them can be rejected.

If something is already wrong in a submitted form, do not panic: the **correction window runs from 7 to 9 December**, and most details and documents can be corrected there. The mobile number and email, though, cannot be changed after payment — so get those right the first time.

## Days 4–8: one section a day (18–22 November)

Now the form is out of the way, and the revision has a shape. One section a day, two focused hours on a school day:

- **Day 4 — Maths.** The heaviest section: 50 questions, 150 marks in Class 6; 50 questions, 200 marks in Class 9. Work the chapters your child gets wrong, not the ones they already enjoy.
- **Day 5 — Language.** Class 6: 25 questions, 50 marks. Class 9 English: 25 questions, 50 marks. Read one unseen passage aloud and answer it without looking back — that is the skill being tested.
- **Day 6 — Intelligence.** Class 6: 25 questions, 50 marks. Patterns, series, odd-one-out. Short, daily sets beat one long sitting.
- **Day 7 — General Knowledge.** Class 6: 25 questions, 50 marks. Class 9 splits into Science and Social Science, 25 questions and 50 marks each. Revise from the child's own notes, not a new book bought this week.
- **Day 8 — the weak section, again.** Whichever section scored lowest in the last mock gets a second day. This is the highest-value day in the whole plan.

Honestly, most guidebooks overcomplicate this part. Your child does not need a new book in the last 17 days. They need their own mistakes, revisited. Keep a mistakes notebook — one line per error, what the right answer was, and why. That notebook is worth more than any fresh chapter right now.

## Days 9–14: the second pass (23–28 November)

This is where the plan tightens. **Sunday 23 November: one full paper, exam conditions.** Class 6: 150 minutes, 2:00 to 4:30 PM if you can manage the timing. Class 9: 180 minutes, 2:00 to 5:00 PM. Phone in another room, OMR-style bubbling on paper, no breaks. Then spend the evening going through every wrong answer — not just marking them, understanding them.

The weekdays in this block are for timed drills: 25 questions in 30 minutes, one section at a time. And for the mistakes notebook, which should be getting shorter, not longer. If a topic is still wrong after two attempts, drop it. In the last fortnight, a dropped topic costs nothing; a topic that eats two evenings costs a section.

## Days 15–17: mocks, light revision, buffer (29 November–2 December)

**Sunday 30 November: the last full paper.** Same conditions as the 23rd. Compare the two scores section by section — that comparison is the real report card of this plan. Monday and Tuesday are for light revision only: the mistakes notebook, the formula sheet, one reading passage. No new topics. No all-nighters. A tired child on 31 January loses more marks than an unrevised chapter ever could.

And the buffer matters. If the form threw up a problem in days 1–3 — a certificate that needed reissue, a photo that kept failing — these three days are your safety net, not your study days. If everything went smoothly, they are rest days with light revision. Either way, 2 December at 11:50 PM is a hard stop: after that, only the late window (3–6 December, ₹500 extra) remains, and after 6 December there is no window at all.

## What comes after 2 December

Submitting the form is not the end of the calendar — it is the start of the quiet stretch. The **exam city slip comes on 11 January 2027**, the **admit card on 25 January 2027**, and the **exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027**. Keep checking the NTA AISSEE page and the registered email and SMS. The answer key and result dates have not been announced yet; they will appear on the NTA website.

## FAQs

**How many days are left to apply for AISSEE 2027?**
17 days. The regular application window closes on 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM. A late window with an additional 500-rupee fee runs from 3 to 6 December, and the correction window runs from 7 to 9 December 2026.

**What should I finish first in the last 17 days before the AISSEE deadline?**
The form itself. Gather the photo, signature, thumb impression, birth certificate, domicile and caste certificates, pay the fee and download the confirmation page within the first three days. Revision can continue after that, but a form left to the last day is the one risk you cannot recover from.

**Can I change my exam cities or medium after submitting the AISSEE form?**
The medium of the question paper cannot be changed after submission, so choose it carefully. You pick four exam cities in order of preference; NTA tries to give one of them but can allot another city. The correction window from 7 to 9 December allows corrections to most details, but not the mobile number or email after payment.

**How many hours a day should my child study in the last 17 days before the AISSEE deadline?**
Two focused hours on school days and three to four on Sundays is enough at this stage. One section a day, one full 150-minute or 180-minute paper every Sunday, and a short daily revision of mistakes beats long, tired sittings that the child starts dreading.

**What happens after the AISSEE 2027 form is submitted?**
The exam city slip comes on 11 January 2027, the admit card on 25 January 2027, and the exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027 — Class 6 from 2:00 PM to 4:30 PM and Class 9 from 2:00 PM to 5:00 PM. Keep checking the NTA AISSEE page and the registered email and SMS after applying.

**What is the AISSEE 2027 application fee?**
850 rupees for General, OBC (NCL) and wards of Defence personnel and ex-servicemen, and 700 rupees for SC and ST candidates. The fee is not refundable, and a submitted application cannot be withdrawn.
