## Your child's second family has a name

Ask a Sainik School alumnus which school he went to and you will get an answer. Ask him which house he belonged to and you will get a story. That is the first thing to understand about the house system: it is not an administrative arrangement for beds and rooms. It is the group a cadet belongs to from the day he joins until the day he passes out — the team he eats with, marches with, and competes for.

A house is a group of students first and a building second. That distinction matters, because it explains why two cadets from different houses in the same school can have such different daily experiences, and why house loyalty survives decades after school ends.

## How houses get their names

Houses usually carry names that the school wants its cadets to live up to: generals, freedom fighters, rivers and legendary figures. The names differ from school to school, which is part of the point — each school's houses tell its own story.

- **Sainik School Goalpara** has nine houses split into junior and senior groups: senior houses Udaygiri, Lohit, Lachit, Chilarai, Aniruddha and Eklavya, junior houses Bhaskar and Abhimanyu, and a girls' house, Chandraparabha. Lohit is named after the Brahmaputra, Lachit after the Ahom general Lachit Borphukan, and Eklavya after the Nishadha prince.
- **UP Sainik School, Lucknow** has houses including Tandon, Pant, Sampurnanand, Shastri, Rani Lakshmibai, Kidwai and Nehru — a roll-call of national life rather than a single theme.
- **Sainik School Kodagu** has Subroto (after Air Marshal Subroto Mukherjee), Cariappa, Katari and Menon houses, named after armed forces chiefs.

When your child says "I am in Lachit House" or "I am in Pant House," they are not naming a dormitory. They are naming a lineage they are expected to honour.

## Who runs a house

Every house has a **housemaster** — a teacher who is responsible for that house's cadets. The housemaster takes roll call in the house (the published daily routine of Sainik School Chittorgarh lists "Roll Call in respective houses by House Master" at 10:15 PM), supervises evening prep, handles discipline, and is the adult who notices first when a child is struggling, homesick or falling behind. For parents, the housemaster is usually the most important single contact in the school.

Under the housemaster sit the cadet leaders: the **house captain** and house prefects, usually senior cadets who run house activities, lead the house team in competitions and mentor younger cadets. This is where leadership training stops being a slogan — a Class 12 house captain is genuinely responsible for dozens of younger children, and the school watches how they handle it.

## The year-long contest: points, trophies, Cock House

The house system exists, in one school's own description, "mainly for the purpose of healthy competition, based around the philosophy of inclusion and team work." Houses earn points across the year, and the points add up to one title: **Cock House**, the overall winning house of the year.

The competition calendar is far wider than sport alone. A typical year includes:

| Arena | Examples of inter-house events |
|---|---|
| **Sports and physical** | Athletics meets, football, kabaddi, cross-country, obstacle courses |
| **Drill and military** | Drill competitions, NCC contests, obstacle races |
| **Cultural** | Dramatics, group song, dance, debates, quizzes |
| **Academic** | House points for class performance and academic contests |

Real results show how seriously schools take this. In October 2026, Tandon and Pant houses jointly won the inter-house dramatics competition at UP Sainik School Lucknow, with Sampurnanand and Shastri second and Rani Lakshmibai third. At Sainik School Balachadi, Shivaji House won the inter-house obstacle competition for 2025-26 in 17 minutes and 4 seconds. At Kodagu, Subroto House won the athletic meet with 99 points. At Gokuldas Sainik School, Pandey House took the kabaddi championship with 20 points. These are not token events — they are the school year, measured in trophies.

## What the house means for a new cadet

For a Class 6 child arriving at a boarding school for the first time, the house does three things no timetable can.

**First, it shrinks the school.** A school of hundreds becomes a house of dozens. The child knows every face in the house within a week, and every senior in the house knows the child's name.

**Second, it gives instant belonging.** From the first inter-house match, the child has a side to cheer for and a side that cheers for them — the strongest counterweight to first-term homesickness.

**Third, it teaches losing as a group.** Houses lose as often as they win. A child who has cried over a lost house football final and then lined up for the next event has learned something no classroom teaches: that your team matters more than your mood.

## What parents should know — and ask

- **Allotment varies.** Some schools split houses into junior (Classes 6–7) and senior (Classes 8–12) groups, as Goalpara does; others mix ages. Ask how your child's school allots houses, and whether siblings are placed together.
- **House points are not exam marks.** A house can win Cock House without topping academics, because points span sport, culture, drill and discipline. If your child is not the most academic cadet, the house still has a place where they can be the reason their house wins.
- **The housemaster is your contact.** At admission, find out who your child's housemaster is and how the school prefers parents to reach them. That one relationship will matter more than any other school contact in the first year.
- **Rivalry is friendly by design.** Houses compete hard and celebrate hard, but the system is built so that every cadet, whatever their talent, contributes points somewhere. Ask the school how it includes quieter children — the answer tells you a lot about the house culture.

## FAQs

**What is a house in a Sainik School?**

A house is a cadet's permanent group within the school, not just a building. It is the team a cadet lives with, competes for and belongs to for the whole school journey, with its own housemaster, captains and traditions.

**Are houses named after real people?**

Often, yes. Houses commonly carry the names of national heroes, generals and freedom fighters — for example, UP Sainik School Lucknow has Tandon, Pant, Sampurnanand, Shastri, Rani Lakshmibai, Kidwai and Nehru houses, while Sainik School Goalpara has houses named after Lachit, Lohit and Eklavya.

**What does a housemaster actually do?**

The housemaster is the adult in charge of a house: they take roll call, supervise prep and discipline, look after cadets' welfare, and are usually the first person parents contact about their child's daily life in the house.

**What is Cock House?**

Cock House is the title for the overall winning house of the year. Houses earn points across academics, sports, drill, cultural events and discipline through the year, and the house with the most points at the end wins the title.

**Can my child choose their house?**

Generally no — the school allots houses, and siblings are often placed together, but allotment rules vary by school. Ask the school directly at admission time rather than assuming a choice is possible.

**Do houses compete against each other every week?**

Competition runs through the year rather than every week: inter-house events are spread across the calendar, covering athletics, team games, drill, obstacle courses, debates, dramatics and cultural contests, with points adding up to the Cock House race.
