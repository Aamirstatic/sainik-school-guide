## Maharashtra just admitted what parents already knew

On 20 November 2026, Maharashtra's Chief Minister ordered an overhaul of the state's 38 sainik schools — and the reason was refreshingly honest. A government review had found that several schools were not meeting essential standards, that students were not getting adequate military training, and that the schools had "a meagre share" of students reaching the NDA. [Read the full story here.](/news/maharashtra-sainik-school-overhaul-fadnavis-grading-nda-2026-11-21/)

Here is the part that matters to you. The state will now grade every school, every year, on five things: academic outcomes, administrative health, military-training results, and — above all — how many students clear the NDA. Good schools get rewards. Weak schools get action.

You do not have to wait for a government grade sheet to do the same check yourself. The AISSEE 2027 form closes on 2 December, the exam is on 31 January 2027, and after the result you will fill school preferences in AISSAC counselling. That preference order is where most parents make their biggest mistake: they pick names they have heard of, not schools they have actually judged. Here are the five markers, and how to check each one.

## Marker 1: The NDA pipeline — the number that cannot be faked

A sainik school has one job above all others: to send young people into the armed forces as officers. Everything else is secondary. So the first question is simple — how many of this school's cadets made it to the NDA merit list in the last three years?

Look at what good looks like. [Sainik School Kunjpura put 10 cadets on the NDA-157 merit list, with the best rank at AIR 136.](/blog/aissee-2027-gk-current-affairs-october-november-2026-10-stories/) Punglwa produced AIR 61 in the same list. And [Haryana's sainik schools sent 35 women cadets to the NDA](/news/aissee-2027-haryana-35-women-nda-cadets-420-girls-seats-2026-11-20/) — a number that tells you the pipeline works for girls too, which matters now that 420 girls' seats exist across the system.

Ask for three years, not one. A single good year can be luck or one brilliant batch. Three steady years is a system. If a school cannot — or will not — tell you its last three years of NDA selections, treat that silence as an answer.

## Marker 2: Board results, not brochure adjectives

Every school's brochure says "excellent academic record". The board results say whether it is true. Ask for the Class 10 and Class 12 pass percentages and the number of distinctions over the last three years. A school that is proud of its results publishes them; a school that hides them usually has a reason.

Do not be impressed by one topper's photograph. One child scoring 98% tells you about that child. A whole batch averaging well tells you about the school.

## Marker 3: Real military training, not a parade once a year

This is the marker Maharashtra's own review caught schools failing at — and it is the hardest one to judge from a distance, so it is the one most parents skip. Do not skip it.

Ask: who conducts the physical training, and how often? Is there a qualified PT staff and an NCC unit? Do cadets go on camps, treks and obstacle courses through the year, or does the "military" part of the school appear only on annual day? A child who spends six years in a sainik school should come out fitter, more disciplined and more confident than they went in. That does not happen from a morning assembly alone.

If you can visit, watch one morning PT session. Ten minutes of watching tells you more than ten pages of brochure.

## Marker 4: Where your child will actually live

Your child will sleep, eat and fall ill in this school for six years. So check the hostel, the mess and the medical room with the same seriousness you check the NDA numbers.

How many children share a room? Is the mess food cooked in-house, and can you see the kitchen? Is there a medical room with a resident nurse, and a hospital nearby for anything serious? How many teachers live on campus? A school where teachers live on campus is a school where someone notices, at 10 PM, that a child is unwell or unhappy.

Here is a small story I have seen repeat itself. A parent chose a famous school two states away, and the child spent the first year homesick and underweight because the food did not suit him and no one noticed. The school's NDA numbers were excellent. The child left in Class 8. A great school on paper is not a great school for your child if the daily living fails.

## Marker 5: Transparency — the test that costs nothing

The last marker is the simplest, and you can test it from your phone today. Call the school office and ask three questions: the fee structure for the year, the number of seats in Class 6, and the last three years of NDA selections. Then email the same questions.

A good school answers clearly, in writing, within a few days. A school that dodges, delays or tells you to "come and see" is telling you how it will behave when you have a real problem later — a fee dispute, a medical emergency, a complaint. Transparency is not a courtesy. It is a character test.

## The scorecard: judge every school the same way

Do this for every school on your shortlist, before the result comes out. Score each marker from 1 to 3 — 1 is weak, 2 is acceptable, 3 is strong.

| Marker | What good looks like | Red flag | Where to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| NDA pipeline | Cadets on the merit list most years, over 3 years | No figures shared, or one good year only | School website, merit-list news, alumni |
| Board results | Steady pass % and distinctions, 3 years | Only a topper's photo, no batch data | School website, CBSE results |
| Military training | Daily PT, NCC, camps, qualified staff | Parade-only; no camps or NCC | Visit a morning PT session |
| Living conditions | Sensible room sharing, in-house mess, medical room, resident teachers | Overcrowded dorms, outsourced food, no nurse | Visit the hostel and mess |
| Transparency | Fees, seats and results shared in writing, promptly | Dodged calls, "come and see" | One phone call and one email |

A school scoring 12 or more out of 15 is a strong pick. A school scoring 9 or below needs a very good reason to stay on your list — and "everyone has heard of it" is not a good reason.

## How this connects to your choice-filling

When AISSAC counselling opens after the result, you will submit your schools in preference order, and there are around six rounds. Two facts from the official rules should shape that order.

First, **67% of seats in the 33 central Sainik Schools are reserved for home-state candidates**. Your child competes in a much smaller pool at home. A strong home-state school usually beats a famous out-of-state school, because the famous one is fought over in the 33% other-states pool.

Second, the preference order is not something to improvise on result day. Sit with the scorecard now, while there are still 71 days to the exam, and rank your schools by the five markers — not by reputation, not by distance, and not by what a neighbour's child did.

Maharashtra's grading system will eventually do this job for its 38 state schools. For the 33 central schools — and for every other state — the job is yours. The good news is that it takes one phone call, one email, and one honest visit per school. That is a small price for a six-year decision.

## FAQs

**What is the best way to judge a Sainik School before choice-filling?**

Look at five things: NDA and officer selections over the last three years, board exam results, the quality of military training, living conditions in the hostel and mess, and how transparent the school is about fees and admissions. A school that is strong on all five is a safe pick; a school that is weak on three or more is a risk, however famous its name.

**How many NDA selections should a good Sainik School produce each year?**

There is no official minimum, and small schools will naturally produce fewer. What matters is the trend: a school that puts cadets on the NDA merit list most years, like Kunjpura with 10 cadets in NDA-157, is doing its job. Ask for the last three years, not just the best year.

**Does the Maharashtra grading system affect the 33 central Sainik Schools?**

No. The grading covers Maharashtra's 38 state-run sainik schools. The 33 central Sainik Schools, including Satara and Chandrapur in Maharashtra, admit through AISSEE and are run by the Sainik Schools Society, not the state government.

**Should I pick my home-state school or a famous school in another state?**

Home state usually wins: 67 percent of seats in the 33 central schools are reserved for home-state candidates, so your child competes in a smaller pool. Pick a famous out-of-state school only if it is clearly stronger on the five markers and you are comfortable with the 33 percent other-states competition.

**When do parents fill school preferences in AISSAC counselling?**

After the AISSEE result, during e-counselling on the AISSAC portal. There are around six rounds, and the preference order you submit decides which school your child is offered. The order cannot be casually changed later, so judge schools before the result, not after.

**Where can I check a school's real record instead of its brochure?**

Start with the school's own website for NDA selections and board results, then cross-check with news reports of merit lists, and talk to parents whose children study there. If a school will not share three years of NDA selections or board results, treat that silence as an answer.
