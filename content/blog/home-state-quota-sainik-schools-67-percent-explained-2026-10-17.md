## The 67/33 rule, stated plainly

In the Sainik Schools Society schools (the original network — Satara, Purulia, Amaravathinagar, Korukonda and the rest), seats are divided by the candidate's state of domicile relative to the school (CONFIRMED — AISSEE information bulletin, Careers360):

- **67% — Home State quota:** candidates whose domicile is the same state or UT where the school is located.
- **33% — Other States quota:** candidates domiciled anywhere else in India.

"Home state" means **the school's state**, not where the exam centre is, and domicile is proved by the **domicile certificate** of that state/UT — the same document the AISSEE form lists among required uploads (CONFIRMED — Careers360).

The cleanest real-world illustration came up this year with Sainik School Korukonda: the school has moved to Andhra Pradesh, and **students from Telangana are no longer eligible for its 67% home-state quota — they now apply under the 33% other-states pool** (CONFIRMED — Careers360, opened today). One administrative line, and an entire state's candidates shifted pools overnight.

## Worked example 1: how the pools fill up

Imagine a school with 120 Class VI vacancies this year. This is an **illustration of the official formula**, not a published seat matrix — the real matrix comes out with the counselling schedule each year.

| Pool | Calculation | Seats (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Home State | 67% of 120 | 80 |
| Other States | 33% of 120 | 40 |

Now each pool is further divided by category (CONFIRMED — bulletin):

- **SC: 15%** → 12 home / 6 other
- **ST: 7.5%** → 6 home / 3 other
- **OBC-NCL: 27%** → ~22 home / ~11 other
- Of what remains, **25% for wards of defence personnel** (serving and retired)

Run it for the home pool: 80 − 12 − 6 − 22 = 40 remaining; 25% of 40 = 10 seats for defence wards. The same arithmetic repeats inside the 40-seat other-states pool. Notice what this means: a General-category, non-defence child from the home state is competing for a modest slice of those 80 seats — while the same child from another state is competing for an even thinner slice of 40.

## Worked example 2: two children, one family, different pools

Take a boy domiciled in West Bengal:

- **Sainik School Purulia:** home-state pool. He competes within the 67%.
- **Sainik School Satara (Maharashtra):** other-states pool. He competes within the 33%.

Now a girl domiciled in Maharashtra:

- **Sainik School Satara:** home-state pool — and girls admissions flow through the same pools, with the **girls' quota of 10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more** (CONFIRMED — bulletin). The girls' quota does not create a third pool; it operates inside these two.

The practical lesson: when your family fills e-counselling choices, rank your home-state school first unless you have a specific reason not to. The 67% pool is where your child is statistically strongest. The 33% pool is real and winnable — thousands of children do get in every year — but it is where the all-India competition concentrates.

## What parents consistently get wrong

**"Admission is to Sainik Schools in general."** No. You qualify through AISSEE, then fill **school choices in e-counselling**, and the home-state rule applies **school by school** (CONFIRMED — Careers360: choice-filling by priority, seats filled by merit and choices).

**"Defence wards jump the whole queue."** The defence reservation is **25% of the seats remaining after** SC, ST and OBC-NCL reservations — it sits inside the pools, not above them (CONFIRMED — bulletin).

**"The same rule applies to New Sainik Schools."** The 67/33 scheme is the Society's own scheme for its schools. **New Sainik Schools** — the approved partnership schools — follow their own approved admission norms (EXPECTATION based on scheme structure — always verify against the specific school's own admission notice before assuming the split applies).

**"Domicile can be sorted out later."** The domicile certificate is an upload on the application form itself. If your family has moved states recently, **arrange the certificate in the state you are claiming now** — before the form window, not during counselling. ## Your home-state checklist

1. **Confirm your domicile state** and that the certificate is in hand, with the correct address and issuing authority.
2. **Identify your true home-state school** — the one in your state — and understand its 67% pool is your primary battlefield.
3. **Read the counselling seat matrix** when it publishes: actual vacancies per school per pool per category, which is where the illustrative numbers above get replaced by real ones.
4. **For girls**, note the 10%-or-10-seats rule and check the girls' seat count for your target school in the matrix.
5. **Defence families**: the 25%-of-remainder quota applies within each pool — claim it in the right category on the form.

## FAQs

**What is the home state quota in Sainik Schools?**

67% of seats in the Sainik Schools Society schools are reserved for candidates domiciled in the state or UT where the school stands; 33% go to candidates from other states and UTs.

**Does the 67% home-state rule apply to girls as well?**

Yes. Girls compete within the same two pools according to their domicile, with the girls' quota (10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more) operating inside the overall matrix.

**Can I apply to a Sainik School in another state?**

Yes — the 33% other-states quota exists precisely for this. But competition there is all-India and stiffer, so prioritise your home-state school in choice-filling unless you have a strong reason otherwise.

**Does the home-state quota apply to New Sainik Schools?**

The 67/33 rule is the Society's scheme for its own schools. New Sainik Schools follow their own approved admission norms — verify on that school's own notice.

**How do I prove my home-state domicile for AISSEE?**

With a domicile or residence certificate of the state or UT, issued by the competent authority. It is among the documents the AISSEE form asks you to upload, so arrange it before the window opens.

**Do defence wards get a separate quota on top?**

Yes — 25% of the seats remaining after the SC, ST and OBC-NCL reservations are reserved for wards of serving and retired defence personnel, computed separately inside each pool.
