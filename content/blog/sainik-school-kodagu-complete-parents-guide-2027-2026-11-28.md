## The school the Air Force chief just praised — and why it matters

Yesterday, 27 November 2026, Air Marshal S Shrinivas — the Air Officer Commanding-in-Chief of the Indian Air Force's Training Command — walked into Sainik School Kodagu, laid a wreath at its War Memorial, took a Guard of Honour from its cadets, and then said something worth a parent's attention. He praised the school for a "historic achievement": **24 cadets qualified the UPSC NDA/NA written examination in the academic year 2026-27**.

That is the number to hold in your head while you read this guide. Kodagu is not the biggest Sainik School, and it is not the oldest. But right now, in the year your child would apply, it is producing NDA written qualifiers at a rate most schools would envy — and the Air Force's training chief came in person to say so. He also inaugurated the school's newly renovated Horse Stable, and praised its new SHIKHAR NDA Classroom and VISMAYA Robotics Lab.

This is the 23rd guide in our parents' guide series, covering every one of the 33 established Sainik Schools. Here is Kodagu, honestly, with the numbers checked.

## A school built in the land of two Field Marshals

Kodagu — Coorg to most of India — is not an obvious place to put a military school until you know its history. The district is the birthplace of the river Cauvery, and it has given the Indian Armed Forces two of its greatest names: **Field Marshal K. M. Cariappa**, India's first Commander-in-Chief, and **General K. S. Thimmaiah**, who led the Army and later commanded the UN force in Korea. Both were Kodavas. The idea of a Sainik School here was, in the school's own words, that Kodagu "has contributed brave soldiers to the Armed Forces many of whom rose to the rank of Brigadiers, Generals and even India's Field Marshal".

The school was established on **18 October 2007**, the 22nd Sainik School in the country and the second in Karnataka after Bijapur. The first academic session began in temporary buildings with **64 cadets, four teachers and two administrative staff**. The founder Principal was Lt Col Prakash Narahari. The campus at Kudige village, about 6 km from Kushalnagar, now covers about 66 acres of hillside — an academic block with laboratories for Biology, Computer Science, Chemistry, English communication and Mathematics, a mess that seats a thousand, two football fields, three basketball courts and two volleyball courts, a swimming pool, and horse riding.

That last detail is not a brochure line. Kodagu is one of the few Sainik Schools where horse riding is part of the routine — it is in the morning PT rotation, and horse riding is charged at ₹2,000 a year from Class VIII. Yesterday the Air Marshal inaugurated the newly renovated Horse Stable. If your child has never sat on a horse, that is fine; nobody expects them to have. But it tells you something about the school's character: it takes the cavalry tradition seriously, not decoratively.

The home-state boys' seats split by category as SC 8, ST 4, OBC-NCL 15, Defence 6 and General 22. The 67% home-state quota is the whole story here: 60 of the 90 Class 6 seats are reserved for Karnataka-domiciled children, competing only against each other. A child from Bengaluru or Mysuru with a Karnataka domicile is playing a very different game from a child applying from Tamil Nadu or Maharashtra.

Two things to note. First, **there are no Class 9 seats for girls this year** — the 20 Class 9 places are all for boys. Girls' entry to Kodagu is through Class 6 only, with 10 seats. Second, the bulletin lists these vacancies as tentative. The bulletin itself insists: list Kodagu as a choice even if no vacancy is shown for your category. Seats open up when cadets leave, and they are redistributed by the reservation rules. Across the 33 established Sainik Schools there are 3,160 Class 6 seats this year — Kodagu's 90 sit in the middle of the pack. A few things to note before you budget. First, this is the school's own fee document for **2026-27 — the latest year it has published** — and the charges are marked "subject to revision". Fees rise about 10% a year under Sainik Schools Society rules, so the 2027-28 figure will be higher; confirm it from the school before paying. Second, the fee is paid in **three instalments** for a new Class 6 admission: ₹1,31,141 first, then ₹42,972 and ₹42,972, paid online through Canara Easy Fee or the school website. Third, horse riding (₹2,000) is charged only from Class VIII, so it is not in the Class 6 bill.

For context, Kodagu sits above the middle of the pack. Goalpara, the cheapest of the 33, charges ₹1,77,560 for 2026-27; older schools like Purulia are above ₹2 lakh. Kodagu's ₹2,17,085 will have risen by the time your child joins, but the order of magnitude is right: budget roughly ₹2.2–2.4 lakh for the first year, all in.

## What the Air Marshal's visit tells you about the school

A VIP visit is not, by itself, a reason to choose a school. But what the visitor chose to praise is. Air Marshal Shrinivas singled out four things on 27 November, and each one answers a question parents actually ask:

- **The NDA pipeline is working.** 24 cadets qualified the UPSC NDA/NA written examination in 2026-27. The school runs a dedicated **SHIKHAR NDA Classroom**, and SSB-style training — cross country, obstacle course, inter-house literary activities — starts from Class 6 itself.
- **The academics are modernising.** The **VISMAYA Robotics Lab** and the renovated Multi Gym were both praised, and the academic block's laboratories were described as transformed. This is not a school coasting on its 2007 founding.
- **Sport is a strength, not an afterthought.** Kodagu won the overall championship at the All India Sainik Schools Intra Group Football Tournament and the Kodagian Football Tournament, and finished runners-up at the All India Sainik Schools Inter Group Football Tournament (Pre-Subroto Cup) 2026.
- **The campus keeps growing.** The renovated Horse Stable inaugurated yesterday joins a campus the school describes as spread over 66 acres, with a mess that seats a thousand.

The school's own motto — *Veeratha, Drudhatha, Praamanikatha*: be thou valiant, be thou determined, be thou honest — is carved into its crest alongside the Ganda Berunda, the legendary two-headed bird of Karnataka, and the symbols of all three services. It is a good motto to test a school against, and on yesterday's evidence, Kodagu is living up to it.

## How to apply — and the questions to ask

Admission is only through AISSEE. For 2027-28, apply online at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026** (11:50 PM), with a late-fee window of 3–6 December (₹500 extra). Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8. The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. The application fee is ₹850 (General/OBC-NCL/Defence) or ₹700 (SC/ST). There is no direct admission and no management quota — anyone offering a seat for money is running a scam.

Before you list Kodagu as a preference, ask yourself three honest questions:

1. **Is your child ready for the hills?** Kodagu is 90 km from Mysuru, in the Western Ghats. The nearest town, Kushalnagar, is 6 km away. That distance is part of the school's charm — clean air, no distractions — and part of its cost: home visits take planning, and the first term away from home will feel longer than it does at a school near a city.
2. **Can you afford ₹2.2 lakh a year, rising?** The fee is above the Sainik School average, and it rises roughly 10% a year. Scholarships exist but are adjusted the following year — do not plan on them reducing the first bill.
3. **Does your child actually want the uniform?** The 5 AM reveille, the PT, the NCC, the horse riding — none of it works for a child who was pushed into it. The 24 NDA qualifiers this year were children who wanted to be there. If yours does, Kodagu is a strong choice: a mid-size school, a real NDA pipeline, and a campus that the Air Force's own training chief praised yesterday.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Kodagu have for 2027-28?**

Kodagu has 90 Class 6 seats — 80 for boys (55 Karnataka domicile and 25 other states) and 10 for girls (5 Karnataka and 5 other states) — and 20 Class 9 seats, all for boys (13 Karnataka and 7 other states). The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Kodagu founded?**

It was established on 18 October 2007 and is the 22nd Sainik School in India and the second in Karnataka. The first academic session began with 64 cadets, four teachers and two administrative staff in temporary buildings at Kudige village, about 6 km from Kushalnagar.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Kodagu?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8. There is no direct admission or management quota.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Kodagu?**

The school's own fee document for 2026-27 — the latest it has published — puts a new Class 6 cadet's first year at Rs 2,17,085 (Rs 2,15,585 for SC/ST), including tuition of Rs 1,28,615 and dietary charges of Rs 57,820. Fees rise every year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure from the school before paying.

**Does Sainik School Kodagu admit girls?**

Yes — for 2027-28 there are 10 girls' seats in Class 6 (5 Karnataka and 5 other states). There are no girls' seats in Class 9 this year. Girls have 10 percent of the Class 6 vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, across the 33 Sainik Schools.

**How strong is Sainik School Kodagu's NDA record?**

Strong and current: 24 cadets qualified the UPSC NDA/NA written examination in the academic year 2026-27, a figure the visiting Air Marshal publicly praised on 27 November 2026. The school also runs a dedicated SHIKHAR NDA Classroom and gives SSB-style training from Class 6 itself.
