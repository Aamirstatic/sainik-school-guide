## What TES actually is

Most Class 12 students who want to wear the olive green think the only road runs through the NDA written exam. There is a second road, and it suits a specific kind of student: the **10+2 Technical Entry Scheme (TES)**.

TES lets you join the Army as an officer **straight after Class 12** — no UPSC written exam. The Army shortlists you on your **JEE Main rank**, puts you through the standard 5-day SSB interview and medicals, and then trains you for four years: three years of engineering at a Cadet Training Wing, one year at the Indian Military Academy, Dehradun. You walk out as a **Lieutenant with a Permanent Commission and a B.Tech degree**.

Right now, this is not theory. The **TES-57 notification for the July 2027 course is live**: 90 provisional vacancies, applications open **14 October to 12 November 2026 (12:00 noon)** on the official Army recruitment portal. If your son passed Class 12 this year with PCM and appeared in JEE Main 2026, the window is open today.

## The 4-year training, phase by phase

This is where TES quietly beats the standard graduate route. You are not studying engineering in a civilian college and hoping to clear the SSB later — you are in uniform from day one.

| Phase | Duration | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase I | 3 years | Cadet Training Wing — CME Pune, MCTE Mhow or MCEME Secunderabad | B.Tech engineering + military training (no stipend) |
| Phase II | 1 year | Indian Military Academy, Dehradun | Post-commissioning training; stipend ₹56,100/month |

The degree depends on the wing allotted: **Civil/Mechanical at CME Pune, Telecommunication and IT (dual degree) at MCTE Mhow, Electronics and Mechanical at MCEME Secunderabad**. After commissioning, pay is Level 10 (₹56,100–₹1,77,500) plus Military Service Pay.

There is one number parents should see before anyone celebrates: on arrival for training, cadets are expected to run **2.4 km in 10 minutes 30 seconds**, do 40 push-ups, 6 pull-ups and 30 sit-ups, plus squats, lunges, and basic swimming. TES does not test this at the SSB — it assumes you will be fit on day one. Read that sentence twice if your son's fitness has been "I'll start tomorrow" since Class 10.

## Why Sainik School cadets fit this entry unusually well

Let me be straight first: **the Army publishes no school-wise breakup of TES selections**, so anyone quoting a percentage of TES seats going to Sainik School students is inventing it. What I can say, from years of watching cadets through SSBs, is that TES's filters map onto Sainik School daily life almost point for point:

- **The 60% PCM bar** is unremarkable for a Sainik School student who survived the school's internal academic rhythm — the school selects for studious discipline at 11, and PCM is exactly where that discipline pays.
- **The JEE Main requirement** rewards schools where students take the engineering track seriously in Classes 11–12 rather than treating it as a backup plan.
- **The SSB's group tasks** (the GTO outdoor series, the progressive group tasks, command tasks) are the exact shape of life in a house system where you lead teams of juniors by Class 10 — obstacle-course familiarity, quick decision-making under shouting seniors, and team voice are not new to them.
- **The fitness gate** is where a day scholar stumbles and a Sainik School boy barely notices. PT at dawn, games every evening, drill, cross-country runs — six years of that is not "preparation," it is simply what your body has been doing.
- **Training-life tolerance** matters more than people admit. Boys drop out of academy training because they miss home, cannot handle regimented days, or clash with the hierarchy. A Sainik School cadet has lived that routine since age 11. The shock absorber is already built.

None of this guarantees selection — the SSB rejects plenty of Sainik School boys too, usually on personality projection (too rigid, too coached) or weak English communication. But the structural fit is real: **TES is, in many ways, the entry Sainik School was unknowingly training you for.**

## TES vs NDA: which one to chase?

If both doors are open, the choice is practical, not emotional:

- **NDA** gives you a tri-services option (Army, Navy, Air Force), a full B.Sc./B.A. plus military training at Khadakwasla, and the classic written-exam route. It suits students who are strong test-takers and want the wider career menu.
- **TES** skips the written exam entirely and hands you an engineering degree in uniform. It suits strong PCM students who appeared in JEE Main anyway — and crucially, it runs **two courses a year** (January and July), so a missed NDA attempt does not mean a wasted year.

A smart Class 12 PCM student applies to both. The exams do not clash, the SSB experience from one sharpens you for the other, and TES-57's deadline (12 November 2026) is arriving weeks before most NDA cycles peak.

## FAQs

**What is the TES entry after Class 12?**
The 10+2 Technical Entry Scheme (TES) is the Indian Army's direct officer entry for unmarried male Class 12 students with Physics, Chemistry and Maths. There is no written exam; shortlisting is on JEE Main rank, followed by a 5-day SSB interview and medicals, then 4 years of engineering training leading to a Permanent Commission as Lieutenant.

**What are the TES 57 dates and eligibility?**
TES-57 (July 2027 course) opened applications on 14 October 2026 and closes on 12 November 2026 at 12 noon. Candidates need 60 percent aggregate in PCM and must have appeared in JEE Main 2026, and must be 16.5 to 19.5 years old as on 1 July 2027.

**Is there any application fee for TES?**
No. The Indian Army charges no application fee for the Technical Entry Scheme, for any category.

**What engineering degree do TES cadets get?**
After 3 years at a Cadet Training Wing, cadets earn a B.Tech — Civil and Mechanical from CME Pune, Telecommunication and IT (dual degree) from MCTE Mhow, or Electronics and Mechanical from MCEME Secunderabad — then one year at IMA Dehradun before commissioning.

**Do Sainik School students really do better in TES?**
No published data breaks TES selections down by school type, so no honest figure exists. But the entry's filters — daily physical fitness, group-task behaviour in the SSB, and comfort with regimented training life — are exactly what Sainik School routines build over six or seven years, which is a structural advantage, not a guarantee.

**Can girls apply for TES?**
No. The 10+2 Technical Entry Scheme is open only to unmarried male candidates.
