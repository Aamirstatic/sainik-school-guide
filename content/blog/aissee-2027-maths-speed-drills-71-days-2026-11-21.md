## Maths is half the paper. Most children lose it to the clock, not to the sums

Maths carries **150 of the 300 marks** in the AISSEE Class 6 paper — 50 questions, half the total. And here's the thing nobody tells parents: when a child scores low in maths, it is rarely because they couldn't do the sums. It is because they ran out of time, or spent four minutes on one stubborn question and never reached the five easy ones after it.

The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027** — 71 days from today. That is enough time to rebuild speed properly, but only if you train speed the way you train anything else: in short, regular, timed bursts. Not by doing more sums. By doing sums faster, on purpose.

Last year, a parent told us his son finished only 34 of the 50 maths questions in a mock — and got 30 of those 34 right. The knowledge was there. The clock ate the other 16. This article is for that child.

## The six drills

**1. The 10-minute mental maths warm-up, every single day.** Before any study session, ten minutes of pure mental arithmetic: tables to 20, squares to 20, two-digit addition and subtraction, quick division. No paper, no pen — say the answer out loud or write just the answer. Speed in the paper starts here, because every written calculation you skip is seconds saved. Do it at the same time daily so it becomes a habit, not a chore.

**2. Timed sets of 10, not untimed sets of 50.** Give your child 10 maths questions and 12 minutes. That's it. Short, sharp, and over quickly — which is exactly why children don't dread it. After each set, check two numbers: how many right, and how long the slowest question took. The goal is not 10 out of 10 on day one. The goal is that the same set takes less time in week three than in week one.

**3. The two-pass rule — easy first, stubborn later.** Train this until it is automatic: first pass, answer everything you can do in under a minute and circle the rest. Second pass, return to the circled ones with the time you saved. Children who fight one hard question for five minutes lose the easy marks sitting two questions later. And remember — there is **no negative marking**, so in the final minutes, every unanswered question still gets an attempt.

**4. Bubble in blocks, not one by one.** This is the cheapest speed gain in the whole paper. Bubbling after every question means moving hand, eyes and attention between the question paper and the OMR sheet 125 times. Instead: solve five questions, then bubble all five together. Or ten, if your child is steady. Practise it in every mock so it feels natural on exam day — and always double-check the question number before bubbling a block.

**5. The error log — one line per mistake.** After every timed set, your child writes one line per wrong answer: the question type, and what went wrong — "rushed", "misread", "wrong formula", "calculation slip". Within two weeks a pattern appears, and it is almost always the same two or three error types. Fixing those is worth more than fifty new questions, because the same mistakes repeat in every mock.

**6. The Sunday full-paper simulation.** Once a week, the full 150-minute paper, phone in another room, OMR sheet, proper bubbling, no breaks. This is where the first five drills come together — and where your child learns the one thing no drill can teach: what 150 minutes actually feels like. [Our Sunday readiness audit](/blog/aissee-2027-one-sunday-readiness-audit-150-minute-check-72-days-left/) explains how to run this check properly. If you want the minute-by-minute split of those 150 minutes, [our maths time-budget guide](/blog/aissee-2027-maths-50-question-time-budget-class-6-2026-11-20/) lays it out section by section.

## The 10-week speed plan

Seventy-one days is ten weekends. Here's how the drills stack up:

| Weeks | Focus | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 (21 Nov – 4 Dec) | Drills 1 and 2 only | Mental maths becomes automatic; timed 10-question sets feel normal |
| 3–4 (5 – 18 Dec) | Add drill 3 (two-pass) | Your child stops getting stuck; attempted questions go up |
| 5–6 (19 Dec – 1 Jan) | Add drills 4 and 5 | Bubbling in blocks; the error log shows the real weak spots |
| 7–8 (2 – 15 Jan) | Add drill 6 (Sunday paper) | Full-paper stamina; the clock stops being scary |
| 9–10 (16 – 31 Jan) | Taper | Short drills only, no new topics; [the 30-day January plan](/blog/aissee-2027-30-day-revision-plan-january-calendar-2026-11-19/) takes over |

Notice what is missing from this plan: more hours. The drills add about 25 minutes a day. Speed is not built by studying longer — it is built by studying faster, in short bursts, every day. Ten exhausted hours beat nobody; three sharp hours beat ten distracted ones.

## What not to do

Don't buy a new guidebook for speed. The questions your child already has are enough — speed comes from repeating familiar types faster, not from new material. Don't time every single thing; if the stopwatch is always running, the child starts fearing the clock instead of using it. And don't compare your child's speed with a cousin's or a neighbour's. Some children are naturally fast and careless; some are slow and accurate. The goal is not to be the fastest child in the room — it is to finish all 50 maths questions with time to check the bubbling.

## FAQs

**How many maths questions are in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
50 questions for 150 marks — half the paper's marks. The whole paper is 125 questions for 300 marks in 150 minutes, with no negative marking.

**How much time should my child spend on maths in the AISSEE exam?**
Around 60 minutes for the 50 maths questions works for most children — roughly 70 seconds per question on average, with easy questions faster and hard ones slower. Our time-budget guide breaks down the full 150 minutes section by section.

**Can speed in maths really be improved in 71 days?**
Yes — speed is mostly a trained habit, not talent. Daily 10-minute mental-maths drills and weekly timed sets typically show a clear difference within three to four weeks, because the same question types repeat.

**Should my child skip hard maths questions or attempt them?**
There is no negative marking, so every question should eventually be attempted. But attempt in two passes: answer the easy ones first, mark the hard ones, and come back to them only after the rest of the paper is done.

**Does OMR bubbling really waste time in AISSEE?**
Yes — children who bubble after every question lose several minutes to hand movement and re-finding their place. Bubbling in blocks of five or ten questions is faster and causes fewer marking errors.

**What is the qualifying mark for maths in AISSEE Class 6?**
A candidate must score at least 25 percent in each section and 40 percent overall — 25 percent of 150 marks in maths means at least 37.5 marks in the section. The 25 percent rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates.
