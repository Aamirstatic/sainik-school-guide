The income certificate is the quietest document in the AISSEE file — nobody asks about it at the form-filling stage, and then suddenly, in February, a scholarship form demands it and the tehsil office quotes three weeks. A father called us last year in exactly this situation: his son had cleared the written exam, a state defence-welfare scholarship was on the table, and the income certificate took 24 days — arriving two days after the scheme's document deadline. The scholarship was lost over paperwork, not marks.

This article is the complete, honest picture: what the income certificate is, who actually asks for it in the Sainik School journey, how to apply, how long it takes, and how long it stays valid.

## What the income certificate actually is

An income certificate is an official document issued by your state's revenue authority — usually the tehsildar or SDM — that states your family's annual income. It is not a tax document and it is not a salary slip; it is the government's own attestation of what your household earns, based on the proof you submit and the local verification the office conducts.

Why does it matter for a Sainik School family? Because several layers of the system are means-tested:

- **State scholarship schemes** for students in residential schools often set an annual family-income ceiling. Below the ceiling, the child qualifies; above it, not.
- **Fee concessions and remissions** at some Sainik Schools and in state-run defence welfare schemes reference family income.
- **Defence welfare schemes** for wards of serving personnel, ex-servicemen, and in some cases martyrs' families sometimes ask for income proof alongside service records.

Two honest caveats. First, the income certificate is **not** part of the AISSEE application form — NTA does not ask for it at the exam stage, and not having one will not block your child from sitting the exam. Second, scheme names, income ceilings, and benefit amounts change year to year and state to state. Any website quoting you a specific ceiling as a universal fact is guessing. The right move is to have the document ready and read the specific scheme notification when it comes.

## When in the journey you will need it

The income certificate is rarely urgent in October — which is exactly why October is when you should get it. Here is where it typically surfaces:

| Stage | When | How the certificate is used |
|---|---|---|
| AISSEE application (NTA) | Oct–Nov 2026 | **Not asked.** Focus on domicile, birth certificate, photo, signature. |
| Result + AISSAC counselling | Feb–May 2027 | Some state quotas and school-specific concessions may ask for income proof at document verification. |
| Scholarship applications | Feb–Aug 2027 | State and central schemes for residential-school students usually require a valid income certificate with the application. |
| Fee concession / remission requests | After admission | Schools and welfare boards that grant income-linked concessions need it on file. |

Notice the pattern: everything that needs it happens *after* selection, but everything that delays it — tehsil verification, objections, re-application — must happen *before*. A certificate applied for in October is in your hand by November. A certificate applied for in February is a gamble.

## How to apply: the process, step by step

Almost every state now issues income certificates online through its citizen-services portal, with CSC (Common Service Centre) kiosks as the offline alternative. The portal names differ — Delhi and several states use e-District, Haryana uses e-Sathi (Saral), Bihar uses RTPS, Karnataka uses Seva Sindhu, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana use MeeSeva — so search for your state's official portal rather than trusting a random link.

The steps are similar everywhere:

1. **Register on your state's portal** with a mobile number; note the application or acknowledgement number.
2. **Fill the income certificate form** — applicant details (usually the earning parent), family details, and declared annual family income from all sources.
3. **Upload the documents** your state lists (see the checklist below). Scan clearly — blurred uploads are the single most common reason for objections.
4. **Pay the fee if any** — many states issue it for a nominal fee or free; the portal states the amount.
5. **Track the application.** The tehsil office verifies, sometimes with a field enquiry. Respond to any objection *within the portal's deadline*, not after.
6. **Download the digitally signed certificate** and keep both the PDF and a printout.

If the portal route feels unfamiliar, walk into the nearest CSC kiosk with your documents — the operator files it for you for a small service charge, and you get the acknowledgement on the spot.

## Document checklist (keep these ready before you apply)

The exact list varies by state — treat your state's portal checklist as final — but the usual set is:

- Identity proof of the applicant (Aadhaar is standard)
- Address proof (Aadhaar, voter ID, utility bill, or ration card — as the state requires)
- Proof of income: salary slips, or an affidavit declaring family income where the state allows self-declaration
- Ration card, where the state uses it as family-composition proof
- Passport-size photograph of the applicant, in the size the portal specifies
- In some states: a declaration form downloaded from the portal itself, signed and uploaded

One practical tip from the tehsil counter: the name and address on your income certificate should match your other documents — especially the domicile certificate — exactly. Mismatched spellings across documents are the classic verification-stage headache. If you are also fixing the birth certificate this month, finish that first and keep every document consistent.

## Validity: how long it lasts and when to renew

In most states, an income certificate is valid for **one financial year** — a certificate issued in October 2026 would typically be treated as valid for 2026–27. But "most states" is not "your state," and individual scholarship schemes sometimes demand a certificate issued within the last six months regardless of the state's general validity. Two rules of thumb:

- **Apply fresh in the admission year.** Even if you hold an older certificate, a 2026-issued one is cleaner for 2027 scholarship applications.
- **Read the scheme notification.** If a scholarship says "income certificate issued after 1 April 2027," no older certificate will do, however valid it technically is.

Keep the digitally signed PDF safe — re-downloads from state portals are not always available after long gaps, and a lost certificate means re-applying from zero.

## FAQs

**Is an income certificate compulsory for AISSEE 2027 admission?**
No. The income certificate is not part of the AISSEE application form itself. It becomes important after selection — for state and central scholarship schemes, fee concessions, and defence welfare schemes where eligibility is means-tested. Having it ready in October means you never scramble for it in February.

**How long does an income certificate take to be issued?**
Typically 10 to 30 days depending on the state and the verification load at the tehsil office. That is the whole reason to apply now: if an objection comes back, October still leaves you months to fix it before counselling and scholarship deadlines.

**How long is an income certificate valid?**
In most states an income certificate is valid for one financial year — a certificate issued in October 2026 would typically cover the 2026-27 financial year. But validity rules vary by state and by scheme, so check the specific scholarship notification that asks for it before relying on an old certificate.

**Where do I apply for an income certificate online?**
On your state's citizen-services portal — e-District in Delhi and several states, e-Sathi in Haryana, RTPS in Bihar, Seva Sindhu in Karnataka, MeeSeva in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, and similar portals elsewhere. CSC (Common Service Centre) kiosks offer the same service offline if you prefer to apply in person.

**What documents are needed for an income certificate?**
The usual set is an identity proof (Aadhaar), address proof, proof of income or an affidavit declaring family income, and sometimes the ration card or salary slips. The exact list varies by state — the portal shows its checklist before you submit, so follow your state's list, not a generic one from the internet.

**Whose income certificate is needed — the father's or the child's?**
The family's — in practice, the certificate is issued in the name of the earning parent or guardian and reflects total family income. Scholarship schemes that are means-tested look at family income, so it is the parent's income certificate that matters.
