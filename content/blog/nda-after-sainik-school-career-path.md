Almost every Sainik School parent carries the same picture in their head: their child, one day, in uniform — an officer. This article is the map from that picture to reality. NDA after Sainik School is a genuine, well-trodden path, but I want to start with the uncomfortable truth, because respecting it will make you a better guide for your child: **the school gives the best possible preparation, but zero guarantee.** Selection happens through the UPSC written exam and the SSB interview alone. No school — however prestigious — can hand your child a commission.

With that said, let's walk the whole road, stage by stage, from Class 6 admission to the day the rank is pinned on.

## The full journey at a glance

| Stage | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. AISSEE admission | Class 5 or 8 | Sainik School entrance exam (✅ 31 Jan 2027, tentative) |
| 2. School life | Classes 6–12 (7 years) | Academics + PT + an NDA-oriented personality |
| 3. UPSC NDA written | During / after Class 12 | Maths 300 + GAT 600 = 900 marks |
| 4. SSB interview | After clearing the written | 5 days, 900 marks — a personality assessment |
| 5. Medicals | After SSB recommendation | Armed forces medical standards |
| 6. Training | After making the merit list | 3 years at NDA + 1 year at IMA / INA / AFA |
| 7. Commission | After training | Lieutenant / Sub-Lieutenant / Flying Officer |

## Stages 1–2: The seven-year foundation

Everything about a Sainik School is designed with the NDA in mind — morning PT, the disciplined daily routine, leadership roles in the house system, the habit of following current affairs. These seven years prepare a child for the SSB *naturally*. An outside student typically starts SSB preparation in Class 12; a Sainik School child has been living in that preparation since age eleven.

Your job as a parent in this phase is simpler than you think: follow the [admission process for 2027](/blog/sainik-school-admission-2027-guide/) carefully, and then give your child a pressure-free home to return to on holidays. The school handles the rest.

It's 5:45 in the morning. A fourteen-year-old is already on the parade ground, fog on the grass, shoes laced tight. Nobody had to convince him to care — he just does. That's the quiet truth of this path: by the time the NDA exam arrives, the hardest part of discipline is already years behind you.

## Stage 3: The UPSC NDA written exam

UPSC conducts the NDA exam **twice a year** (NDA-I and NDA-II). The pattern is fixed and well known:

- **Paper 1 — Mathematics:** 300 marks, 2½ hours
- **Paper 2 — General Ability Test (GAT):** 600 marks, 2½ hours — English, general knowledge, science, and current affairs

**Eligibility — UPSC's established rules, stated plainly:**
- **Age:** 16.5 to 19.5 years (exact cut-off dates are given in each notification)
- **Education:** Class 12 pass — any stream for the Army wing; **Physics, Chemistry and Maths are compulsory for the Air Force and Navy wings**
- **Marital status:** unmarried (this applies to both male and female candidates)

Here's a practical tip most parents miss: because the Air Force and Navy require PCM, the large majority of Sainik School students choose the science stream in Classes 11–12. It keeps all three wings open. Choosing humanities closes two doors on day one — fine if the child is certain about the Army, but a real limitation otherwise.

The age window of 16.5 to 19.5 years means most candidates get several attempts at the exam — the exact number depends on the child's birth date and where it falls between notifications. I won't give you a fixed number of "attempts" because it genuinely varies; check it against your child's date of birth when the time comes.

## Stage 4: The SSB interview — where selections are actually made

The written exam is only the ticket; the selection happens at the SSB. It's a five-day process:

1. **Screening** — picture perception and officer intelligence rating tests
2. **Psychology tests** — thematic apperception, word association, situation reaction
3. **GTO tasks** — group discussions, outdoor group activities, command tasks
4. **Personal interview**
5. **Conference** — the final board decision

Rote learning is useless here. The SSB tests personality — leadership, teamwork, composure under pressure, honesty. And this is precisely why Sainik School students tend to do well: seven years of hostel life build leadership, teamwork, and confidence as a matter of daily routine. Here's my honest opinion — no coaching institute on earth can manufacture seven years of character in a seven-day crash course. The assessors have seen thousands of candidates; they can tell the difference within an hour.

## Stages 5–6: Medicals and training

After SSB recommendation comes the medical examination — eyesight, height-weight standards, and overall fitness against armed forces criteria. Then the merit list, drawn from written + SSB marks combined.

Training runs like this:
- **NDA, Khadakwasla (Pune):** 3 years — a university degree alongside military training
- **Then 1 more year** at IMA Dehradun (Army), INA Ezhimala (Navy), or AFA Hyderabad (Air Force), depending on the wing

Four years of training in total. It's demanding by design — but by this point, your child has already lived a version of it for seven years.

## Stage 7: Commission — the picture becomes real

On completing training, the officer is commissioned: **Lieutenant** in the Army, **Sub-Lieutenant** in the Navy, **Flying Officer** in the Air Force. From here begins a career that is respected, secure, and — for the right temperament — deeply meaningful.

## If the NDA doesn't happen — keep a plan B ready

Every parent needs to hear this: the NDA is fiercely competitive, and good, deserving candidates miss it every single year. Having a plan B isn't pessimism; it's parenting.

- **TES (Technical Entry Scheme):** shortlisting on Class 12 marks, straight to SSB — no written exam
- **CDS / AFCAT:** after graduation, for those who still want the uniform
- **JEE / NEET / UPSC:** the wider world of [career options after Sainik School](/blog/life-after-sainik-school-career-options/) stays completely open

And please — don't make "you *have* to clear the NDA" the soundtrack of your child's Class 11 and 12. Pressure doesn't improve SSB performance; the interview is specifically designed to detect strain, and a tense candidate reads as a tense candidate. Support beats pressure, every time.

## FAQs

**Q1. Does studying in a Sainik School guarantee NDA selection?**
No. Selection is through the UPSC written exam and SSB interview only — the school gives outstanding preparation but no guarantee and no reserved seat.

**Q2. Which stream should my child take in Class 11 for the NDA?**
PCM (Physics, Chemistry, Maths). It keeps all three wings — Army, Navy, Air Force — open. Without PCM, only the Army wing is available.

**Q3. When should SSB preparation formally begin?**
In a Sainik School it begins naturally in Class 6. Formal, focused SSB orientation in Classes 11–12 is plenty — earlier than that, just let the school's routine do its work.

**Q4. How many attempts does a candidate get at the NDA exam?**
It depends on the birth date — the 16.5 to 19.5 age window spans multiple UPSC notifications. Work it out against your child's exact date of birth rather than trusting a fixed number.

**Q5. Can girls join the NDA after Sainik School?**
Yes — girls have been eligible for NDA entry since 2021, and Sainik Schools now admit girls. The eligibility rules (age, education, marital status) apply equally.
