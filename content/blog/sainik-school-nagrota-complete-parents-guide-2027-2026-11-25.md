## Jammu's "Gateway to NDA" — and it just got a new arena

If you are filling the AISSEE 2027 form this week and your home state is Jammu and Kashmir or Ladakh, one school should be at the top of your list for a reason no brochure can manufacture: **Sainik School Nagrota** has been sending cadets to the Armed Forces for 56 years, and it is the only Sainik School in the state run by the Sainik Schools Society under the Ministry of Defence.

And today, 25 November 2026, it got a little better. J&K Lieutenant Governor Manoj Sinha inaugurated a brand-new **multi-utility indoor sports arena** on campus — badminton, basketball and table tennis, trainable all year round regardless of weather. Our news report on [today's inauguration](/news/sainik-school-nagrota-indoor-sports-arena-lg-manoj-sinha-2026-11-25/) has the details.

## A school built on the Tawi, in the first decade of the idea

Nagrota opened on **22 August 1970**, inaugurated by the then Defence Minister, the late **Jagjivan Ram**, with **Commander N. N. Seth** as founder principal. It was among the earlier Sainik Schools — the idea itself was barely a decade old — and it was built for a state that has sent its sons to the forces in large numbers for generations.

The campus sits on the banks of the **River Tawi**, on the Jammu–Srinagar highway, and the school's motto is *"Gyan, Veerata aur Anushasan"* — knowledge, courage and discipline. Ask any Nagrota old boy and the first thing he will tell you is the milestone at the entrance: "NDA 2001 km" — the distance from the school gate to the National Defence Academy. The destination is literally signposted from the day a cadet walks in.

Jammu and Kashmir has a second Sainik School — **Manasbal**, founded in 1981 — but it is run by the state government, not the Ministry of Defence. Our [Jammu and Kashmir guide](/states/jammu-kashmir/) covers both schools and the state picture in full.

## Seats for 2027-28: 75 in Class 6, 10 in Class 9

Here is the number that decides your child's odds. From Appendix VI-A and VI-A1 of the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin:

| **Class 6 — boys** | 44 | 21 | 65 |
| **Class 6 — girls** | 5 | 5 | 10 |
| **Class 6 — total** | — | — | **75** |
| **Class 9 — boys** | 7 | 3 | 10 |
| **Class 9 — girls** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Class 9 — total** | — | — | **10** |

Three things jump out. First, Class 6 is the real door — seven and a half times the size of Class 9. Second, the **67% home-state quota** means a J&K or Ladakh-domiciled child competes for 49 of the 75 Class 6 seats, while an out-of-state child chases 26 — our [home-state quota explainer](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-67-percent-explained-2026-10-17/) works through the arithmetic. Third, **Nagrota has no Class 9 seats for girls this year** — girls enter Nagrota in Class 6 or not at all, for 2027-28. Our [girls' seats explainer](/news/aissee-2027-7-days-left-girls-351-class-6-seats-2026-11-25/) lists the schools that do have Class 9 girls' seats.

The seat figures are tentative — the bulletin says so itself — but they are the numbers NTA published, and they are the ones to plan around.

## Admission: only through AISSEE, and the clock is ticking

There is no management quota, no donation route and no direct admission to Nagrota. Entry is to **Class 6 and Class 9 only**, through AISSEE, conducted by NTA.

For 2027-28, the form is open at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society** until **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM**. The fee is Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and Rs 700 for SC and ST. A late window runs 3–6 December with an extra Rs 500 — but treat that as an emergency exit, not a plan.

The age rules are the standard AISSEE ones: **10–12 years as on 31 March 2027 for Class 6** (born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017, studying in Class 5) and **13–15 years for Class 9** (born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014, studying in Class 8). The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. There is no negative marking, and the qualifying bar is 25% in each section with 40% aggregate — our [worked examples of the 25% rule](/blog/aissee-2027-qualifying-marks-25-percent-rule-worked-examples/) show exactly what that means for your child's target score.

## Four houses, and a campus that keeps adding to itself

Nagrota's cadets live in **four houses — Trikuta, Chenab, Sindh and Jhelum** — named after the rivers and mountains of the region. Each has its own House Master and staff who act as the cadets' guides and guardians.

The campus has hockey, football, volleyball and basketball fields, a mini-stadium, an obstacle course, a swimming pool, an auditorium, a library, labs, an infirmary and a cadets' mess modelled on an officers' mess. In August 2026 the school added new synthetic tennis, volleyball, basketball and badminton courts, and today the indoor sports arena joined them. Nagrota also hosted the All India Sainik Schools National Games this year and won the Group A title on home turf.

Then there is the record that gives the school its nickname. Nagrota has contributed **more than 600 officers** to the Armed Forces, and in 2025 it won the **Raksha Mantri Trophy** — awarded to the school sending the highest percentage of cadets to the NDA — for the NDA 154 course, with **35 cadets clearing the written exam and 11 clearing the SSB**. That last pair of numbers is the honest part: even at the best school in the state, two out of three written qualifiers do not make it through the SSB.

## Fees and cutoffs: what is reported, and what is not verified

This is the section where honesty costs us a clean number — and that is the point. No verified 2027 fee figure for Nagrota is available from the school or the NTA bulletin. One coaching portal reports tuition at the older Sainik Schools at **Rs 1,28,615 a year for 2026-27**. Treat that as a reported figure, not a bill: the all-in cost — fee plus uniform, books, travel and extras — is always higher than the headline tuition figure, and scholarships exist for defence families and SC/ST cadets. Confirm the current fee from the school before you budget.

On cutoffs: NTA does not publish school-wise cutoffs, and we have no verified or credibly reported Nagrota cutoff to quote — so we will not invent one. The sound advice: **aim for 280 or more out of 300**, and treat the 25%-per-section qualifying bar as a floor, not a target.

## Is Nagrota the right first choice for your child?

Ask yourself three questions before you put it at the top of your preference list:

1. **Is your child genuinely drawn to the armed forces?** Nagrota's whole system — dawn PT, NDA coaching, SSB preparation, four-house rivalry — is built for one destination. A child who wants it thrives here.
2. **Can your family handle the distance and the boarding?** Nagrota is 18 km from Jammu Tawi and a day's journey from most of India, and term breaks become the rhythm of family life for six years. If you can, visit the campus before you fill the preference list.
3. **Is Class 6 the entry point?** With 75 seats against 10 in Class 9 — and no Class 9 seats for girls at all — Class 6 is the sensible target. Class 9 entry is a second chance, not a plan.

If the answer to all three is yes, Nagrota is not just the oldest Sainik School in Jammu and Kashmir — it is a school that, 56 years after it opened, is still adding buildings, still winning trophies and still sending its cadets to the Academy. The form closes on 2 December. Seven days is enough time to fill it carefully. It is not enough time to fill it twice.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Nagrota have for 2027-28?**

Nagrota has 75 Class 6 seats — 65 for boys and 10 for girls — and 10 Class 9 seats, all for boys. There are no Class 9 seats for girls at Nagrota this year. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin.

**When was Sainik School Nagrota founded?**

It was inaugurated on 22 August 1970 by the then Defence Minister Jagjivan Ram, with Commander N. N. Seth as the founder principal. It sits on a 108-acre campus at Nagrota in Jammu, on the banks of the River Tawi beside the Jammu-Srinagar highway.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Nagrota?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Nagrota?**

No single verified 2027 fee figure is available. One coaching portal reports tuition at the older Sainik Schools at Rs 1,28,615 a year for 2026-27, so parents should confirm the current figure from the school before budgeting.

**Does Sainik School Nagrota admit girls?**

Yes, in Class 6 — 10 girls' seats for 2027-28, five for Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh girls and five for girls from other states. There are no Class 9 seats for girls at Nagrota this year. The school currently has 52 girl cadets on campus.

**How many boarding houses does Sainik School Nagrota have?**

Four — Trikuta, Chenab, Sindh and Jhelum — named after the rivers and mountains of the region. Each house has its own House Master and staff who act as the cadets' guides and guardians.
