## The short answer: no — not for AISSEE

If you are a Maharashtra parent and someone has told you that your child cannot apply for AISSEE without a caste validity certificate, you can relax — with one important condition. For the AISSEE form itself, what the National Testing Agency asks for is a **caste or category certificate** in the format given in the information bulletin's appendix, issued by a competent authority (District Magistrate, Collector, Sub-Divisional Magistrate, Tehsildar-level revenue officer, or equivalent). A Maharashtra **caste validity certificate is not among the AISSEE bulletin's required formats**.

That distinction matters because the two documents are often confused, and the confusion costs families months. A validity certificate takes far longer to obtain than a caste certificate, and parents who chase the wrong document first can end up missing the simpler one.

## Caste certificate vs validity certificate

| Document | Issued by | What it proves | Time typically needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caste certificate | Tehsildar / SDO / competent revenue authority | That you belong to a stated caste or community | Roughly 15–30 days (reported) |
| Caste validity certificate | District Caste Scrutiny Committee (Maharashtra) | That your caste certificate itself is genuine, after scrutiny of ancestral records | Typically 3–6 months (reported); committee decision expected within 3 months, plus up to 2 months in exceptional cases |

Think of it this way: the caste certificate is the claim, and the validity certificate is Maharashtra's second-level check on that claim. Most of India stops at the first level. Maharashtra, for its own state admissions and jobs, adds the second.

## So who actually needs a validity certificate?

You should still apply for one — early — if any of these apply to your family:

- **You live in Maharashtra and your child may also apply through state-level admissions.** Validity is demanded for Maharashtra state processes such as MHT-CET and NEET state-quota admissions and state government jobs, according to Maharashtra guides. A child aiming at both AISSEE and a state route needs both documents ready.
- **You are applying for a Maharashtra scholarship or benefit that asks for validity.** Some state schemes treat validity as the final proof; a caste certificate alone will not close the file.
- **Your caste claim could face scrutiny.** If your family's caste records are old, from another district, or based on a relative's documents, the scrutiny committee process is exactly where those questions get settled — and it is slow, so starting early is the whole strategy.

If none of these apply — if AISSEE and the Sainik School route are your only plan — then the caste certificate, in the right format and from the right authority, is the document to get right. Our [caste certificate guide](/blog/caste-certificate-format-aissee-reservation-claims/) covers the bulletin formats, the issuing authorities and the OBC trap in detail.

## The OBC condition that trips up more families than validity does

For AISSEE, there is a separate condition that causes more rejections than any validity question: **OBC candidates must be on the central OBC list**, not just their state list, and must hold non-creamy-layer status. Our [OBC-NCL validity guide](/blog/obc-ncl-certificate-validity-rule-aissee-2027/) explains the one-financial-year rule and the declaration the bulletin requires. Do not confuse this "validity" — the currency of your OBC-NCL certificate — with Maharashtra's validity certificate. They are two different things with an unfortunately similar name.

## How to apply for a Maharashtra validity certificate

Applications go to the **District Caste Scrutiny Committee** for your district. In practice, families apply with the caste certificate itself, identity and address proof, and — critically — the ancestral evidence the committee scrutinises: school leaving certificates, old revenue records, and caste proofs of blood relatives. This is why validity takes months: the committee is not re-issuing a certificate, it is verifying a family history.

A Pune guide reports that Maharashtra moved the process to a fully online system via the BARTI/CCVIS portal in October 2026, with deficiencies resolvable by video conference. Treat that as reported, and verify the current route on the official portal — the committee office for your district remains the fallback.

Plan backwards from the deadline that actually needs validity:

| If you need validity by | Start by (working backwards) |
|---|---|
| A state admission round in mid-2027 | October–November 2026 — now |
| A scholarship deadline in early 2027 | Immediately; a 3–6 month timeline leaves no slack |
| AISSEE document verification only | Not needed for AISSEE — prioritise the caste certificate instead |

## What if validity is delayed?

Maharashtra itself recognises the delay problem. For professional-course admissions in 2026-27, the state government allowed admitted SEBC and OBC students six months from admission to submit their caste validity certificates, warning that failure to submit within that period would lead to cancellation of admission. That relief was specific to those state courses — it is not an AISSEE rule, and it is not a reason to delay applying. If a state process gives you a grace period, use it to finish the application, not to start it.

## What other states ask for

Outside Maharashtra, the pattern is simpler: a caste or category certificate from a competent authority, in the format the relevant bulletin prescribes. Domicile proof is separate — see our [state-wise domicile guide](/blog/domicile-certificate-online-apply-state-wise-process-2026-10-11/).

## FAQs

**Do I need a caste validity certificate for AISSEE 2027?**

No, not for the AISSEE form itself. The AISSEE information bulletin asks for a caste or category certificate in its appendix format, issued by a competent authority. A Maharashtra caste validity certificate is a separate state-level verification and is not listed among the AISSEE bulletin formats.

**What is the difference between a caste certificate and a caste validity certificate?**

A caste certificate, issued by a Tehsildar or equivalent authority, states which caste or community you belong to. A caste validity certificate, issued by a District Caste Scrutiny Committee in Maharashtra, legally verifies that the caste certificate itself is genuine.

**How long does a Maharashtra caste validity certificate take?**

Typically 3 to 6 months, according to Maharashtra guides, with the scrutiny committee expected to decide within 3 months and up to 2 extra months in exceptional cases. Apply months before any state admission deadline that needs it.

**Who should still apply for a caste validity certificate?**

Maharashtra-resident reserved-category families who may also use state-level admissions, scholarships or jobs where validity is demanded. If your only plan is AISSEE, the caste certificate is the document to get right.

**What do other states ask for instead of a validity certificate?**

Just the caste or category certificate from a competent authority. For AISSEE, OBC candidates must additionally match the central OBC list and hold non-creamy-layer status, which is a separate condition from any state validity process.

**What happens if a Maharashtra validity certificate is delayed?**

For Maharashtra professional-course admissions in 2026-27, the state government allowed admitted students six months from admission to submit the validity certificate, failing which admission could be cancelled. That relief was specific to those state courses, not to AISSEE.
