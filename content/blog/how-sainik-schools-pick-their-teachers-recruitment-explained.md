## No central exam — each school hires its own staff

Parents often assume Sainik School teachers arrive through one national test, like the exams for Kendriya Vidyalayas or Navodaya Vidyalayas. They do not. Each Sainik School — there are more than 30 of the older schools plus the newer ones — issues its own recruitment advertisement, under the rules of the Sainik Schools Society of the Ministry of Defence, and runs its own selection.

That is why the vacancy news on this site looks the way it does: Kodagu advertised 7 posts in October 2026, Goalpara advertised 13, Rewari advertised 5, and Chittorgarh called a walk-in interview for its posts — all in the same month, all with different last dates, fees, and selection stages. A teacher who misses one school's deadline can still apply to the next school's advertisement. A parent comparing schools, meanwhile, should understand that two schools with the same name-board can have quite different staffing stories.

## Regular vs contractual: the distinction that matters most

The single most important line in any Sainik School advertisement is whether the post is regular or contractual. In the recent notifications, the pattern is consistent: a small number of posts are regular, and most are contractual.

| | Regular post | Contractual post |
|---|---|---|
| Job security | Permanent appointment under school rules | Fixed term, usually one year at a time |
| Extension | Not applicable | On performance, often up to three years in total |
| Recent examples | Quarter Master at Goalpara; PGT and TGT posts at Kodagu | Most Goalpara posts; all five Rewari posts |
| What it means for pupils | A teacher likely to stay for years | A teacher who may change every year or two |

Contractual does not mean unqualified — the eligibility bar is the same — but it does mean turnover. A school that fills the same subject with a new contractual teacher every year will feel different in the classroom from a school where the physics teacher has taught the same syllabus for a decade. That is the honest reason parents should ask about staffing stability on a school visit, not just about buildings and playgrounds.

## What qualifications actually count

Teaching posts follow a familiar pattern across notifications, with the details set by each advertisement:

- **PGT (Post Graduate Teacher):** a master's degree in the subject with at least 50% marks, plus a B.Ed. from an NCTE-recognised institution. Kodagu's PGT Physics advertisement follows this pattern, and Goalpara's requires the same combination.
- **TGT (Trained Graduate Teacher):** a bachelor's degree in the relevant subject with at least 50% marks, a B.Ed., and a qualified CTET or STET Paper-II. Goalpara's TGT Mathematics and TGT Social Science posts both require this trio.
- **Specialist and support roles:** these vary the most. Rewari's October 2026 advertisement sought a Medical Officer (MBBS), a counsellor (psychology or child-development qualification), a nursing assistant (nursing diploma or degree), and a PGT in physical education — each with its own qualification and age band.

Age limits also vary by notification rather than following one national rule: Rewari set 18–50 years for some posts and 21–50 for others, while Chittorgarh set 21–35 for its TGT posts as of 31 October 2026. Never assume the age limit from one school's advertisement applies to another's.

## How selection actually works

The stages are broadly similar, but the mix changes by school and post:

1. **Application, usually offline.** Most Sainik School recruitments still run on paper: download the prescribed form from the school website, attach self-attested documents and a demand draft for the fee, and send it by registered or speed post so it reaches the school by the last date. Kodagu and Goalpara both work this way; Rewari also accepts applications by hand.
2. **Shortlisting.** Applications are screened against the eligibility conditions in the advertisement. Ineligible applications are rejected at this stage, which is why the qualification percentages and CTET/STET requirements deserve a careful read.
3. **Written test and classroom demonstration.** Teaching posts typically face a written test, a class demonstration, and sometimes a proficiency or skill test, followed by an interview. Chittorgarh's advertisement lists exactly this combination.
4. **Interview and document verification.** Shortlisted candidates are interviewed, and original documents — degrees, B.Ed., CTET/STET certificate, experience letters, caste certificate where applicable — are verified before appointment.

Fees are modest but real: Goalpara charged Rs 300 for General candidates and Rs 200 for SC/ST/OBC candidates; Kodagu charged Rs 500 for General/OBC and Rs 350 for SC/ST; Rewari charged Rs 500 with SC/ST candidates exempt. Every fee is paid by demand draft in favour of the school's principal, and the draft itself is a common reason applications get rejected — wrong payee name, wrong amount, or a missing draft.

## What the pay figures really tell you

Reported monthly pay in the October 2026 notifications ranges widely: roughly Rs 21,600 to Rs 57,120 at Goalpara, Rs 25,000 to Rs 47,600 at Kodagu, and consolidated figures of Rs 40,800 to Rs 84,960 at Rewari, where the Medical Officer post pays the most. Regular posts on a pay matrix (for example, Chittorgarh's TGT posts at Level 7) also carry dearness allowance and other allowances, while contractual posts are usually quoted as a flat consolidated figure.

Read these numbers as a snapshot of specific notifications, not as a promise. Two schools advertising the same post title can pay differently, and the same school can revise its figures in its next advertisement.

## Why staffing varies so much between schools

Three forces explain the variation. First, older schools with settled rolls recruit mainly to replace retirements, while newer schools are still building their full staff strength. Second, location matters: a school in a remote district fills some specialist posts more slowly than one near a city. Third, the contractual model itself creates churn — a teacher on a one-year contract who finds a permanent post elsewhere leaves a gap that the next advertisement must fill.

None of this is visible in a school prospectus. It becomes visible only in the recruitment advertisements themselves, which is why following them is useful even for parents who are not job-hunting: a school advertising the same subject post every year is telling you something about its classroom stability.

## A parent's staffing checklist for a school visit

- How many teaching posts are vacant right now, and in which subjects?
- How long have the current teachers of Class 6 and Class 9 core subjects been at the school?
- Are the key subjects taught by regular staff, contractual staff, or a mix?
- When was the school's last recruitment, and did all advertised posts get filled?
- For a new Sainik School, what is the staffing plan as classes are added year by year?

Ask these questions politely and directly. A school confident about its staff will answer them; a school that cannot is giving you an answer of a different kind.

## FAQs

**Is there a single national exam to become a Sainik School teacher?**

No. Each Sainik School advertises its own vacancies under Sainik Schools Society rules. Candidates apply to individual schools, not through one central test.

**What qualifications does a TGT post in a Sainik School need?**

As reported in recent notifications, a bachelor's degree in the relevant subject with at least 50 percent marks, a B.Ed. from an NCTE-recognised institution, and a qualified CTET or STET Paper-II.

**Are Sainik School teaching posts permanent?**

Some are regular appointments and many are contractual, typically for one year at a time and extendable on performance. The advertisement for each post states which kind it is, so check that line before applying.

**How are Sainik School teachers selected?**

The usual stages are shortlisting on eligibility, a written test and classroom demonstration for teaching posts, an interview, and document verification. The exact combination varies by school and post.

**What pay do Sainik School teachers get?**

Pay varies widely by school, post and regular or contractual status. Recent notifications report roughly Rs 21,600 to Rs 57,120 a month for Goalpara posts and Rs 25,000 to Rs 47,600 for Kodagu posts. Treat every figure as reported for that notification, not as a national scale.

**What should parents ask about teachers when visiting a Sainik School?**

Ask how many teaching posts are vacant, how long the current teachers have been at the school, and whether key subjects are taught by regular or contractual staff. Stability in the classroom matters more than any single qualification on paper.
