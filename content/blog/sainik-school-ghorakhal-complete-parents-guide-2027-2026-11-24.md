## Why Ghorakhal is the school other Sainik Schools measure themselves against

If you are filling the AISSEE 2027 form this week and your child dreams of the uniform, one school's name will keep coming up in every conversation: **Sainik School Ghorakhal**. It is not the biggest Sainik School, and it is not the easiest to reach. But on the one measure parents care about most — how many cadets actually make it to the National Defence Academy — no other Sainik School in India has matched it.

Ghorakhal holds the national record for winning the **Raksha Mantri Trophy ten times since 2000**. The trophy goes to the Sainik School that sends the most cadets to the NDA each year. Winning it once is a proud year. Winning it ten times is a system.

And the system is still producing. In the **NDA (II) 2026 written examination, 44 Ghorakhal cadets qualified** — 30 from the current Class XII batch and 14 from the March 2026 batch, as reported by SSBCrack in October 2026. That is the number to hold in your head when you weigh this school against the others on your preference list.

## A school built on a Nawab's estate, after the 1962 war

Ghorakhal was formally inaugurated on **21 March 1966**, one of the early Sainik Schools created after the 1962 war with China, when Defence Minister V. K. Krishna Menon pushed the feeder-school idea across the states. The founding principal was **Wing Commander Jaimal Singh** of the Indian Air Force, who organised the first cadet batches and carved academic buildings out of the palace structures on the estate.

The estate itself is part of the story. The 500-acre campus once belonged to the **Nawab of Rampur**, a colonial-era property in the hills above Bhowali. The name *Ghorakhal* comes from the regional words *ghora* (horse) and *khal* (a mountain pond or saddle) — local lore ties it to a British general's horse that died drinking from a pond here during the 1857 uprising. Whether or not you believe the story, the name has stuck for over a century, and the school's alumni still call themselves *Ghorakhalis* — or simply *Ghoras*.

The school marked its **Golden Jubilee in May 2016**, fifty years after that first batch. By then it had already produced generals, admirals and air marshals — EducationWorld's older profile records **358 cadets having joined the NDA by 2008** alone, and names alumni like Vice Admiral Harish Bisht and Lt General L. K. Pandey among its senior officers.

Two things jump out. First, Class 6 is the real door — twice the size of Class 9, as it is in almost every Sainik School. Second, the **67% home-state quota** means a Uttarakhand-domiciled child competes for 54 of the 80 Class 6 seats, while an out-of-state child chases 26. The seat figures are tentative — the bulletin says so itself — but they are the numbers NTA published, and they are the ones to plan around.

## Admission: only through AISSEE, and the clock is ticking

There is no management quota, no donation route and no direct admission to Ghorakhal. Entry is to **Class 6 and Class 9 only**, through AISSEE, conducted by NTA.

For 2027-28, the form is open at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society** until **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM**. The fee is Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and Rs 700 for SC and ST. A late window runs 3–6 December with an extra Rs 500 — but treat that as an emergency exit, not a plan.

The age rules are the standard AISSEE ones: **10–12 years as on 31 March 2027 for Class 6** (born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017, studying in Class 5) and **13–15 years for Class 9** (born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014, studying in Class 8). The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027** — Class 6 is a 150-minute, 300-mark paper; Class 9 is 180 minutes and 400 marks. There is no negative marking, and the qualifying bar is 25% in each section with 40% aggregate — our [worked examples of the 25% rule](/blog/aissee-2027-qualifying-marks-25-percent-rule-worked-examples/) show exactly what that means for your child's target score.

## Houses, campus life and the hill adjustment

Ghorakhal runs a house system with **ten houses**: the senior houses are Shiwalik, Kesari, Garhwal, Kumaon and Singh, and the junior houses are Dhruv, Luv, Kush, Abhimanyu and Bharat. Houses are where a cadet's daily life actually happens — dormitory, competitions, cross-country runs, discipline and friendships — and inter-house rivalry is one of the school's oldest traditions. The school's cross-country and athletic meets are regular news in the Nainital press, and Kesari House took the cross-country title in the school's 2026 meet.

Then there is the hill itself, and parents should not romanticise it. The campus sits at about **1,800 metres**. Winters are properly cold, mornings start early, and the routine is fully residential from day one. A child from the plains who has never lived away from home will adjust — thousands have — but the adjustment is real: cold, altitude, homesickness and a regimented day, all at once. If you can, visit in winter before you fill the preference list, and speak to parents of current cadets about the first term, not just the prospectus.

On the practical side: the school is CBSE-affiliated (affiliation number 3580002), Classes XI and XII run the Science stream only, and the campus has a library of more than 15,000 books, an auditorium seating about 600, and computer and science laboratories. In August 2026 the school commissioned the **Bachendri Pal girls' hostel block**, inaugurated by the Union Minister of State for Defence — the clearest sign yet that girls' induction at Ghorakhal is being built for the long term.

## Fees: what is reported, and what is not verified

This is the section where honesty costs us a clean number — and that is the point. Published secondary figures for Ghorakhal's annual fee **conflict sharply**: one school directory quotes about Rs 1,15,000, another about Rs 1,70,000, a third a range of roughly Rs 1,70,600–1,74,100, and a coaching guide about Rs 1,80,000 including hostel, food and other charges. None of these is a verified 2027 figure from the school or the NTA bulletin, so this guide will not pick one and call it the fee.

What is safe to say: Sainik School fees are charged term-wise, scholarships exist for defence families and for SC/ST cadets, and the all-in cost — fee plus uniform, books, travel and extras — is always higher than the headline tuition figure. Confirm the current fee from the school's own notice before you budget. The [girls' quota explainer](/blog/girls-quota-sainik-schools-explained-2026-10-22/) also covers the 10% girls' seats that apply at Ghorakhal.

## Is Ghorakhal the right first choice for your child?

Ask yourself three questions before you put it at the top of your preference list:

1. **Is your child genuinely drawn to the armed forces?** Ghorakhal's whole system — PT at dawn, NDA-focused coaching in XI–XII, SSB preparation — is built for one destination. A child who wants it thrives here. A child who is being pushed into it will feel every 5 AM reveille.
2. **Can your family handle the hill and the distance?** Nainital district is not a day trip from most of India. Term breaks are the rhythm of family life for six years — make sure that works for you.
3. **Is Class 6 the entry point?** With 80 seats against 40 in Class 9, and the home-state advantage on top, Class 6 is the sensible target. Class 9 entry is a second chance, not a plan.

If the answer to all three is yes, Ghorakhal is not just a good choice — it is the choice with the strongest NDA record in the country. The form closes on 2 December. Eight days is enough time to fill it carefully. It is not enough time to fill it twice.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Ghorakhal have for 2027-28?**

Ghorakhal has 80 Class 6 seats — 70 boys and 10 girls — and 40 Class 9 seats — 36 boys and 4 girls. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin.

**When was Sainik School Ghorakhal founded?**

It was formally inaugurated on 21 March 1966 on the 500-acre estate of the Nawab of Rampur, near Bhowali in Nainital district, Uttarakhand. Its motto is Buddhiryasya Balam Tasya — wisdom brings strength.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Ghorakhal?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Ghorakhal?**

No single verified 2027 fee figure is available. Published secondary figures conflict, ranging from about Rs 1.15 lakh to about Rs 1.80 lakh per year, so parents should confirm the current figure from the school or the AISSEE 2027 bulletin before budgeting.

**Does Sainik School Ghorakhal admit girls?**

Yes. For 2027-28 the bulletin lists 10 Class 6 seats and 4 Class 9 seats for girls at Ghorakhal, and the school commissioned a dedicated girls' hostel block, named after Bachendri Pal, in August 2026.

**Is Ghorakhal really the best Sainik School for NDA preparation?**

Its record is the strongest in the country on one measure: it holds the national record for winning the Raksha Mantri Trophy ten times since 2000, and 44 of its cadets cleared the NDA (II) 2026 written exam. But NDA selection still depends on the child — the school opens the door, it does not carry them through it.
