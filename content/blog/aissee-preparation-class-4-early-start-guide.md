## First, the timeline: your child is not late, and not early either

If your child is in Class 4 in the 2026–27 school year, here is the honest calendar:

| School year | Class | What AISSEE means then |
|---|---|---|
| 2026–27 | Class 4 | Foundations only — no form, no coaching batch, no mock tests |
| 2027–28 | Class 5 | The real preparation year — form opens (EXPECTED October 2027), exam in January 2028 (EXPECTED) |
| 2028–29 | Class 6 | Admission year, if selected through AISSEE and counselling |

That is the whole point of this guide: you have roughly two school years, and the first one should feel almost nothing like exam preparation. Parents who drill a 9-year-old with mock papers usually arrive at Class 5 with a child already tired of the exam. Parents who build reading, number sense and study habits in Class 4 arrive with a child ready to work.

## What Class 4 preparation should actually look like

### 1. Reading, every day, without a test at the end

AISSEE Class 6 English is, at its core, a reading test: comprehension passages, vocabulary in context, basic grammar. The cheapest way to build that is 20 minutes of daily reading — storybooks, children's newspapers, anything the child actually enjoys. Do not quiz every page. A child who reads for pleasure in Class 4 reads faster under exam pressure in Class 6, and no workbook teaches that.

### 2. Maths fluency, not maths syllabus

The Class 6 paper gives Maths the biggest weight, but Class 4 is not the time to race ahead into Class 6 chapters. What pays off is fluency: tables up to 20 without hesitation, quick addition and subtraction, and fractions as a visual idea (half a chapati, quarter of a pizza). A child who can do 7 × 8 without counting on fingers has an advantage no amount of Class 4 "advanced" worksheets can buy. Our [maths speed drills](/blog/aissee-maths-speed-improvement-guide/) work just as well as games at this age as they do as exam drills later.

### 3. Thinking puzzles, not intelligence-section coaching

The AISSEE intelligence section tests pattern recognition, series, coding and spatial sense. In Class 4, the right version of this is play: picture puzzles, odd-one-out games, number series as a dinner-table challenge, building blocks and simple chess. If it feels like a game, it is working. If it feels like homework, stop.

### 4. Handwriting and sitting stamina

Two unglamorous skills decide more marks than parents expect: legible handwriting at speed, and the ability to sit and focus for 45 minutes, then an hour. Build stamina gradually in Class 4 through reading and homework routines, not timed mock papers. By Class 5, a [realistic daily timetable](/blog/aissee-daily-timetable-class-6/) will feel normal instead of shocking.

### 5. General awareness from life, not from GK books

Class 6 GK rewards children who notice the world: state capitals from a map on the wall, national symbols, famous sportspeople, simple science from everyday observation. A wall map and dinner-table conversation beat a memorised GK list at this age — and the knowledge sticks longer.

## The Class 4 weekly rhythm

Keep it light. A realistic week, on top of normal school homework:| Day | 30–45 minutes extra | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Monday–Friday | 20 min reading + 10 min tables or mental maths | Same time every day, so it becomes a habit |
| Saturday | One puzzle activity or educational game | Chess, puzzles, map games — the child's pick |
| Sunday | Free reading or a family outing with a "notice three things" game | No study pressure; curiosity is the lesson |

Total extra study: about 3–4 hours a week. That is enough — and Class 4 needs far less than the [hours a Class 6 student should study](/blog/aissee-how-many-study-hours-class-6/).

## What NOT to do in Class 4

- **Do not join an AISSEE coaching batch.** Coaching designed for Class 5 exam patterns wastes a year of childhood and risks burnout before the year that counts.
- **Do not buy Class 6 mock-test books.** Testing a child on a syllabus they have not studied teaches them that exams are about failure.
- **Do not skip Class 4 schoolwork to "prepare".** The Class 4 syllabus is the foundation; a child weak in Class 4 fractions will struggle with Class 5 maths no matter how many guidebooks you buy.
- **Do not compare with other parents' children.** The only race that matters starts in Class 5, and the child who arrives fresh usually outlasts the child who started exhausted.
- **Do not promise admission.** Seats are limited and merit-based. Tell your child the truth: the goal is to try your best and learn discipline along the way, whatever the result.

## The Class 4 parent checklist

- [ ] Child reads something enjoyable for at least 20 minutes most days
- [ ] Tables up to 20 are becoming automatic, not counted on fingers
- [ ] A fixed homework-and-reading time exists, even if it is short
- [ ] The child can sit and focus for 45 minutes without a meltdown
- [ ] Birth certificate, Aadhaar and school records are in order (you will need them for the form in Class 5)
- [ ] You know the AISSEE Class 6 age rule (10–12 as on 31 March of the admission year) and your child's birth date fits the 2028 cycle
- [ ] No coaching batch, no mock-test series, no pressure about a result two years away

## When to switch gears

Start watching for the AISSEE 2028 notification in the second half of 2027, when your child is in Class 5 — that is when this site's notification watch, document checklists and exam-pattern guides become your daily reading. Until then, the best preparation is a child who likes reading, is quick with numbers, can sit still and work, and still likes school. Protect that, and Class 5 will take care of itself.

## FAQs

**Can a Class 4 student apply for AISSEE?**

No. AISSEE Class 6 admission requires the child to be studying in Class 5 in the academic year of the exam. A child in Class 4 now will be eligible when they reach Class 5, which means the exam in the following January cycle.

**What age must a child be for AISSEE Class 6?**

Under the AISSEE 2026 bulletin pattern, a Class 6 applicant must be between 10 and 12 years old as on 31 March of the admission year. Check the 2028 bulletin when it releases, as the reference date can change.

**Should a Class 4 child join AISSEE coaching?**

No. At this age, coaching built around exam drilling usually backfires — it burns out the child before the real preparation year. Strong Class 4 schoolwork, daily reading and mental maths build more than any coaching batch.

**How much should a Class 4 child study for AISSEE?**

Beyond regular school homework, 30 to 45 minutes a day of reading, tables and puzzle-type thinking is plenty. The goal at this age is habit and confidence, not hours.

**What subjects matter most in Class 4 for later AISSEE success?**

Maths fluency (tables, four operations, fractions basics), English reading comprehension, and general awareness from everyday reading. These three feed directly into the AISSEE Class 6 paper later.

**When should serious AISSEE preparation start?**

Serious, exam-pattern preparation belongs in Class 5 — the year of the exam. Class 4 is for foundations: reading speed, number sense, handwriting and the habit of sitting down to study without a fight.
