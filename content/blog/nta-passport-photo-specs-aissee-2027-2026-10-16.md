## Why a passport photo can wreck an application

Every AISSEE season, the photo upload page quietly rejects more forms than any other field. Not the eligibility section, not the fee payment — the photo. A parent uploads a five-year-old studio print scanned at home, or a selfie cropped out of a family function photograph, or a scan where the face is 40% of the frame against a blue curtain. Then the form goes through, the fee is paid — and weeks later an NTA message arrives on the registered email asking for a re-upload, or worse, the application is flagged.

Here's the thing parents miss: NTA runs the same photo verification machinery across its exams. The specs it applies to JEE and NEET applicants are the specs it applies to a Class 5 child applying for AISSEE. Get it right once, before the bulletin even releases, and one whole category of form-day panic disappears. This guide gives you the exact numbers and the mistakes to avoid.

## The exact specs (AISSEE 2026 + NTA standing rules)

For AISSEE 2026, the NTA form asked for uploads in these file sizes (per the AISSEE reporting by education portals):

| Document | Format | File size (AISSEE 2026) |
|----------|--------|------------------------|
| Candidate's photograph | JPG / JPEG | **10 KB – 200 KB** |
| Candidate's signature | JPG / JPEG | **4 KB – 30 KB** |
| Category certificate (SC/ST/OBC/EWS) | PDF | 50 KB – 300 KB |
| PwD certificate | PDF | 50 KB – 300 KB |
| Result-awaited attestation form (if applicable) | PDF | 50 KB – 500 KB |

🔶 EXPECTED: AISSEE 2027 will carry the same or very close numbers — NTA rarely changes these between years. Note the difference from NTA's general ceiling of **10–300 KB** for photos on some other exams; if you keep your photo between 50 and 200 KB, you sit safely inside both windows.

## The photo rules NTA enforces across exams

File size is only the gate. NTA's photo guidance — applied to JEE and NEET applicants and reported by NTA in official notices — adds composition rules:

1. **Recent colour passport photo, plain white background.** Not cream, not light blue — white. A "recent passport-size photo with a white or light-colored background" is also how the AISSEE-specific guidance phrases it.
2. **Face about 80% of the frame, both ears clearly visible.** This is the single most common rejection reason: a photo taken from two metres away where the face is a small oval.
3. **Name and date of the photograph printed on it.** For JEE/NEET, NTA requires the candidate's name and the date the photo was taken to appear on the photograph (typically printed below the image by the studio). 🔶 EXPECTED: AISSEE 2027 will follow the same convention — get your studio to print these.
4. **No cap, no mask, no sunglasses.** Spectacles are allowed **only if worn normally**.
5. **No Polaroid, no computer-generated or morphed photos.** NTA's language is blunt: fabricated or computer-made photos mean application rejection, treated as unfair means. Criminal action can follow in extreme cases.
6. **No hand-drawn touch-ups, no tampering.** Self-editing in a phone app to "brighten" the photo counts as manipulation. If NTA's system flags it, you get a re-upload notice on email/SMS — and you'd better not miss it.
7. **Keep 6–8 identical copies.** NTA advises candidates to keep the same photographs for the admit card, exam-day attendance sheet and counselling. One studio visit, one batch — consistency matters.

## Signature rules (often ignored, equally strict)

- Sign on **plain white paper with a black pen**, in running hand. **Capital letters are not allowed** — a signature in block capitals can be rejected.
- 4 KB to 30 KB JPG for AISSEE 2026. Sign at a natural size; do not sign tiny and then zoom the scan, which pixelates the strokes.
- Your exam-day attendance signature is compared against this upload. Practise one signature and stick to it.

## The 9 photo mistakes that trigger re-upload notices

1. **Uploading a cropped family photo.** Background objects, other people's shoulders, party lighting — all immediate red flags.
2. **Old photograph.** A photo from two years ago when the child looked visibly younger. NTA expects recent; NEET's rule caps it at a few months old.
3. **Face too small in the frame.** Under 80% face coverage — the studio should frame head-and-shoulders tight.
4. **Ears hidden by long hair.** Tie hair back so both ears show clearly.
5. **Non-white background.** Curtains, walls, car interiors. White only.
6. **Selfie with filters.** Beautified, smoothened, reshaped — the verification system reads this as tampering.
7. **Scanning a laminated photo.** Glare and reflection distort the scan; use the unlaminated print.
8. **Wrong file size or format.** PNG, HEIC or a 2 MB scan — compress to JPG within the limit first.
9. **Writing the name and date by hand on the print.** Get the studio to print them; handwriting on the photo can itself look like tampering.

## Pre-upload checklist: do this the week before the form opens

- [ ] Visit a photo studio; ask for "NTA exam form photos" — studios near any exam coaching centre know the template
- [ ] White background, head-and-shoulders, 80% face, ears visible
- [ ] Candidate's name and the date printed below the image by the studio
- [ ] Scan at 200 DPI, save as JPG, check file size sits in the **50–200 KB** comfort zone
- [ ] Sign on white paper with a black pen (running hand, no capitals), scan at 4–30 KB JPG

## FAQs

**What is the passport photo file size required for AISSEE forms?**

For AISSEE 2026, the candidate's photograph had to be a JPG between 10 KB and 200 KB. NTA's general guidance across its exams allows 10 KB to 300 KB. The AISSEE 2027 bulletin will fix the exact figure when it releases — keep your scan in the 50–200 KB range to be safe on either side.

**Does the photo need my name and the date written on it?**

For most NTA exams including JEE and NEET, yes — the candidate's name and the date the photograph was taken must appear on the photo, typically printed below the image by the photo studio. Do not hand-write it yourself on an old print.

**How much of my face should show in the NTA photo?**

About 80% of the face should be visible, with both ears clearly showing, against a plain white background. No cap, no mask, no sunglasses — and no Polaroid or computer-generated images.

**Can the photo be an old one from my school album?**

Risky. NTA expects a recent photograph — NEET guidance requires it to be no older than a few months, and re-upload notices are common for clearly old photos. Get a fresh studio photo for the form season.

**What are the signature upload specs for NTA forms?**

Sign in running hand on white paper with a black pen — capital letters are not allowed. For AISSEE 2026 the signature had to be a JPG between 4 KB and 30 KB. Sign naturally and match it at the exam centre; it is compared on the attendance sheet.

**What happens if my uploaded photo does not meet the specs?**

NTA flags non-compliant photos and asks for a re-upload through a message on your registered email and mobile number — a delayed or missed correction notice can lead to rejection of the application. It may also be treated as use of unfair means if the photo appears tampered or fabricated.
