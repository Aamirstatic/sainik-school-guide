## The final 80 days are a different exam

Eighty days to the AISSEE 2027 exam. That is eleven weeks and a bit — long enough to change a result, short enough that a wrong turn now is hard to undo.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: most children who miss the cutoff in January did not fail because they started late. They failed because the final stretch was spent doing the wrong things, energetically. A parent from Kanpur called us last month — her daughter had solved four guidebooks cover to cover and still scored 190 in a mock. The books were not the problem. The plan was.

## The five mistakes, and their fixes

| Mistake | What it quietly costs | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Treating the form as "later" | No form, no exam — however good the mock scores are | Submit before 2 December; save the confirmation page the same day |
| 2. Piling on new material | Half-learned chapters crowd out the ones that were nearly solid | Freeze the syllabus by mid-December; revise, don't expand |
| 3. Mock tests without review | The same errors repeat in every paper, invisible to everyone | Log every wrong answer; revisit the log before the next mock |
| 4. Ignoring the 2 PM slot | A sleepy, heavy-lunch afternoon on exam day | Practise full papers at 2 PM weekly; light lunch, early night before |
| 5. Turning the pressure up | A tense, tired child who stops trying rather than trying harder | One calm conversation a week; praise effort, never compare with others |

## Mistake 1: The form is still "for later"

The application window closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**, and the correction window runs **7–9 December**. After that, the form is frozen. We keep saying this because every year, a handful of families discover on 3 December that the photo was rejected or the payment failed — and the late-fee window (3–6 December, plus Rs. 500) becomes their only option.

Do it this week. Documents first — photograph, signature, thumb impression in the specified sizes, domicile and category certificates if they apply — then the form, then the fee (Rs. 850 General/OBC/defence wards, Rs. 700 SC/ST), then print the confirmation page and put it somewhere safe. One evening of admin buys you 80 days of peace. Our [two-deadlines explainer](/news/aissee-2027-20-days-left-form-vs-prep-two-deadlines-2026-11-12/) lays out the form track and the prep track side by side.

## Mistake 2: More new chapters, less revision

With 80 days left, the instinct is to cover more — one more chapter, one more guidebook, one more YouTube series. Honestly, most guidebooks overcomplicate this. By mid-December your child should be revising, not discovering.

The pattern is fixed and known: Class VI is **150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks** (Maths 50 questions/150 marks, Language 25/50, Intelligence 25/50, GK 25/50). Class IX is **180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks**, in English only. Qualifying marks are **25% in each section and 40% in aggregate** (not applicable to SC/ST). None of that rewards breadth you cannot hold — it rewards the chapters your child can answer correctly under time pressure. Freeze new material by mid-December and spend the last six weeks on revision, previous papers and the weak topics the mock-test log exposes.

## Mistake 3: Mock tests that nobody analyses

A mock test your child takes and forgets is just a stressful afternoon. The value is entirely in the hour after: which questions went wrong, and why — a silly calculation, a misread question, a topic never revised, or time running out?

Keep an error log. One notebook, one line per mistake: question type, reason, the correct method. Before the next mock, your child reads the log, not a new chapter. Our [mock-test calendar for the final 80 days](/blog/aissee-2027-mock-test-calendar-final-80-days/) and the [exam-hall time plan](/blog/aissee-2027-exam-hall-time-plan-split-150-180-minutes-section-wise/) give this a shape: one full timed paper a week through December, two a week in January, always reviewed the same day.

## Mistake 4: Preparing for a morning exam that happens at 2 PM

This one is easy to miss. AISSEE 2027 is an afternoon exam — **2:00 to 4:30 PM for Class VI, 2:00 to 5:00 PM for Class IX**. A child who has only ever practised at 9 in the morning is walking into a different body clock: post-lunch drowsiness, a long sit, and fading concentration in the last half hour, exactly when the GK and Intelligence sections need a clear head.

The fix is free. Once a week, sit a full paper at 2 PM, at a desk, with an OMR sheet and a watch — no phone, no breaks, no "just five more minutes." Eat the kind of lunch your child will eat on 31 January (light, familiar, nothing new) and see how the afternoon feels. Small thing, real marks. Our [2 PM exam-aligned daily timetable](/blog/aissee-2027-daily-timetable-2pm-exam-aligned-plan/) is built around exactly this.

## Mistake 5: Turning the pressure up at home

This is the mistake that does the most damage and the one parents least want to hear about. In the final weeks, the house gets quieter, the comparisons get louder — "Sharma ji's son scored 260" — and the child starts associating study with fear instead of curiosity. A frightened child does not revise better. A frightened child revises less, sleeps worse, and blanks on questions they knew at home.

Watch for the signs: sleep getting shorter, appetite changing, tears over small mistakes, or the sudden "I don't want to go." We wrote a full guide to [burnout signs and what to do about them](/blog/aissee-2027-child-burnout-signs-last-82-days/) — read it before you need it. One calm conversation a week beats ten anxious check-ins a day. Praise the effort you can see ("you finished the whole paper on time") rather than the score you wish for. And keep one honest line ready: this exam matters, but it is one exam, and your child is more than its result.

## A simple rhythm for the next 80 days

- **Now to 2 December:** form submitted; daily study in 45-minute blocks; one mock a week.
- **3–9 December:** correction window if needed; freeze new material by mid-month.
- **Mid-December to 10 January:** revision cycles, error-log reviews, weak-topic drills.
- **11 January:** exam city slip — note the city, plan travel.
- **25 January:** admit card — download, print, verify every detail the same day.
- **26–30 January:** light revision only, OMR practice, early nights.
- **31 January, 2 PM:** the exam. Reach early, carry the admit card and ID, and attempt every question — there is **no negative marking** (CONFIRMED), so a guess costs nothing.

If your child does one thing differently after reading this, let it be the error log. It is the cheapest, least glamorous habit in exam preparation, and it is the one that turns 80 days of effort into marks on the day.

## FAQs

**How many hours a day should my child study in the final 80 days before AISSEE 2027?**

For a Class 6 child, about 2 to 3 focused hours a day on top of school, in 45-minute blocks with real breaks, works better than long exhausted sittings. Quality and consistency beat total hours — and sleep matters more than an extra chapter at 11 PM.

**Should my child attempt every question in AISSEE 2027?**

Yes. There is no negative marking in AISSEE (CONFIRMED, NTA pattern), so a wrong answer and a blank answer both score zero. Attempt every question, and use the last 10 minutes to fill any blanks you skipped.

**How many mock tests should my child take before 31 January 2027?**

One full timed paper a week through December, moving to two a week in January, is a sensible rhythm for most children. What matters more than the count is the review afterwards — every wrong answer should be logged and revisited, not just scored and forgotten.

**Is it too late to start AISSEE preparation with 80 days left?**

No, but the plan has to be honest. Eighty days is enough to cover the Class 6 syllabus once with NCERT and previous papers if your child studies daily. Skip the expensive crash course and start with the weakest subject first, not the favourite one.

**What if I find a mistake in my child's AISSEE 2027 form?**

The correction window runs from 7 to 9 December 2026. Log in to the official NTA portal and fix the details there. Do not wait for the admit card — by 25 January 2027, corrections are no longer possible.

**Does the 2 PM exam timing change how my child should prepare?**

Yes, a little. The AISSEE 2027 exam runs from 2:00 PM (Class VI to 4:30 PM, Class IX to 5:00 PM), so practise full papers at 2 PM at least once a week. A heavy lunch and a sleepy afternoon are the two most avoidable ways to lose marks.
