## December pressure is not September pressure

When we first wrote about exam pressure at the end of September, the AISSEE exam was 123 days away — far enough to feel abstract. Today it is **73 days** away. That changes the feeling completely, and if your 11-year-old seems more on edge this week, they are not imagining it.

December squeezes from three sides at once. First, the deadlines cluster: the **JNVST exam lands on 28 November**, and the **AISSEE form closes on 2 December** — two high-stakes dates in five days. Second, mock-test scores suddenly feel real. In October a low score was "just practice"; in December it feels like a prediction. Third, winter break is coming, and with it the relatives asking how the preparation is going — at exactly the age when a child hears that question as a judgement.

None of this means your child is failing. It means the calendar is doing what calendars do. The job now is not to add hours. It is to give the next 73 days a shape your child can hold in their head.

## The December pressure calendar

Pin this to the fridge. When your child asks "what happens next?", point at it instead of answering from memory — children calm down when the future has dates on it.

| Date | What happens | What to do — and what to stop worrying about |
|---|---|---|
| 28 Nov 2026 | JNVST Class 6 exam, 11:30 AM–1:30 PM | Revise, sleep early the night before, reach by 10:30 AM. After it, stop thinking about it — the result comes in March. |
| 2 Dec 2026 | AISSEE form deadline, 11:50 PM | Submit by 30 November if you can. Check the confirmation page the same day. Do not leave it to the last evening. |
| 3–6 Dec 2026 | Late window, ₹500 extra fee | The safety net, not the plan. If a payment failed, this is your second chance. |
| 7–9 Dec 2026 | Correction window | Fix name, photo or detail mistakes. Nothing else changes here. |
| Late Dec | Winter break | Revision, not new chapters. One mock a week, then rest. |
| 11 Jan 2027 | Exam city announced | Check the city, plan travel if needed. The city is not the centre. |
| 25 Jan 2027 | Admit card out | Download, print two copies, check every detail the same evening. |
| 31 Jan 2027 | AISSEE exam, 2:00 PM | Class 6: 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks. No negative marking — attempt everything. |

Notice what is missing from that table: any week where your child is supposed to study ten hours a day. There isn't one, because that week does not exist in a plan that works.

## Three December traps — and how to walk around them

**Trap 1: the 2 December panic.** In the last week of November, the form eats the whole household. Parents refresh the portal at midnight; children sense the tension and assume something is wrong with them. Flip the order: finish the form by 30 November, print the confirmation page, and then tell your child, out loud, "the form is done, now it's just the exam." One sentence, said once, removes a whole layer of worry.

**Trap 2: the bad mock spiral.** A low mock score in December hurts more than the same score in October, and the usual response — three extra hours the next day — makes the next mock worse, because the child walks in tired. Break the loop differently: after a bad mock, spend one evening on *why* the marks went, not on more questions. Was it silly mistakes? Slow maths? One weak chapter? Fix one cause, then take the next mock. A child who fixes one thing a week is improving, whatever the total says. Our guide to that kind of analysis is here: [AISSEE Maths Speed: The 7 Traps That Eat Your Minutes](/blog/aissee-maths-speed-7-traps-that-eat-your-minutes/).

**Trap 3: the winter-break cram.** Relatives visit, cousins compare notes, and suddenly the break becomes a 10-hour study camp. It backfires every year — by mid-January the child is exhausted and the revision hasn't stuck. Keep winter break boring and steady: two focused hours in the morning, one mock a week, and the rest of the day for play, sleep and family. The exam rewards a fresh child, not a tired one.

## The 10-minute daily reset

Pressure builds in the body before it shows up in words. This takes ten minutes, every evening after study, and it works better than any lecture about staying calm:

- **Two minutes of movement** — a walk around the house, ten jumping jacks, anything that isn't sitting.
- **Three minutes of talking about anything except the exam** — school gossip, cricket, a funny video. The topic doesn't matter; the break does.
- **Five minutes of tomorrow, on paper** — write down the three things to do tomorrow, in order. A child who can see tomorrow in three lines sleeps better than one carrying it in their head.

Do it at the same time every day. The routine itself becomes the calm — children trust what repeats.

## When pressure becomes a problem

Most December pressure is normal, and the calendar above plus the reset will carry most children through it. But watch for the line where it stops being normal: sleep that stays broken for more than a week, stomach aches or headaches with no medical cause, withdrawing from friends and family, or panic before every mock. If you see those, don't wait for January — talk to the school counsellor now, and read our guide to the warning signs: [AISSEE 2027: Child Burnout Signs in the Last 82 Days](/blog/aissee-2027-child-burnout-signs-last-82-days/).

And one honest thing, parent to parent: your child is eleven. They will remember how December *felt* long after they forget their mock scores. A calm house in December is not soft parenting. It is the preparation.

## FAQs

**How many hours should an 11-year-old study daily in December for AISSEE 2027?**

Two to three focused hours on school days, in 40 to 50 minute blocks with real breaks, is enough for most Class 6 aspirants. December is not the month to add hours — it is the month to protect sleep, keep one mock a week, and fix the weakest section. Ten exhausted hours beat nobody.

**My child also has the JNVST exam on 28 November. How do we handle both exams?**

Treat 28 November as a rehearsal, not a second crisis. The JNVST paper is 2 hours with no negative marking, and the AISSEE paper comes two months later on 31 January 2027. In the JNVST week, revise JNVST-style questions in the morning and keep one short AISSEE maths drill in the evening. After 28 November, the whole focus returns to AISSEE.

**My child's last mock test score was bad. Should we panic?**

No. One mock score is a diagnosis, not a verdict. Look at which section lost the marks and why — silly mistakes, slow maths, or a weak chapter. Fix one cause before the next mock. A child who improves one section a week is on track, whatever the total says today.

**Is it okay to take a full day off from study in December?**

Yes — a planned day off is part of preparation, not a break from it. Sundays work best: no new chapters, no mock, just rest, play and family time. An unplanned collapse after three weeks of non-stop study costs far more than one planned rest day.

**What if my child says they don't want to do AISSEE anymore?**

Listen first, don't argue. Often it means 'I am tired' or 'I am scared of failing', not 'I have decided'. Ask what part feels worst, shrink that one part, and give them a small win the same day. If the feeling lasts more than two weeks, or comes with sleep trouble and withdrawal, talk to the school counsellor.
