Parents ask us this every week: *"My brother's son is in Bihar, mine is in UP — whose exam is harder?"* Fair question. Here is the honest answer first: **no official "toughest state" ranking exists.** NTA and the Sainik Schools Society publish cutoffs school by school, class by class, quota by quota — never as a state league table.

But that doesn't mean we can't answer it. The published AISSEE 2026 closing marks tell a clear story. Let me walk you through the real numbers and what they mean for your child.

## The two cutoffs parents keep mixing up

Before the comparison, get this distinction right — it is the single most misunderstood fact in AISSEE admissions.

| Concept | What it is | Class 6 value | Class 9 value | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Qualifying marks** | Minimum to enter the merit list and e-counselling | 25% per section + 40% aggregate (120 of 300) | 25% per section + 40% aggregate (160 of 400) | ✅ CONFIRMED (2026 bulletin, expected for 2027) |
| **Closing cutoff** | Marks of the *last admitted candidate* at a school, per class/quota/category | Varies by school (up to ~285 of 300) | Varies by school (up to ~370+ of 400) | ✅ CONFIRMED (published 2026 lists) |

Qualifying is the entry gate. The closing cutoff is the real door — and at popular schools it sits dramatically higher than the gate. SC/ST candidates are exempt from the qualifying requirement at the 33 established schools (but not at New Sainik Schools), per the official bulletin.

## What the real 2026 numbers show: Mainpuri (Uttar Pradesh)

Sainik School Mainpuri is in Uttar Pradesh and draws enormous interest — UP is the country's largest applicant pool, and the UP home quota is fiercely contested. Here are the published AISSEE 2026 closing marks for Mainpuri's home-state quota:

**Class 6 (out of 300), AISSEE 2026:**

| Category | Girls marks | Girls AI rank | Boys marks | Boys AI rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home Unreserved | 285 | 126 | 276 | 827 |
| Home OBC | 283 | 219 | 271 | 1,587 |
| Home Defence | 274 | 1,107 | 267 | 2,458 |
| Home SC | 268 | 2,165 | 259 | 4,669 |
| Home ST | 150 | 58,060 | 204 | 25,314 |

**Class 9 (out of 400), AISSEE 2026:**

| Category | Girls marks | Boys marks |
|---|---|---|
| Home Unreserved | — (girls not in this intake slice) | 364 |
| Home SC | — | 362 |

Look at that girls' unreserved figure for Class 6: **285 out of 300 — a 95% score, and an all-India rank of 126.** That is the level at which a girl from UP closes the door behind her. Boys' unreserved closed at 276 (rank 827) — 92%. Meanwhile the qualifying mark was 120. The gap between "eligible" and "admitted" is the whole story.

## The three-year trend: the bar keeps rising

The same school's home unreserved Class 6 closing marks tell a story parents should take seriously:

| Year | Girls | Boys |
|---|---|---|
| 2024 (Round 1) | 277 | 271 |
| 2025 | 276 | 280 |
| 2026 | 285 | 276 |

Three years, one consistent message: **the closing cutoff at a popular school stays parked in the 270–285 band, not the 120-qualifying band.** Year-to-year wobble is small; the band itself is the signal. Nothing about this pattern suggests 2027 will be easier — AISSEE 2027 applications are expected in Oct–Nov 2026 and the exam is tentatively set for 31 January 2027 (per the NTA exam calendar published 16 Sept 2026).

## So which state is toughest? The surprise answer

Here's where the story gets interesting — and why you should distrust anyone who claims a fixed ranking. I pulled a second school's published numbers, **Sainik School Nalanda (Bihar)**, for the same 2025 admission cycle as Mainpuri:

| School, Class 6 (2025), Home Unreserved | Girls | Boys |
|---|---|---|
| Mainpuri (Uttar Pradesh) | 276 | 280 |
| Nalanda (Bihar) | 289 | 287 |

Nalanda's 2025 numbers — **CONFIRMED** from the same published list source (Careers360, from NTA school-wise merit lists; page updated 17 Sept 2026) — are *higher* than Mainpuri's in the same year: girls 289 vs 276, boys 287 vs 280. Yet in 2026, Mainpuri's girls' unreserved closed at 285, a jump from its own 2025 figure. So "which state is toughest?" doesn't have one answer — it shifts by school, year, class and category.

With the data above in hand, here's what can — and can't — be said:

**The state doesn't set the cutoff; the school does.** Within one state, cutoffs differ by school, round, class and category. Two schools in the same state can have closing marks 30 marks apart.

**But states with bigger applicant pools do face harsher competition.** Uttar Pradesh's home quota is contested by one of the largest candidate pools in the country, which is why Mainpuri's 2026 numbers look so steep — a girl needed rank 126 nationally to claim the last unreserved home seat. States where demand is thinner relative to seats give parents more breathing room. That is the mechanism; "toughness" is really applicant-to-seat pressure, not a fixed state ranking.

**The 67/33 split is the hidden multiplier.** 67% of seats go to the school's home state and 33% to all other states combined. That means the other-state quota is effectively a *national* competition for one-third of the seats — which is why other-state closing marks often land as high as, or higher than, the home-state figures for the same school. This is the single biggest lever in where you stand.

**Girls' competition is its own league.** Girls' seats are capped (10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, at the 33 established schools), so girls' closing marks consistently run above boys' in the same category — 285 vs 276 at Mainpuri in 2026.

## What to do with this: your 5-point checklist

1. **Pull your school's last-year list.** NTA publishes school-wise, category-wise merit lists with each round. Find *your* school, *your* class, *your* quota (home/other), *your* category — that one number is your real target, not a state's average.
2. **Add a buffer of 10–15 marks** over last year's closing figure. Trends at popular schools creep upward; Mainpuri's girls' unreserved rose from 277 (2024) to 285 (2026).
3. **Treat 92%+ as the safe zone for Class 6** at high-demand schools. "Just qualify" (40%) is a counselling ticket, not a seat.
4. **Remember the quota math.** If your child qualifies for the home-state quota, that is usually the least competitive door. Other-state candidates need to plan for even higher marks.
5. **For Class 9, the bar is 350+ of 400** at schools like Mainpuri (2026 boys' unreserved: 364). Start prep at least a year out.

## FAQs

**Which state has the toughest Sainik School cutoff?**

There is no official state-wise ranking. Cutoffs are published per school, class, quota and category. States with the largest applicant pools — Uttar Pradesh being the clearest example — show the steepest closing marks; at Sainik School Mainpuri in 2026, girls' home unreserved closed at 285/300 (rank 126).

**What was the Sainik School Mainpuri cutoff for Class 6 in 2026?**

For the home-state quota: Home Unreserved — girls 285 (rank 126), boys 276 (rank 827); Home OBC — girls 283, boys 271; Home Defence — girls 274, boys 267; Home SC — girls 268, boys 259; Home ST — girls 150, boys 204.

**Is the Sainik School cutoff the same as the qualifying marks?**

No. Qualifying is 25% in each section and 40% aggregate (Class 6: 120 of 300; Class 9: 160 of 400). The actual selection cutoff — the last admitted candidate's score — is far higher at popular schools.

**Why is the other-state quota cutoff sometimes higher?**

Only 33% of seats are reserved for other states, so many more applicants compete per seat in that quota than in the 67% home-state quota. This national competition pushes other-state closing marks up.

**Can I get a Sainik School seat with just qualifying marks?**

Qualifying marks make you eligible for the merit list and e-counselling (AISSAC), but selection is by rank. At most established schools the final admitted candidate scores well above the qualifying marks — so plan your prep target from last year's closing cutoff, not the qualifying line.
