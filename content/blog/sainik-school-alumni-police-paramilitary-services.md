> **Last verified: 3 November 2026.** The entry routes described here are the standard public recruitment routes for India's police and paramilitary services. Exact age limits, physical standards and vacancy details change with each notification — always check the official UPSC, SSC or state recruitment notification for the year you apply, and treat anything in this guide as orientation, not a substitute for it.

## The uniform does not have to be olive green

Ask a Class 6 cadet what he wants to become and you will usually hear "Army officer." Ask a passing-out batch and the answers spread out: Army, Navy, Air Force — and, quietly, a good number who end up in khaki.

That surprises some parents. It shouldn't. A Sainik School day — early PT, assembly, house duties, games in the evening, seniors responsible for juniors — is essentially a rehearsal for the life of a police or paramilitary officer. The discipline is the same. The physical standard is the same. The habit of taking responsibility for a group of people is the same. Only the employer changes.

So if your child is drawn to uniformed service but the armed forces route does not work out, or simply is not their first choice, here is the honest map.

## The routes, from officer level down

| Route | Entry exam | Leads to | What it demands |
|---|---|---|---|
| IPS officer | UPSC Civil Services Examination (after graduation) | Indian Police Service, posted across state police and central organisations | Degree, a very competitive written exam and interview |
| State police officer (DySP and above) | State Public Service Commission exam | State police service, rising to senior ranks | Degree, state-level written exam, physical standards |
| CAPF Assistant Commandant | UPSC CAPF (AC) Examination | Officer posts in CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP, SSB | Degree, written exam, physical and medical tests, interview |
| Sub-Inspector | SSC CPO / state police recruitment | SI in Delhi Police, CAPFs or state police | Degree (for most SI posts), written exam, physical tests |
| Constable / GD | SSC GD Constable / state police recruitment | Constable in CAPFs or state police | Class 10 or 12 depending on the post, written exam, physical tests |

Two things to notice. First, none of these routes reserves seats for Sainik School alumni — the school gives an edge in preparation, not in selection. Anyone promising otherwise is selling something. Second, the officer-level routes all need a bachelor's degree first, which means the real preparation window opens after school, not during it.

## What Sainik School actually contributes

It is easy to overclaim here, so let's be precise about what transfers and what doesn't.

**Fitness becomes a habit, not a crash course.** A boy who has run, drilled and played games for six or seven years does not panic when a physical test is announced. He has a base to build on. That is a genuine advantage — but the standards (running times, measurements, medical fitness) are fixed and identical for everyone, and no school certificate waives them.

**Leadership is practised, not theorised.** House captains, prefects and team captains learn to manage people younger than them, handle complaints, and take blame when their house loses a competition. Our guide to [cadet leadership ranks](/blog/sainik-school-cadet-leadership-ranks-house-captain-school-captain/) describes how those roles work. An interview board notices that kind of record quickly — not because of the title, but because the candidate can talk about real situations instead of rehearsed answers.

**Comfort with hierarchy and hardship.** Hostel life, fixed routines, limited comforts — a cadet who has lived that way adapts faster to police academy training than someone meeting regimentation for the first time at twenty-one.

**What doesn't transfer:** academics still have to be earned. UPSC and state exams are won on general studies, reasoning and language, not on drill. A cadet who coasts academically because he is good at PT will find the written exam unforgiving.

## A realistic timeline for a cadet

- **Classes 6–10:** Build the base. Stay fit, keep academics strong, take on house and team responsibilities. Do not start UPSC-style preparation — it is far too early and it burns children out.
- **Classes 11–12:** Choose subjects on interest and strength, not on rumours about which stream "suits" police exams. Any stream can lead to a degree and then to these exams.
- **Graduation years:** This is when serious preparation begins — general studies, current affairs, reasoning, and physical standards maintained alongside study.
- **Final year of graduation:** First serious attempts at the relevant exam, with the physical tests trained for, not hoped for.

Honestly, most alumni who make it into these services will tell you the same thing: school gave them the temperament, graduation gave them the knowledge, and the exam itself was won in the last two years of hard, boring, daily preparation.

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Assuming NDA failure ends the uniform dream.** It doesn't. The police and CAPF routes are separate doors, and many officers walked through them after an NDA attempt that didn't work out.
2. **Chasing coaching too early.** A Class 8 student does not need a civil services foundation course. He needs good marks, good fitness and a childhood that isn't entirely consumed by exams.
3. **Ignoring the medical standards.** Eyesight, height, chest and general medical fitness are part of selection. Get a routine check-up during school years so there are no surprises later.
4. **Trusting agents.** No one can buy a police or CAPF seat. Recruitment runs through UPSC, SSC and state bodies only, and the process is public.

## The bottom line

Sainik School does not manufacture police officers. What it does, quietly and over years, is produce young people who are comfortable with discipline, responsibility and physical effort — which happens to be exactly the raw material the police and paramilitary services need. The exams still have to be cleared on merit, the degree still has to be earned, and the physical tests still have to be passed. But a cadet starts that race a few steps ahead, and in a competition measured in marks and seconds, a few steps matter.

## FAQs

**Can a Sainik School student become an IPS officer?**

Yes, through the same route as anyone else: a bachelor's degree followed by the UPSC Civil Services Examination, with IPS as a service preference. Sainik School itself does not reserve IPS seats; it prepares students for the competition.

**What is the CAPF Assistant Commandant route?**

The UPSC conducts the Central Armed Police Forces (Assistant Commandants) Examination for officer posts in forces like the CRPF, BSF, CISF, ITBP and SSB. It involves a written exam, physical and medical standards tests and an interview.

**Does Sainik School training help in police physical tests?**

It helps a great deal with fitness, stamina and drill, but it does not exempt anyone from the tests. Every candidate still has to clear the same running, physical measurement and medical standards as other applicants.

**Is NDA the only way from Sainik School into uniformed service?**

No. NDA leads to the armed forces as officers. The police and paramilitary services have their own separate recruitment exams, and many alumni enter through those instead, or alongside an NDA attempt.

**When should a cadet start preparing for police services?**

The serious preparation starts after school, during graduation, since most officer-level exams require a degree. School years are best used for fitness, academics and leadership roles that build the record and the habits.
