## The headline rule: no refund, no carry-forward

The AISSEE information bulletin says it in plain language: **"The examination fee once deposited will not be refunded. Fee will neither be carried forward to a future examination nor refunded."** Applications once submitted cannot be withdrawn, and the NTA's published FAQ is equally blunt: *"You can neither withdraw your application nor get refund of the application fee paid."*

Read that twice before you pay. Because once the money leaves your account and the confirmation page generates, it belongs to the exam — whatever happens next.

A parent asked me last year whether the fee comes back if the child falls ill on exam day. I wish I could say yes. The answer is no.

## The fee scenarios, honestly laid out

| Situation | Refund? | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Form submitted and confirmed | No — non-refundable | Nothing. Move on to preparation. |
| Form rejected (wrong documents, errors) | No — non-refundable | Fix and reapply only if the window is still open; the old fee is gone. |
| Child found ineligible (age, class) | No — non-refundable | Confirm eligibility **before** paying — this is the costliest mistake. |
| Child can't appear on exam day | No — non-refundable | No refund and no carry-forward to next year's exam. |
| Paid twice by mistake (duplicate payment) | ✅ Yes — auto-refund after bank reconciliation | Keep both transaction references; wait 5–7 working days. Don't pay a third time. |
| Payment deducted, no confirmation page | ✅ Usually returned automatically | Wait; check application status first. Only retry once reconciliation settles. |
| Answer-key challenge fee | No — non-refundable per question | Challenge only the questions you have strong evidence for. |

Two of these rows deserve a closer look, because they're where parents lose money unnecessarily.

## The two cases where money DOES come back

**Duplicate payments.** If you clicked "pay" twice and both transactions went through, don't panic. NTA's bank reconciliation catches duplicate payments and sends the extra money back to the original payment source — typically within **5 to 7 working days**. Save both transaction references and screenshots. Do not pay a third time thinking the second one didn't count — that's how a ₹1,700 problem becomes a ₹2,550 one. Our detailed guide on [what to do when the AISSEE fee payment fails](/blog/aissee-fee-payment-failed-what-to-do/) walks through the full wait-versus-retry logic.

**Deducted but no confirmation page.** This is the scary one: money gone from your account, but no confirmation page. Here's the thing — mere deduction is not proof of payment. If NTA's system didn't record the payment, reconciliation usually returns the amount automatically. Wait before retrying, check your application status on the portal, and only then attempt payment again. The full checklist for this situation is in our [fee-payment troubleshooting guide](/blog/aissee-fee-payment-failed-what-to-do/).

## How to make sure you never need a refund

Honestly, the cheapest refund policy is the one you never have to use. Before the form opens:

- **Confirm the age window first.** Class 6: projected born 1 April 2015 – 31 March 2017; Class 9: born 1 April 2012 – 31 March 2014. The final 2027 dates arrive in the bulletin, but if your child is clearly outside the window, don't pay and hope. The fee won't come back.
- **Fill slowly, pay last.** The bulletin is explicit that incomplete or incorrect applications "are liable to be rejected without any further intimation" — and rejected forms still cost you the fee. Our [application form guide](/blog/aissee-application-form-2027-guide/) covers the exact order to follow.
- **Pay on a reliable connection, once.** Most double payments happen on patchy networks where parents hit "retry" on a pending transaction. One payment, one confirmation page, done.
- **Keep the receipts.** Save the confirmation page, the transaction ID and the payment screenshot. If a refund is ever due, these are your evidence.

## FAQs

**Is the AISSEE application fee refundable?**

No. Per the NTA information bulletin, the examination fee once deposited will not be refunded, and it cannot be carried forward to a future exam. The NTA FAQ adds that an application cannot be withdrawn and the fee cannot be refunded.

**What if my form is rejected or my child turns out ineligible — do I get the fee back?**

No. NTA does not refund the fee even if the form is rejected or the candidate is found ineligible. Confirm age and eligibility before paying.

**I paid the AISSEE fee twice by mistake. Will I get a refund?**

Duplicate payments are typically refunded automatically to the original payment source after bank reconciliation, usually in 5 to 7 working days. Keep both transaction references and do not pay a third time.

**My payment was deducted but no confirmation page appeared. Should I pay again?**

Not immediately. A deducted payment without a confirmation page usually means the transaction didn't complete on NTA's side; reconciliation typically returns the money automatically. Wait, check your application status first, and only then retry.

**Is the answer-key challenge fee refundable?**

No. The per-question challenge fee is non-refundable even if your objection is accepted and the answer key is corrected.

**Can I withdraw my AISSEE application and get the money back?**

No. Applications once submitted cannot be withdrawn, and the fee cannot be refunded. This is stated explicitly in the NTA FAQ.
