# AISSEE Application Form: Photo and Signature Requirements

> **🔄 Last verified: 30 September 2026** — Specs below are ✅ **CONFIRMED** from NTA's AISSEE information bulletins (2026 and earlier cycles), cross-checked across four independent sources. The AISSEE 2027 bulletin is 🔶 **NOT released yet** — the 2027 specs are 🔶 **EXPECTED** to match, but verify against the new bulletin the day it drops.

Every year, a small number of AISSEE applications die for the most avoidable reason of all: a photo that was too big, a signature drawn in block letters, a thumb impression nobody thought to prepare. Not a tough paper. Not a missed cutoff. A JPEG.

With the AISSEE 2027 notification expected in October 2026 and the form window likely to last barely three weeks, you don't want to be compressing images at 11 PM on the last day. Here's everything NTA asks for — the exact numbers, how to get each file right at home, and the mistakes that get uploads rejected.

## The exact specifications (2026 cycle)

This table is the heart of the article. Save it, screenshot it, stick it on the fridge until the form is submitted.

| Upload | File size | Format | Key rule |
|--------|-----------|--------|----------|
| Candidate's photograph | **10 KB – 200 KB** | JPG / JPEG | Passport size, white background, face clearly visible |
| Candidate's signature | **4 KB – 30 KB** | JPG / JPEG | Full signature in running handwriting, white background |
| Left-hand thumb impression | **10 KB – 50 KB** | JPG / JPEG | Blue ink on white paper; right thumb allowed only if left is unavailable |
| Certificates (birth, domicile, caste/category, service) | **50 KB – 300 KB** | PDF | Must match the originals you'll show at document verification |

Two things to note honestly. First, these are the 2026-cycle bulletin specs — NTA has kept them stable for several years, so the 2027 bulletin will very likely repeat them, but treat the numbers as 🔶 **EXPECTED** until the new bulletin confirms them. Second, the certificate size range has varied slightly between cycles (some years 100–400 KB), so prepare your PDFs in the middle of the range and you'll be safe either way.

## The photograph: how to take a compliant one at home

You do not need a studio. You need a white wall and five minutes.

1. **Background:** plain white wall or a white bedsheet pinned up. No doors, no patterns, no shadows across the face.
2. **Lighting:** face a window in daylight. Never use flash — it washes out the face and creates the red-eye that makes photos look tampered with.
3. **Framing:** head and shoulders, face looking straight at the camera, both ears visible if possible. No cap, no goggles, no dark glasses. Religious head coverings are fine as long as the full face is visible.
4. **Expression:** neutral, mouth closed. This is an ID photo, not a school annual-day picture.
5. **Freshness:** use a recent photo — ideally taken within the last few months. The same photo gets pasted on the admit card and the exam-day attendance sheet, and the invigilator compares it with the child sitting in front of them.

Take the photo on a phone, then check the file size. A modern phone photo will be 2–4 MB — far too big. You'll compress it down (more on that below), but always keep the original safe too.

## The signature: the running-handwriting trap

This is where Class 6 applicants stumble most. NTA wants the **candidate's own signature in running handwriting** — not the parent's, not block capital letters, not just initials.

- Practice on white paper with a black or blue pen until the child has one consistent signature. It should look the same every time — the exam-day attendance sheet will carry this same signature.
- Sign inside a box roughly 4 cm × 2 cm, then scan or photograph just that area on a white background.
- Keep it between **4 KB and 30 KB**. This is a small file — signatures compress well because they're mostly white space. If yours is over 30 KB, you're scanning at too high a resolution; rescan at 150 DPI or photograph from slightly further away.

One more thing parents get wrong: the signature must be the **child's**, even for a 10-year-old whose signature is wobbly. A parent's confident signature on a child's form is a mismatch the system is designed to catch.

## The thumb impression: the upload everyone forgets

Yes, NTA asks for the candidate's **left-hand thumb impression** — 10 KB to 50 KB, JPG/JPEG. (If the left thumb is unavailable for any reason, the right thumb is accepted.)

Practical method: put a small amount of blue stamp-pad ink on the left thumb, press it once — firmly, without rolling — on clean white paper, and let it dry completely before scanning or photographing. Two attempts is usually enough; the ridges should be clearly visible, not a blue smudge. If you're photographing instead of scanning, hold the phone parallel to the paper to avoid distortion.

## The certificates: PDFs, not photos of paper

Birth certificate, domicile certificate, caste/category certificate (if applicable), and service certificate or PPO for defence-category applicants go up as **PDFs, 50–300 KB each**. The classic mistake is photographing a certificate and uploading the JPG. Convert to PDF first — every phone has a free "print to PDF" or document-scanner option — and check the size. A single-page certificate scanned at reasonable resolution lands comfortably inside the range.

And the golden rule from the bulletin itself: if the documents you upload can't be corroborated with the originals at admission time, the candidate is liable to be rejected and admission denied. Upload what you actually hold.

## File too big? File too small? Fix it in two minutes

**Too big** (the usual problem): open the image on your phone and use any free compressor, or take a screenshot of the image zoomed to fill the screen — screenshots are dramatically smaller than camera photos. Re-check the size before uploading.

**Too small** (under 10 KB for a photo): the image is probably too low-resolution to show the face clearly. Retake it rather than stretching it — an upscaled blurry photo fails the "clearly visible" test even if the kilobyte count passes.

**Wrong format**: phone cameras sometimes save as HEIC or PNG. Convert to JPG/JPEG before uploading — the portal expects JPG/JPEG for images and PDF for certificates, nothing else.

## Five rejections I've seen (and how to dodge each)

1. **The WhatsApp-forward photo.** A photo that's been forwarded three times is compressed to mush. Always use the original file.
2. **Sunglasses or cap in the photo.** Instant non-compliance. Plain face, plain background.
3. **Parent's signature.** The form wants the child's running-hand signature. Practice with them for ten minutes instead.
4. **Multiple applications.** One candidate, one form — NTA rejects all forms if a candidate submits more than one. (Confirmed across bulletins; don't test it.)
5. **Paying the fee but skipping the confirmation page.** If the confirmation page doesn't generate after payment, the transaction is treated as cancelled — download and print it, and keep the fee receipt. No confirmation page, no application.

## Pre-upload checklist

Run through this the evening before you fill the form — not during:

- [ ] Photograph: JPG/JPEG, 10–200 KB, white background, recent, no cap/goggles
- [ ] Signature: JPG/JPEG, 4–30 KB, child's own running handwriting, white background
- [ ] Thumb impression: JPG/JPEG, 10–50 KB, left thumb, blue ink, ridges visible
- [ ] Certificates: PDF, 50–300 KB each, matching the originals exactly
- [ ] Child's name and date of birth match the birth certificate letter-for-letter
- [ ] One active email ID and mobile number (the parents') for all NTA communication
- [ ] Fee amount ready: 🔶 expected ₹850 (General/OBC-NCL/defence wards) or ₹700 (SC/ST) — 2026 rates, confirm in the 2027 bulletin
- [ ] After payment: confirmation page downloaded, printed, and filed

## FAQs

**What is the photo size required for the AISSEE application form?**
10 KB to 200 KB in JPG or JPEG format, passport-size with a white background. This is the 2026-cycle specification from NTA's information bulletin; the 2027 bulletin is expected to keep the same limits.

**What are the signature specifications for AISSEE?**
4 KB to 30 KB in JPG or JPEG format, full signature in running handwriting on a white background. Block letters and initials-only signatures are the most common reason for rejection at upload.

**Is a thumb impression required for the AISSEE form?**
Yes — the candidate's left-hand thumb impression, 10 KB to 50 KB in JPG or JPEG format. If the left thumb is unavailable, the right-hand thumb impression may be used.

**Can I use a phone camera photo for the AISSEE application?**
Yes, if it meets the specs: plain white background, face clearly visible, no cap or goggles, and the file compressed to between 10 KB and 200 KB in JPG/JPEG. A studio photo is not mandatory.

**What happens if my uploaded photo does not meet the specifications?**
NTA's portal can reject the upload outright, and a non-compliant photo can cause problems at admit-card verification and document verification later. Get it right before submitting — the correction window is short and never guaranteed.
