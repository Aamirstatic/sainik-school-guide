## The result is out. Your child is watching your face.

Results day is the one moment when a parent's reaction writes itself permanently into a child's memory. Long after the marks are forgotten, your son or daughter will remember what your face looked like when the news came. So this article starts with the most important advice we will ever give on this site: **the conversation you have tonight matters more than the exam.**

Most children who miss the AISSEE cutoff did not fail because they were careless. They missed because this is a genuinely hard exam — one seat for many applicants, a 300-mark paper for Class 6 and a 400-mark paper for Class 9, with qualifying marks of 25% per section plus 40% aggregate. Thousands of bright, hardworking children fall short every year. Your child is not the exception to competence; they are the norm of a tough competition.

## The first 24 hours: what to say and what not to say

Here is a simple table. On the left, things parents say with love but children hear with pain. On the right, what to say instead.

| Instead of this… | Say this… |
|------------------|-----------|
| "Why didn't you study harder?" | "I saw how hard you worked. That effort stays with you whatever the result." |
| "Do you know how much money we spent?" | "The money was an investment in your learning, not a ticket that had to pay out." |
| "Your cousin cleared it in his first try." | "Your journey is yours. Comparisons don't make anyone stronger." |
| "It's okay, forget it" (said too fast) | "It's okay to feel bad about this. I feel it too. Let's sit with it today." |
| Silence, or walking away | "I'm proud of you for attempting something this difficult." |

Three rules for result night:

1. **Let them feel it.** Don't rush to fix the sadness. "It's okay to feel bad" is the most honest sentence in this article.
2. **Separate the child from the outcome.** They did not fail as a person. An exam result is data, not a verdict on character.
3. **Do not start planning the future tonight.** Plan B deserves a fresh mind, not a wounded one. Sleep first.

## The most common re-attempt question: can they try again?

This is where facts help more than feelings. Here is the honest picture.

| Situation | Answer |
|-----------|--------|
| Child applying for Class 6, age 10–11 on 31 March of admission year | **Yes — can attempt again** next cycle, comfortably within the 10–12 age window |
| Child applying for Class 6, already 12 (upper edge) | May be ineligible next year — check the age calculation in the official bulletin when it releases |
| Child applying for Class 9, age 13–14 | **Yes — can attempt again** within the 13–15 window |
| Child applying for Class 9, already 15 | Likely last eligible attempt — time to widen the plan |
| AISSEE 2027 cycle | Notification not yet released (as of October 2026); the exam is tentatively scheduled for 31 January 2027 |

A very common pattern we see: the second attempt is the successful one. The child already knows the paper pattern, the OMR routine, the time pressure, the exam-hall feeling. A near-miss this year is often the best possible training for next year. And the application fee (Rs. 850 for General/OBC/Defence wards, Rs. 700 for SC/ST per the 2026 rates) is not refundable — but it was never wasted; it bought a full rehearsal.

If a re-attempt is on the table, here is our [parent's guide to the final 100 days of preparation](/blog/aissee-2027-final-100-days-parent-motivation-guide-2026-10-14/) — written for exactly the family that is restarting with determination.

## Plan B is not a consolation prize: real alternatives

A Sainik School seat is one door among several. If AISSEE didn't open it, these doors are worth a serious look — many families prepare for them with the same books and the same effort:

- **RMS CET** — Rashtriya Military Schools' Common Entrance Test, Class 6 and 9 entry, conducted by the IHQ of MoD (Army). [Compare AISSEE vs RMS CET here](/blog/aissee-vs-rms-cet-which-exam-is-tougher-2026-10-09/).
- **RIMC Dehradun** — Rashtriya Indian Military College, the oldest and most selective route to the NDA. See [Sainik School vs RIMC: which to pick](/blog/sainik-school-vs-rimc-which-to-pick/).
- **New PPP Sainik Schools** — the 100 new Sainik Schools being approved in partnership mode; many offer Class 6 admission through the same AISSEE result under the 40%/60% route criteria.
- **NDA later** — if your child is in Class 9 entry age now, remember the long game: clear Class 12 and attempt the UPSC NDA written, the way Sainik School cadets do every year.

Tell your child this clearly: **a plan B is a plan, not a punishment.** Children accept new goals quickly when parents present them with confidence instead of resignation.

## A one-week checklist for the family

Don't improvise this week. Follow a sequence:

- [ ] **Day 1 (result day):** Feelings only. No planning, no blame, no comparisons. Say the words from the table above.
- [ ] **Day 2–3:** Normal life. School, play, rest. Let the child decompress. You decompress too.
- [ ] **Day 4–5:** Gently talk about what the child enjoyed in preparation (many children genuinely liked the maths or GK). Note what worked and what didn't — without grading.
- [ ] **Day 6–7:** One calm family discussion. Two questions only: "Do you want to try again?" and "What else interests you?" Listen more than you speak.
- [ ] **Week 2:** School studies resume priority. Re-attempt preparation, if chosen, begins after 3–4 weeks, not before.

## What if it was the last eligible attempt?

This is the hardest case, and it deserves honesty. If age rules close the AISSEE door, grieve it properly — and then widen the frame. The qualities you built in your child during preparation — discipline, time management, the habit of sitting with a hard problem — are the actual prize. Those transfer to every exam that comes next: board exams, RMS, NDA, JEE, anything. The exam was a vehicle; the vehicle reached a different station, but the passenger is still moving forward.

## FAQs

**Can my child attempt AISSEE again next year?**

Yes, if they are still within the age window. Class 6 allows 10–12 years as on 31 March of the admission year, so a 10 or 11-year-old can comfortably attempt again; check the eligibility table in this article. A missed attempt is the most common reason for a successful second try.

**Is the AISSEE application fee refundable if my child didn't qualify?**

No. The NTA does not refund the AISSEE application fee once paid, whether or not the child clears the exam. Details in our fee guide: [is the AISSEE application fee refundable?](/blog/is-aissee-application-fee-refundable-2026-10-12/)

**What are good alternatives if my child didn't clear AISSEE?**

RMS CET (Rashtriya Military Schools), RIMC Dehradun, and the new PPP Sainik Schools offer parallel paths. Many are open to Class 6 entry and value similar preparation.

**Should I tell my child the exact marks they scored?**

Only if you frame it. A near-miss score is motivating; a low score can be shared later when the sting fades. What matters first is the message: effort is seen, and there is a next step.

**How long should I wait before starting preparation again?**

Give it a week of genuine rest, then restart with school studies only. Serious re-attempt preparation can begin after 3–4 weeks, once the disappointment has settled into determination.
