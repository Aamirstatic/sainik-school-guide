## India's smallest Sainik School intake — and why that matters

Here is the fact that shapes everything else in this guide: **Sainik School Jhansi has 50 Class 6 seats for 2027-28 — the smallest intake of any Sainik School in India.** Sujanpur Tira has 59, Imphal has 70. Every other school in the country takes more children than Jhansi does.

That is not a judgment on the school. Jhansi is one of the youngest Sainik Schools — it started only in August 2019 — and its small numbers reflect its size, not its quality. But it does change the arithmetic for your family in a very practical way. With 50 seats and the whole of Uttar Pradesh competing for the 34 home-state places, your child needs a strong score, not just a pass. The qualifying marks (25% in each section, 40% overall) will get your child nowhere here; the cut-off will be far above them. If Jhansi is your first choice, plan for that.

## A young school in a city of defiance

Jhansi needs no introduction to an Indian parent. Rani Lakshmibai, who led the 1857 resistance from the fort that still watches over the city, made this place a symbol of defiance. It is entirely fitting that a Sainik School stands here — and the school knows it. Its magazine is called *The Virangana*, after the warrior queen, and its mini-auditorium is named after Second Lieutenant Arun Khetarpal, PVC, the youngest recipient of the Param Vir Chakra, who died at 21 in the Battle of Basantar in 1971.

The school itself is new. It was sanctioned by the Sainik Schools Society in 2015, on the request of the Uttar Pradesh government, and it is the 31st Sainik School sanctioned by the Ministry of Defence. It started functioning on **2 August 2019 with 90 cadets of Class 6** — the first batch, in a school still being built around them. It is the second Sainik School in Uttar Pradesh; the first, at Mainpuri, was established the same year.

That youth cuts both ways, and you should weigh it honestly. On one side, the campus is still growing — the school's own plans speak of a Phase-II development with an international-size swimming pool, a larger auditorium, a multipurpose hall-cum-gymnasium and an assembly hall for 700 cadets. On the other side, a young school means small batches, a teacher-to-cadet ratio of about 1:19, and a staff that knows every child by name. For a ten-year-old leaving home for the first time, that second fact may matter more than the first.

The home-state boys' seats split by category as SC 4, ST 2, OBC-NCL 8, Defence 3 and General 12. The 67% home-state quota is the whole story here: 34 of the 50 Class 6 seats are reserved for Uttar Pradesh-domiciled children, competing only against each other. A child from Jhansi, Kanpur or Lucknow with a UP domicile is playing a very different game from a child applying from Madhya Pradesh or Delhi.

One detail the bulletin itself insists on: list Jhansi as a choice even if no vacancy is shown for your category. Seats open up when cadets leave, and they are redistributed by the reservation rules. Across the 33 established Sainik Schools there are 3,160 Class 6 seats this year — Jhansi's 50 are the smallest share of them.

A few things to note before you budget. First, this is the school's own fee document for **2025-26 — the latest year it has published** — and the amounts are marked "tentative", with actuals charged at year end. Fees rise about 10% a year under Sainik Schools Society rules, so the 2027-28 figure will be higher; confirm it from the school before paying. Second, the fee is paid in two instalments for a new admission: ₹1,17,023 first, then ₹63,030 (₹61,530 for SC/ST), through SBI Collect only. Third, the full fee is payable irrespective of scholarship — any state scholarship is adjusted the following year, so don't plan on it reducing the first bill.

For context, Jhansi sits in the middle of the pack. Goalpara, the cheapest of the 33, charges ₹1,77,560 for 2026-27; older schools like Purulia are above ₹2 lakh. Jhansi's 2025-26 figure will have risen by the time your child joins, but the order of magnitude is right: budget roughly ₹2 lakh for the first year, all in.

There is a dedicated NDA motivational hall, and the Local Board of Administration is chaired by the GOC of the 31 Armoured Division.

## The honest question: what has Jhansi actually achieved?

This is where a young school has to be judged differently from an old one. Goalpara can point to 200-plus officers over six decades. Jhansi cannot — its first batch joined in Class 6 in 2019 and has only just reached the end of school. There is no alumni record of commissioned officers yet, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

What exists is early evidence, and it is genuinely encouraging. The school's first Class 10 batch, whose CBSE results came out on 13 May 2024, produced two cadets — Aaradhya Dixit and Samarth Gupta — with 98% each, the first rank in Jhansi district, followed by Sushant and Abhay Yadav at 96.4%. And the school's own website announces that **19 of the 60 cadets who appeared qualified the NDA-II written examination in 2026**. Written qualification is not selection — the SSB interview still lies ahead — but 19 out of 60 from a school's first NDA cohort is a serious number.

So the honest way to put it: Jhansi has no history to lean on, but its first results point the right way. If you want a school with a proven NDA pipeline, an older school is the safer bet. If you want a small, young school where your child will be known by name and where the first results are strong, Jhansi is a real option — just go in with your eyes open about what "new" means.

## How to apply

Admission is through **AISSEE 2027 only** — there is no direct admission, no management quota, and no donation route. The form is open now on the official NTA portal, **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society**, and it closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**.

Eligibility is the same as everywhere: Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027 (born 1 April 2015 – 31 March 2017) and studying in Class 5; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 (born 1 April 2012 – 31 March 2014) and a passed Class 8. Girls are admitted in both classes — 10 seats in Class 6 and 2 in Class 9 this year — with a dedicated girls' hostel on campus. The application fee is ₹850 (General, OBC-NCL, Defence) or ₹700 (SC/ST), plus ₹500 if you use the late window of 3–6 December. After the exam come the answer key, the result, e-counselling on the AISSAC portal, and then the medical examination and document check.

Before you fill the form, read our [AISSEE 2027 age-limit guide](/blog/sainik-school-age-limit-2027-detailed-guide/) and our [guide to choosing which Sainik Schools to list](/blog/aissee-2027-form-which-sainik-schools-to-list-rank-order/) — with only 50 seats at Jhansi, the order of your choices matters as much as the score. And if your child is also sitting the JNVST today, our [Navodaya vs Sainik School comparison](/blog/navodaya-vs-sainik-school-which-to-choose-after-clearing-both/) will help if a choice has to be made later.

## Checklist: before you choose Jhansi

- [ ] Check the domicile rule: 34 of 50 Class 6 seats need a Uttar Pradesh domicile — make sure yours is in order before the form closes on 2 December.
- [ ] Budget the first year at roughly ₹2 lakh (2025-26 fee ₹1,79,853, rising ~10% a year), paid in two instalments through SBI Collect.
- [ ] Aim well above the qualifying marks — with 50 seats, the cut-off will sit far above 40% overall.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Jhansi have for 2027-28?**
Jhansi has 50 Class 6 seats — 40 for boys (29 Uttar Pradesh domicile and 11 other states) and 10 for girls (5 Uttar Pradesh and 5 other states) — and 12 Class 9 seats (10 boys and 2 girls). The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1. Its 50 Class 6 seats are the smallest intake of any Sainik School in India.

**When was Sainik School Jhansi founded?**
It was sanctioned by the Sainik Schools Society in 2015 and started functioning on 2 August 2019 with 90 cadets of Class 6. It is the 31st Sainik School in India and the second in Uttar Pradesh, after Mainpuri. The campus is at Shankargarh village, Bhagwantpura, about 12 km from Jhansi railway station.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Jhansi?**
Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8. There is no direct admission or management quota.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Jhansi?**
The school's own fee document for 2025-26 — the latest it has published — puts a new Class 6 cadet's first year at Rs 1,79,853 (Rs 1,78,353 for SC/ST), including tuition of Rs 1,16,923 and dietary charges of Rs 48,970. Fees rise about 10 percent a year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure from the school before paying.

**Does Sainik School Jhansi admit girls?**
Yes — girls have been admitted since the 2021-22 academic session. For 2027-28 there are 10 girls' seats in Class 6 (5 Uttar Pradesh and 5 other states) and 2 in Class 9, with a dedicated girls' hostel on campus.

**Has any Sainik School Jhansi cadet joined the NDA yet?**
Not yet as commissioned officers — the school is only seven years old and its first batch is still working through the pipeline. But the early signs are strong: 19 of the 60 cadets who appeared qualified the NDA-II written examination in 2026, according to the school's own website, and its first Class 10 batch topped Jhansi district in the 2024 CBSE results.
