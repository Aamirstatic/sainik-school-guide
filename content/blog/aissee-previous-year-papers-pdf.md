## The 5-Step Method to Use Previous Year Papers Effectively

Most students solve previous year papers the wrong way. They solve, check answers, calculate marks, and move on. This is a waste of a powerful preparation tool.

Here is the method that consistently produces top rankers:

### Step 1: Solve Without a Timer (Diagnostic Phase)

**When:** 3–4 months before the exam
**Paper:** Start with the oldest available paper (e.g., AISSEE 2021)

- Solve every question without any time pressure
- Do not guess — if you do not know a question, leave it
- After solving, check answers using the official NTA answer key
- **Create an error log** — write down every wrong answer with the topic name

**Purpose:** This gives you an honest baseline of where you stand.

### Step 2: Analyze and Categorize Your Errors

**When:** Immediately after Step 1
**Action:** Create a table like this:

| Topic | Questions Attempted | Questions Wrong | Error Type |
|-------|:------------------:|:--------------:|------------|
| Mensuration | 7 | 4 | Conceptual gap |
| Current Affairs | 6 | 5 | Lack of knowledge |
| Coding-Decoding | 3 | 1 | Careless mistake |
| Grammar (Tenses) | 4 | 3 | Conceptual gap |

**Error Types:**
- **Conceptual Gap** — You do not understand the topic. Need to study it from scratch.
- **Careless Mistake** — You knew the concept but made a silly error. Need more practice.
- **Lack of Knowledge** — Especially in GK. Need to read more current affairs.
- **Time Pressure** — Could not attempt. (This won't apply in Step 1 since no timer.)

### Step 3: Fix Your Weak Areas (Targeted Study)

**When:** 1–2 weeks after Step 1
**Action:** Based on your error log:

- **Conceptual gaps** → Study from NCERT textbooks. Watch YouTube explanations. Solve basic-level problems first.
- **Careless mistakes** → Practice 50 more questions from that topic. Focus on accuracy, not speed.
- **Lack of knowledge** → For GK, start reading a daily newspaper or current affairs summary. For other subjects, revise the relevant chapters.
- **Use the topic-wise tables above** to prioritize — focus on high-frequency topics first.

### Step 4: Solve With a Timer (Simulation Phase)

**When:** 2–3 months before the exam
**Paper:** AISSEE 2023 or 2024 paper

- Set a timer for **2 hours 30 minutes** (exact exam duration)
- Sit at a desk, no phone, no breaks — simulate real exam conditions
- Solve the entire paper in one sitting
- After the timer ends, check answers and calculate your score

**Compare with Step 1 score.** If you have improved by 30+ marks, your preparation is on track. If not, you need more focused study on weak areas.

### Step 5: Repeat and Refine

**When:** Final 1–2 months before the exam
**Papers:** AISSEE 2025 and 2026 papers (most recent = most relevant)

- Repeat Steps 2–4 with each paper
- Track your score improvement across papers
- By the time you solve the 2026 paper, you should be scoring **above the expected cutoff**

## Common Mistakes When Solving Previous Year Papers

Avoid these errors that waste your preparation time:

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | What to Do Instead |
|---------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Solving without checking answers** | You repeat the same errors without knowing | Always check answers immediately after solving |
| **Not timing yourself** | Exam pressure will slow you down significantly | Use a timer from the 2nd paper onwards |
| **Solving only 1–2 papers** | Not enough data to identify patterns | Solve at least 5 years (2022–2026) |
| **Ignoring GK in papers** | Easy marks being missed; GK is 50 marks in Class 6 | Study current affairs separately; do not skip GK questions |
| **Solving papers from before 2020** | Pattern changed significantly when NTA took over from Sainik Schools Society | Focus on 2021–2026 papers only |
| **Not maintaining an error log** | You cannot track improvement or identify weak areas | Create a detailed error log after every paper |
| **Solving the same paper twice** | You will remember answers, giving a false sense of improvement | Solve each paper only once; use fresh papers for re-attempts |
| **Skipping the Intelligence section** | Students think it is "common sense" — but it has specific patterns | Practice Intelligence questions separately; it is the easiest section to improve |

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Where can I download AISSEE previous year question papers for free?

You can download AISSEE previous year question papers for free from the **official NTA portal** at [exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society/](https://exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society/). Papers from 2021 to 2026 are available for both Class 6 and Class 9. Additionally, platforms like Ezyschooling, Sainik Guru, and coaching apps like Shaurya Bharat provide solved papers with answer keys. Always verify the paper with the official NTA answer key to ensure accuracy.

### How many previous year papers should I solve for AISSEE 2027?

You should solve at least **5 years of previous papers** (AISSEE 2022 to 2026) for thorough preparation. This covers 600+ unique questions for Class 6 and 750+ for Class 9, giving you comprehensive exposure to every topic that NTA tests. If time permits, solving all 6 available papers (2021–2026) is ideal. However, quality matters more than quantity — solve each paper with proper analysis, not just answer checking.

### Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?

No, there is **no negative marking** in AISSEE. This applies to both Class 6 and Class 9 exams. This means you should **attempt every question**, even if you are unsure of the answer. An educated guess has a 25% chance of being correct (since each question has 4 options), and there is no penalty for wrong answers. Never leave any question blank in the AISSEE exam.

### What is a good score in AISSEE previous year papers?

For Class 6, a **safe target score is 190+ out of 300** for General category students. For Class 9, aim for **260+ out of 400**. These scores put you well above the typical cutoff range. For reserved categories (OBC/SC/ST), the targets are lower — 150–170 for Class 6 and 200–230 for Class 9. However, always aim higher than the minimum cutoff to account for year-to-year variations in difficulty and competition.

### Are AISSEE previous year papers available in Hindi?

Yes, AISSEE previous year papers are available in **Hindi, English, and regional languages**. NTA provides question papers in multiple mediums. However, some students report that the **English medium papers** have more straightforward translations, while Hindi/regional language papers occasionally have minor translation inconsistencies. If your child is comfortable with English, it is advisable to practice with the English medium paper.
