# The 30-Day Revision Plan Before AISSEE 2027: A Week-by-Week Countdown to 31 January

> **🔄 Last verified: 28 September 2026** — Exam date **31 January 2027** is the tentative date listed in NTA's official Examination Calendar (notice of 16 September 2026); the AISSEE 2027 notification with the final date is still awaited. This plan is anchored to the last 30 days before the exam, whenever NTA finally fixes it — shift it with the date. ✅ **CONFIRMED:** AISSEE has no negative marking (per the NTA information bulletin pattern, [details here]({{< relref "aissee-negative-marking-honest-answer.md" >}})); Class VI paper is 150 minutes, Class IX is 180 minutes, both pen-and-paper OMR.

Every year I watch the same thing happen in January. A child who has studied honestly for a year starts the last month and quietly falls apart — not because the syllabus is unfinished, but because there is no *plan*. They re-read chapters they've already read, panic-attempt a new mock every day, and arrive at the OMR sheet exhausted.

The final 30 days before AISSEE 2027 don't need more effort. They need a different *kind* of effort: closure, rehearsal, and calm. Here's the four-week structure I've recommended to hundreds of families. It works because it's simple enough that a tired parent and a nervous 11-year-old can both follow it.

## The one rule of the final month

**Revise, don't re-learn.** If your child is starting new chapters in January, something went wrong in October. The last 30 days are for tightening what is already known — the only acceptable "new" work is finishing chapters that are 80% done.

## The 4-week countdown at a glance

| Week | Dates (EXPECTED, anchored to 31 Jan) | Theme | Daily focus | Mock |
|------|--------------------------------------|-------|-------------|------|
| Week 1 | 1–7 Jan 2027 | **Syllabus closure** | Finish half-done chapters; one formula sheet per subject | — |
| Week 2 | 8–14 Jan 2027 | **Full-syllabus sweep** | Timed topic drills; first full mock | Mock #1 (Sunday) |
| Week 3 | 15–21 Jan 2027 | **Weak-spot surgery** | Error-log drills only; second mock | Mock #2 (Sunday) |
| Week 4 | 22–31 Jan 2027 | **OMR rehearsal & taper** | Light revision; third + fourth mocks; sleep reset | Mock #3 (25th), Mock #4 (28th), exam (31st) |

### Week 1: Close the syllabus (1–7 Jan)

Goal: zero open chapters. Go through the [full syllabus list]({{< relref "aissee-2027-syllabus-class-6-class-9.md" >}}) topic by topic and mark each as *done*, *almost*, or *gap*. Spend the week only on the *almost* ones. End the week with one handwritten formula/concept sheet per subject — Maths formulas, English grammar rules, GK facts your child keeps mixing up. That sheet is what gets re-read in week 4; everything else goes in a cupboard.

**Daily rhythm:** two 90-minute sessions (morning + evening), no phone in between. Three hours of real study beats six hours of sitting.

### Week 2: Full-syllabus sweep + Mock #1 (8–14 Jan)

The sweep: 45-minute timed drills per subject, rotating daily. Speed matters now — your child has been studying at "thinking pace" for months, and the exam is at "OMR pace." The first full mock goes on Sunday, on an actual OMR sheet, at the exact exam timing (check the admit card when it arrives), with the full 150 or 180 minutes. Then the non-negotiable part: a **full error analysis** — every wrong answer gets tagged: *silly mistake*, *concept gap*, or *time pressure*.

### Week 3: Weak-spot surgery (15–21 Jan)

No full syllabus this week. Every drill comes from the error log of mocks #1 and earlier tests. If fractions and articles keep bleeding marks, the week is fractions and articles. This is the week the plan quietly does its magic — children improve fastest when they stop practising what they're already good at. Mock #2 on Sunday, same OMR conditions. Compare scores with mock #1; you want to see the *silly-mistake* count falling, even if the total hasn't moved.

### Week 4: Rehearsal and taper (22–31 Jan)

Two mocks — on the 25th and the 28th — and nothing harder than that. Mornings: the formula sheets from week 1. Evenings: one easy drill to keep the hand warm. The last three days are about the body more than the brain: sleep by 10 PM, no new material, one full OMR bubbling practice (bubbling speed alone costs children 5–8 marks — see our [OMR mistakes guide]({{< relref "silly-omr-mistakes-cost-sainik-school-seat.md" >}})). The day before the exam: no mock, no new anything. A walk, an early dinner, the documents in a folder by the door.

## The error log: your secret weapon

Buy a notebook on 1 January and label it **Mistakes I Will Not Repeat**. Every wrong answer from every mock goes in, with the *reason*. By the third week, patterns jump out — and patterns are fixable. Children who keep error logs almost always say the same thing after the exam: the paper felt familiar, because they'd already met every mistake before. For the full routine — five mistake buckets, a topic tracker, and a 45-minute post-mock checklist — see our [error-log method guide]({{< relref "aissee-mock-test-analysis-error-log-method.md" >}}).

## What parents should do (and not do)

- **Do:** protect sleep, cook familiar food, keep the house calm, celebrate completed mocks like small festivals.
- **Don't:** introduce a new test series in January, compare with the neighbour's child, or discuss "what if you don't get in" — not once.

The 30-day plan is not a heroic effort. It's a professional one: close, sweep, fix, rehearse. On the morning of 31 January, your child should feel like a cricketer who has already played the match in the nets a hundred times. That's what these four weeks are for.

## FAQs

**When should I start the 30-day revision plan for AISSEE 2027?**
If the exam is held on 31 January 2027 as tentatively listed in NTA's official calendar, start the final revision block around 1 January 2027. If the date shifts, shift the plan with it — the structure works for any 30-day window.

**How many mock tests should I attempt in the last 30 days?**
Four full-length OMR mocks — one every Sunday — plus 8–10 section-wise drills on weekdays. Quality matters more than quantity; each mock must be followed by a full error analysis.

**Should my child study new chapters in the last 30 days?**
No. The last 30 days are for consolidation, not new learning. New topics in the final month add stress without adding marks. The only exception: a chapter already half-learned that needs one hour to close.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**
No — there is no negative marking in the AISSEE. Wrong answers and blank answers both score zero. In the last 30 days, train your child to attempt everything, but only after the first-pass/second-pass selection.

**How many hours should a child revise per day in the final month?**
Three focused hours for a Class 6 aspirant is plenty; four for Class 9. Beyond that, attention collapses and error rates rise. Two deep sessions beat one exhausted six-hour sitting.
