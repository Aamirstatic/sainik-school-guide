## What the PPP model actually means — in plain language

The 33 original Sainik Schools are run entirely by the government — under the Ministry of Defence, through the Sainik Schools Society. Government land, government management, government rules.

New Sainik Schools run on a **Public-Private Partnership (PPP)** model. In plain terms: these are **private schools** — run by private managements, trusts, or NGOs — that the Sainik Schools Society has approved and affiliated. They follow the Sainik School pattern: admission through AISSEE, a similar curriculum focus, and defence-oriented activities. But the management sitting in the principal's office is private.

Put simply: **a traditional Sainik School is run by the government; a new PPP Sainik School is a private school carrying the government's tag.** That one sentence explains almost every difference you'll read about below — especially the one about fees.

## Traditional vs new (PPP) Sainik Schools — the real differences

| Point | 33 traditional schools | New PPP schools |
|---|---|---|
| Management | Sainik Schools Society (Ministry of Defence) | Private school / trust, affiliated to the Society |
| Admission | Through AISSEE | Through AISSEE — the same single exam |
| Counselling | AISSAC at pesa.ncog.gov.in | Same AISSAC counselling — both types appear as choices |
| Fees | Standardised and relatively modest; scholarships and concessions available | 🔶 Set by each school — check before choice filling; can be higher than traditional schools |
| Reservation | 67% home-state / 33% other-states; SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27%; defence-ward provisions | 🔶 No Society-prescribed reservation pattern for new schools; school-wise rules apply — confirm in the bulletin |
| Girls quota (10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, in Class 6) | ✅ Applies across the 33 schools (AISSAC SOP) | 🔶 May vary school to school |
| Campus and lifestyle | Fully residential, military-style | Depends entirely on the individual school |

## Admission: the same AISSEE, no separate form

This confuses a lot of parents, so let me be explicit: **there is no separate application for new Sainik Schools.** You fill one AISSEE form. In AISSAC counselling, both traditional and new schools appear as choices, and you rank them together. The counselling itself is run online by the Sainik Schools Society at pesa.ncog.gov.in — fully online, no counselling fee, running to roughly six rounds.

Two categories matter for Class 6 admissions to new schools (per the AISSAC SOP):
- **Category A — 40% of seats:** filled on all-India merit through e-counselling
- **Category B — 60% of seats:** reserved for students already studying in approved new Sainik Schools, filled on school-wise merit

And unlike the 33 traditional schools, there is **no Society-prescribed reservation pattern for new schools** — quotas, if any, are school-specific. Don't assume the 67/33 home-state split or the SC/ST/OBC percentages apply; verify for each school in the bulletin.

The application fee for AISSEE is 🔶 **EXPECTED** at ₹850 for General/OBC/Defence-ward candidates and ₹700 for SC/ST — based on the 2026 rates. Final confirmation comes with the bulletin.

## My honest advice for parents

1. **Don't treat a new school as an automatic "second choice."** Some PPP schools have better facilities and results than some traditional ones. Judge each school individually — visit if you can, ask alumni if you can't.
2. **Check the fee before you rank.** PPP schools set their own fees, and they can run higher than traditional schools. Read our [scholarship and fee-concession guide](/blog/sainik-school-scholarship-fee-concession-2027/) and the [all-schools fee comparison](/blog/sainik-school-fee-comparison-all-schools/) before choice filling.
3. **Rank on three things, not one.** Distance from home, fee, and facilities — not just the name on the gate. A great school six hours away can be a worse choice than a good one nearby.

## FAQs

**Q1. Is the education in new Sainik Schools the same as in traditional ones?**
The pattern and curriculum focus are similar, but each school has its own private management — so quality genuinely varies from school to school. Check a school's track record before ranking it.

**Q2. What are the fees in new Sainik Schools?**
🔶 Each school sets its own fee, and it can be higher than a traditional school's. Exact figures surface around the bulletin and counselling. Compare carefully using our [fee comparison](/blog/sainik-school-fee-comparison-all-schools/).

**Q3. Does reservation apply in new Sainik Schools?**
🔶 There is no Society-prescribed reservation pattern for new schools the way there is for the 33 traditional ones. Anything school-specific will be clarified in the NTA bulletin. For the traditional-school quotas, see our [SC/ST/defence quota guide](/blog/sainik-school-sc-st-defence-quota-2027/).

**Q4. What does the "100 new schools" announcement mean for my child?**
More seats over time — which should ease competition a little. The announcement came in March 2026; which of those schools actually join the 2027 cycle is something only the bulletin will tell us.

**Q5. Can a child from a new PPP school still join the NDA?**
Yes. NDA selection is through the UPSC exam and SSB — the school's management model doesn't decide it; the child's preparation does. The full journey is mapped in our [NDA career path guide](/blog/nda-after-sainik-school-career-path/).
