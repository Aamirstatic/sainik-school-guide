## One school, three different years

Parents imagining Sainik School life usually picture one fixed day: early PT, classes, games, study, lights out. The shape of that day is real — our [morning routine guide](/blog/sainik-school-morning-routine-pt-assembly-first-bell/) walks through it — but the details change with the season, sometimes sharply. A cadet's June day and the same cadet's August day can feel like two different schools, even though the timetable on paper looks almost the same.

That matters for parents twice over. First, the phone call home sounds different by season: a tired, heat-drained child in May and a muddy, rain-soaked one in July are both normal, and knowing which season causes what stops a lot of worry. Second, what you send from home — clothes, footwear, medicines, snacks — should match the season your child is about to live through, not the season you packed for last term.

## Summer: the day moves to the edges

Summer is the hardest season in a Sainik School year, especially for a new cadet in Class 6. The heat does not cancel the routine; it reshapes it around the cooler hours. Morning PT and parade typically start earlier, while the sun is still bearable, and the middle of the day belongs to classrooms, which are shaded and still. The late afternoon games period — normally the most energetic part of the day — is where schools watch the heat most closely: shorter bursts of activity, more water breaks, and a faster decision to stop if a child shows signs of heat exhaustion.

This is also the season when the small habits matter most. Cadets learn quickly that a water bottle is not optional, that a cap or head covering on the way to class is sense rather than fussiness, and that the heaviest meal of the day sits badly before an afternoon games period. Parents can help before the term starts by sending light, breathable innerwear, a spare water bottle, and oral rehydration sachets — the unglamorous items that get used daily while the new uniform gets the attention.

One summer fact surprises many parents: summer is also when a cadet's fitness improves fastest. Regular PT in the heat, however uncomfortable, builds stamina quickly, and by the end of the first summer term most cadets handle the routine with far less complaint than they did in the first fortnight. The tired phone calls of April and May are usually the sound of adjustment, not of a problem.

## Monsoon: wet, muddy, and still on schedule

When the rains arrive, the change is visible within a day. The parade ground that was dust becomes mud, the shoes that lasted a week need drying every night, and the morning assembly that happened outdoors moves under cover. But — and this is the part parents most need to hear — the school does not slow down in the monsoon. Classes continue normally. It is the outdoor parts of the day that get rearranged: PT moves indoors or becomes indoor physical training, games shift to covered areas or to indoor sports where the school has them, and route marches and outdoor drills wait for a dry window.

The monsoon routine has its own disciplines, and cadets pick them up fast. Wet uniforms go on the line immediately, not in a heap. Shoes are cleaned and dried the same evening, because a pair left wet overnight is unwearable the next morning. Beds and lockers get checked for dampness, and the school's health routine sharpens: mosquito protection in the evenings, clean drinking water, and a lower tolerance for stomach upsets, since the season brings them more often.

For parents, the monsoon packing list is different from the summer one. Extra socks and innerwear matter more than extra uniforms, because those are what get wet and what need changing. A sturdy raincoat or umbrella, a waterproof cover for the bag, and a small stock of basic medicines (for colds and stomach upsets, as advised by the family doctor) are the items that actually get used. And a practical tip from many boarding parents: label everything. In a dormitory where thirty raincoats hang in a row, an unnamed one is a lost one.

## Summer vs monsoon at a glance

| Part of the day | Summer | Monsoon |
|---|---|---|
| Morning PT / parade | Earlier start, heat watched closely | Moved indoors or replaced when ground is unsafe |
| Classes | Normal, shaded classrooms | Normal — rain rarely affects teaching |
| Afternoon games | Shorter bursts, more water breaks | Shifted indoors or to covered areas |
| Health watch | Heat exhaustion, dehydration | Mosquitoes, stomach upsets, damp-related colds |
| What gets wet / worn out | Water bottles, innerwear, caps | Socks, shoes, rain gear, bags |
| What parents should send | Light innerwear, ORS, spare bottle | Extra socks, raincoat, waterproof bag cover |

## The season parents forget: the changeover weeks

The hardest weeks are rarely the middle of a season — they are the fortnights when one season turns into another. The first heavy rains after months of heat catch cadets who have stopped carrying rain gear. The first hot weeks after the monsoon catch cadets whose fitness was built in cooler, wetter weather. Schools handle these transitions by adjusting the timetable gradually, but parents can help by asking, in the changeover weeks, one specific question on the phone: not "how are you?" but "what changed in your routine this week?" The answer tells you more about how your child is settling than any general reassurance.

## What this means for your first year as a parent

If your child is joining in the next academic session, the seasonal rhythm is part of the adjustment, not an obstacle to it. The summer will tire them, the monsoon will soak them, and both will pass while the routine — classes, PT, games, study, the house system — carries on underneath. By the time the [winter routine](/blog/winter-in-sainik-school-uniform-routine-health/) arrives, most cadets have stopped noticing the timetable altogether and started living inside it. That is the real sign of settling in, and it usually arrives sooner than parents expect.

One last note: everything above describes the typical pattern. A school in the hills runs a different summer from a school in the plains, and a school with covered sports facilities handles rain differently from one without. When a school notice contradicts this guide, the notice wins — it was written for your child's ground, in your child's weather.

## What to do next

- **Before the summer term:** Pack light innerwear, a spare water bottle and ORS; expect tired phone calls in the first fortnight.
- **Before the monsoon term:** Pack extra socks and innerwear, a raincoat, and a waterproof bag cover; label every item.
- **In changeover weeks:** Ask what changed in the routine this week, not just how your child feels.
- **All year:** Trust the school's own timetable over any general guide, including this one.

## FAQs

**Does PT still happen at Sainik School during the monsoon?**
Outdoor PT and parade are usually moved indoors or replaced with indoor physical training when rain makes the ground unsafe. The routine does not stop; it shifts. Each school decides based on that morning's conditions.

**How do Sainik Schools handle the summer heat?**
Schools typically shift outdoor activities to cooler hours, increase water breaks, and watch for heat exhaustion during sports and drill. Exact timings vary by school and region, since a summer day in Rajasthan and one in Kerala are very different.

**What should parents pack differently for the monsoon term?**
Extra pairs of socks and innerwear, a sturdy raincoat or umbrella, waterproofing for shoes and bags, and mosquito protection. Cadets get wet often in the monsoon, so quick-drying items matter more than extra sets of uniform.

**Do classes continue normally in heavy rain?**
Yes, academic classes normally continue in the monsoon. It is outdoor activities, PT, games and parades that are rescheduled or moved indoors, not classroom teaching.

**Is the summer term harder for new cadets?**
The first summer is often the toughest part of the year for a new cadet, because heat, homesickness and a full routine arrive together. Most cadets adjust within a few weeks as fitness improves and the routine becomes familiar.

**Does the routine change again in winter?**
Yes. Winter brings its own version of the timetable, with later mornings in cold regions and different sports emphasis. Our winter routine guide covers that season separately.
