## The question the form does not explain

Somewhere in the middle of the AISSEE 2027 form, after the photograph and the certificates, comes the question most parents rush past: which schools do you want?

It looks like a formality. It is not. The order you list your schools in is, after your child's score, the single biggest factor in where they actually get a seat. And the form gives you no guidance at all — no warning that your home-state school is statistically your best shot, no note that the shiny new school down the road reserves most of its seats for children already studying there, no hint that the famous school at the top of your list may not even have girls' seats.

So before you submit — the window closes on 2 December, with a late window to 6 December — let us walk through how to build the list properly. It takes twenty careful minutes. It is worth more than twenty hours of extra revision.

## First, understand what you are choosing from

The AISSEE 2027 bulletin covers two very different kinds of school, and the rules are not the same for both.

**The 33 established Sainik Schools** are the originals — Korukonda, Kunjpura, Kazhakootam, Kapurthala, Lucknow, Purulia and the rest, run directly under the Sainik Schools Society. Across them there are about **3,160 tentative Class 6 seats and 732 Class 9 seats** for 2027-28.

**The approved New Sainik Schools** — 80 of them taking Class 6, 40 taking Class 9 — are existing private and state schools approved into the Sainik School system under the PPP model. They follow the Sainik School pattern and curriculum, but they are newer to the system, and their seats work differently. That difference is the most important thing in this article, so let us spell it out.

## The three rules that should shape your list

**Rule 1: your home-state school is your strongest chance.** In the 33 established schools, **67% of seats are reserved for home-state candidates and 33% for other states**. Within those, 15% are for SC, 7.5% for ST and 27% for OBC (NCL), and 25% of the remaining seats are for wards of defence personnel and ex-servicemen. What that means in plain language: two-thirds of the seats at your own state's school are contested only by children from your state. If your home-state school is a good one, it belongs at or near the top of your list. Parents who list only out-of-state famous schools are voluntarily giving up the biggest structural advantage in the whole system.

**Rule 2: the new-school 40/60 split changes the maths.** In approved New Sainik Schools, at least **40% of seats (Category A)** are filled from the All-India AISSEE merit list — open to everyone. But up to **60% (Category B)** go to students already enrolled in that same school. If your child is not already studying at that new school, only the 40% Category A seats are open to you. A new school with 50 Class 6 seats may therefore have only about 20 seats genuinely contestable by an outsider. That does not make new schools bad choices — but do not rank one above an established school on the assumption that all its seats are up for grabs. Check whether your child is already enrolled there; if they are, the same school suddenly becomes a much stronger bet.

**Rule 3: check the girls' seats before you rank anything.** In the 33 established schools, **10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, are for girls in Class 6**. But "for girls" does not mean "at every school" — the girls' seats exist where the school offers them, and the seat matrix in the bulletin is the only reliable list. If you are applying for a daughter, open the seat matrix first and build your list only from schools that actually have girls' seats. Ranking a school with no girls' seats is a wasted preference, however good the school is.

## Old vs new: an honest comparison

| Factor | 33 established schools | Approved New Sainik Schools |
|---|---|---|
| Home-state quota | 67% home-state / 33% other-states | No 67/33 split; Category A/B instead |
| Seats open to outsiders | Most seats, via merit + quotas | ~40% (Category A); up to 60% for existing students |
| Girls' seats (Class 6) | 10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever more | As per the school's approved seats |
| Track record | Decades of NDA selections, alumni networks | Newer to the system; varies by school |
| Fees | Vary by school; scholarships and concessions apply | Vary by school — check before ranking |
| What to check first | Your home-state school's cutoff trend | Whether your child is already enrolled there |

Fees vary widely from school to school — one reported example puts a year at Sainik School Korukonda at about Rs. 2.06 lakh for new admissions, reduced by scholarships — and they are revised from time to time. Check the fee for every school on your list now, while you can still reorder it for free.

## How to build the list: a worked example

Imagine a family in Uttar Pradesh with a son in Class 5, expecting a solid but not spectacular score. Here is how their list should look:

1. **Sainik School Lucknow (UP) first** — the home-state school, 67% quota working for them, a strong school, and the one they can realistically reach for holidays.
2. **A second established school in a neighbouring state** — still a real chance through the 33% other-states quota, and a genuine preference, not a lottery ticket.
3. **A new Sainik School near home** — but only after checking the Category A seat count, and ranked honestly for what it is: a good school with limited open seats.
4. **One school where admission is comfortably within reach** — the safety net. Every list needs one. A list of only dream schools is not a strategy; it is a gamble.
5. **Nothing you would refuse.** Do not list a school your child could never attend — too far, too expensive, the wrong medium. Every preference on the list is a place your child might actually be sent.

Now run the same exercise for your own family. The shape of the list will be different; the discipline is the same: home-state strength first, realistic options in the middle, one safe option at the end, and nothing you would refuse.

## The two mistakes that cost seats every year

**Mistake 1: ranking by reputation alone.** The most famous schools are famous precisely because everyone ranks them first. A child with a good-but-not-top score who lists only the three most sought-after schools can end up with nothing, while the same score would have secured a seat at their home-state school. Reputation matters — but the quota matters more.

**Mistake 2: treating the form as a draft.** The correction window runs from **7 to 9 December 2026**, and it is a genuine safety net for slips — a misspelt name, a wrong digit. But do not count on it for rethinking your school ranking. Get the list right before you submit, and use the correction window for what it is for: fixing mistakes, not making decisions. Our [step-by-step form guide](/blog/aissee-application-form-2027-guide/) and the [bulletin read-through](/blog/aissee-2027-information-bulletin-what-actually-matters/) walk through the rest of the form; this article is only about the one decision the form does not explain.

One last thing. The exam itself is on Sunday, 31 January 2027, at 2 PM — Class 6 is 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks; Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks; no negative marking; 25% in each section and 40% in aggregate to qualify. None of that changes with your school list. What changes is where a good score lands.

## FAQs

**How many Sainik Schools can I choose in the AISSEE 2027 form?**

The form covers admission to the 33 established Sainik Schools and the approved New Sainik Schools — 80 of them for Class 6 and 40 for Class 9. List your schools in genuine order of preference, because allotment follows your ranking and your merit position.

**Does the home-state quota apply to all Sainik Schools?**

The 67% home-state / 33% other-states split applies to the 33 established Sainik Schools. That is why your home-state school is usually your strongest chance — two-thirds of its seats are reserved for children from your state.

**What is the 40/60 seat split in New Sainik Schools?**

In approved New Sainik Schools, at least 40% of seats (Category A) are filled from the All-India AISSEE merit list, and up to 60% (Category B) go to students already enrolled in that same school. If your child is not already studying there, only the 40% Category A seats are open to you.

**Can girls apply to all Sainik Schools in AISSEE 2027?**

Girls can apply, but seats for girls exist only where the school offers them — in the 33 established schools, 10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more, are for girls in Class 6. Check the seat matrix for the schools on your list before ranking them.

**Should I list only the famous Sainik Schools in my AISSEE form?**

No. Listing only the most sought-after schools wastes your lower preferences. Rank a mix: your home-state school first if it is strong, then realistic options where your child's expected score is competitive, and include at least one school where admission is comfortably within reach.

**Can I change my school choices after submitting the AISSEE form?**

The correction window runs from 7 to 9 December 2026, but do not count on changing school preferences there — get the ranking right before you submit. The post-result choice-filling in AISSAC counselling is a separate stage with its own rules.
