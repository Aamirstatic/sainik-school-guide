## The oldest Sainik School in Rajasthan, in the shadow of a fort

If you are filling the AISSEE 2027 form this week and Rajasthan is your home state, one school should be at the top of your list for a reason no brochure can manufacture: **Sainik School Chittorgarh** opened on **7 August 1961**, one of the first five Sainik Schools in India. It has been sending cadets to the armed forces for 65 years — longer than any other school in the state.

And the setting does half the recruiting. The 272-acre campus sits on Bhilwara Road in Chittorgarh, in the shadow of the **Chittorgarh Fort** — the UNESCO-listed hill fort of Maharana Pratap's Mewar, a place where schoolchildren grow up with stories of valour as local history, not textbook chapters. For a child who dreams of the uniform, that is not decoration. It is the air the school breathes.

## A school built on Mewar's ground, after the first five

Chittorgarh was among the first batch of Sainik Schools created in 1961, when Defence Minister V. K. Krishna Menon pushed the feeder-school idea across the states. The school's own history describes its ground as "pious land replete with laudable history" — and it is hard to argue. The city is synonymous with Rajput valour: Maharana Pratap, Rani Padmini, the sieges of the fort. Cadets here do not need a history lesson to understand sacrifice. They walk past it every day.

Rajasthan has only one other Sainik School — Jhunjhunu, founded in 2012 — so Chittorgarh carries the state's oldest defence-school tradition by more than fifty years. Our [Rajasthan guide](/states/rajasthan/) covers both schools and the state picture in full.

## Seats for 2027-28: 100 in Class 6, 15 in Class 9

Here is the number that decides your child's odds. From Appendix VI-A and VI-A1 of the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin:

| Class | Home state (Rajasthan) | Other states | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Class 6 — boys** | 62 | 28 | 90 |
| **Class 6 — girls** | 5 | 5 | 10 |
| **Class 6 — total** | — | — | **100** |
| **Class 9 — boys** | 10 | 5 | 15 |
| **Class 9 — girls** | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| **Class 9 — total** | — | — | **15** |

Three things jump out. First, Class 6 is the real door — nearly seven times the size of Class 9. Second, the **67% home-state quota** means a Rajasthan-domiciled child competes for 67 of the 100 Class 6 seats, while an out-of-state child chases 33. Our full explainer on [the 67% home-state quota](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-67-percent-explained-2026-10-17/) works through the arithmetic with examples. Third — and this matters if your daughter is in Class 8 — **Chittorgarh has no Class 9 seats for girls this year**. Girls enter Chittorgarh in Class 6 or not at all, for 2027-28. Our [girls' seats explainer](/news/aissee-2027-7-days-left-girls-351-class-6-seats-2026-11-25/) lists the schools that do have Class 9 girls' seats.

The seat figures are tentative — the bulletin says so itself — but they are the numbers NTA published, and they are the ones to plan around.

## Admission: only through AISSEE, and the clock is ticking

There is no management quota, no donation route and no direct admission to Chittorgarh. Entry is to **Class 6 and Class 9 only**, through AISSEE, conducted by NTA.

For 2027-28, the form is open at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society** until **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM**. The fee is Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and Rs 700 for SC and ST. A late window runs 3–6 December with an extra Rs 500 — but treat that as an emergency exit, not a plan.

The age rules are the standard AISSEE ones: **10–12 years as on 31 March 2027 for Class 6** (born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017, studying in Class 5) and **13–15 years for Class 9** (born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014, studying in Class 8). The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027** — Class 6 is a 150-minute, 300-mark paper; Class 9 is 180 minutes and 400 marks. There is no negative marking, and the qualifying bar is 25% in each section with 40% aggregate — our [worked examples of the 25% rule](/blog/aissee-2027-qualifying-marks-25-percent-rule-worked-examples/) show exactly what that means for your child's target score.

## Eleven houses, and a campus built for the outdoors

Chittorgarh holds a distinction no other Sainik School can claim: **11 boarding houses**, the most in the country. Each is named after a Rajput warrior or ruler — **Ashok-I, Ashok-II, Badal, Hamir, Jaimal, Kumbha, Kush, Luv, Padmini, Pratap and Sanga** — and each has its own House Master, tutors and support staff. Houses are where a cadet's daily life actually happens: dormitory, competitions, drill, friendships. With eleven of them, inter-house rivalry here is a proper tournament, not a formality.

Then there are the facilities that set Chittorgarh apart from most Sainik Schools. The campus has a **Brigadier Ransher Archery Range**, a **General Mandhata Shooting Range** and an **Equitation Centre** for horse riding — three specialised training grounds most schools simply do not have — alongside an indoor stadium, a swimming pool and a gymnasium. NCC is compulsory, and the school runs a dedicated NDA preparation programme with systematic UPSC NDA coaching, SSB mock interviews and guidance from former defence officers. The school publishes an NDA Honour Roll of cadets who have joined the Academy, and its alumni association — the Chittorians — runs mentoring and reunions across generations.

The daily routine is the standard Sainik School rhythm: wake-up and PT at 05:30, classes from 07:30 to 01:30, games from 03:00 to 05:00, evening prep, and lights out at 10:00 PM. It is a full day, every day — and that is the point parents should weigh honestly before choosing a boarding school for a ten-year-old.

## Fees and cutoffs: what is reported, and what is not verified

This is the section where honesty costs us a clean number — and that is the point. No verified 2027 fee figure for Chittorgarh is available from the school or the NTA bulletin. One coaching portal reports tuition at the older Sainik Schools at **Rs 1,28,615 a year for 2026-27**. Treat that as a reported figure, not a bill: the all-in cost — fee plus uniform, books, travel and extras — is always higher than the headline tuition figure, and scholarships exist for defence families and SC/ST cadets. Confirm the current fee from the school before you budget.

The same caution applies to cutoffs. Coaching portals report Chittorgarh's expected 2025 Class 6 cutoffs at roughly **275–277 out of 300 for general-category home-state boys** (271–274 for other-state boys), and higher for girls — around **284–287 for general home-state girls** — because ten seats concentrate the competition. Their advice, which is sound whatever the exact number: **aim for 280 or more**. These are reported estimates, not NTA figures — NTA does not publish school-wise cutoffs — but they tell you the honest truth about Chittorgarh: it is one of the most competitive Sainik Schools in the country, because Rajasthan's children apply in large numbers.

## Is Chittorgarh the right first choice for your child?

Ask yourself three questions before you put it at the top of your preference list:

1. **Is your child genuinely drawn to the armed forces?** Chittorgarh's whole system — dawn PT, NDA coaching, SSB preparation, eleven-house rivalry — is built for one destination. A child who wants it thrives here. A child who is being pushed into it will feel every 5:30 AM reveille.
2. **Can your family handle the distance and the boarding?** Chittorgarh is not a day trip from most of India, and term breaks become the rhythm of family life for six years. If you can, visit the campus before you fill the preference list, and speak to parents of current cadets about the first term, not just the prospectus.
3. **Is Class 6 the entry point?** With 100 seats against 15 in Class 9 — and no Class 9 seats for girls at all — Class 6 is the sensible target. Class 9 entry is a second chance, not a plan.

If the answer to all three is yes, Chittorgarh is not just the oldest Sainik School in Rajasthan — it is one of the five schools that started the whole idea, still running on the same ground, in the shadow of the same fort. The form closes on 2 December. Seven days is enough time to fill it carefully. It is not enough time to fill it twice.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Chittorgarh have for 2027-28?**

Chittorgarh has 100 Class 6 seats — 90 boys and 10 girls — and 15 Class 9 seats, all for boys. There are no Class 9 seats for girls at Chittorgarh this year. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin.

**When was Sainik School Chittorgarh founded?**

It opened on 7 August 1961, one of the first five Sainik Schools in India, on a 272-acre campus on Bhilwara Road in Chittorgarh, Rajasthan, beside the historic Chittorgarh Fort.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Chittorgarh?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Chittorgarh?**

No single verified 2027 fee figure is available. One coaching portal reports tuition at the older Sainik Schools at Rs 1,28,615 a year for 2026-27, so parents should confirm the current figure from the school before budgeting.

**Does Sainik School Chittorgarh admit girls?**

Yes, in Class 6 — 10 girls' seats for 2027-28, five for Rajasthan girls and five for girls from other states. There are no Class 9 seats for girls at Chittorgarh this year.

**How many boarding houses does Sainik School Chittorgarh have?**

Eleven — the most of any Sainik School in India. The houses are named after Rajput warriors and rulers: Ashok-I, Ashok-II, Badal, Hamir, Jaimal, Kumbha, Kush, Luv, Padmini, Pratap and Sanga.
