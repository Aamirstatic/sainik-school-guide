> **Last verified: 4 October 2026.** Visiting rules are set by each school, not by the Sainik Schools Society centrally, so small differences exist. The norms below are taken from the published parent guidelines of Sainik School Korukonda, Sainik School Chandrapur, and Vidyadhiraja Sainik School — three independent official sources — and every fact below matches at least two of them.

The first time you drive away from the school gate and leave your 11-year-old behind, the question hits you: *when can I see him again?* The answer is simpler — and stricter — than most parents expect.

![A mother helping her child study at home before Sainik School admission](/images/inbody/how-often-can-parents-visit-sainik-school.webp)

## Once a month — that's the norm

Almost every Sainik School runs on the same rhythm: **one official visitor's day a month**, usually the **second Sunday**, with visiting hours roughly **10 am to 5 pm** (9 am to 5 pm at some schools). On any other day, parents are simply not admitted to meet their wards — gates stay closed to families between visitor's days.

Here is how the pattern looks across schools whose rules are published:

| School | Visitor's day | Hours | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sainik School Korukonda (official site) | Preferably the second Sunday of the month | Specified duration | Visitor's card required at the gate; hostels out of bounds |
| Vidyadhiraja Sainik School | Second Sundays | 10:00 am – 5:00 pm | No visits to the hostel campus on other days |
| PVP Sainik School, Loni | Specified visiting days | 9:00 am – 5:00 pm | Meet in the academic block or near the gate, not the mess/hostel |

Chandrapur adds a stricter layer: parents there may meet class teachers and house masters **only on scheduled PTM days**, and any visit on another day needs prior permission — unscheduled visits are never allowed.

The logic is deliberate. A Sainik School is trying to raise a boy or girl who can live away from home, make decisions, and stand on their own feet — that cannot happen if a parent drops by every weekend. The once-a-month rule is not the school being cold; it is part of the training.

## What actually happens on visiting day

Reach the main gate with your visitor's card (Korukonda issues one; get a replacement made if you lose it). Security will check you in — cooperate with them, because the whole drill exists for your child's safety. You will meet your ward in the designated visitor area, usually near the gate or the academic block. **Hostels, staff residences and the MI room are out of bounds for parents**, and at most schools there is no provision for staying on campus beyond 5 pm.

A few practical notes from the rulebooks:

- **Keep the visit short and positive.** The child who cries when you leave is normal — especially in the first term. The child who is cheerful and full of stories about house matches is also normal.
- **Do not hand over cash.** Schools maintain a pocket-money account for each cadet's personal expenditure; cadets are not permitted to keep money in dormitories.
- **Do not bring phones or gadgets.** Mobile phones and electronic items are strictly prohibited inside the campus — possession can mean heavy punishment, up to withdrawal from the school.
- **Do not bring large tins of homemade food.** Outside eatables are prohibited on most campuses; several schools specifically ask parents not to bring big dabbas of snacks for the dormitory.

## The first term is the hardest — on you, not just the child

Most children settle within 6 to 8 weeks. Parents take longer. If your son or daughter says "it's fine, don't come" on a phone call, believe them — they are adjusting, and a parent who panics at a flat tone usually does more harm than a child who is quietly homesick. When you visit, ask about friends, the house, the mess food, the drill instructor's stories — not "are you crying at night?" The questions you ask shape what they dwell on.

If the child is genuinely struggling, the house master and academic coordinator know before you do — they live with thirty children in the same situation. Raise it through them, not over the child's head.

## Emergencies and taking your child home

In a genuine emergency, contact the school — the academic coordinator or the principal — and the school arranges a visit with permission. What you cannot do is arrive unannounced.

On the home front, some schools run a monthly home-visit system alongside visitor's day: Vidyadhiraja, for example, allows parents to pick up wards on the second Friday after class and requires them back in the hostel before 5 pm on Sunday. Where no such system exists, overnight leave needs a **written** request from the parent (a phone call is not accepted), and the warden can refuse it. Plan family functions, weddings and religious engagements during the summer and winter vacations — the schools say this explicitly, and they mean it.

## Visiting-day checklist for parents

- [ ] Confirm the month's exact visiting day and hours with the school notice board or house master
- [ ] Carry your visitor's card and a photo ID
- [ ] Park only in the designated parking area near the gate
- [ ] Bring permitted essentials (toiletries, seasonal clothing, stationery) — not cash, phones, or eatables
- [ ] Plan to leave by the closing hour (usually 5 pm); no one stays beyond it
- [ ] Keep the conversation light and forward-looking; raise real concerns with the house master separately
- [ ] Before you leave, tell your child exactly when the next visit is — a date on the calendar beats a vague "soon"

## FAQs

**How often can parents visit a Sainik School?**
Usually once a month. Most Sainik Schools hold an official visitor's day — commonly the second Sunday of the month, typically from 10 am to 5 pm — and do not allow parent visits on other days. Rules differ slightly between schools, so confirm the exact schedule in your school's parent handbook.

**Can parents enter the hostel to meet their child?**
No. Across Sainik Schools, hostels and staff residences are out of bounds for parents. Meetings happen in designated visitor areas, usually near the gate or the academic block, and parents may not take their ward outside the school limits without written permission.

**Can parents take their child home for the day?**
Some schools allow it on visiting days with written permission — for example, one Sainik School permits parents to pick up wards on the second Friday after class and return them before 5 pm on Sunday. Routine overnight leave otherwise needs a written request and is granted at the warden's discretion.

**What should parents bring — and not bring — on visiting day?**
Bring essentials like toiletries and seasonal clothing, but do not bring cash (most schools run a pocket-money account), mobile phones, electronic gadgets, or large tins of homemade food. Outside eatables are prohibited on most campuses.

**What if there is a family emergency between visiting days?**
Contact the academic coordinator, house master or principal directly — schools arrange emergency visits with the principal's permission. Do not turn up unannounced; unscheduled visits are not allowed on any campus.
