## The certificate is the claim

For most AISSEE documents, a small error is fixable. For a disability claim, the document *is* the claim. If the certificate is from the wrong authority, shows the wrong percentage, has expired, or does not match the name and date of birth on the application form, the claim can be rejected at document verification — months after the exam, when there is nothing left to appeal with.

So before you tick any disability-related option in the AISSEE 2027 form (open 3 November to 2 December 2026 — see our [countdown guide](/news/aissee-2027-28-days-left-to-apply-what-to-finish-this-week-2026-11-04/)), run through this guide first.

## What counts: benchmark disability, 40 percent or more

Under the RPwD Act, 2016, which recognises 21 categories of disability, a disability certificate is issued to a person whose disability is assessed at **40 percent or more** — this is called a benchmark disability. That threshold is the first thing to check. A medical report that describes a condition but does not certify a percentage, or certifies one below 40 percent, does not give you a certificate-based claim.

The certificate itself must state three things clearly: the type of disability, the percentage assessed, and whether it is permanent or temporary. If any of those three is missing or unclear, get it clarified by the issuing authority before you apply.

## Who issues it: the government medical board, not a private clinic

A valid disability certificate is issued by a **medical board constituted by the Central or State Government**. In practice, that board works through the district Chief Medical Officer (CMO) or Civil Surgeon, and includes specialists relevant to the disability — an orthopaedician, ophthalmologist, ENT specialist, psychiatrist, clinical psychologist, or others, depending on the case. For a single disability, the certificate is signed by the concerned medical authority; for multiple disabilities, specialists in each area sign and the board head countersigns.

A letter from a family doctor or a private hospital, however detailed, is not a government medical board certificate. If your only document is a private report, take it to the district medical board for the formal certificate.

## The UDID card: useful, but not a substitute

The Unique Disability ID (UDID) project, run by the Department of Empowerment of Persons with Disabilities, digitises applications, assessment and renewal through the portal swavlambancard.gov.in. The usual route is: register on the portal, upload documents, get assigned to the district medical authority for assessment, and receive the certificate and UDID card after the board's recommendation.

Keep both the certificate and the UDID card, and check that the details match — name, disability type, percentage. If they differ, fix the mismatch at the CMO's office, not at the school gate.

## Permanent vs temporary: check the expiry date now

A permanent certificate — for a condition that will not change — is valid for life. A temporary certificate is valid for **five years** and must be renewed after a fresh medical examination. A certificate valid when the child was small may have expired by AISSEE verification. If yours is temporary, check the validity date today. If it expires before mid-2027, renew it now, while the AISSEE window is still open and the medical board has time.

## What the certificate actually gets you in AISSEE

Under the AISSEE pattern in recent bulletins, physically handicapped candidates qualify with at least 25 percent in each subject and **35 percent aggregate**, against 40 percent aggregate for other candidates — a relaxation, not a waiver. (EXPECTED for 2027: confirm it in the AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin.) Qualifying only makes a child eligible; admission still depends on merit rank, school choices in counselling, and the school's medical fitness standards.

That last point matters for Sainik Schools specifically. These are residential, physically demanding schools, and every admitted child faces a medical fitness check. A disability certificate supports a category claim; it does not exempt a child from the school's medical standards. Our [medical certificate guide](/blog/medical-certificate-format-sainik-school-admission-2026-10-16/) covers the fitness documentation side.

## The rejection checklist

| Check | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Percentage | 40 percent or more, stated on the certificate |
| Issuing authority | Government medical board / CMO, not a private clinic alone |
| Details | Type of disability, percentage, permanent or temporary — all present |
| Validity | Permanent, or temporary and still valid through verification in 2027 |
| Name match | Name and date of birth match the birth certificate and the AISSEE form exactly |
| UDID match | UDID card details agree with the certificate |

If any row fails, fix it at the issuing authority before 2 December 2026. The correction window (7–9 December) is for form particulars, not for obtaining a fresh certificate.

## What to do this week

1. Dig out the certificate and check the percentage, authority, and validity date against the table above.
2. If anything is missing or expired, apply through the UDID portal or the district CMO's office immediately.
3. Read the AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin's certificate appendix before ticking the category in the form.
4. Keep a clear scan of both the certificate and the UDID card ready for upload and for verification day.

A valid certificate from the right board is a straightforward claim. An almost-valid one is discovered last — at verification, when it is hardest to fix.

## FAQs

**What disability percentage is needed for a certificate to count?**
Under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016, a person with a benchmark disability — assessed at 40 percent or more — can get a disability certificate. Below that threshold, the certificate route for reservation benefits does not apply, so check the percentage on the certificate, not just that one exists.

**Who issues a valid disability certificate?**
A medical board constituted by the Central or State Government, usually working through the district Chief Medical Officer or Civil Surgeon, with specialists relevant to the disability. A certificate from a private clinic alone does not carry the same standing.

**Is a UDID card the same as a disability certificate?**
They are linked but not identical. The UDID project digitises the certificate system and issues a Unique Disability ID card, but the underlying certificate — with the disability type, percentage and validity — is what admission authorities verify. Keep both, and make sure the details match.

**Does a disability certificate guarantee admission to a Sainik School?**
No. It supports a category claim and, under the AISSEE pattern, a relaxed qualifying rule. Admission still depends on merit, school choices, medical fitness and verification at the allotted school.

**What if my child's certificate is temporary or about to expire?**
Temporary certificates are valid for five years and must be renewed after a fresh medical examination. If the validity runs out before document verification, renew it now — an expired certificate is one of the easiest claims to reject.

**Should I claim a disability benefit if I am not sure the certificate is valid?**
Do not guess. Get the certificate checked by the issuing medical authority first, and confirm the exact requirement in the AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin. A wrong claim can complicate verification far more than applying without it.
