## The child who qualified but got nothing

Every year, a few thousand children clear the AISSEE — real, published, qualifying marks — and still don't get a Sainik School seat. The merit list went down to rank 480; their child is at rank 512. Or the seat came through in a school they never expected and they refused it, thinking a "better" round was coming. Then the rounds end, the portal goes quiet, and the family assumes the door is shut.

It often isn't. Sainik School seats keep moving long after the main allotment rounds. Children fail the medical test. Documents turn out to be wrong. Families take the seat and then withdraw. Every one of those events frees a seat, and every freed seat is refilled through a fixed, official sequence — not luck, not connections, not phone calls to the school. This guide explains that sequence, in the order it actually happens.

## The machinery, in order

Understanding the order matters because each stage feeds the next. Based on the AISSAC 2026 cycle (the last completed cycle), the sequence runs like this:

| Stage | What happens | Who is considered |
|---|---|---|
| Online allotment rounds | Seats are allotted on merit-cum-preference through the AISSAC e-counselling portal | All registered, qualified candidates |
| Waiting lists during rounds | Unfilled seats are offered to wait-listed candidates in strict merit order | Candidates who registered but missed the main merit list |
| Final category adjustments | Empty reserved seats are reallocated between home-state and other-state lists, and between categories, per the Information Bulletin order | Waiting-list candidates, by category rules |
| Offline (spot) round | Any seats still vacant are published on the portal and allotted on merit | Registered candidates; participation does not guarantee a seat |
| Inter-school offers | A school announces its unfilled seats by category and the cutoff of the last admitted candidate; other schools' waiting-list candidates can apply | Combined merit list of applicants |

For 2027, this table is the **pattern**, not the schedule. If the AISSEE 2027 bulletin follows the established design, the same five stages will run again — but the round counts, dates, and portal notices will be whatever NTA publishes.

## How vacant seats actually become available

Three events free up seats mid-process, and all three are routine:

1. **Medical failure.** Admission depends on clearing the medical fitness test. A child can be a strong rank-holder and still be declared unfit — those seats come back into the pool.
2. **Document failure.** Birth certificate, domicile, category certificate — any mismatch during verification cancels the allotment, and the seat is reoffered.
3. **Non-joining.** A family accepts the seat, then withdraws — a change of plans, a better option, money. The seat returns to the system.

This is why the waiting list moves at all. It is also why the single most common mistake is withdrawing from the counselling process in frustration after the main rounds. **If you leave the system, you forfeit every later stage.** Staying registered costs you nothing; leaving costs you the spot round.

## The order in which empty seats are refilled

When seats stay empty after the main merit list and counselling, schools refill them step by step according to the Information Bulletin. The order is precise — and it is worth reading once, slowly, because it tells you exactly where your child stands in line:

- Empty **Other States/UT reserved-category** seats (SC/ST/OBC-NCL, List B) go to the **same reserved category in the Home State/UT** (List A).
- Empty **Other States/UT General** seats (List B) go to the **Home State/UT General** candidates (List A).
- Empty **Defence category** seats (Lists A and B) first go to **wards of Coast Guard and Assam Rifles personnel**, on merit, from the school's waiting list.
- Remaining **Other States/UT Defence** seats go to **Home State/UT Defence** candidates; remaining **Home State/UT Defence** seats then go to **Home State/UT General** candidates.
- Empty **Home State/UT reserved** seats (List A) go to **Other States/UT reserved** candidates (List B).
- If **girl seats** under any category stay empty, they may go to **boy candidates of the same category**.

If seats are still vacant after all of that, the school announces its unfilled seats by category — along with the cutoff marks of the last admitted candidate — and invites applications from **other Sainik Schools' waiting lists**, admitting from a combined merit list. (CONFIRMED from the Information Bulletin rules described by two independent portals)

## The offline (spot) round: rules worth knowing

The official AISSAC 2026 FAQs are unusually blunt about the offline round, and every one of these points is a parent-trap to avoid:

- **It only happens if seats are actually vacant.** "Strictly subject to availability of vacancies" — there is no guaranteed spot round.
- **Your first preference school is the default** for offline counselling, unless you removed it during the online rounds. If you played games with your preference list early on, they follow you here.
- **Registering is enough to stay eligible.** Even if you took no action during the online rounds, you can still be considered for offline admission — but only if you registered on the portal before the online rounds began. (CONFIRMED)
- **The candidate must appear in person** on the Offline Admission day. Maximum two parents or legal guardians may accompany. No intermediaries, no agents, no representatives. (CONFIRMED)
- **There are no authorised agents.** The FAQs say this in plain words: no e-counselling cells or agents are authorised by the Sainik Schools Society; parents must fill the portal themselves, and the Society takes no responsibility for unauthorised intermediaries. Anyone asking for money to "get a seat in the spot round" is running a scam. (CONFIRMED)

## The special tie that creates a seat out of nothing

One lesser-known rule deserves its own paragraph, because it occasionally *creates* seats rather than filling them. If fewer seats exist in a category than the number of candidates with the **same category-wise rank** seeking admission to the same school, the school offers seats to all of them by creating **supernumerary seats** — extra seats above the sanctioned strength — after exhausting the inter-se merit of tied candidates. In plain language: a genuine tie at the cutoff can get your child in without displacing anyone. (CONFIRMED from the official SSS standard operating procedure)

## Your "stay in the game" checklist

The parents who win vacant seats are not the best-connected — they are the best-prepared. Keep this list pinned until the last round closes:

- [ ] **Stay registered and responsive.** Never withdraw from the portal out of frustration; every later stage needs you inside the system.
- [ ] **Check the portal and school websites on a fixed schedule.** Seat announcements and waitlist movements are published online — weekly checks miss things.
- [ ] **Keep every original document ready.** Birth certificate, domicile, category certificate, previous class mark sheets, photographs — document failure is a top seat-killer.
- [ ] **Don't postpone the medical mindset.** If your child has a condition that might affect fitness standards, know it early rather than losing a seat at verification.
- [ ] **Order your preferences honestly.** The default offline school is your first preference; game-playing with the list follows you into the spot round.
- [ ] **Watch other schools' announcements.** Inter-school offers from another Sainik School's waiting list can be your route in — but only if you see the announcement in time.
- [ ] **Never pay an agent.** No authorised counselling agents exist; money paid to one buys nothing and is gone forever.
- [ ] **Be ready to travel.** Offline admission means physical presence — often at short notice. Budget for the trip now.

## FAQs

**What is the AISSAC spot round (offline admission) for Sainik Schools?**

After all online counselling rounds finish, any seats still vacant are offered through an Offline Round, strictly subject to availability. Unfilled vacancies are published on the portal, and allotment is merit-based — participating does not guarantee a seat.

**Does being on the AISSEE waiting list guarantee admission?**

No. The waiting list only lets you be considered for vacant seats, filled in strict merit and category order. You must also clear the medical fitness test and document verification to get admission.

**Can a vacant seat in another Sainik School be offered to my child?**

Yes. If a school still has empty seats after using its own waiting list, it announces the unfilled seats by category with the cutoff of the last admitted candidate, and candidates from other Sainik Schools' waiting lists can apply. Admission is then based on a combined merit list.

**Do I need to be present in person for the AISSAC offline admission round?**

Yes. The candidate must appear physically on the Offline Admission day; only parents or legal guardians (maximum two) may accompany them. No intermediaries or agents are permitted to participate.

**How are unfilled girl seats handled in Sainik Schools?**

If seats reserved for girls under any category remain empty, the school may allot them to boy candidates of the same category, as per the Information Bulletin rules.

**Are there authorised agents for AISSAC e-counselling?**

No. The official AISSAC FAQs state clearly that no e-counselling cells or agents are authorised. Parents must read the SOP and fill the portal details themselves; the Sainik Schools Society is not responsible for anything done through unauthorised intermediaries.
