## First, let's say the quiet part out loud

Every parent preparing a child for AISSEE 2027 has thought it, usually late at night: *what if it doesn't work out?*

It is a fair question. Thousands of children apply for a limited number of seats, and most of them will not get in. That is not a verdict on your child. It is arithmetic. And it is also not the end of the road — not even close.

The mistake is not thinking about Plan B. The mistake is thinking about it *instead* of preparing, or never thinking about it at all and then panicking in February. So let's do it properly, once, calmly — and then get back to the 82 days of preparation still ahead.

## What "not clearing" actually means

"Not clearing AISSEE" is not one single outcome. After the written exam on 31 January 2027, the process runs through school-wise merit lists, a medical examination, document verification and e-counselling. A child can miss the merit list for one school and still have options in counselling. The result date has not been announced yet, so there is nothing to dread on a calendar — just a process with several stages.

Knowing this matters because Plan B is not a single fallback either. It is a small set of real options, each with its own timing.

## Your Plan B, at a glance

| Option | When | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|
| Re-attempt AISSEE in 2028 | Application opens around October 2027 | Children still inside the age band and still in Class 5 (for Class 6 entry) |
| JNVST 2027 (Navodaya) | Exam 28 November 2026 | Families who already registered (registration closed 31 July 2026) |

## Option 1: Try again next year

This is the option most parents forget exists. AISSEE is an age-banded exam, not a one-shot exam. For AISSEE 2027, Class 6 candidates must be born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017. A child born in the younger half of that window — say, in 2016 — will still be 10 to 12 years old on 31 March 2028, and if they are still in Class 5 in the 2027–28 session, they can attempt AISSEE 2028.

That is 🔶 **EXPECTED**, projected from the 2027 bulletin's age bands — the final rule will be in next year's bulletin, and you must check it. But the principle holds: a child who misses by a few marks at age 10 or 11 is often a far stronger candidate at 11 or 12, with a full extra year of maths and reading behind them. Many cadets in Sainik Schools today are second-attempt candidates. Nobody asks them which attempt it was.

## Option 2: JNVST — the parallel exam, right now

If your family also registered for the Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya Selection Test, that exam is very close: **Saturday, 28 November 2026, 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM**, in a single phase for all JNVs. Admit cards were released on 28 October 2026 on the NVS portal, and results are expected by the end of March 2027.

Be honest about this one: registration for JNVST 2027 closed on 31 July 2026. If you did not register, this door is shut for this cycle — do not let anyone sell you a late entry. But if you did register, your child has a second exam just 18 days away, and the preparation overlaps heavily with AISSEE. The two exams test similar ground: mental ability, arithmetic and language.

For Class 9, JNVST also runs lateral-entry tests in the spring — the published schedule lists **10 April 2027** for Class 9 and Class 11 lateral entry. Treat that date as something to confirm on navodaya.gov.in closer to the time, but keep it on your list if your child is in Class 8 now.

## Option 3: RMS, RIMC and the state Sainik Schools

The December exam season is already crowded, and most of these windows have closed for this cycle — which is exactly why they belong in a Plan B for *next* year, not a panic move this year.

The RIMC entrance (RIMCEE 2026) is on **6 December 2026**. The Rashtriya Military Schools' CET 2026 exam falls in December too, with applications having closed on 20 October. The UP Sainik School entrance exam is scheduled for **20 December 2026**. If your child is registered for any of these, they are live options in the coming weeks. If not, note their cycles: RMS and RIMC applications typically open in the autumn, so the 2027 rounds are a genuine second chance, not a consolation prize.

## Option 4: The long road — NDA after Class 12

Here is the fact that puts everything in perspective: the National Defence Academy entrance, conducted by UPSC, is open to students from *any* school after Class 12. Sainik School is one route to the NDA. It is not the only one, and plenty of officers never set foot in a Sainik School.

A child who misses AISSEE at 11, studies well in a good day school, stays fit and disciplined, and clears NDA at 17 or 18 ends up in exactly the same place. The uniform does not ask which school you came from.

## What to say to your child

This is the part no exam guide covers, and it matters more than any of the dates above.

If the result disappoints, your child will watch your face before they process their own feelings. So decide now what you will say: that one exam at 11 does not measure their worth, that the effort itself counted, and that there is a next step already planned — because there is. You have just read it.

And one more thing, honestly: do not let Plan B become an excuse to ease off. There are 82 days to 31 January. The children who clear AISSEE are rarely the most talented in the room. They are the ones whose families kept the routine steady in November and December, when everyone else was slowing down. Prepare for the exam in front of you. Keep the safety net folded, quietly, in the drawer.

## What to do this week

- **If you registered for JNVST 2027:** download the admit card from navodaya.gov.in and check the exam centre. The exam is 28 November — 18 days away.
- **If your child is young for their class:** note the re-attempt possibility, but do not discuss it with them yet. Let them focus.
- **If AISSEE is your only application:** that is fine. Make sure the form itself is submitted well before 2 December, and use the correction window (7–9 December) if anything needs fixing.
- **For every family:** keep the evening study routine exactly as it is. Plan B works best when it is never needed.

## FAQs

**Can my child attempt AISSEE again next year?**

Often, yes — it depends on the age band. For AISSEE 2027, Class 6 candidates must be born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017. A child born in that window's younger half can usually attempt again the following year, provided they are still in Class 5 then. Check the age rule in the next year's bulletin before assuming.

**When is the JNVST 2027 exam?**

Saturday, 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM. Registration for JNVST 2027 closed on 31 July 2026, so it only helps families who already registered. Admit cards were released on 28 October 2026.

**What other boarding-school entrance exams exist besides AISSEE?**

JNVST for Navodaya Vidyalayas, RMS CET for Rashtriya Military Schools, RIMCEE for the Rashtriya Indian Military College, and state Sainik School exams such as the UP Sainik School entrance. Each has its own calendar and age rules.

**Can my child still join the armed forces without a Sainik School?**

Yes. The NDA entrance exam after Class 12 is open to students from any school, and Sainik School is only one route to it. Many officers never attended a Sainik School.

**When will the AISSEE 2027 result be announced?**

NTA has not announced a result date yet. After the written exam on 31 January 2027, the process continues with school-wise merit lists, a medical examination, document verification and e-counselling.

**Should we stop preparing for AISSEE 2027 and focus on Plan B instead?**

No. With 82 days left to the exam, AISSEE 2027 is still the main goal. Plan B is a safety net you prepare quietly in the background — it should never become the reason to ease off now.
