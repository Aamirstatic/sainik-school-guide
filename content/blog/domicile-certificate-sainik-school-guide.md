Domicile issuance rules differ by state — the process below is the standard pattern followed by state revenue departments; always confirm the exact document list on your own state's portal.

Every year I meet parents who prepare for months, get a great rank, and then freeze at document verification because of one missing paper. More often than not, that paper is the **domicile certificate**.

Here is the uncomfortable truth: your child's rank gets them into the merit list, but the domicile certificate decides *which* list they are in — the 67% home-state pool or the 33% other-state pool. The second pool is brutally competitive. A single certificate shifts your child's odds more than two extra marks.

## Why this one document matters so much

- **67% of seats** in every Sainik School are reserved for students of the school's home state (CONFIRMED — Sainik Schools Society reservation policy, consistent across AISSEE cycles).
- Your home state is proved by one thing: the domicile certificate.
- Without it, your child competes only for the 33% "other states" seats — where cutoffs run significantly higher.
- The certificate is checked at **document verification**, after the written exam and counselling. You do not need it on day one of the form — but you absolutely need it before verification, and getting one takes weeks, not hours.

If you are applying to state-run schools too, the rule is even stricter: for [UP Sainik Schools, for example](/news/up-sainik-school-deadline-24-days-left-2026-10-01/), the applicant's family must hold UP domicile to be eligible at all.

## What a domicile certificate actually is

It is a government-issued proof that your family is a resident of a particular state. It is issued by the **state revenue department** — usually through the tehsil office, the Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM), or the District Magistrate. Different states call it different things: *niwas praman patra* (UP, Bihar), *domicile/residence certificate*, and so on. Whatever the name, Sainik School counselling accepts whichever format your state officially issues.

The point of this table: **October–November is your safe window.** If you apply for the certificate now, any rejection or mismatch gets fixed with months to spare. If you start in January, you are gambling your seat on a tehsil clerk's calendar.

This connects directly to [choosing your schools in AISSAC counselling](/blog/how-to-choose-school-preferences-aissac-counselling/) — your home-state school is your highest-probability seat, and the domicile certificate is what unlocks it.

## How to get it: 5 steps

1. **Find your state's portal.** Search "<your state> domicile certificate apply online". Most states have an e-District or citizen-services portal; UP parents use the state's e-District portal, Bihar parents the RTPS portal, and so on. In-person application at the tehsil/SDM office also works everywhere.
2. **Fill the application** with names exactly as they appear on the child's Aadhaar and school records. This is the step where most rejections begin — a spelling mismatch between Aadhaar, school records, and the domicile application.
3. **Upload the documents** (checklist below). Scan them clearly; blurry uploads are a common rejection reason.
4. **Track the application** with your reference number. If the status stalls beyond the state's stated timeline, visit the tehsil office — a polite in-person follow-up moves files faster than waiting.
5. **Collect and verify.** When you receive the certificate, read every line: name spelling, father's name, address, date of issue. Errors caught now cost you a re-issue; errors caught at school verification cost you a seat.

## Document checklist (keep ready before you apply)

- [ ] Child's Aadhaar card
- [ ] Child's birth certificate
- [ ] Parent's Aadhaar + address proof (ration card, voter ID, or utility bill)
- [ ] Passport-size photographs
- [ ] School bonafide certificate (some states ask for it)
- [ ] Old residence proof, if the family moved recently (rent agreement, old ration card)

Keep a printed set and a scanned set. You will upload scans during [form filling and counselling](/blog/aissee-application-form-photo-signature-requirements/), and carry originals to verification.

## 5 reasons domicile applications get rejected

1. **Name mismatch** between Aadhaar, school records, and the application. Fix the spelling first, then apply.
2. **Address mismatch** — the application address does not match the address proof documents.
3. **Insufficient residence duration.** Some states require a minimum years-of-residence; families who moved recently should check their state's rule before applying.
4. **Wrong issuing authority** — a certificate from a notary or gazetted officer is not a domicile certificate. It must come from the revenue department.
5. **Expired or outdated format** — if your certificate is years old, confirm it is still in the format your state currently issues.

## FAQs

**Is a domicile certificate compulsory for Sainik School admission?**
Yes, in practice. Your domicile certificate proves your home state, and 67% of seats in every Sainik School are reserved for home-state students. Without it, your child cannot claim the quota and is pushed into the much tougher 33% other-state pool.

**Who issues the domicile certificate?**
Your state's revenue department, usually through the tehsil, sub-divisional magistrate (SDM) or district magistrate's office. Most states also let you apply online through their e-District or citizen-services portal.

**How long does it take to get a domicile certificate?**
It varies by state — anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. Some states issue it online in under a week; tehsil offices with manual verification can take longer. Apply at least a month before you need it.

**What documents do I need for a domicile certificate?**
Typically: the child's Aadhaar card, birth certificate, a parent's Aadhaar and address proof (ration card, voter ID or utility bill), passport-size photos, and sometimes a school bonafide certificate. Requirements differ slightly by state, so check your state's portal.

**Can I use my old domicile certificate from years ago?**
Check the certificate's validity. Many states issue domicile certificates that are valid for years or are considered permanent, but names, addresses and the issuing format change. If the name spelling differs from the AISSEE form or Aadhaar, get a corrected one issued now rather than fighting it at document verification.
