## The Question Every Parent Asks

When parents tour a Sainik School, they ask about the mess food, the dormitories and the discipline. The question they should ask more often is a quieter one: *who studied here before my child, and where are they now?*

A school's alumni network is its longest-running product review — sixty-plus years of graduates, visible in public life. And few school networks in India are as publicly visible as the Sainik Schools'. This is not sentiment. It is a practical asset your child inherits on day one, and it works in ways most brochures never explain.

## First, the Proof: Names You Already Know

Sainik Schools were set up from 1961 as feeder institutions for the National Defence Academy, and at present there are 33 of them run as joint ventures of the Centre and the states. Here is what that pipeline has produced, all verified from published reporting:

| Dr Randeep Guleria | Sainik School alumnus | Former Director, AIIMS New Delhi |

Two details deserve a pause. Generals Dwivedi and Tripathi were classmates — the same batch, the same class, passing out of Sainik School Rewa in 1981 — and went on to lead the Army and the Navy simultaneously. Admiral Tripathi is the only Navy Chief publicly listed as a Sainik School alumnus. And the civilian column matters as much as the uniformed one: a Vice President, an RBI Governor, a DRDO Chairman, chief ministers of Nagaland and Haryana, a former BSF Director General. The network does not only produce officers; it produces people who run institutions.

The pipeline is still flowing. In the UPSC NDA 2 2025 final merit list, Sainik School Ghorakhal alone placed 23 cadets — including Yuvraj Saxena at AIR 5, Aditya S Dharnia at AIR 17 and Ayushman S Bisht at AIR 22. That is not history; that is last year.

## How the Network Actually Helps Your Child

A famous alumni list is nice for the school's website. Here is what it means in practice for a cadet:

**1. Mentorship with a map.** Seniors who cleared the NDA written and the SSB know the exact terrain — which books, which mistakes, how the psychology tests actually feel. Advice from someone who sat in the same dormitory three years ago lands differently from coaching-centre generic guidance.

**2. A preparation culture, not just preparation.** When the boys in the next dormitory are solving previous years' papers every evening, your child does not need to be motivated — the environment does the motivating. Alumni success stories told at morning assembly turn "I want to join the NDA" from a dream into a routine.

**3. Career doors beyond the uniform.** The civilian alumni — in the IAS, medicine, research, banking — matter enormously for the cadet who discovers in Class 11 that the armed forces are not for him. Our piece on [career paths after Sainik School](/blog/nda-after-sainik-school-career-path/) covers this in detail: the network gives a teenager proof that there are many respectable exits, not just one.

**4. Institutional memory.** Old boys' associations often fund scholarships, sports facilities and library upgrades. They invite serving officers to speak, they show up for passing-out parades, and they remind the school — gently — of the standard it is supposed to hold.

## How Your Child Can Actually Use the Network: A Checklist

- [ ] **Ask about the alumni association on your first school visit.** Most established schools have an old boys' body — find out how active it is, not just whether it exists.
- [ ] **Attend alumni talks.** When former cadets visit campus, make sure your child is in the audience, not in the dormitory. One honest Q&A is worth ten motivational posters.
- [ ] **Connect with recent pass-outs** for NDA/SSB guidance — a senior from two years ago remembers the current pattern better than anyone.
- [ ] **Use the civilian alumni stories** if your child leans away from defence. A former cadet turned doctor or civil servant is the most credible career counsellor available.
- [ ] **Give back early.** The network is two-way: cadets who help juniors become the seniors everyone remembers — and the officers everyone wants in their unit.
- [ ] **Parents, talk to parents of alumni.** They will tell you things no prospectus contains — including the honest trade-offs we described in our [first-day guide](/blog/first-day-at-sainik-school-what-to-expect-2026-10-07/).

## The Honest Caveats

Two things the network cannot do. First, it cannot get your child admitted — admission is purely through [AISSEE merit](/blog/sainik-school-to-nda-roadmap/), and no connection, recommendation or old-boys' handshake can influence it. Anyone who suggests otherwise is running a scam. Second, it cannot clear the SSB for your child. The network opens doors; your child still has to walk through them on merit. Treat alumni stories as proof that the path exists, not as a promise that it is easy.

## FAQs

**Do Sainik School alumni get any advantage in the NDA exam or SSB?**

No official advantage — the UPSC written exam and SSB are strictly merit-based. The real edge is the preparation culture, mentorship from seniors who cleared it, and a school routine built around the NDA pattern.

**Which Sainik School has the strongest alumni network?**

There is no official ranking of alumni networks. Naturally, the older schools founded in the 1960s have larger, more established alumni bodies simply because they have more decades of graduates.

**Do Sainik School alumni succeed outside the armed forces too?**

Yes. Verified alumni include the Vice President of India, a former RBI Governor, a former DRDO Chairman, the former AIIMS Delhi Director, and several chief ministers — the network extends well beyond the uniform.

**Can the alumni network help with Sainik School admission itself?**

No. Admission is purely through AISSEE merit and counselling — no recommendation or connection can influence it. The network becomes useful after your child joins, not before.

**How can my child connect with alumni while still in school?**

Through the school's old boys' association, alumni talks during school events, and seniors who visit campus. Ask the school office about its alumni association when you visit — most established schools have one.
