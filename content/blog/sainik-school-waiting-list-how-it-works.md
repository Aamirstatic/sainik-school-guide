## The uncomfortable truth about "the waiting list"

Every year after the AISSEE result, thousands of parents ask the same question: *"What number is my child on the waiting list?"* Here is the honest answer: **there is no such number.** The Sainik Schools Society does not publish a waiting list for AISSEE counselling. You will not find your child's name at position 47 on any PDF.

What exists instead is better, if you understand it. AISSAC — the All India Sainik Schools Admission Counselling, run online by the Society at `pesa.ncog.gov.in` (not by NTA) — runs **around six rounds** of seat allotment. Every round, the system re-runs the allotment for everyone still in the process. If your child didn't get a seat in Round 1, they are automatically still in contention for Round 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. **The rounds are the waiting list.** The families who grasp this — and stay in — are the ones who convert qualifying scores into admission letters.

## How seats actually move, round by round

Think of the counselling like a train where passengers keep getting off at every station, and the waiting passengers board one station at a time:

| Round | What happens to seats | What a "waiting" family should do |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | Top ranks take their picks; some allotted candidates choose **Reconsider** or **Exit** instead of Accepting | If nothing allotted: do nothing, stay in. If allotted: choose Accept or Reconsider wisely |
| Rounds 2–3 | Vacated seats + unfilled seats go to the next ranks; you may **modify your choices** | Add realistic school options if your first list was all famous names |
| Rounds 4–5 | Serious movement: medical failures and document failures start vacating seats | Keep documents ready; respond fast when allotted |
| Round 6 | Final merit movement — the last scheduled churn | This is where patience pays most |
| Spot round | Leftover seats, if any; decisions on the spot | Be ready to say yes immediately |

**Seats open up for specific, human reasons.** An allotted candidate's family decides the fees are too high and exits. Another child fails the medical fitness test — and the seat passes to the next eligible candidate. Another family's category certificate turns out to be expired, and document verification kills the admission. None of this is predictable for any single seat, but in aggregate it happens every single cycle. That is why Round 1 is for toppers and Rounds 3–6 are for patient, prepared families.

## What genuinely improves your chances (and what doesn't)

**What works:**

1. **Stay till the last round.** This is the whole game. Every cycle, parents exit after Round 1 or 2 in disappointment — and seats that would have been theirs go to someone who stayed. Register once on `pesa.ncog.gov.in` and never withdraw.
2. **Update your preferences between rounds.** The system lets you modify choices. If Round 1 taught you that your list was too ambitious, add schools you can realistically get — including newer and PPP-model schools, which were part of choice filling in 2026 and are expected in 2027.
3. **Keep documents ready from day one.** A birth certificate, Aadhaar, domicile certificate, valid category certificate, and the AISSEE admit card and scorecard. An expired certificate at verification stage is how seats get *lost*, not won.
4. **Do a medical checkup early.** A failed medical sends your seat to the next candidate. Our [medical test guide](/blog/sainik-school-medical-test-2027/) explains what gets checked — get it done privately before counselling, not after allotment.
5. **Understand your real competition.** Allotment weighs five things together: AISSEE rank, domicile (the 67% home-state quota is your biggest statistical ally), gender, category, and preference order — not rank alone.

**What doesn't work:**

- **Paying anyone.** Registration and choice filling are completely free (CONFIRMED). There is no "management quota", no paid waiting-list number, no agent who can move a file. Every such offer is fraud.
- **Filling only famous schools.** Ten preference slots exist; using four of them on the most competitive schools is the classic way to watch six rounds pass you by.
- **Hoping for a call.** Nobody will call you. You check the portal every round, yourself.

## The one story I tell every parent

A mother from Patna told me her story last year. Her son had scored well above the qualifying marks. In Round 1 she filled just four schools — all famous names. He got nothing, and feeling defeated, she skipped Round 2 entirely. In Round 4, a boy with *lower* marks took a seat she could easily have had. ## FAQs

**Does AISSAC publish an official waiting list?**

No. The Sainik Schools Society does not publish waiting-list numbers. Unallotted candidates automatically stay in the process for subsequent rounds — the rounds themselves are the waiting mechanism.

**How many counselling rounds are there?**

Around six rounds per the 2026 cycle, plus a possible spot round for leftover seats. Each round's allotment is based on AISSEE rank, domicile, gender, category, and your preference order together.

**Why do seats open up in later rounds?**

Because some allotted candidates exit the process, fail the medical test, fail document verification, or never accept their seat. Every vacated seat goes to the next eligible candidate in the following round.

**What actually improves my child's waiting chances?**

Staying registered for every round, updating school preferences between rounds with realistic options, keeping documents ready, and not exiting the counselling early out of disappointment.

**Is there any fee to stay in later counselling rounds?**

No. AISSAC registration and choice filling are completely free. Anyone asking for money for a seat or a "waiting list number" is running a scam.
