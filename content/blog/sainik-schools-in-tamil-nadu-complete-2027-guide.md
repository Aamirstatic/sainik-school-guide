## One old school, six new ones — and a real advantage for Tamil Nadu families

If you live in Tamil Nadu and your child is aiming for a Sainik School, here is the honest picture: there is exactly one historic Sainik School in the state, and it is one of the oldest in the country. But the 2027 cycle adds six new Sainik Schools in Tamil Nadu, and between them the state now offers more Class 6 seats than most people realise.

The form closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026, at 11:50 PM** — nine days from today. This guide puts everything a Tamil Nadu parent needs in one place: the schools, the seats, the quotas, the fees as honestly as we can state them, and the timeline.

## Sainik School Amaravathinagar: the 64-year-old anchor

Amaravathinagar opened on **16 July 1962** — one of the very first Sainik Schools ever established, founded by then Defence Minister V. K. Krishna Menon. It began life as Sainik School Madras and was renamed Amaravathinagar in **1975**. The campus sits at Amaravathinagar, Udumalpet Taluk, Tiruppur district, in the foothills near the Amaravati Dam — about 700 cadets across Classes 6 to 12, CBSE-affiliated, English medium, residential.

A few identity markers parents notice on day one: the school motto is **"Can Do It"**, the houses are **Chera, Chola, Pandya and Pallava** — named for Tamil Nadu's great dynasties — and Class 6 cadets start in two feeder houses, **Valluvar** and **Bharathi**, before moving to their senior houses. Alumni call themselves **Amaravians**. For the full school profile, read our [Sainik School Amaravathinagar parent's guide](/blog/sainik-school-amaravathinagar-complete-parents-guide-2026-10-17/).

Here is the number that matters most for 2027: Amaravathinagar has the **second-largest Class 6 boys' intake of any Sainik School in India** — 110 boys' seats, behind only Tilaiya in Jharkhand with 126. That is not a small school with a handful of seats. It is a big door, and Tamil Nadu domicile holds the key to most of it.

Two things to notice. First, the new schools add **740 Class 6 seats** in Tamil Nadu — six times Amaravathinagar's intake. Second, only The Vikasa School offers Class 9 seats among the new schools, and only 12 of them. If your child is applying for Class 9, Amaravathinagar's 8 seats are the whole established-school story in the state.

All seat numbers are tentative — the bulletin says vacancies can change before counselling. But they are the numbers the form itself will use, so plan on them.

## The quota that decides who gets in

At Amaravathinagar, the rules are the national ones for the 33 established schools: **67% of seats are reserved for Tamil Nadu-domiciled children**, 33% for other states. That means about **76 of the 110 boys' seats** are for Tamil Nadu domicile — a real structural advantage, but only if the domicile certificate is ready. Get it sorted before you submit the form, not after. On top of that: SC 15%, ST 7.5%, OBC-NCL 27% within the state and All-India pools, and **25% of the remaining seats for children of defence personnel**. Girls get **10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more** — at Amaravathinagar that works out to 12 girls' seats in Class 6.

The new schools play by different rules, and this is where parents get caught out. In an approved new Sainik School, **up to 60% of seats can go to students already studying in that school**, with at least 40% filled by All India merit through AISSEE. So if your child is not already a student of, say, Vallaba Vidyalaya or Vidyaa Vikas, you are competing for the 40% merit pool — still 80 seats at Vallaba alone, but a different contest from Amaravathinagar's home-state quota. Check each new school's own fee and domicile rules too: they are set by the school, not by NTA.

## Fees: the honest, annoying answer

Fee figures for Amaravathinagar disagree across public sources. Trackers report roughly **₹1.17 lakh a year** (tuition, boarding and diet, 2026-27) on one listing and roughly **₹1.69 lakh** on another, with older listings lower still. We are not going to pick one and pretend it is certain. **Do not budget on any single number** — confirm the current fee schedule on the school's official website (sainikschoolamaravathinagar.edu.in) before you apply. What we can say plainly: it is a government-aided residential school, so the fee is far below a comparable private boarding school, and there is financial assistance for deserving cadets — ask the school office about it directly. The new schools set their own fees, which vary widely; check each school before listing it as a preference.

## Your timeline: 9 days to apply

| Date | What happens |
|---|---|
| **2 December 2026, 11:50 PM** | AISSEE 2027 form closes (Class 6 and Class 9) |
| 3–6 December 2026 | Late-fee window — extra ₹500 on top of the regular fee |
| 7–9 December 2026 | Correction window — fix permitted details, one time only |
| 11 January 2027 | Exam city announced |
| 25 January 2027 | Admit cards released |
| **31 January 2027 (Sunday)** | **AISSEE 2027 exam** — Class 6, 2:00–4:30 PM; Class 9, 2:00–5:00 PM |

The fee is **₹850** for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and **₹700** for SC/ST. The exam is pen-and-paper OMR, with **no negative marking** — 150 minutes and 300 marks for Class 6, 180 minutes and 400 marks for Class 9.

## The Tamil Nadu parent's checklist

- [ ] **Submit the form by 2 December, 11:50 PM** — not on 2 December evening, when the portal is at its slowest. The late window costs ₹500 for the same form.
- [ ] **Domicile certificate ready** — it is what unlocks the 67% home-state quota at Amaravathinagar. A missing certificate at counselling is the most avoidable heartbreak in this process.
- [ ] **List Amaravathinagar first if it is your first choice** — but do not leave the six new schools off the preference list. A seat at a new Sainik School is a Sainik School seat, and the 40% merit pool is genuinely winnable.
- [ ] **Check the new schools' fees before preferring them** — they are private schools running a Sainik section, and their fees are their own.
- [ ] **If your child studies in Tamil medium**, read our [Tamil Nadu language and admission guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-tamil-nadu-language-admission-guide-2027/) before filling the medium and exam-city fields — those choices cannot be fixed later.
- [ ] **Keep the correction window in mind (7–9 December)** — it is one-time-only, and some fields stay locked even then. Fill the form slowly the first time.

Last year, a father from Madurai told us he had skipped the new schools entirely because he had never heard of them — and then watched a neighbour's son take a Class 6 seat at one of them with a lower score than his own son's. The old school is the dream. The new schools are the plan B that is, increasingly, a plan A. Use both.

## FAQs

**How many Sainik Schools are there in Tamil Nadu?**

One established Sainik School — Amaravathinagar, founded in 1962 — plus six approved new Sainik Schools taking admissions for 2027-28: The Vikasa School, Kailash Maansarovar School, Jayanthi Public Senior Secondary School, Sri SPK Public Sr Sec School, Vallaba Vidyalaya and Vidyaa Vikas International School.

**How many Class 6 seats does Sainik School Amaravathinagar have in 2027?**

122 seats in total for Class 6 — 110 for boys (76 for Tamil Nadu domicile, 34 for other states) and 12 for girls. For Class 9 there are 8 seats. The numbers are tentative and come from the AISSEE 2027 Information Bulletin.

**What is the home-state quota for Tamil Nadu children?**

At the 33 established Sainik Schools, 67% of seats are reserved for children domiciled in the home state — so about 76 of Amaravathinagar's 110 boys' seats are for Tamil Nadu domicile. The new Sainik Schools work differently: up to 60% of their seats can go to the school's own students, with at least 40% filled by All India merit.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Amaravathinagar?**

Published fee figures disagree across sources — trackers report roughly Rs 1.17 lakh to Rs 1.69 lakh a year including boarding — so do not budget on any single number. Confirm the current fee schedule on the school's official website, sainikschoolamaravathinagar.edu.in, before you apply.

**When does the AISSEE 2027 form close?**

Wednesday, 2 December 2026, at 11:50 PM, for both Class 6 and Class 9. The late-fee window runs 3 to 6 December with an extra Rs 500. The exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027.

**Is the medium of instruction English at Amaravathinagar?**

Yes — English is the medium of instruction. If your child studies in Tamil medium, read our Tamil Nadu language and admission guide, which explains how the Class 6 paper works for Tamil-medium children.
