## The sight that changed the question

On 30 May 2025, at the Khetarpal Parade Ground in Pune, 17 women cadets crossed the 'Antim Pag' alongside over 300 male counterparts — the first co-ed Passing Out Parade in the National Defence Academy's history. In one morning, the question parents had been asking since 2021 — "is NDA really open for girls, or only on paper?" — got its answer: it is real, it has happened, and those cadets are now proceeding to their service academies.

If your daughter is in Class 6 to 10 right now and dreams of the uniform, this guide is her complete 2027 roadmap: how the door opened, how many seats exist, who is eligible, what the exam looks like, and the honest path from a Sainik School classroom to that parade ground.

## How the door opened: a short timeline

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2021 | Supreme Court direction — UPSC must allow women to apply for the NDA |
| 2022 | First cohort of women cadets joins the NDA's 148th course |
| 2025 | First batch of 17 women cadets graduates (30 May 2025); 339 cadets conferred degrees in the 148th course |
| 2026 | Women compete for 24 reserved vacancies in each NDA notification; girl cadets clear the written stage in growing numbers |

Before 2021, NDA entry was male-only. Women joined the forces through other routes — OTA, CDS, AFCAT — but the Academy itself was closed. The court's intervention didn't just open admissions; it created a permanent, notification-by-notification vacancy stream for women.

## The seats: 24 women in every NDA notification

The NDA exam runs twice a year through UPSC. The (I) 2026 and (II) 2026 notifications each announced **394 total vacancies, with 24 reserved for women**. The wing-wise split for women candidates:

| Wing / entry | Women vacancies (of total) |
|---|---|
| NDA — Army | 10 (of 208) |
| NDA — Navy | 5 (of 42) |
| NDA — Air Force (Flying) | 2 (of 92) |
| NDA — Air Force (Ground Duties, Tech) | 2 (of 18) |
| NDA — Air Force (Ground Duties, Non-Tech) | 2 (of 10) |
| Naval Academy (10+2 Cadet Entry) | 3 (of 24) |
| **Total** | **24 (of 394)** |

Two honest observations. First, 24 out of 394 is about 6% — small in absolute terms, which is exactly why preparation must be serious: every reserved vacancy attracts intense competition. Second, these figures are provisional in every notification; UPSC can revise them. Always verify the current count in the latest UPSC notification, not in last year's article.

For the next cycle: the NDA (I) 2027 notification is EXPECTED around December 2026 (the (I) 2026 notification came in December 2025), and NDA (II) 2027 is EXPECTED around May 2027 — both following the established UPSC rhythm.

## Eligibility: the non-negotiables

- **Age:** 16.5 to 19.5 years on the notification's cut-off dates. For NDA (II) 2026, that meant candidates born between 1 January 2008 and 1 January 2011 — and the window shifts similarly each cycle.
- **Marital status:** Unmarried, with an undertaking not to marry until training is complete.
- **Education:** Army wing — Class 12 pass in any stream. Navy and Air Force wings, and the Naval Academy 10+2 entry — **Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics**. This is the single most consequential subject choice a girl makes: without PCM in Class 11-12, the Navy and Air Force wings are closed to her.
- **Physical standards:** Height, vision and fitness norms apply; women cadets train on gender-neutral standards with adjustments for physiological differences.

## The Sainik School route for girls

Here's where this gets directly relevant to our readers. Sainik Schools now admit girls under a **10% quota — 10% of vacancies or 10 seats, whichever is more** — so a girl entering Class 6 in 2027 can plausibly ride the full Sainik School → PCM → NDA pipeline. And it's already working: [17 girls from Mai Bhago AFPI cleared the NDA (II) 2026 written exam](/news/mai-bhago-afpi-17-girls-clear-nda-written-2026-10-13/) this October, reportedly the institute's highest-ever tally — proof that the written stage is very much within reach.

The honest version of the pipeline looks like this:

1. **Class 6 entry** — AISSEE with the girls' quota; girls compete within their own merit mechanics.
2. **Classes 6–10** — the Sainik School environment builds exactly the habits NDA rewards: physical routine, discipline, group living.
3. **Class 11–12 with PCM** — non-negotiable for Navy/Air Force wings. Choosing PCM is choosing to keep all three wings open.
4. **NDA written at 16.5–19.5** — typically two attempts fall in Class 12 and the year after.
5. **SSB + medical** — the [five-day SSB process](/news/nda-written-qualifiers-ssb-stage-explained-2026-10-14/) screens for officer-like qualities; our explainer covers each stage in detail.

Note the important nuance: clearing the written exam is not selection. The written is 900 marks (Mathematics 300 + General Ability Test 600) and the SSB is another 900 — and merit lists for men and women are prepared separately.

## A realistic preparation checklist

**For parents of girls in Class 5–8:**
- Target Sainik School admission for Class 6 — the 10% girls' quota is a real, functioning entry route.
- Protect mathematics early. NDA's Maths paper is 300 marks of Class 11–12 syllabus; a girl who fears maths at 12 will not suddenly love it at 16.
- Build a sport into the weekly routine. Physical conditioning is the area where late starters struggle most.

**For girls in Class 9–12:**
- Choose PCM in Class 11 if the Navy or Air Force wings interest her. This one decision opens or closes two-thirds of the academy.
- Begin NDA-written preparation alongside Class 11 — the GAT's English and GK sections reward years of reading, not months of cramming.
- Track the UPSC notification cycle: (I) around December, (II) around May. Missing a notification date means waiting six months.

**For everyone:**
- Treat the 24 vacancies as what they are — few, and fiercely contested. Preparation should assume a high cut-off, not a comfortable one.
- Follow the written-result → SSB → merit-list sequence patiently; [this year's written results](/news/nda-ii-2026-sainik-school-scoreboard-2026-10-14/) show how the pipeline flows in practice.

## FAQs

**Can girls join the NDA?**
Yes. Since the Supreme Court's 2021 direction, women candidates apply through the same UPSC NDA examination. The first batch of 17 women cadets graduated from the NDA on 30 May 2025, completing the 148th course.

**How many NDA vacancies are reserved for women?**
In the NDA (I) and (II) 2026 notifications, UPSC announced 394 total vacancies with 24 for women: Army 10, Navy 5, Air Force flying 2, Air Force ground duties technical 2, Air Force ground duties non-technical 2, and the Naval Academy's 10+2 entry 3.

**What is the eligibility for girls in NDA?**
Unmarried female candidates aged 16.5 to 19.5 years. The Army wing needs a Class 12 pass; the Air Force and Navy wings (and the Naval Academy 10+2 entry) need Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics.

**How many marks is the NDA written exam, and is there a separate merit list for women?**
The written exam is 900 marks (Mathematics 300 plus General Ability Test 600), followed by a 900-mark SSB interview. Merit lists for men and women are prepared separately.

**How can a girl from a Sainik School reach the NDA?**
Through the 10% girls' quota for Class 6 admission in Sainik Schools, then Class 11-12 with PCM, then the UPSC NDA exam at 16.5 to 19.5 years. Several girl cadets are already clearing the NDA written stage — 17 girls from Mai Bhago AFPI cleared the NDA (II) 2026 written exam.
