## The mismatch parents discover too late

It usually surfaces at the worst possible moment: the AISSEE form is open, the clock is ticking, and a parent notices that the child's name on Aadhaar does not match the birth certificate. Sometimes it is a single letter — "Mohd" on one document, "Mohammad" on another. Sometimes the date of birth differs by a year, because an old enrolment form carried an approximate year. Sometimes the surname spelling changed when the family moved schools. None of this is anyone's fault, and all of it is fixable — but only if it is caught before the form is submitted, not after.

Here is the principle that keeps families out of trouble: **the AISSEE form must match the birth certificate and the school record, because those are the documents the admission process verifies against at every later stage.** Aadhaar is an identity document, and a mismatch with it is a problem worth fixing — but the fix belongs at UIDAI, not inside the AISSEE form. Copying a wrong Aadhaar spelling into the form just to make the two match creates a second wrong record instead of fixing the first one.

## The five mismatches that actually cause trouble

Not every difference matters equally. This is where parents should spend their checking time:

1. **Name spelling across documents.** Even a one-letter difference between the birth certificate, the school record and Aadhaar can trigger questions at document verification. Check all three side by side, character by character, including the father's and mother's names where the form asks for them.
2. **Date of birth.** This is the highest-stakes field in the whole process, because AISSEE eligibility is decided by age as on 31 March of the admission year — 10 to 12 years for Class 6 and 13 to 15 for Class 9 under the 2026 rules. A wrong year on any document can put eligibility itself in doubt.
3. **Gender and father's/mother's name.** Small errors here are common when an old Aadhaar enrolment was done quickly, and they are cheap to fix if caught early.
4. **Address.** This matters less for the form itself, but the domicile certificate — which decides the 67% home-state quota claim — must agree with the address story. A family that moved states needs the domicile question settled separately, not papered over.
5. **Photograph and signature.** These are not Aadhaar issues, but they are the most common rejection triggers on the form itself, and they are covered in detail in our photo and signature guide.

## How to fix Aadhaar, step by step

Start with the document that is right — almost always the birth certificate — and change everything else to match it. For Aadhaar, there are two routes:

**Online, through the myAadhaar portal.** Name and address updates can be submitted online: log in with the Aadhaar number and the OTP sent to the registered mobile, choose the field, type it exactly as it appears on the supporting document, upload a clear scan of that document, and pay the fee. Keep the Service Request Number (SRN) — it is the only way to track the request.

**At an enrolment centre, for date of birth and trickier cases.** Date of birth correction is tightly restricted by UIDAI: it generally allows only one correction, and once a date of birth has been verified with a proof document it is effectively locked. A centre visit is needed for most date-of-birth cases, with the birth certificate or Class 10 marksheet as proof, biometric verification, and an acknowledgement slip carrying an Update Request Number (URN). Do not hand this to an agent — pay only at the centre counter.

On cost and time, plan conservatively. UIDAI's demographic update fee at a centre is around **Rs 50**, and most updates complete in roughly **7 to 15 working days** — but verification-heavy cases can stretch to 30 days or more, and UIDAI's outer standard runs up to 90 days. That is the real reason to start now, while the AISSEE 2027 notification is still awaited: a correction started today is likely done before the form opens, while one started the week the form opens may not be.

| Mismatch | Fix it where | Route | Time to allow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name spelling in Aadhaar | UIDAI | myAadhaar online or centre | 7–15 working days |
| Date of birth in Aadhaar | UIDAI | Enrolment centre, birth certificate as proof | 15–30 days (start now) |
| Name/DOB in school record | School office | Written request + birth certificate | Before the form opens |
| Address in Aadhaar | UIDAI | myAadhaar online | 7–15 working days |
| Photo/signature on form | The form itself | Follow the portal's size/format specs | Same day |

## If time runs out before the fix lands

Sometimes the form opens before the correction finishes. Do not panic, and do not delay the form waiting for Aadhaar — a missed form deadline is worse than a pending correction. Fill the form with the **birth-certificate details**, keep the SRN or URN acknowledgement safe, and carry both the old and the corrected documents to every later stage. If a correction window opens after submission, use it for form fields the window actually allows — and check our correction-window guide first, because not every field is editable.

One more caution: never pay anyone who promises to "fix" Aadhaar or "guarantee" an AISSEE seat for a fee. UIDAI updates cost around Rs 50 at official channels, and no agent can change what the bulletin or the verification officer will accept. The honest version of this process is slow, paper-based and cheap — which is exactly why starting today, rather than the week the form opens, is the whole trick.

## FAQs

**Which document should the AISSEE form match if Aadhaar and the birth certificate differ?**

Match the birth certificate and the school record, which are the documents the admission process verifies against. Fix Aadhaar separately through UIDAI rather than copying a wrong Aadhaar spelling into the form.

**Can I correct a name or date of birth in Aadhaar online?**

Name and address updates can be made through the myAadhaar portal, while date of birth correction is tightly restricted and usually needs a visit to an enrolment centre with a valid proof document such as the birth certificate.

**How much does an Aadhaar update cost and how long does it take?**

UIDAI charges around Rs 50 for a demographic update at a centre, and most updates take roughly 7 to 15 working days, though the outer standard can run up to 30 to 90 days for verification-heavy cases, so start early.

**What if the Aadhaar mismatch is not fixed before the AISSEE form closes?**

Fill the form with the birth-certificate details, keep proof of the pending Aadhaar correction request, and carry both documents to every later stage, since verification stages compare the form against the certificates, not against Aadhaar alone.

**Is Aadhaar mandatory for the AISSEE form?**

The AISSEE 2026 bulletin asked for the standard set of certificates — birth, domicile and category where applicable — and the 2027 bulletin will confirm the exact document list. Do not assume any single document is optional until that bulletin is out.
