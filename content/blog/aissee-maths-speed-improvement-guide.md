"My child knows all the formulas but still leaves 10-15 maths questions unattempted."

I hear some version of this from almost every parent. The child understands the concepts, solves problems correctly at home — and then the clock eats half the paper. The uncomfortable truth: **AISSEE tests not just what your child knows, but how fast they can show it.** Speed in maths is not a talent, though. It is a trainable skill, and four to five weeks of the right drills can change a child's pace dramatically.

## Why maths decides the AISSEE outcome

✅ **CONFIRMED** (AISSEE 2026 bulletin; 🔶 **EXPECTED** for 2027):

**Class 6:** 150 min, 125 questions, 300 marks. Maths: **50 questions, 150 marks** — half the paper. Language, Intelligence and GK are 25 questions / 50 marks each.

**Class 9:** 180 min, 150 questions, 400 marks. Maths: **50 questions, 200 marks** — again half the paper.

A child quick at maths frees up time for everything else. A child stuck on five long maths questions loses those marks *and* the time owed to three other sections.

One more fact that should shape your strategy: ✅ **no negative marking in AISSEE** (CONFIRMED per NTA pattern). Fast students attempt everything; slow ones leave marks on the table. The rule is simple — *no question goes unattempted*.

## Diagnose the speed problem first

Watch your child solve one timed set and find out *why* they are slow:

1. **Slow calculation** — knows the method, but 47 × 36 by hand takes a minute. → Drills 1, 3, 8
2. **No triage habit** — spends six minutes on one hard question, never reaches the easy ones. → Drill 2
3. **Silly mistakes under pressure** — accuracy collapses against the clock. → Drills 4, 6
4. **OMR dread** — solves fine but panics at bubbling. → Drill 5

## The 9 drills

### 1. The 20-minute set

Pick 20 maths questions. Timer: 20 minutes — one minute per question. When it rings, stop; no finishing "just this one." Week one, most children finish 12-14. By week three, 18-20. This builds the most valuable exam skill: the instinct for when a question is eating too much time.

### 2. First-pass / second-pass triage

Two sweeps. **First pass:** solve only questions answerable in under a minute; star the rest and move on. **Second pass:** return to starred ones with the time saved. This banks quick marks early; with no negative marking, finish by attempting everything left.

### 3. The 10-minute mental-maths routine

Every morning: tables to 20, squares to 30, cubes to 15, fraction–decimal–percentage equivalents (1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/5, 1/8, 3/4 and their % values). A spoken breakfast-table quiz, not homework. When 14² = 196 lives in memory instead of on rough paper, 90-second questions start taking 30.

### 4. Estimate first, calculate second

For percentage, profit-loss, average and ratio questions, rough-estimate *before* exact working. A 15-second estimate often kills three of four options. Estimation also catches silly mistakes — a wild mismatch between estimate and answer means recheck.

### 5. The OMR bubbling routine

AISSEE is offline pen-and-paper OMR (CONFIRMED) — bubbling is where slow students silently lose 10 minutes. Bubble in batches after every page of questions: never one-by-one (distracting), never at the end (risky). Fill circles completely; reserve the last 5 minutes for an OMR-only check that every answer has exactly one bubble in the right row.

### 6. The error log notebook

Every mistake goes in: the question, the wrong answer, one line on *why* ("rushed the multiplication"). Review every Sunday. Within three weeks patterns emerge — most children repeat the same two or three errors, and fixing one type is worth 6-9 marks. The notebook becomes final-week revision material.

### 7. The weekly mock under real conditions

One full mock weekly: timed, seated, OMR sheet, no phone. Then spend equal time *reviewing* — slow questions, skipped ones, new error-log entries. A mock without review is just a long practice session. A mock with review is a diagnostic.

### 8. Formula flashcards

Thirty to forty cards, one formula each: area, perimeter, volume, simple interest, speed-distance-time, HCF/LCM, algebraic identities for Class 9. Ten minutes a day, shuffled. Instant retrieval means working time goes to solving, not remembering.

### 9. The calm exam-morning routine

Full sleep, a normal breakfast, reach early, read the paper two minutes before touching a pen. Calm is a speed strategy. More on the big day: [AISSEE exam-day tips for 2027](/blog/aissee-exam-day-tips-2027/).

## The 2-week speed plan

- [ ] **Daily:** one 20-minute timed set + 10 min mental maths + 10 min flashcards
- [ ] **Every session:** two-pass triage — mark hard questions, move on
- [ ] **Every session:** estimate before calculating on percentage/ratio/average questions
- [ ] **After every set:** log mistakes in the error notebook
- [ ] **Each weekend:** one full mock under real conditions + full review
- [ ] **Each Sunday:** re-read the error log; note shrinking error types
- [ ] **Night before mocks/exam:** normal dinner, full sleep, calm morning

Two honest weeks typically take a child from "leaves 10 questions" to "finishes with review time."

## Slow-down traps to avoid

- **Solving in order.** The paper is not ordered by difficulty *for your child*. Triage beats sequence.
- **Long division when estimation would do.** Every hand-written digit costs seconds.
- **Re-reading the question twice.** Read once, underline the numbers and the ask, solve.
- **Checking answers between questions.** Verification belongs in the review block, not mid-flow.
- **Practising without a timer.** Untimed practice builds understanding; only timed practice builds speed.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much time per maths question for Class 6?**
About 75-80 seconds — roughly 65 minutes for the 50 maths questions, 75 minutes for the other three sections, 10 minutes for review and OMR check.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**
No (CONFIRMED per NTA pattern). Attempt every question. A guess on an unanswered question costs nothing and can gain marks.

**Accurate but slow — speed or accuracy first?**
Accuracy first, always. These drills protect accuracy while building pace: estimation as a safety net, the error log for repeat mistakes, a dedicated review block.

**Which chapters matter most for maths speed?**
Number systems, fractions and decimals, percentages, profit and loss, simple interest, ratio and proportion, averages, speed-distance-time, mensuration, and for Class 9, algebra and basic geometry. High weightage and drill-friendly — they respond fastest to timed practice.

**How many mocks before the exam?**
One full mock per week until 31 January 2027. What matters more is the review: analyse each mock as long as you took it, and update the error log.
