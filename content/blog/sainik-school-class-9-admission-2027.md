Missed Class 6? Your child still has a genuine second door — **Sainik School Class 9 admission 2027**, through the same AISSEE exam, on the same date, 31 January 2027. That's the short answer to the question I hear most often, and it's worth saying plainly: the door is real. But it's a narrower door than Class 6, and the exam paper is entirely in English. If you plan for those two facts from day one, Class 9 entry is absolutely winnable.

A father from Lucknow called me last month. His son had missed Class 6 by eight marks in 2025. "He's in Class 8 now," the father said. "Have we missed the bus?" No. But — and I told him this directly — the Class 9 bus has fewer seats, and it runs in English. He nodded, and they started preparing the very next week. That attitude is what this guide is about.

## The Class 9 paper — and how it differs from Class 6

Here's the honest comparison, from the 2026 bulletin (2027 expected to repeat it):

| | Class 9 | Class 6 |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 180 minutes | 150 minutes |
| Questions | 150 | 125 |
| Total marks | **400** | 300 |
| Mathematics | 50 Q / **200 marks** | 50 Q / 150 marks |
| English | 25 Q / 50 marks | 25 Q / 50 marks |
| Intelligence | 25 Q / 50 marks | 25 Q / 50 marks |
| General Science | 25 Q / 50 marks | — |
| Social Studies | 25 Q / 50 marks | — |
| Medium | English only | Hindi / English / regional |

✅ CONFIRMED pattern (AISSEE 2026; 2027 repeat 🔶 expected). Offline OMR, all MCQs, **no negative marking**. Qualifying bar: 25% in each section and 40% aggregate (the 40% rule doesn't apply to SC/ST candidates).

Now, the part that actually matters. In Class 9, **each Maths question carries 4 marks** — in Class 6 it's 3. Maths is 200 out of 400. Half the paper. A silly calculation error doesn't cost you a mark here; it costs you four. I say this not to scare you but to set priorities: if your child masters Class 8 Maths and keeps English decent, half the battle is fought in one subject.

## Seats: the uncomfortable truth

Let's not sugarcoat it. Class 9 seats are genuinely limited. The 33 established schools admit far fewer Class 9 students than Class 6 — in some schools it's a handful of seats. In the 2026 cycle, 19 of the newer PPP-model schools also admitted Class 9 students, which helps. The final 2027 list of participating schools will come with the bulletin (🔶 expected October 2026). Girls are eligible for Class 9, but girls' seats vary from school to school — check the bulletin, don't assume.

Because seats are fewer, your **choice-filling strategy in counselling** matters even more for Class 9 than Class 6. Our [counselling guide](/blog/sainik-school-counselling-2027/) walks through exactly how to order your preferences so a good score doesn't go to waste.

## A 4-month study plan that actually fits

You have roughly four months until 31 January 2027. Here's how I'd split it:

**Maths (200 marks — the decider).** NCERT Class 8, cover to cover, then R.S. Aggarwal for volume. Algebra, geometry, mensuration — all three need to be automatic. Thirty questions a day, every day. No exceptions.

**English (50 marks).** NCERT Class 8 (Honeydew and It So Happened) plus Wren & Martin for grammar basics. One comprehension passage daily. The paper is long, so reading speed matters as much as grammar.

**General Science (50 marks).** NCERT Class 8 — numericals in physics chapters, key reactions in chemistry, labelled diagrams in biology. Examiners love diagrams.

**Social Studies (50 marks).** NCERT Class 8 History, Geography and Civics, plus basic map work. Don't neglect this — it's the easiest 50 marks if you've read the textbook twice.

**Intelligence (50 marks).** R.S. Aggarwal's reasoning section, twenty questions a day. This is the most scoring section on the paper.

## Class 9 entry vs Class 6 entry — which route is better?

Parents ask me this constantly: "We missed Class 6 — should we even try Class 9, or is it too late?" My answer: try. Definitely try. But understand the differences:

| | Class 6 entry | Class 9 entry |
|---|---|---|
| Seats | More (main intake) | Fewer (tougher competition) |
| Paper medium | Hindi / English / regional | English only |
| NDA runway | 7 years of preparation | 4 years of preparation |
| Syllabus base | NCERT Class 5 | NCERT Class 8 |

Four years in a Sainik School is still plenty of runway for NDA — see [the NDA career path after Sainik School](/blog/nda-after-sainik-school-career-path/). Late arrival beats no arrival. Every year.

## FAQs

**Q1. Is Class 9 admission harder than Class 6?**
Honestly? Yes, in one sense — fewer seats means stiffer competition. But the syllabus is just NCERT Class 8, which your child is studying anyway. A well-prepared student has a very real shot.

**Q2. My child studies in Hindi medium. Can they still attempt it?**
They can — but the paper is in English only. Start the switch now: English textbooks, English practice papers, even English newspaper reading. Four months is enough to build the habit if you start today.

**Q3. Can girls apply for Class 9?**
Yes, girls are eligible. The number of girls' seats differs by school, so check the bulletin when it's released.
