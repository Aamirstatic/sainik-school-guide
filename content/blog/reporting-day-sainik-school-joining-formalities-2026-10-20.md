Specific dates, fee heads and kit lists differ by school and year; treat those as per **your child's call-up letter**, not this guide.

The merit list is out, the allotment letter names a school, and now comes the part nobody writes about: reporting day. Parents call us asking the same three questions — what papers to carry, whether they can stay, and what happens to their child in the first week. This guide answers all three, plainly.

## The one document table that matters

Everything else on reporting day flows from paperwork. Carry originals **plus two sets of self-attested photocopies** of each. Schools verify originals against the copies and keep the copies.

| Document | Why they ask | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Allotment letter (AISSAC) | Proves the seat is yours | Print it; phone screenshots are not enough |
| Birth certificate | Age verification against AISSEE eligibility | Must match the name on the form exactly |
| Transfer certificate (TC) | Confirms the child left the previous school cleanly | Get it countersigned by the previous school's principal |
| Previous class report card | Academic eligibility check | Class 5 pass for Class 6 entry; Class 8 for Class 9 |
| Domicile certificate | Confirms home-state quota claim | Only if you claimed the 67% home-state quota |
| Category certificate (SC/ST/OBC-NCL) | Reservation claim | OBC-NCL must be valid for the relevant financial year |
| Defence/service certificate | Defence-quota claim | Only if you applied under the defence category |
| Medical fitness certificate | Some schools ask for one at reporting | If your letter does not mention it, the school's own check covers it |
| Passport photos (8–10) | File, ID card, medical records | Recent, same batch |
| Aadhaar card of child | Identity | Handy even when not listed |

See our [counselling-day master checklist](/blog/issee-counselling-day-document-checklist-2026-10-12/) for the fuller pre-reporting version of this list.

One honest warning: **name mismatches are the single biggest reporting-day headache.** If the birth certificate spells the name "Vikram" and the TC says "Vikram Singh", get an affidavit or a school correction *before* reporting day, not on it.

## What the day itself looks like

Arrive early — queues form fast and the whole exercise runs on a timetable, not on request. A typical reporting day runs in this order:

1. **Registration desk.** Allotment letter shown, the child's name is ticked off the master list, and you get a token or a queue number.
2. **Document verification.** Originals checked against photocopies, category and domicile certificates scrutinised, and the file stamped. This is where prepared parents sail through and unprepared ones stall.
3. **Fee counter.** The school's prescribed fees for the year are collected as per the admission letter — bring the exact payment mode the letter mentions (demand draft or online receipt; carrying both is not a bad idea).
4. **Medical check.** Most schools do a general medical examination around admission — eyesight, hearing, general fitness, and any conditions the school's standards bar. It may happen on reporting day itself or be scheduled within the first days; your call-up letter sets the timing.
5. **House allocation and kit issue.** The child is assigned a house (and a bed/roll number), and receives the school kit — uniforms, house colours, and basic issue items as per that school's list.
6. **The goodbye.** Parents leave. This is a residential school; there is no parent accommodation, and lingering does not help the child settle.

## A typical first week (illustrative)

Every school runs its own induction, but the shape is remarkably similar across schools:

| Day | What usually happens |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Reporting, kit issue, settling into the dormitory |
| Days 2–3 | Induction: rules, routine, campus orientation, house introductions |
| Days 4–5 | Classes begin; PT and games routine starts |
| Weekend | First letter/phone window as per school rules; the worst of the homesickness usually peaks here |
| Week 2 | Routine takes over — early mornings stop feeling impossible |

## The parent playbook for reporting week

- **Do not overpack.** The kit list in the joining letter is the authority; extra luggage just sits in a small locker. Gadgets and valuables stay home.
- **Set expectations before the drive home.** Tell your child you will be reachable on the school's designated days. Vague promises ("call anytime") backfire when the phone is locked up.
- **Money.** A small amount of pocket money as the school permits is fine; large cash is not.
- **Medicals.** If your child wears glasses, carries medication, or has any condition, declare it honestly at the medical check. Undisclosed conditions discovered later cause far more trouble than disclosed ones handled properly.
- **Resist the rescue urge.** The first fortnight is the hardest and also the fastest-adjusting phase. Calling the school daily, visiting unannounced, or pulling the child home for a "short break" resets the adjustment clock. Most schools, and most seniors' parents, will tell you the same.

## FAQs

**What documents do I need on Sainik School reporting day?**
The allotment letter, birth certificate, transfer certificate, previous class report card, domicile and category certificates if you claimed them, medical fitness certificate if requested, and recent photographs — originals plus two self-attested copies of each.

**Do parents stay on campus after reporting day?**
No. Once verification and joining are done, parents leave the same day. Sainik Schools are fully residential and do not provide parent accommodation.

**Is there a medical check on reporting day?**
Most schools conduct or verify a medical examination around admission time. Timing varies by school — follow your call-up letter.

**Do I pay fees on reporting day?**
Fees for the year are payable around joining time as stated in the admission letter. Keep the payment mode the letter specifies ready.

**What should my child carry besides documents?**
Follow the school's kit list: a few sets of personal clothing and essentials. Leave valuables and gadgets at home; lockers are small and phones are typically restricted for new students.
