# AISSEE GK Preparation: How to Balance Static GK and Current Affairs (Class 6 & 9)

> **Last verified: 29 September 2026.** Exam pattern facts marked CONFIRMED come from the AISSEE 2026 cycle; anything marked EXPECTED follows that pattern and awaits the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin (not released yet).

A parent from Bhopal called me last month: her son had a notebook full of current-affairs facts and had barely touched states and capitals. "Should he memorise every chief minister?" she asked. That backwards balance costs children the easiest 50 marks in the paper.

So here's the straight answer to the **AISSEE GK preparation** puzzle: the GK section is **25 questions for 50 marks** in Class 6, and the Class 9 Social Studies section is the same size. Most of those questions come from **static GK** — the stuff that doesn't change. Current affairs is the smaller slice. Study them like it.

The exam is on **31 January 2027** (CONFIRMED tentative, NTA exam calendar 2026-27). The AISSEE 2027 notification isn't out yet — the application form is 🔶 EXPECTED around October–November 2026, going by last year's pattern. Four months of GK prep from now is plenty — if you split the effort right.

## Know exactly what GK is worth in your child's paper

The real numbers (CONFIRMED, AISSEE 2026 pattern):

| Paper | GK section | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Class 6** | General Knowledge | 25 | 50 (of 300, 150 minutes) |
| **Class 9** | Social Studies (SST) | 25 | 50 (of 400, 180 minutes) |

Every question is worth **2 marks**, and there is **no negative marking** (CONFIRMED) — so every GK question is a free attempt. Full topic map: [AISSEE GK important topics guide](/blog/sainik-school-gk-questions-important-topics/).

## The split: how much static, how much current affairs

NTA publishes no official topic-wise breakup — anyone quoting exact static-vs-current question counts is guessing. Recent papers show static GK dominates; current affairs is the smaller, scoreable slice.

A working split for your timetable (our analysis of recent papers, not NTA-published):

| Class | Static GK | Current affairs |
|---|---|---|
| **Class 6** | ~70% | ~30% — a short daily habit |
| **Class 9** | ~70% | ~30% — often links back to SST (a new dam → geography) |

Three-quarters of the marks come from facts that don't change. The Bhopal family flipped to static GK on weekdays with current affairs as a 10-minute add-on — mock scores climbed from the teens into the thirties.

## Static GK: the topics that actually repeat

The buckets AISSEE questions keep returning to:

| Bucket | What to cover |
|---|---|
| **Geography** | states & capitals, rivers, mountains, neighbours, landmarks, soils & crops |
| **History** | freedom struggle, national leaders, monuments, key dates |
| **Polity / Civics** | President/PM/CM roles, rights & duties, Parliament |
| **Science facts** | vitamins & deficiency diseases, planets, inventions, units, body facts |
| **Sports & awards** | national game, Olympics basics, famous players, national honours |
| **Misc** | books & authors, neighbouring currencies, important days, UN basics |

Class 6: NCERT Class 6 books cover most of this. Class 9: go deeper via the NCERT Class 9 Geography, History and Civics chapters.

Geography and history pay the most — start there. Before buying any guidebook, read our [NCERT vs guidebooks comparison](/blog/ncert-vs-guidebooks-aissee-what-works/).

## Current affairs for kids: a source list that won't drown them

The method that works without becoming a second syllabus:

**The window:** roughly the **six months before the exam** (August 2026 to January 2027), plus the year's headline events — our recommended window, not an official syllabus.

**Kid-friendly sources (pick two, max):**

1. **A daily newspaper's front page + sports page, 10 minutes.** Headlines, who won, who was appointed. A Hindi paper works as well as an English one.
2. **School assembly news.** Most schools announce 2–3 items — treat those as free revision.
3. **One "GK diary" notebook.** Every fact goes in, dated, in the child's own handwriting — five lines a day. This diary is the entire December revision.
4. **The "in the news" boxes in NCERT books.** Those are exactly the level AISSEE asks at.

**Skip:** deep political analysis, economic commentary, opinion columns. If your child can't explain a fact in one sentence, it's too deep for this exam.

## AISSEE GK preparation routine: a sample week that fits a school day

Twenty to twenty-five minutes a day. Here's a week built around that slot:

| Day | 20–25 minute slot | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | Static geography flash facts | states, capitals, rivers, neighbours |
| **Tuesday** | Current affairs catch-up | last week's headlines, into the GK diary |
| **Wednesday** | Static history & civics | freedom struggle / Constitution (alternate weeks) |
| **Thursday** | Static science facts (Class 6) / NCERT SST chapter (Class 9) | The "heavy" day |
| **Friday** | Sports, awards & misc | National honours, famous players, important days |
| **Saturday** | 25-question mixed GK mini-mock | timed 30 minutes; wrong answers into the diary |
| **Sunday** | Light revision or off | re-read the week's diary — retrieval, not new study |

With no negative marking (CONFIRMED), a skipped question is a donated mark — so attempt all 25, always. Class 6 families: pair this slot with our [Class 6 maths formula cheat sheet](/blog/aissee-class-6-maths-formula-cheat-sheet/).

## This week's GK checklist

- [ ] Noted the target (Class 6: 25Q GK / Class 9: 25Q SST)
- [ ] Made a dedicated GK diary notebook
- [ ] Listed the six static buckets; ticked which NCERT covers
- [ ] Read one geography chapter of the NCERT SST book together
- [ ] Started the 10-minute newspaper habit (front page + sports page)
- [ ] Wrote 5 facts in the diary, dated, in the child's own words
- [ ] Did one 25-question GK set, attempting all 25
- [ ] Revisited Monday's static facts on Thursday (spaced revision, not cramming)

## Mistakes parents make (and how to dodge them)

1. **Studying 12 months of current affairs.** Low yield, high effort. Six months is enough.
2. **A diary written but never re-read.** The Sunday re-read is where the marks come from.
3. **Treating GK as "we'll do it in January."** January is mock-test season. Build static GK now; revise it then.
4. **Skipping questions in mocks.** On a no-negative-marking paper, a skipped question is a donated mark.

## FAQs

**How many GK questions are there in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
The Class 6 paper carries a GK section of 25 questions for 50 marks (CONFIRMED, AISSEE 2026 pattern). Each correct answer is worth 2 marks.

**Is there a separate current-affairs section in AISSEE?**
No. NTA doesn't officially split the paper — both sit inside the Class 6 GK or Class 9 SST section. Static GK is the larger share; current affairs is the smaller, very scoreable slice.

**How far back should my child study current affairs for AISSEE 2027?**
Roughly the six months before the exam (August 2026 to January 2027), plus the year's major events — our recommended window, not an official syllabus.

**Are NCERT books enough for the Class 9 Social Studies section?**
NCERT Class 8 and 9 Social Science books are the core — read them cover to cover, because the SST section is drawn from them. Add a short daily current-affairs habit on top, and use guidebooks for practice questions rather than as the primary source.

**Is there negative marking in the AISSEE GK section?**
No. There is no negative marking anywhere in the AISSEE (CONFIRMED). Your child should attempt every GK question — a guess costs nothing.
