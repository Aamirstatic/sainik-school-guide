## One school, one exam — and why that changes your strategy

Parents searching for "Sainik Schools in Odisha" usually expect a list. The honest core answer is shorter: Odisha has **one school under the Sainik Schools Society** — **Sainik School Bhubaneswar**. Every Odisha family aiming for a Society school through AISSEE is, in effect, aiming at the same campus. That concentrates the competition, but it also simplifies your planning: one school to research, one campus to visit, one preference to get right.

Around it, newer partnership-mode schools have appeared under the Sainik Schools Society's expansion. This guide separates the two layers so you do not mix them up — the partnership schools follow their own admission arrangements, while Bhubaneswar admits through AISSEE, conducted by NTA.

Bhubaneswar is one of the oldest Sainik Schools in the country — founded in the first wave of the 1960s, when the Sainik Schools system was created to prepare boys for the National Defence Academy. That six-decade history is the school's real strength for parents: a settled campus in the state capital, a large alumni base, and a routine refined over generations of cadets.

## The 67% Odisha quota — and why it helps you

Sainik Schools reserve **67% of seats for candidates from the home state** and **33% for candidates from other states**. For an Odisha-domicile child, that is a genuine advantage: most of Bhubaneswar's seats are contested only among Odisha children, not against the whole country. Within each quota, the standard reservation pattern applies — 15% SC, 7.5% ST, 27% OBC (non-creamy layer) — with 25% of the remaining seats for children of serving and ex-servicemen, and a 10% girls' quota in Class 6.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly. An Odisha child competes in a smaller pool than an out-of-state child applying to the same school. But the pool is still large, because there is only one Society school in the state. Do not treat the home-state quota as a guarantee — treat it as the reason Bhubaneswar, rather than a school in another state, should be your first preference.

## Seats: read the recent pattern, not a single number

Seat counts move from year to year, and secondary sources disagree, so here is the pattern rather than a pretended exact figure. A 2024-25 listing put Bhubaneswar's Class 6 intake at **80 boys plus 10 girls — 90 seats in total**. An older Press Information Bureau release for the 2023-24 session notified **103 boys' vacancies and 12 girls' vacancies**. The honest reading: recent Class 6 intakes have sat around 90–115 seats, with girls' seats at roughly a tenth of the total.

Class 9 is a different story everywhere, and Bhubaneswar is no exception. Class 9 entry opens only against vacancies — most seats fill at Class 6 — so treat a Class 9 attempt as a high-competition attempt and keep a realistic backup school in the preference list. The AISSEE 2027 seat matrix, when published, is the only figure to budget your expectations on.

## Fees: one reported figure, and the Odisha help that changes it

Careers360's 2026 school-wise fee list puts Sainik School Bhubaneswar at **Rs 1,62,822** as a lumpsum annual figure. Treat that as reported, not confirmed — school fees are revised periodically, and the school's own fee notice is the figure to budget from. For comparison, a lower-quality listing elsewhere quotes Rs 1,40,000, which shows why a single web figure should never be your budget.

What genuinely changes the affordability picture in Odisha is the state support, reported in a 2022 Press Information Bureau release:

- **Uniform scholarship scheme:** Odisha parents with annual income up to **Rs 3 lakh** receive scholarship support at par with the tuition fee charged by the school.
- **Diet subsidy:** Odia students received **Rs 75 per day for 295 working days — Rs 22,125 a year** — across SC, ST, OBC (non-creamy layer), defence and general categories.

Those two measures together were presented as bringing Sainik School education within reach of the common family. Both date from the 2022 release, so confirm the current scheme, income limit and subsidy rate with the school before relying on them — but do not skip the question, because for an eligible family they transform the fee from a barrier into a manageable cost. Keep your income certificate ready; our guide to [income certificates for Sainik School scholarships](/blog/income-certificate-sainik-school-scholarships-2026-10-11/) explains what to arrange in advance.

## Admission: the AISSEE route, step by step

1. **Watch the bulletin.** As of 27 October 2026, the AISSEE 2027 notification is still awaited on the NTA portal — see our [AISSEE 2027 notification guide](/blog/aissee-2027-notification-guide/) for how to check. Do not trust any site claiming the form is already out until the NTA portal shows it.
2. **Register online** at the NTA AISSEE portal when the window opens, and fill Class 6 or Class 9 with Bhubaneswar as your preferred school.
3. **Check the age window.** Class 6 means 10–12 years as of 31 March 2027; Class 9 means 13–15 years. Confirm the exact date-of-birth window in the bulletin.
4. **Prepare for the written test.** Class 6 covers Mathematics, Intelligence, Language and General Knowledge (300 marks); Class 9 covers Mathematics, Intelligence, English, General Science and Social Science (400 marks). There is no negative marking.
5. **After the merit list:** e-counselling (AISSAC), document verification and a medical examination decide the final admission.

For the full walkthrough, see our [AISSEE registration step-by-step guide](/blog/aissee-2027-registration-step-by-step-walkthrough/).

## Distance and language: the two questions Odisha parents actually ask

**Distance.** Bhubaneswar sits in the state capital, which is an advantage for most Odisha families: the nearest airport is Biju Patnaik International Airport, about 7 km away, and the city is well connected by rail and road to Cuttack, Puri, Berhampur and the western districts. For a family in Koraput or Sundargarh, it is still a long journey — plan the visit, and the admission trip, as an overnight stay rather than a day trip.

**Language.** The medium of instruction at Bhubaneswar is English, and the AISSEE papers are set accordingly — Class 9 is English-medium only, and Class 6 offers a choice of languages for the language section as listed in the bulletin. An Odia-medium-background child can absolutely succeed here, but the honest preparation advice is to build English reading comfort early: the exam rewards children who read the question correctly, not just children who know the answer.
