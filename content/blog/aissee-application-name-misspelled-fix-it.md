Every year the same panic arrives, usually in November. A parent submits the AISSEE form in a hurry — the deadline is days away, the photo took three attempts, the payment page timed out once — and hits *Submit* without reading the preview screen. A week later, while printing the confirmation page, they see it: the child's name reads "AADITYA" instead of "ADITYA", or the father's middle name has an extra letter, or the surname's spelling doesn't match the birth certificate.

Take a breath. A misspelled name is one of the most fixable form mistakes in the AISSEE process — *if* you handle it in the right order and in the right window. This is the step-by-step for that exact situation.

## Why the name matters more than other typos

Most form mistakes are cosmetic. The name is not. Here's why:

- **The admit card carries your name.** On exam day, the invigilator matches the admit card against the photo ID. Small spelling differences rarely cause a problem here, but they are where the trail starts.
- **Document verification is where it bites.** After the written exam and e-counselling, candidates go through medical and document verification before final admission. The verification officer compares the name on the application against the birth certificate, school records and category certificate. A mismatch there is an official discrepancy — the kind that needs explaining at a moment when you have no time.
- **Scholarship and quota documents must align.** If your child claims a reservation or a defence quota, the name on the caste certificate, service certificate and the application form must agree. Mismatched names across these documents is the classic reason files get held up.

So the rule is simple: **the name on the application should match the birth certificate and the school record, letter for letter.** Not the name as the family pronounces it, not the name as it appears on an older Aadhaar, not the nickname.

## Step 1: Decide which spelling is "correct"

Before you fix anything, decide which spelling is the truth. This is where most parents go wrong — they "fix" the form to match the wrong document.

| Document | Role | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Birth certificate | The legal identity document | **Highest — this is your source of truth** |
| School record (Class V/IX certificate) | The academic identity | Very high — verification compares these two |
| Aadhaar card | Commonly used photo ID | High, but align it too if it differs |
| Previous school TC / report cards | Supporting evidence | Useful if there's a dispute |

**Pick the spelling on the birth certificate and the school record** (they usually agree — if they don't, you have a bigger problem, and it's worth sorting that out with the school authorities before filling the form, not during it). Everything — the AISSEE form, the admit card, future counselling — should use this spelling.

One honest note: in many Indian families, children are known by slightly different names at home than in documents. The form does not care what the family calls him. It cares what the certificate says. Use the certificate.

## Step 2: The pre-submit checklist (if you haven't submitted yet)

If you're reading this while the form is still open, you're in the best possible position. Do this before clicking submit:

1. **Open the birth certificate and the form side by side.** Compare the candidate's full name — first, middle, last — character by character. Not a glance: read it aloud, letter by letter, at least once.
2. **Do the same for both parents' names.** Father's and mother's name spellings are checked against certificates too, and these are misspelled more often than the child's name because parents type them from memory.
3. **Check initials and expanded names.** If the birth certificate says "S. Ramesh", expanding it to "Suraj Ramesh" on the form creates a mismatch. Copy the certificate's format.
4. **Look at the preview screen, not the form fields.** NTA shows a confirmation preview before final submission. Errors that your eyes skip in the input boxes jump out in the preview. Screenshot or print this page.
5. **Don't submit in the last hour.** Server load near the deadline is exactly when hurried mistakes happen. Submit at least 24 hours before the deadline, when the confirmation page can be read calmly.

And while you're at it — the name is only one of the form's rejection traps. Our [photo and signature requirements guide](/blog/aissee-application-form-photo-signature-requirements/) covers the other classic form-killer.

## Step 3: The correction window (if you've already submitted)

Already submitted with a misspelled name? This is your lifeline.

After the application deadline closes, NTA opens a correction portal for a short period. Last year's window ran **2 to 4 November 2025 — three days** (CONFIRMED, via the Times of India and Careers360 reporting the NTA bulletin). Three days is not a safety net; it is a single, final opportunity.

Here's what the experience usually looks like (EXPECTED for 2027, based on past NTA practice):

- **Name spelling is typically editable.** Personal details — candidate name, parents' names, date of birth, category — have historically been correctable during the window. The final editable list for 2027 will be in the information bulletin, and you should check it before assuming.
- **Log in early, not on day three.** The portal slows down on the last day. Make the correction on day one, then download and print the corrected confirmation page.
- **Print everything twice.** Keep the original confirmation page (with the mistake) and the corrected one. If anyone ever asks what changed and when, you have the paper trail.

## Step 4: If the correction window has already closed

This is the section nobody wants to read, so let's be honest about it.

**NTA does not reopen the correction window.** There is no back-door edit, no helpline override, and — importantly — no legitimate "agent" who can fix it for a fee. Anyone offering to change your NTA form after the window closes is either running a scam or asking you to commit fraud. Report them; don't pay them.

What you *can* do:

- **Prepare your document file.** Gather the birth certificate, the school's Class V/IX record, and any government ID that carries the correct spelling. If the form and the certificates disagree by a letter or two, verification officers at the counselling stage generally reconcile obvious typos against the original certificates — but "generally" is not "always", and the stronger your supporting stack, the less it depends on anyone's generosity.
- **Get the underlying documents aligned now.** If the Aadhaar card carries the misspelling, start the Aadhaar correction process early (it takes weeks). If the school record itself has the wrong spelling, talk to the school — that correction is far more important than the form.
- **Don't re-apply with a fresh form.** Duplicate applications get flagged and can get *both* forms rejected. One application, one correction attempt, done properly.

## The one-minute summary

- The name on the form must match the **birth certificate and school record, letter for letter** — not the home name, not the Aadhaar, not the memory of it.
- Before submitting: compare documents side by side, read the preview screen aloud, submit a day early.
- After submitting: use the correction window (last year: 2–4 November, three days). Log in on day one, print the corrected confirmation.
- Window closed: no back-door fixes exist. Build your document file, align your underlying certificates, and never pay a tout.

A spelling mistake feels like a disaster at 11 pm in November. Handled in daylight, in the right window, it's a two-minute fix.

## FAQs

**Can I fix a misspelled name after submitting the AISSEE form?**

Usually yes — during the correction window NTA opens after the application deadline. Name spelling is typically among the editable personal details, though the 2027 editable list will only be confirmed in the information bulletin.

**When is the AISSEE 2027 correction window?**

No date has been announced yet; the 2027 notification is still awaited. Last year's window ran 2 to 4 November 2025, just three days (CONFIRMED via TOI and Careers360), so expect a similarly short window right after the application deadline.

**Which name should I use in the AISSEE application?**

The name exactly as it appears in the birth certificate and the child's school record. These are the documents verified at the counselling stage, so consistency across them is what matters — not the home name or a nickname.

**What happens if I miss the correction window?**

NTA does not reopen the window once it closes. A mismatch that survives the window will surface at document verification during counselling, so carry every supporting document that shows the correct spelling — and never pay anyone who claims they can reopen it.

**Is there a fee for correcting a name in the AISSEE form?**

There has been no separate correction fee in recent AISSEE cycles. A correction that changes your fee category, however, makes the fee difference payable at the time of correction. Confirm the exact rule in the 2027 information bulletin.
