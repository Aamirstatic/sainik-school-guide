## The school that turned Nalanda into an NDA factory

Here is a number that should make any Bihar parent sit up: in April 2025, Sainik School Nalanda was conferred the **Raksha Mantri Trophy for 2023-24** — the award given to the Sainik School that sends the *maximum* number of cadets to the National Defence Academy among all 33 Sainik Schools in the country. Eight of its cadets joined the NDA's 152nd Course in July 2024. It was the school's second time winning the trophy in a decade.

And it was not a one-off. In October 2026, **23 Nalanda cadets cleared the written stage of the NDA (II) 2026 examination** — the UPSC test held on 13 September 2026 — and moved on to the SSB interview. We covered that result in detail in our [report on the 23 NDA written-stage clears](/news/sainik-school-nalanda-nda-written-2026-results-2026-10-12/).

That is the context for everything else in this guide. Nalanda is not the oldest Sainik School, and it is not the biggest. But right now, it is one of the most effective at the one job Sainik Schools exist for: turning schoolchildren into officers.

A note on the campus size, because parents will find conflicting numbers: Wikipedia and the school's own profiles describe a **52-acre** campus. An older mirror of the Wikipedia page says 108 acres. We use 52 acres, and flag the difference honestly rather than picking the bigger number to sound impressive. What is not in dispute is the setting — a riverside campus at Nanand village in Silao block, near the historic sites of Nalanda and Rajgir, with Pawapuri, the Jain pilgrimage town, just 2.5 km away.

## A school that started in a rented building

Nalanda's story is worth telling your child, because it is a story about starting small. The school was inaugurated on 12 October 2003 at Rajgir — but it had no campus of its own. For its first twelve years it functioned from a **rented building of the Zila Parishad at Rajgir**, the Inspection Bungalow. It still won regional and national awards in academics and extra-curricular activities from that rented accommodation.

The move to its own building at Nanand came only in **December 2015**, when Chief Minister Nitish Kumar inaugurated the new campus. That same day in 2003 carries another distinction: **Bihar became the first state in India to have two Sainik Schools**, because Sainik School Gopalganj at Hathwa was inaugurated on the very same day, 12 October 2003, by the same Raksha Mantri.

The school's motto is *Vidya, Mritasya Va Chetana* — knowledge as the consciousness of the deathless. The houses are named for the great figures of ancient Magadha: **Ajatshatru, Ashoka, Siddhartha and Mahavira**. One caution: some listings name a fifth house, Bimbisara. We could not confirm that from the school's own pages or Wikipedia, so we list the four confirmed houses and treat the fifth as unverified.

## The seats: 85 in Class 6, 48 in Class 9

| Class 6 seats (2027-28) | Boys | Girls | Total |

| Bihar domicile | 52 | 5 | 57 |
| Other states | 23 | 5 | 28 |
| **Total** | **75** | **10** | **85** |

Home-state boys' seats by category: **SC 8, ST 3, OBC-NCL 14, Defence 6, General 21**. That split is what the bulletin actually lists — so a Bihar boy from the General category is competing for 21 seats, not 52. Parents who read only the headline number get a nasty surprise at counselling time.

Class 9 is where Nalanda stands out. **48 seats** — 29 boys from Bihar, 14 boys from other states, and 5 girls (4 Bihar, 1 other state). That is one of the larger Class 9 intakes among the 33 schools, and it makes Nalanda a genuine option for a child who missed Class 6 entry or is switching after Class 8. For comparison, its sister school Gopalganj has **zero** Class 9 seats this year.

One discrepancy to flag: Careers360 lists Nalanda's Class 9 seats as 31, not 48. Five other sources reproducing the bulletin — The Speedy Brains, Vikramaditya Academy, SarkariJob, Enunciate Academy and SkillUp Bihar — all say 48, and the all-India total of 732 Class 9 seats only adds up with 48. We go with 48, and note the outlier so parents who see 31 elsewhere know why.

The **67% home-state rule** is Nalanda's biggest advantage for Bihar families: 57 of the 85 Class 6 seats go to Bihar-domicile children first. Our [home-state quota guide](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-explained/) explains the arithmetic with worked examples, and our [Bihar guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-bihar-complete-2027-guide/) covers both of the state's schools side by side.

## The fee: honest about what we don't know

Here is where this guide is deliberately honest. **Sainik School Nalanda does not publish its own fee figure.** Its official fee notice says only that the whole session's fee is paid at once, or in at most two equal instalments — the first 10 days before the summer vacation ends, the second 7 days before the winter vacation ends.

The best available estimate comes from The Speedy Brains: **about Rs 1.96 lakh a year**, in a range of Rs 1.78 lakh to Rs 2.5 lakh across the 22 Sainik Schools that do publish a 2026-27 fee for new Class 6 cadets. The one solid anchor is tuition, which the Sainik Schools Society sets almost identically everywhere: **Rs 1,28,615 for 2026-27**. Diet, clothing and other charges make up the rest, and those differ by school.

So budget roughly Rs 2 lakh for the first year, and **confirm the exact 2027-28 figure from the school before paying anything**. Any website quoting an exact Nalanda fee as fact is guessing — we would rather tell you that plainly than invent a number.

Timings change with the season, but the shape of the day does not: PT before sunrise, classes until early afternoon, games in the evening, and two solid study blocks. Equestrian training is part of the games hour for detailed cadets. NCC is an integral part of school life, with an Independent Company covering all three wings — Army, Navy and Air Force. The alumni association is called **MOCA — the Mauryan Old Cadets Association** — and the school's own profile says more than 150 of its alumni are serving as officers in the Armed Forces (a figure from the school's Wikipedia profile, reported rather than independently verified).

## How to apply

Admission is only through **AISSEE**, the NTA-run national entrance exam. For 2027-28: apply online at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026 (11:50 pm)**. The late-fee window (Rs 500 extra) runs 3–6 December, corrections are allowed 7–9 December, the exam city is announced 11 January 2027, admit cards come on 25 January, and the exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**.

Class 6 needs age 10–12 on 31 March 2027 (born 1 April 2015 – 31 March 2017). Class 9 needs age 13–15 (born 1 April 2012 – 31 March 2014) and a passed Class 8. The application fee is Rs 850 (General, OBC-NCL, Defence wards) or Rs 700 (SC/ST). The Class 6 paper is 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks — Maths 150, Language, Intelligence and GK 50 each — with **no negative marking**. Qualifying marks are 25% in each section and 40% overall.

## The honest questions to ask

Before you put Nalanda first on the preference list, ask these — at the school, or of a parent whose child is already there:

- **How far is home, really?** Nanand is near Pawapuri, about 90 km from Patna. Term breaks are the only regular visits; the distance matters more in the first term than the brochure suggests.
- **What does the first-year bill actually total?** Get the school's own 2027-28 fee sheet in writing. The estimate above is a planning number, not a bill.
- **How does the school prepare cadets for the SSB, not just the NDA written?** Twenty-three written clears in 2026 is impressive — but the SSB is the harder filter, and the school's dedicated SSB orientation classes are the part worth asking about.
- **Is your child ready for a 5 am start?** The routine above is not a suggestion. A child who cannot wake early and live by a timetable will struggle, however bright they are.

Nalanda's pitch, in one line: a young school — barely 23 years old — that started in a rented building and now sends more cadets to the NDA than any other Sainik School in the country. If your child is a Bihar-domicile aspirant with a realistic shot at the AISSEE merit list, it deserves to be at the top of your preference list.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Nalanda have for 2027-28?**

Nalanda has 85 Class 6 seats — 75 for boys (52 Bihar domicile and 23 other states) and 10 for girls (5 Bihar and 5 other states) — and 48 Class 9 seats (43 boys and 5 girls). The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Nalanda founded?**

It was inaugurated on 12 October 2003 at Rajgir by the then Raksha Mantri, George Fernandes. Bihar became the first state in India to have two Sainik Schools, as Sainik School Gopalganj was inaugurated the same day.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Nalanda?**

Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026. Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Nalanda?**

The school does not publish its own fee figure. The best available estimate, from The Speedy Brains, is about Rs 1.96 lakh a year (range Rs 1.78 lakh to Rs 2.5 lakh across comparable schools). Tuition is set by the Sainik Schools Society at Rs 1,28,615 for 2026-27. Confirm the 2027-28 figure from the school before paying.

**Does Sainik School Nalanda admit girls?**

Yes — girls have been admitted since the 2021-22 academic session. For 2027-28 there are 10 girls' seats in Class 6 (5 Bihar, 5 other states) and 5 in Class 9.

**What is Sainik School Nalanda known for?**

Its NDA record. In April 2025 it won the Raksha Mantri Trophy for 2023-24 as the Sainik School that sent the maximum number of cadets to the NDA, and 23 of its cadets cleared the written stage of the NDA (II) 2026 examination.
