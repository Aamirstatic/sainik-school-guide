## The second exam nobody prepares for

Every January, the same story surfaces in coaching centres. A child who averaged 240 in mocks scores far below that in the real exam — and the questions were not the problem. The OMR sheet was. A skipped question, a half-darkened bubble, a row shifted by one: these are mechanical errors, and mechanical errors are the most fixable thing in this whole preparation. They just need training, the way a bowler trains a run-up.

Here is the thing most parents miss. Your child is not sitting one exam on 31 January — they are sitting two. The first is the paper: Maths, Intelligence, Language, GK. The second is the answer sheet: 125 bubbles for Class 6, 150 for Class 9, each one a small physical act that has to be exactly right. Nobody loses a seat because the Intelligence section was unfair. Children lose seats because of bubbling. And with 85 days left, there is enough time to train the second exam properly.

## Why the 2027 paper changes the OMR game

Three confirmed facts from the 2027 bulletin change how your child should practise.

First, **there is no negative marking**. A wrong answer and a blank answer both score zero. That means your child should attempt every single question — including the ones they are unsure about. But it also means a wrongly bubbled answer is indistinguishable from a wrong answer. Attempt-everything only helps if the bubbling is accurate.

Second, **the exam starts at 2 PM**. Class 6 runs 2:00 to 4:30 PM; Class 9 runs 2:00 to 5:00 PM. Most children do their homework in the morning and their mocks whenever they feel like it. A child whose brain is sharp at 10 AM and sleepy at 2 PM will underperform in the real hall. From this week, the serious mocks happen at 2 PM.

Third, **NTA provides the pens, and almost everything else is barred** — calculators, watches, wallets, belts, caps. So practise with an ordinary ballpoint pen, and practise without a wristwatch. Your child should learn to read the wall clock, because that is all they will have.

And one more fact worth remembering: ties are broken subject by subject, Maths first. Every mark your child saves from a bubbling error is a mark that could decide a tie.

## The 12-week OMR drill plan

Do not try to fix everything at once. One skill per phase, layered on the last.

| Phase | Dates | The drill | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Mechanics | 9 Nov – 6 Dec | One printed OMR sheet a day, 25 questions. Full, edge-to-edge darkening. Finger on the row, eyes on the question number. | Every bubble fully dark, no smudges, no double marks. |
| 2. The 2 PM slot | 7 Dec – 3 Jan | One full section every Sunday at 2 PM, on a real bubble sheet, timed. Skip-and-return practice: circle the skipped number in the question paper, re-match every 25 questions. | No shifted rows after a skip. Section finished with 5 minutes to spare. |
| 3. Full papers + audit | 4 Jan – 31 Jan | One full paper every week at 2 PM, then the 3-minute audit below. Last week: taper — one short drill a day, no new techniques. | The audit finds zero errors. The child finishes calm, not breathless. |

A printed OMR sheet costs nothing — free 100-question bubble sheets are everywhere online. Print a stack this weekend. The rule from today: no mock is ever done in a notebook again. If the answers are not bubbled, the mock did not happen.

## Six mistakes, six drills

**1. The number shift.** The big one. Your child skips question 47, solves 48, and marks 48's answer in 47's row. Everything after that is off by one. *The drill:* the circle-and-finger rule — circle the skipped number in the question paper, keep a finger on the OMR row, re-match every 25 questions.

**2. Half-darkened bubbles.** The scanner reads fully darkened circles. Light shading, tick marks, quick diagonal strokes — the machine may read them as blank. *The drill:* in Phase 1, time 25 bubbles. Speed comes from the wrist, not from sloppiness. If a bubble is not fully dark, it is not done.

**3. Two bubbles, one question.** Your child changes their mind, crosses out the first bubble and darkens a second. Scanners often reject the question entirely. *The drill:* decide before you bubble. If unsure, circle the question number and come back — never bubble twice.

**4. Bubbling everything at the end.** Solve the whole paper, bubble in the last 15 minutes. Then the bell comes early, or 15 minutes turns out to be enough for only half the sheet. *The drill:* bubble as you go, always. It is a non-negotiable rule from the first mock.

**5. The roll-number error.** Children obsess over the answers and rush the top of the sheet — roll number, question-booklet number, category bubbles. A wrong roll number can delay or even invalidate a result. *The drill:* the first two minutes of every mock are spent only on the header, checked twice, before question 1.

**6. Rough work on the answer sheet.** Scribbling calculations on the OMR sheet smudges bubbles and can damage the sheet. *The drill:* all rough work goes in the question paper booklet. The answer sheet stays clean from the first minute to the last.

## The 3-minute pre-submit audit

Teach your child to reserve the last 3 minutes of every paper — mock or real — for this, in order:

- [ ] Roll number and booklet number match the admit card
- [ ] Every attempted question has exactly one dark bubble
- [ ] No question has two bubbles or a crossed-out bubble
- [ ] The last question number on the sheet matches the last question solved
- [ ] Skipped questions are genuinely blank, not half-marked
- [ ] The sheet is clean — no rough work, no smudges, no folds

Run this audit after every mock in Phase 3. When it finds zero errors three mocks in a row, the OMR exam is trained.

## What to practise with at home

You need three things: printed OMR sheets, a ballpoint pen, and a wall clock. That is genuinely it. Sit your child down at 2 PM on Sundays — the same hour as the real exam — and let the body clock learn the slot. Keep the phone in another room. And when the mock is over, mark the OMR sheet the way a scanner would: a half-dark bubble is a wrong answer, a double bubble is a wrong answer, a shifted row is a wrong answer. Honest marking is what turns practice into points.

Eighty-five days is a long time in a 10-year-old's life. It is enough to make careful bubbling automatic — the kind of thing the hands do right even when the brain is tired at 4 PM. That is the whole point. On 31 January, your child will be tired. The training is what carries them through the last 25 questions.

## FAQs

**Should my child bubble each answer as they solve it, or bubble everything at the end?**

Bubble as you go, question by question. Saving all bubbling for the end is the single most dangerous habit in an OMR exam — a child who solves 50 questions but bubbles none of them has 50 zeroes if time runs out. Practise the bubble-as-you-go rhythm in every mock from now on.

**What pen will my child use to fill the OMR sheet in AISSEE 2027?**

NTA provides the pens inside the examination hall, and pens, calculators, watches, wallets, belts and caps are barred from the hall. Practise with an ordinary ballpoint pen, and do not send your child in with a gel pen, a pencil or correction fluid.

**What happens if the OMR sheet is torn or misprinted?**

Tell the invigilator immediately, before writing anything on it. A misprinted or damaged sheet is usually replaced at the start of the exam. A sheet damaged later by overwriting, scribbling or correction fluid is usually not replaced, so keep the answer sheet clean from the first minute.

**Does bubbling speed matter more for Class 6 or Class 9?**

Class 6, slightly. Class 6 gives 150 minutes for 125 questions, about 72 seconds per question, while Class 9 gives 180 minutes for 150 questions, also about 72 seconds per question — but Class 6 children are younger and slower at the physical act of bubbling, so they gain more from deliberate OMR practice.

**How do we practise OMR at home without printed answer sheets?**

Print free OMR practice sheets from the internet — any standard 100-question bubble sheet works — and use them for every mock test. Never practise bubbling in a notebook: the skill is the hand movement and the row-matching, and only a real bubble grid trains it.

**My child skips difficult questions. How do we stop the answers shifting by one row?**

Use the circle-and-finger rule: when your child skips a question, they circle its number in the question paper, keep a finger on the OMR row, and re-match the question number every 25 questions. It takes five seconds and it is the only reliable fix for the number-shift mistake.
