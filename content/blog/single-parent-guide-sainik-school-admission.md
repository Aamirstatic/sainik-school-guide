## First, the reassurance that matters: the form does not judge your family

Every year, single mothers and single fathers ask the same worried question: will the school treat our application differently? The honest answer is no — and it is worth stating plainly because the worry itself costs families sleep. AISSEE is an entrance examination run by NTA for the Sainik Schools Society. Admission is decided on the child's age eligibility, the entrance score, and the counselling merit list, under the reservation rules that apply to every candidate: 67% home-state and 33% other-state seats, with SC, ST, OBC (Non-Creamy Layer), defence and girls' quotas inside that matrix. Family structure is not a merit criterion, not a quota, and not a disqualification.

What single parenthood does change is practical, not official: one adult carries the paperwork, the deadlines, the fees and the emotional labour. This guide is built for that reality.

## The paperwork: same list, two extra habits

The AISSEE document list is the same for every applicant. Keep these ready before the notification drops, because the 2026 window lasted barely three weeks and the 2027 window may be just as short:

| Document | Why it matters | Single-parent note |
|---|---|---|
| Birth certificate | Proves age eligibility (10–12 for Class 6, 13–15 for Class 9 as of 31 March 2027) | The name on this certificate is the anchor for every other form — match spellings exactly |
| Domicile certificate | Decides the 67% home-state quota | Apply early; state portals commonly take weeks, and this is the document families most often leave late |
| Category certificate (if claiming SC/ST/OBC-NCL) | Reservation claim at counselling | Must be in the prescribed format and current for the relevant financial year — see our [caste certificate guide](/blog/caste-certificate-format-aissee-reservation-claims/) |
| School certificate | Confirms the child is in Class 5 (for Class 6 entry) or Class 8 (for Class 9 entry) | Get it from the current school before the winter rush |
| Photograph and signature | Uploaded with the form | Use the bulletin specs exactly; rejected uploads are a common, avoidable failure |

Two habits matter more than any extra paper. First, **make one folder — physical and digital — with everything in it**, because when a correction window or counselling document call comes, you will not have a second adult to search the house while you hold the phone. Second, **fill the form with what is true and verifiable, and nothing invented**. The form asks for parents' details; if a field genuinely does not apply to your family, follow the current bulletin's instruction for that field rather than entering a name or detail that no document supports. If the bulletin is unclear on your situation, the NTA helpline and the bulletin itself are the authorities — not a coaching centre's guess, and not a forwarded WhatsApp list.

One honest gap to flag: some families will face situation-specific questions — custody orders, guardianship papers, a deceased parent's certificate — and the right document depends on the individual case and on what the bulletin and the allotting school ask for at verification. This guide does not invent a universal rule for those cases. If any of them applies to you, sort the paper early, keep certified copies, and confirm with the school office at counselling stage rather than discovering the requirement on reporting day.

## Money: plan for the real costs, not just the fee

The AISSEE application fee in 2026 was Rs 850 for General/OBC/Defence categories and Rs 700 for SC/ST — treat the 2027 figures as expected until the bulletin confirms them. The bigger costs come after admission: annual school fees, uniform, books and travel. Published secondary figures for individual schools vary widely, which is why our state guides show reported ranges rather than single numbers — check the [Chhattisgarh guide](/blog/sainik-schools-in-chhattisgarh-complete-guide-2027/) for how that works in practice.

There is no single-parent fee concession in the AISSEE rules as published. Fee relief works through means-tested scholarships and state schemes, which depend on income and category. If money is tight, do three things early: check the school's scholarship page, check your state's scheme for Sainik School students, and ask the school office directly at counselling — that conversation is far more useful six months before admission than on reporting day. Our [domicile guide](/blog/domicile-certificate-sainik-school-guide/) also covers the certificate that most scholarship and quota claims hinge on.

## The decision only you can make: is boarding right for your child?

Sainik Schools are fully residential. For a single-parent family, that sentence carries extra weight: the school becomes, in part, the second adult in your child's daily life. That is a strength — structured routine, housemasters, peer group — but only if your child is ready for it. Before the counselling choices are locked, weigh these honestly:

- **Has your child spent nights away from home?** A week at a relative's house or a school camp is a better test than any conversation.
- **How does your child handle being upset without you nearby?** Homesickness in the first term is normal and common, not a sign of failure. The question is whether your child can ask an adult for help when upset.
- **Can you visit and call on the school's terms?** Each school sets its own phone and visiting rules, so confirm them with the school rather than assuming a daily call.
- **Who is your backup?** Name one trusted adult — a grandparent, relative or family friend — who can reach the school if you are unreachable, and give the school that number at admission.

If the answers point to "not yet", that is not defeat. A year of preparation, a Class 9 entry attempt instead of Class 6, or a day-school route with NDA preparation later are all legitimate paths, and our [motivation guide](/blog/motivate-child-aissee-without-pressure/) covers how to keep the dream alive without pressure.

## Setting up support before admission day

The families who settle fastest are the ones who set up support in advance. Use this checklist in the month before reporting day:

- [ ] One primary contact number that will actually be answered, plus one backup contact, given to the school in writing
- [ ] A fixed weekly call routine agreed with your child before they leave — same day, same time, so absence of a call means something
- [ ] A "first month" plan: letters or parcels in the first two weeks, a visit on the first permitted visiting day, and a calm response plan for the first homesick phone call
- [ ] Medical notes, allergies and ongoing medication handed over in writing to the school medical staff, not just mentioned verbally
- [ ] A homework-and-holiday rhythm for term breaks, so the return to school after each break is a routine, not a renegotiation

And one thing for you, the parent: find one other parent. The school's parent community — at admission, at the first parents' meeting, in the class group — is where practical answers live: which tailor, which bus, how the first term really goes. Single parenthood can feel isolating in a system built around two-parent assumptions, but in practice, Sainik School parent communities are full of families carrying their own version of the load. You will not be the only one.

## FAQs

**Can a single parent apply for Sainik School admission for their child?**

Yes. AISSEE admission is decided on the child's eligibility, entrance score and counselling merit, not on family structure. A single parent applies as the child's parent or guardian through the same NTA form as every other family.

**What do I fill in the form where it asks for both parents' details?**

Fill in what is true and verifiable, and follow the current information bulletin for the exact instruction. Never invent details for an absent parent. If a field genuinely does not apply, check the bulletin's guidance for that field before submitting, and keep the supporting document that matches what you entered.

**Are there extra documents a single parent must submit for AISSEE?**

The AISSEE document list itself is the same for every applicant: birth certificate, domicile certificate, category certificate if claiming reservation, and the school certificate. Anything beyond that depends on the specific situation and the bulletin's instructions, so verify in the 2027 bulletin rather than trusting forwarded lists.

**Is there a fee concession for single-parent families at Sainik School?**

There is no single-parent fee concession in the AISSEE rules as published. Fee relief at Sainik Schools works through means-tested scholarships and state schemes, which depend on income and category, not on single-parent status alone. Check the school's scholarship page and the state scheme rules for the current criteria.

**My child worries about leaving home for a boarding school. What helps most?**

Honest preparation helps more than reassurance alone: a pre-admission visit if possible, practice nights away from home, a fixed weekly call routine agreed in advance, and a plan for the first difficult month. Talk about homesickness as normal and temporary, not as a reason to quit.

**Who do I contact at the school as a single parent?**

The housemaster or house tutor is the first point of contact for day-to-day matters, and the school office for administrative issues. At admission time, give the school one primary contact number that will actually be answered, plus one trusted backup contact.
