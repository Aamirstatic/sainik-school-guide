The analysis method is editorial guidance from counselling practice, not an NTA recommendation.

Here is a sentence that stings a little: most students who "solve twenty mock tests" before AISSEE have not actually solved twenty mock tests. They have *attempted* twenty and *analysed* two. The rest were just confidence lotteries — a good score made them feel ready, a bad one made them feel hopeless, and neither taught them anything.

A mock test is not a rehearsal. It is a diagnostic scan. And a scan nobody reads is a waste of the hospital's time. Here is how to read yours.

## The 24-Hour Rule

Analysis must happen **within 24 hours** of the test — ideally the same evening. Wait two days and you will look at a wrong answer and think, "I knew that," which is exactly the self-deception that keeps the mistake alive. Fresh memory lets you reconstruct *why* you picked the wrong option, and the "why" is the entire point.

## The Five Mistake Buckets

Every wrong answer in AISSEE belongs to one of five types. Sorting them honestly is the single highest-value study habit a Class VI or IX aspirant can build:

| Bucket | What it looks like | The real problem | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Concept gap** | You had no idea how to start | The topic was never learned | Go back to the chapter, not the solution |
| **Silly error** | Calculation slip, bubbled the wrong option | Attention, not knowledge | Slow-down drills; circle units and keywords |
| **Time trap** | Ran out of time; left 15 questions blank | Pacing, not preparation | Per-section time budgets (see below) |
| **Misread question** | Answered what you *assumed*, not what was asked | Reading under pressure | Underline the actual question stem first |
| **Lucky guess** | Got it right by elimination or fluke | Fragile confidence | Treat it as wrong — log it anyway |

Be ruthless with the labels. Students label 80% of their errors "silly" because "silly" feels forgivable. It isn't. A recurring silly error is a process problem, and process problems are fixed by process — not by wishing.

## The Error Log: Your Real Syllabus

Buy a dedicated notebook. Rule five columns:

| Q No. | Topic | Error type | Correct approach (in your own words) | Revisit date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VI-42 | Fractions, unlike denominators | Concept gap | Find LCM first, then add — practised 5 sums | 6 Oct |
| IX-18 | Time & distance | Time trap | 60 sec/question cap; skip and return | 6 Oct |

Two rules make this work. First, the "correct approach" column must be written **in your own words** — copying the solution teaches nothing. Second, every row gets a revisit date exactly one week later, when you re-solve the question cold. If you get it right, strike the row. If not, it stays, and the topic goes back into the study plan. After a month, this notebook *is* your revision plan — better than any guidebook, because it contains only your weaknesses.

## The Topic Tracker

Once a week, tally the error log by topic. A simple sheet:

| Topic | Errors this week | Total attempts | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maths – Percentages | 4 | 12 | Improving (was 7) |
| GK – Science facts | 3 | 8 | Stuck — revise chapter |
| Intelligence – Series | 1 | 10 | Strong — maintain |

This is where [previous year papers and sample papers earn their keep](https://sainikschooleastsiang.in/blog/aissee-previous-year-papers-vs-sample-papers/): previous year papers calibrate you to NTA's real difficulty, sample papers give you the volume for drilling the weak topics the tracker exposes. Use the tracker to decide *which* sample papers to solve — not the other way round.

## Time Budgets: Where Time Traps Die

Most time traps in Class VI come from Maths, which carries **150 of the 300 marks** (50 questions × 3 marks). A practical budget for the 150-minute paper:

- **Maths:** 70 minutes (about 80 seconds per question)
- **Intelligence:** 25 minutes
- **Language:** 25 minutes
- **GK:** 20 minutes
- **Buffer + OMR bubbling:** 10 minutes

For Class IX (180 minutes, 400 marks), Maths deserves a similar overweight share — 200 of 400 marks. If your error log shows time traps clustering in Maths, the answer is not "go faster"; it is a strict per-question cap. When the cap hits, you mark, skip, and return. Remember: **there is no negative marking**, so every attempted question is free money — but only if you attempt it.

## The 45-Minute Post-Mock Routine

After every full paper, run this checklist before you touch anything else:

1. **Score it honestly** — no partial credit, no "I almost had it."
2. **Sort every wrong and skipped question** into the five buckets (10 min).
3. **Log them** in the error notebook with revisit dates (15 min).
4. **Re-solve the concept gaps** with the chapter open, then closed (15 min).
5. **Update the topic tracker** and pick the week's two weakest topics (5 min).

Then stop. Analysis longer than the test itself breeds fatigue, not insight.

## What Rising Scores Actually Look Like

Parents, this part is for you. A healthy mock trajectory is not a straight line up — it is a jagged climb where the *composition* of errors changes: concept gaps shrink first, silly errors linger longest. If your child's score jumps from 180 to 220 in two weeks, check whether the paper was easier. If the score stays at 200 for a month but time traps have disappeared, that is real progress — the ceiling is about to move.

And when the score dips? Good. A dip after a harder paper, fully analysed, is worth more than three easy-paper highs. ## FAQs

**When should a student start solving full-length AISSEE mock tests?**

Once the syllabus is broadly covered — roughly 10 to 12 weeks before the 31 January 2027 exam. Starting earlier usually produces discouraging scores that teach little.

**How much time should mock test analysis take?**

At least as long as the test itself — budget 45 minutes to an hour after every full paper. A two-hour paper with ten minutes of review is almost wasted.

**Should a student re-solve the same mock test?**

Yes — re-solve every wrong question one week later without looking at the solution. If you get it right, the concept is fixed; if not, it goes back into the revision queue.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**

No. Wrong answers and blanks both score zero, so nothing is deducted for guessing — but your error log should still record which guesses were lucky and which were educated.

**What is the most common reason mock scores stay flat?**

Repeating the same mistake type. Students who log every error by category — concept gap, silly error, time trap, misread question — can see the pattern and fix the right thing instead of just solving more papers.

**How many mock tests are enough for AISSEE 2027?**

Quality beats quantity. One analysed mock per week from October to January, plus two to three previous year papers, is more useful than thirty unreviewed papers.
