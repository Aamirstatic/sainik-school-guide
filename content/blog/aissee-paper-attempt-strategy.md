# How to Attempt the AISSEE Paper: A Question-Selection Strategy That Turns Knowledge Into Marks

> **🟢 Last verified: 28 September 2026** — ✅ **CONFIRMED** = from official/NTA sources. 🔶 **EXPECTED** = 2026-pattern projection.

Two children can know exactly the same syllabus. One walks out of AISSEE with a score 40 marks higher. The difference is rarely intelligence — it's what they did with their 150 or 180 minutes.

I say this to parents every year: **the AISSEE is a selection test, not a school exam.** Your child isn't trying to get everything right. They're trying to collect the maximum marks the paper allows in the time available. That is a strategy problem, and it can be trained. With **31 January 2027** as the exam date (✅ confirmed, tentative), there are four months to build the habit.

![A student writing in an exam hall, focused on question paper](/images/inbody/aissee-paper-attempt-strategy.webp)

## First, Know the Battlefield

The 2026 pattern (🔶 expected to repeat for 2027 — the NTA notification, still not released as of 28 September 2026, will confirm):

**AISSEE Class 6** — ✅ CONFIRMED (2026 bulletin):
- 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks
- Mathematics: 50 questions, 150 marks (half the paper!)
- Language: 25 questions, 50 marks
- Intelligence: 25 questions, 50 marks
- General Knowledge: 25 questions, 50 marks

**AISSEE Class 9** — ✅ CONFIRMED (2026 bulletin):
- 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks
- Mathematics: 50 questions, 200 marks (4 marks each — the highest value per question)
- English: 25 questions, 50 marks (English medium only)
- Intelligence: 25 questions, 50 marks
- General Science: 25 questions, 50 marks
- Social Science: 25 questions, 50 marks

Three rules from the 2026 bulletin shape everything below:
1. **No negative marking** — a wrong answer and a blank both score zero.
2. **25% in each section + 40% aggregate** to qualify.
3. **Pen-and-paper OMR** — the answer sheet is the only thing that gets scored.

## The Two-Pass Method: The Core of Everything

The biggest mistake children make is attempting the paper in order, question 1 to question 125, at whatever pace comes naturally. A hard Maths question at Q37 eats five minutes that belonged to five easy GK questions later.

**The two-pass method** fixes this:

**Pass 1 — the harvest (first 60% of your time).** Read every question once. If you can answer it in under 45 seconds, answer it and bubble it. If not, put a small dot next to it and move on.

**Pass 2 — the hunt (the remaining time).** Return to the dotted questions. Spend 2–3 minutes on the medium ones. For the ones you still can't solve, take your best guess — remember, no negative marking.

Why it works: easy questions carry the same marks as hard ones.

These are starting points. After two or three mocks, adjust: if your child finishes English early, move five minutes to Maths.

## The 25%-Per-Section Trap

Qualifying needs **25% in every section**, not just a good total. A child brilliant at Maths but weak at GK can score 200/300 and still fail with GK under 12.5. This pattern shows up every year: total 190+, one section at 10, result — not qualified.

**Your weakest section gets protected time, not leftover time.** Every section gets its own pass-1 block above. Section minimums first, total second.

## Guessing Rules (Everyone Will Guess)

With no negative marking, guessing is not cheating — it *is* the system. But smart guessing differs from random guessing:

1. **Eliminate first, always.** Even removing one option improves the odds.
2. **In Maths, plug options back in.** Substituting four options into the equation often turns a guess into a solution.
3. **No patterns across answers.** "Three B's in a row" means nothing; each question is independent.
4. **Trust first instincts.** Change an answer only if you spot a genuine error in your working.

Guessing is the *last* resort, not the first. Pass 2 exists so guessing happens after the brain has genuinely tried.

## The OMR Endgame: The Last 10 Minutes

Drill this into muscle memory:

- [ ] **Bubble as you go** — one shifted row corrupts every row after it.
- [ ] **Check row alignment every 10 questions** — question number = OMR row number.
- [ ] **Fill bubbles completely**; half-shaded bubbles can be misread.
- [ ] **One answer per row.** Erase completely if you change your mind.
- [ ] **Final 10 minutes:** count marked rows per section — any blank row gets a guess, now.
- [ ] **Don't change answers in the last 5 minutes** unless you spot a bubbling error.

## Practise It Like the Real Thing

From October, do **one full mock every week** under real conditions: time limit, two-pass method, the plan above, the OMR endgame. ## FAQs

**Is there negative marking in the AISSEE 2027?**
No. Per the NTA's 2026 pattern, a wrong answer and a blank both score zero — never leave a question blank.

**How should I divide my time in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**
For the 150-minute paper: 75 minutes for Mathematics (150 marks), 30 for Language, 25 for Intelligence, 20 for GK — plus 10 minutes for OMR bubbling and a final sweep.

**Should my child attempt all questions in the AISSEE?**
Yes. With no negative marking, an unattempted question scores the same zero as a wrong answer. Use the two-pass method: easy questions first, then come back for the rest.

**What happens if my child scores well overall but fails one section?**
The rule needs 25 percent in each section plus 40 percent aggregate. Falling below 25 percent in any single section disqualifies the result even with a strong total.

**Should my child bubble the OMR as they go or at the end?**
Bubble after each question — one shifted row corrupts every answer after it.

**Is changing answers in the AISSEE a good idea?**
First instincts are right more often than students believe. Change an answer only if you find a genuine error in your working — not because a question felt too easy.
