## Haryana's two Sainik Schools

For a Haryana parent, the Sainik School map is short and clear: the state has exactly two schools under the Sainik Schools Society — one historic, one modern. [Sainik School Kunjpura](https://sskunjpura.org/), near Karnal, is one of the first five Sainik Schools founded in India in 1961. Sainik School Rewari, at Gothra Ahir village in Rewari district, was established in 2008 and is the state's second school. Both are CBSE-affiliated, fully residential, co-educational boarding schools, and both admit only through the national AISSEE exam in Class 6 and Class 9.

## Kunjpura: the 1961 original

Kunjpura is not just Haryana's first Sainik School — it is one of the founding five of the entire Sainik School system. The school's own site records that it was founded on 24 July 1961 by V. K. Krishna Menon, then Defence Minister, and it stands on a property that once belonged to the last Nawab of Kunjpura, on the Grand Trunk Road side of Karnal. Its cadets are known as "Kunjeans", its motto is "Ready to Accomplish", and it runs 10 houses across 20 dormitories.

The record that Haryana parents quote most is the NDA record. Standard school histories report that more than 800 Kunjpura cadets have qualified for the National Defence Academy — a record 16 in 2006, rising to 33 in 2020, by the same accounts. Treat the exact tally as reported rather than official, but the direction is not in doubt: this is a school whose entire purpose, since 1961, has been preparing boys — and now girls — academically, physically and mentally for the NDA. Girls were admitted for the first time in 2021, with 10% of seats reserved for them.

## Rewari: the newer Haryana option

Rewari is the practical second choice that many Haryana families overlook. Established in 2008 at Gothra Ahir village, it offers the same CBSE residential pattern, the same AISSEE route and the same counselling — but with a younger campus and a location in southern Haryana that suits families from Rewari, Gurugram, Mahendragarh and the Delhi side of the state better than Karnal does.

One figure needs honest labelling. An admission directory lists Rewari's tuition fee at **Rs 1,16,923 per year**, plus diet, clothing, pocket money and other charges — but this is a single secondary source, not the school's own fee notice. Do not budget around it as a confirmed figure. The same caution applies to both schools' seat numbers: the exact 2027 seat matrix for Kunjpura and Rewari will only be confirmed in the AISSEE 2027 bulletin, and any coaching-site seat count quoted before that is a guess dressed as a fact.

## Admission: AISSEE, and only AISSEE

Both Haryana schools admit through the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE), conducted by the National Testing Agency for the Sainik Schools Society. There is no separate school-level test and no management-quota back door — the route is the national exam, then e-counselling (AISSAC), document verification and a medical test, in that order.

Two facts about the 2027 cycle need honest labelling. First, the **AISSEE 2027 notification is still awaited** — as of 24 October, no bulletin or registration link had appeared on the official NTA portal, so every 2027 date in this guide is EXPECTED, not confirmed. Second, the exam pattern itself follows the established AISSEE framework: Class 6 is a 300-mark paper (Mathematics, Intelligence, Language, General Knowledge) and Class 9 is a 400-mark paper (Mathematics, English, Science and Social Studies), both OMR-based, with no negative marking.

## The 67% Haryana quota — and what it means for your child

Under the standard Sainik School reservation pattern, **67% of seats are reserved for candidates from the home state** — here, Haryana — and 33% for candidates from other states. In practice, a Haryana-domiciled child competes first within the Haryana pool, which is a genuine advantage over applying to a school in another state, where the same child would fall in the smaller 33% other-state pool. Within that, the pattern reserves 15% for SC, 7.5% for ST and 27% for OBC-NCL candidates, 25% of the remaining seats for children of defence personnel, and 10% of seats for girls.

Two cautions. The home-state claim needs a valid domicile certificate, so get that document ready well before the form opens — the 2026 cycle's application window lasted only about three weeks, and a short window is possible again. And in AISSAC counselling, you list school preferences: a Haryana parent will typically list Kunjpura and Rewari high, but the allotment follows merit and the seat matrix, not preference alone. List honestly, in the order you would actually accept.

## Age limits and documents checklist

The age rules follow the pattern used every year:

- **Class 6:** 10 to 12 years as of 31 March 2027 (born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017, EXPECTED).
- **Class 9:** 13 to 15 years as of 31 March 2027 (born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014, EXPECTED), and studying in Class 8.

Keep this folder ready before the notification drops:

- [ ] Birth certificate (date of birth must match the form exactly)
- [ ] Domicile certificate for Haryana (for the 67% home-state pool)
- [ ] Category certificate, if applicable
- [ ] Candidate photograph, signature and left-hand thumb impression (scanned, in the sizes the bulletin specifies)
- [ ] Defence service certificate or PPO, if claiming the defence category

## What Haryana parents should do this week

1. **Visit if you can.** Karnal and Rewari are both day trips from most of Haryana. A campus visit settles the Kunjpura-vs-Rewari question better than any ranking.
2. **Get the domicile certificate ready.** It is the document that unlocks the 67% pool, and it is the one families scramble for when the window opens.
3. **Watch the NTA portal, not coaching forwards.** The AISSEE 2027 bulletin has not appeared yet; the only trustworthy "form out" news is the NTA portal itself.
4. **Keep the UP distinction clear.** If you also applied for the UP Sainik Schools exam, remember it is a separate form with its own late window closing 31 October — it does not cover AISSEE, and AISSEE does not cover it.
5. **Talk to your child about boarding, honestly.** Both Haryana schools are fully residential from Class 6. A child who has never stayed away from home needs preparation, not just a form.

Haryana gives its parents a rare luxury in this system: two good schools, one historic and one modern, both within the state, both reachable by the same exam. Use the waiting time before the notification to choose between them properly — then, when the form opens, you will be deciding from knowledge, not from panic.
