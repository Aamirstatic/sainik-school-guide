Clearing the AISSEE is the hard part — or so it feels in January. But ask any family that went through it last year, and they'll tell you the real heart-stopping moment came months later: the day they walked into the school for physical verification and medicals, carrying a folder of documents they'd assembled in a panic over the previous fortnight.

Here's the uncomfortable truth most guides skip: **seat allotment through AISSAC does not confirm admission.** Your child's seat is confirmed only after the medical examination and document verification are complete. Families who lose seats at this stage rarely lose them on merit — they lose them on paperwork. This checklist exists so that isn't you.

## Before the checklist: understand the sequence

For AISSAC 2026, the sequence ran roughly like this:

1. **AISSEE result** (late February) — qualified candidates registered on the AISSAC portal for e-counselling.
2. **Online registration and choice filling** — entirely online. Per the official AISSAC 2026 FAQ, **no documents were required during registration**; candidates produce them at physical verification at the admitted school.
3. **Seat allotment** (provisional) — based on AISSEE rank, category, gender and domicile.
4. **Medical examination** — shortlisted candidates reported to allotted medical centres with a specific document set; the AISSAC medical notice (March 2026) stressed that missing the medical window could disqualify the candidate.
5. **Physical verification and final admission** — originals verified, self-attested copies submitted.

The practical takeaway: documents are demanded at two pressure points — the **medical examination** and **physical verification**. The folder you carry to both is what this checklist builds.

## The master checklist (printable)

For each document: get the **original**, plus **2 self-attested copies** (sign each copy yourself and write "self-attested"). In 2026 the medical notice specifically asked for self-certified copies along with originals.

A few of these deserve their own explanations, because the details are where families get tripped up.

## Three documents that need head-starts

**Domicile.** Several states take **2–6 weeks** to issue or re-issue a domicile certificate, and the portal systems are notoriously moody. If your certificate is torn, old-format, or missing, start the re-application now. Our [state-wise domicile process guide](/blog/domicile-certificate-online-apply-state-wise-process-2026-10-11/) walks through portals and timelines.

**Income certificate.** Required both for the counselling document set and for [scholarship and fee-concession claims](/blog/income-certificate-sainik-school-scholarships-2026-10-11/) — it's valid for one financial year, so a certificate issued after April 2026 is what the 2027 cycle will want.

**Birth certificate.** Name or date-of-birth mismatches between the birth certificate, Aadhaar and the AISSEE application are the single most common verification failure. If there's a mismatch, [start the correction process now](/blog/birth-certificate-correction-before-aissee-form-2026-10-10/) — it can take weeks, and the form window won't wait.

## The two mistakes that cost real families real seats

**Mistake 1: "We'll arrange the documents after the allotment."** The medical window is short and the notice is blunt — in 2026, AISSAC stated that a medical examination not completed in time could disqualify the candidate from further counselling. The families who scramble after allotment discover that the tehsil office takes three weeks for an income certificate and the district office needs two visits for a domicile. The folder should be **complete before the AISSEE result**, not after.

**Mistake 2: Mismatched names and dates across documents.** Your child's name spelled "Aarav" on the birth certificate, "Aarav Kumar" on Aadhaar and "Aarav Kr" on the form is a verification problem. So is a date of birth that reads differently on two documents. The school office checks this mechanically at physical verification — and "mechanical" means no sympathy. Walk through your checklist once, comparing every name and date across every document, at least a month before counselling.

## On counselling day itself: the practical playbook

- **Carry originals in one folder, copies in another.** When the officer asks, you hand over originals for verification and self-attested copies for the file — no fumbling.
- **Reach early.** Medical examinations involve actual queues, and late arrivals in 2026 were simply warned by the notice — it said the candidate is responsible for completing the process in time.
- **Keep one master scan of everything** (phone + email). If any document goes missing in transit, a scan buys you time.
- **Don't chase the category certificate after allotment.** If you claimed OBC-NCL, the validity rules apply at verification time — an expired certificate is one of the quietest ways to lose a seat you've already been allotted.

## The bottom line

Think of counselling as a second exam — one with no negative marking, no syllabus, and a very long preparation window. Your child studies for the AISSEE; **you** study this checklist. The families who walk into the school in May with a complete folder don't remember the paperwork. The families who don't remember the day forever.
