## Ten weeks. Ten Mondays. One exam

The AISSEE 2027 exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. Count back from there in whole weeks and you land on **Monday, 23 November 2026** — this coming Monday. Exactly ten weeks. Not "about two months", not "sometime in January" — ten clean Monday-to-Sunday weeks, each with a job to do.

Parents ask us constantly how much time is enough. Here's the honest answer: ten weeks is enough to finish the syllabus, take three full mocks, revise everything once, and walk into the exam hall calm. It is not enough to do all of that *and* panic. The difference between the two is a calendar — a real one, stuck on the wall, with one job per week.

## The 10-week calendar

| Week | Dates | Study focus | Mock / admin job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mon 23 – Sun 29 Nov | Finish the weakest section first — the one your child avoids | **Submit the AISSEE form** (closes Wed 2 Dec, 11:50 PM) |
| 2 | Mon 30 Nov – Sun 6 Dec | Full syllabus: one chapter a day, no new books | **Mock 1** (Sun 6 Dec, full paper, 2:00 PM start) |
| 3 | Mon 7 – Sun 13 Dec | Fix what Mock 1 exposed; start a mistakes diary | Correction window 7–9 Dec — check the confirmation page |
| 4 | Mon 14 – Sun 20 Dec | Second pass: Maths + Intelligence daily, GK notes | Timed section tests (30 min each) |
| 5 | Mon 21 – Sun 27 Dec | Revision round 1: every chapter, fast, no re-reading whole books | Christmas week — keep the daily habit, shorten the hours |
| 6 | Mon 28 Dec – Sun 3 Jan | GK + current affairs catch-up; language section polish | **Mock 2** (Sun 3 Jan, full paper, 2:00 PM start) |
| 7 | Mon 4 – Sun 10 Jan | Fix what Mock 2 exposed; speed drills in Maths | No new material from this week on |
| 8 | Mon 11 – Sun 17 Jan | Revision round 2: only the mistakes diary + formulas | **Mock 3** (Sun 17 Jan); city slip out Mon 11 Jan — plan travel |
| 9 | Mon 18 – Sun 24 Jan | Light revision; one section test a day, max | Admit card out Mon 25 Jan is next week — keep login ready |
| 10 | Mon 25 – Sun 31 Jan | Taper: short revision, early nights, exam-day rehearsal | **Admit card Mon 25 Jan** — print two copies; **exam Sun 31 Jan, 2:00 PM** |

## How to actually use this calendar

**One job per week, not five.** The reason most study plans fail is that they ask a 10-year-old to do everything every day. This calendar asks for one thing each week. Week 1 is about the weakest section — if your child hides from Maths, Maths gets week 1. If GK is the gap, GK gets week 1. Everything else can wait its turn; the weak section cannot, because it is the one that decides whether the 25%-per-section rule is cleared.

**The mocks are at 2:00 PM on purpose.** The real exam starts at 2:00 PM. A child who always practises in the morning will feel slow and sleepy at 2:00 PM on 31 January. All three full mocks in this calendar are on Sundays at 2:00 PM — same start time, same duration (150 minutes for Class 6, 180 for Class 9), same rule: phone in another room, OMR-style bubbling if you can manage it. The point of a mock is not the score. It is the rehearsal.

**The mistakes diary is the whole game.** After each mock, your child writes down every question they got wrong or skipped, in one notebook, with the reason: "didn't know", "knew it but ran out of time", "silly mistake", "misread the question". Weeks 3, 7 and 8 exist only to work through that diary. Children who do this improve by 20–30 marks between Mock 1 and Mock 3.

## The three admin deadlines hiding in the plan

Study plans forget paperwork, and paperwork is what actually disqualifies children. Three dates sit inside these ten weeks — miss one and the studying doesn't matter.

**Wednesday, 2 December 2026, 11:50 PM — the form closes.** If you have not applied yet, week 1 is form week. Do it in week 1, not week 2. The late window (3–6 December, Rs 500 extra) exists for emergencies, not as a plan — and the last-day server rush on 2 December is real. The fee is Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, and Rs 700 for SC/ST.

**7–9 December — the one-time correction window.** You get one chance to fix mistakes in the submitted form, and some fields — name and date of birth among them — cannot be changed at all. Use week 3 to open the confirmation page with your child and read every line out loud: spelling of the name, date of birth against the birth certificate, category, school choices. Fix what can be fixed; note what cannot.

**Monday, 11 January and Monday, 25 January 2027 — city slip and admit card.** The city slip (11 January) tells you only the exam city, so you can plan travel. The admit card (25 January) carries the centre address and reporting time. Download both from the NTA AISSEE portal — exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society — and print two copies of the admit card the same day. Keep the application number and password safe until the entire admission process is over.

## What the exam actually looks like

For families new to this, the paper itself, so the calendar makes sense:

**Class 6:** 125 questions, 300 marks, 150 minutes — Maths 50 questions (150 marks), Language 25 (50), Intelligence 25 (50), General Knowledge 25 (50). The paper can be written in English, Hindi or 11 other languages.

**Class 9:** 150 questions, 400 marks, 180 minutes — Maths 50 questions (200 marks), English 25 (50), Intelligence 25 (50), Science 25 (50), Social Science 25 (50). English medium only.

Both papers are pen-and-paper OMR tests with **no negative marking** — a wrong answer costs nothing, so every question should eventually be attempted. To qualify, a child needs at least **25% in each section and 40% overall**; the 25% rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates.

## The last week, in detail

Week 10 is the week parents most often get wrong, so here it is plainly. **Monday 25 January:** admit card out — download, check every detail, print two copies. **Tuesday to Thursday:** short revision only — formulas, the mistakes diary, nothing new. No mock tests. **Friday:** pack the bag — admit card, a valid ID, two blue/black ballpoint pens — and visit the centre if it is in another town. **Saturday:** an early night, a normal dinner, no studying after 8 PM. **Sunday 31 January:** reach the centre early, seated well before 2:00 PM, and trust the ten weeks.

Last year, a parent told us his son spent the final week doing four full mocks, one a day, and walked into the exam exhausted. The child knew the syllabus. He just had nothing left. Ten weeks of steady work beats one week of panic — every single time.

## FAQs

**When is the AISSEE 2027 exam?**

Sunday, 31 January 2027, from 2:00 PM. The Class 6 paper runs 2:00 to 4:30 PM (150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks) and the Class 9 paper runs 2:00 to 5:00 PM (180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks). Both are pen-and-paper OMR tests with no negative marking.

**How many weeks are left for AISSEE 2027?**

Exactly ten weeks from Monday, 23 November 2026 to the exam on Sunday, 31 January 2027. That is ten full Monday-to-Sunday weeks — enough for three full mock tests, a complete revision cycle, and a proper taper in the final week.

**When does the AISSEE 2027 form close?**

The regular window closes on Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM. A late window runs from 3 to 6 December with a Rs 500 late fee, and a one-time correction window runs from 7 to 9 December. Do not plan to use the late window — it exists for emergencies, not as a plan.

**When do the AISSEE 2027 city slip and admit card come?**

The exam city intimation slip is announced on Monday, 11 January 2027, and the admit card is released on Monday, 25 January 2027, both on the NTA AISSEE portal. The city slip tells you the exam city only; the admit card carries the centre address and reporting time.

**How many mock tests should my child take in the last 10 weeks?**

Three full-length mocks, one each in weeks 2, 6 and 8 of this calendar, plus short timed section tests in between. More mocks than that usually means less time for fixing what the mocks reveal — and the fixing is where the marks come from.

**What is the qualifying mark for AISSEE 2027?**

At least 25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate. The 25 percent section rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates. For Class 6 that means 75 of 300 marks in each section's share; for Class 9, 100 of 400 marks overall.
