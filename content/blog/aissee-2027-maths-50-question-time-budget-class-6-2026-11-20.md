## Maths is half the paper. It does not get half the time — it gets 60 minutes

Here is the mistake most families make when they hear "Maths is 50 questions for 150 marks." They hear: *Maths is the most important section, so give it the most time.* That is true. Then they quietly slide into: *give it as much time as it needs.* That is where the marks leak.

Your child has 150 minutes for the whole paper. Maths is worth half the marks, but the other three sections — Language, Intelligence and GK — are worth the other half, 150 marks, in 75 questions. Every minute past 60 that goes to Maths is a minute stolen from 75 questions worth just as much.

So this article is not another "practise more sums" piece. It is a budget. A number your child can write at the top of the question paper on 31 January and actually follow.

## The budget: 150 minutes, four sections, one buffer

| Section | Questions | Marks | Minutes | Pace |
|---------|-----------|-------|---------|------|
| **Mathematics** | 50 | 150 | **60** | ~72 seconds per question |
| **Language** | 25 | 50 | **25** | ~60 seconds per question |
| **Intelligence** | 25 | 50 | **35** | ~84 seconds per question |
| **General Knowledge** | 25 | 50 | **20** | ~48 seconds per question |
| **Buffer + final pass** | — | — | **10** | Review, guesses, OMR check |
| **Total** | **125** | **300** | **150** | |

Three things to notice. First, Intelligence gets more time per question than Maths, because pattern and series questions genuinely take longer to see — you cannot rush an answer into appearing. Second, GK gets the least time per question, because your child either knows it or does not; staring at a GK question for two minutes has never once produced the capital of a state. Third, the 10-minute buffer is not spare time. It is the most valuable 10 minutes in the paper: the final pass, when every blank question gets an answer, because there is no negative marking.

## A worked example: what the paper actually feels like

Take a real Sunday mock, 2:00 to 4:30 PM, and watch the clock the way the budget says to:

**2:00–2:05 — the opening five minutes.** Read the instructions, check the OMR sheet, and do the first five Maths questions. They are almost always the easy ones — simple arithmetic, a fraction, a percentage. Five quick correct answers in the first five minutes does something no pep talk can do: it tells an 11-year-old's nervous system that this paper is beatable.

**2:05–3:00 — Maths, 60 minutes, with a hard stop.** Work through the 50 questions in order, but with one rule: any question that has not yielded an answer after 90 seconds gets a small circle in the margin and is left alone. That is not giving up. That is the budget working. The circled ones come back in the buffer — and here is the thing most children discover only in mocks: half of the circled questions solve themselves in 20 seconds on the second look, because the brain kept working on them in the background.

**3:00–3:25 — Language, 25 minutes.** The Language section is the fastest marks in the paper for most children. Comprehension, grammar, vocabulary — the questions are short, and the answers are usually visible. Do not re-read a passage three times. Read it once, properly, answer, move on.

**3:25–4:00 — Intelligence, 35 minutes.** This is where the paper slows down, and that is planned. Series, odd-one-out, coding, mirror images — each one needs a moment of looking before the pattern clicks. If a question does not click in 90 seconds, circle it and move on. Intelligence is the section where stubbornness costs the most, because one stuck question can quietly eat five minutes.

**4:00–4:20 — GK, 20 minutes.** Fast. Answer what you know, circle what you half-know, skip what you do not. A child who finishes GK in 15 minutes has just earned five extra buffer minutes.

**4:20–4:30 — the final pass.** Go back to every circled question and answer all of them — guess if you must, because a wrong answer costs exactly the same as a blank one: zero. Then check the OMR sheet: every bubble filled, no double-marking, question numbers matching. This is also when you catch the classic error — an answer marked against the wrong question number after a skipped question.

Now compare that with what actually happens to most children without a budget. They spend 85 or 90 minutes on Maths, because Maths feels like the real exam. They reach GK at 4:15 with 25 questions left and 15 minutes on the clock. They guess wildly, or worse, they leave ten questions blank — ten free marks, abandoned, in a section that needed 48 seconds a question.

## The three clock moments that decide the paper

Write these three times on the question paper before the exam starts. They are the only three numbers your child needs to remember:

1. **3:00 PM — Maths must be over.** Not "nearly over." Over, with circled questions left for later. If your child is still on question 44 at 3:00, the budget has already failed, and the rest of the paper pays for it.
2. **4:00 PM — Intelligence must be over.** GK is waiting, and GK is fast marks. A child who starts GK at 4:05 still has time. A child who starts GK at 4:20 does not.
3. **4:20 PM — stop solving, start finishing.** The last ten minutes belong to the circled questions and the OMR sheet. No new thinking. Just completion.

Honestly, most guidebooks overcomplicate this. Speed in AISSEE Maths is not a talent. It is a habit built in exactly one way: full papers, timed, on Sundays, with the clock written on the paper. ## How to practise the budget at home

Do not practise it in pieces. A child who does 50 Maths questions in 60 minutes at home, on a Tuesday evening, has practised Maths. A child who sits a full 150-minute paper on a Sunday at 2:00 PM, with the section clocks written on the paper and a parent keeping time, has practised the exam.

Three rules for the Sunday mock:

- **Same time as the real exam.** 2:00 to 4:30 PM. The brain learns the rhythm of an afternoon paper only by doing afternoon papers. Morning mocks do not count.
- **The clock on the paper.** Write 3:00, 4:00 and 4:20 at the top of the question paper before starting. The budget only works if it is visible.
- **Analyse the clock, not just the score.** After the mock, do not only count the marks. Ask: where did the minutes actually go? [Our mock-test analysis routine]({{< relref "how-to-analyse-aissee-mock-tests-83-day-routine.md" >}}) walks through exactly that — and the answer is usually one section that quietly stole 15 minutes from the others.

One more thing, and it matters more than any of this. Last year, a parent called us in a panic because her son had finished his mock with 20 questions unattempted — and 15 of them were GK, the easiest marks in the paper. He had not run out of knowledge. He had run out of clock, on question 38 of Maths. That is the whole story of this article, in one phone call.

With 72 days left until 31 January, there is time for about ten Sunday mocks. Ten chances to practise a budget that, on the day, is worth more than ten extra sums. Start this Sunday.

## FAQs

**How many minutes should my child spend on the AISSEE Class 6 Maths section?**

About 60 minutes for the 50 Maths questions, out of the 150-minute paper. That is roughly 72 seconds per question on average, with easy questions taking far less and the hard ones borrowing from a 10-minute buffer kept for review.

**How many marks is Maths worth in AISSEE Class 6?**

150 marks out of 300 — exactly half the paper. Maths has 50 questions of 3 marks each. Language, Intelligence and GK are 50 marks each, with 25 questions apiece.

**What happens if my child spends too long on Maths in AISSEE?**

Every extra 10 minutes on Maths is 10 minutes taken from the other three sections, which together are also worth 150 marks. The most common score leak is not wrong Maths answers — it is unattempted GK and Language questions at the end of the paper.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**

No. Wrong answers and blank answers both score zero, and nothing is deducted. That is why the budget below always ends with a final pass: every unanswered question is a free lottery ticket in the last 10 minutes.

**How can my child practise this time budget before the exam?**

With one full 150-minute paper every Sunday, timed exactly like the real exam — 2:00 to 4:30 PM, phone in another room, OMR sheet, and the section clock written on the question paper. Practise the budget, not just the sums.

**Which Maths topics take the longest in the AISSEE Class 6 paper?**

Multi-step word problems and mensuration (area, perimeter, volume) usually take the longest, because they need two or three steps before the answer appears. Fractions, decimals and percentages are faster once the method is automatic — which is why they are the best place to bank time.
