## Mizoram's only Sainik School — and the one that changed the rules for girls

Here is the fact that shapes everything else in this guide: **Sainik School Chhingchhip has 120 Class 6 seats and 90 Class 9 seats for 2027-28.** The Class 6 intake ties it with several of the biggest schools in the country. The Class 9 intake is more unusual still — 90 seats is the largest Class 9 intake of any Sainik School in India. Most Sainik Schools take 10 to 30 children in Class 9. Chhingchhip takes 90.

That matters because Class 9 entry is usually the harder door. Fewer seats, older children, a tougher paper. At Chhingchhip, the door is wider than anywhere else — and for a family in Mizoram, it is also the only door. Chhingchhip is Mizoram's only Sainik School, and for many families in the Northeast, it is the school their child can actually reach.

And then there is the history. In June 2018, six girls walked through these gates and became the first girl cadets in the 57-year history of Sainik Schools. The pilot worked so well that within three years, every Sainik School in India was admitting girls. This guide tells you what the school is, what it costs, what the seats look like, and the honest questions to ask before you choose it.

## The girls' story — the reason this school is in the history books

Sainik Schools were conceived in 1961 to prepare boys for the National Defence Academy. For 57 years, they admitted boys only. Then the Ministry of Defence picked one school for a pilot: the newest one, in a small Mizoram village, where the campus had already been built to suit girls.

In August 2017, the school was told to reserve 10 percent of its Class 6 strength for girls. Within three weeks, 31 girls from across Mizoram had applied. They sat the written exam in Aizawl, Lunglei and Chhingchhip in January 2018; 21 were called for interview; and six were finally admitted. They arrived on 4 June 2018: Zonunpuii Lalnunpuia, Jurisa Chakma and Malsawmthari Khiangte, all 11, and Alicia Lalmuanpuii, Lalhminghlui Lallianzuala and Elizabeth Malsawmtluangi, all 12. Six girls among 154 boys.

The Lok Sabha was told on 4 April 2018 that girls' admission at Chhingchhip had been approved from the 2018-19 session on a pilot basis. The pilot succeeded. In 2020, five more schools turned co-educational. From the 2021-22 session, all 33 Sainik Schools admitted girls — 312 girl cadets that first year. The first six are now young adults; some of them have begun preparing for the NDA exam, where they would once again be firsts.

For a parent today, the practical meaning is simple: Chhingchhip is not experimenting with girls' education. It has been doing it longer than any other Sainik School in India, and it has the hostel, the staff and the routine to show for it.

## Seats for 2027-28 — the numbers that decide your child's chances

The AISSEE 2027 bulletin seat matrix is tentative — it can change before e-counselling — but it is the best picture available before the 2 December form deadline:

| Entry | Seats (2027-28, tentative) |
| --- | --- |
| Class 6 — total | **120** |
| Class 6 — boys, Mizoram domicile | 75 |
| Class 6 — boys, other states | 33 |
| Class 6 — girls (7 Mizoram, 5 other states) | 12 |
| Class 9 — total | **90** |
| Class 9 — boys, Mizoram domicile | 55 |
| Class 9 — boys, other states | 25 |
| Class 9 — girls (5 Mizoram, 5 other states) | 10 |

Two things deserve your attention. First, the home-state quota does real work here: 67 percent of seats are for Mizoram-domicile children, and the school's own staff said at a press meet in Aizawl in November 2026 that those Mizoram seats often go unfilled — and are then taken by children from other states. If your child is Mizoram-domicile, the competition for those 75 boys' seats is thinner than the headline number suggests. Apply. That is the whole point the school was making.

Second, the Class 9 door. Ninety seats, against 731 across all 33 schools. If your child is in Class 8 now and missed the Class 6 window years ago, this is the widest Class 9 door in the country. The home-state split inside Class 6 boys' seats is SC 11, ST 5, OBC-NCL 21, Defence 9 and General 29 — the standard reservation pattern, with 25 percent of the remainder for defence wards.

## What it costs — and the scholarship most Mizoram parents miss

A new Class 6 cadet pays **Rs 2,49,866 for 2026-27** (Rs 2,48,366 for SC/ST), as per the school's official fee document. The pieces:

| Item | Amount (2026-27) |
| --- | --- |
| Tuition fee | Rs 1,28,615 |
| Dietary charges (Rs 265 × 295 days) | Rs 78,175 |
| Annual term deductions (entertainment, newspapers, washing, communication, olympiad, books and stationery, exam fees, barber, pocket money) | Rs 18,000 |
| Security money (Gen/Def) | Rs 3,000 |
| Security money (SC/ST) | Rs 1,500 |
| Clothing (maximum, if all items procured) | Rs 22,076 |
| **Total, General** | **Rs 2,49,866** |
| **Total, SC/ST** | **Rs 2,48,366** |

Existing cadets pay Rs 2,24,790 — no security money, clothing only as required. Term deductions and clothing are charged on actual expenditure, with the balance carried forward. Fees are paid online or by bank draft payable at Chhingchhip, and they rise every year — confirm the 2027-28 figure with the school before you pay.

Now the part most parents outside Aizawl never hear about: the **Mizoram Scholarship Board runs a scheme specifically for Chhingchhip cadets**, funded from the State Plan, for Mizoram-domicile children admitted through the entrance exam, with income limits set by the Board. The school's staff also told the Aizawl press meet that fee reimbursement from the government is available. Ask the school office about both when you apply — a Rs 2.5 lakh fee looks very different once the state scholarship is counted.

The exam itself: Class 6 is 150 minutes, 125 questions, 300 marks (Maths 50 questions for 150 marks; Language, Intelligence and GK 25 questions for 50 marks each). Class 9 is 180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks (Maths 200). Both are pen-and-paper OMR with no negative marking, and the qualifying bar is 25 percent in each section and 40 percent overall. Apply only on the official portal, exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society — NTA has no agents, and anyone offering to file the form for a fee is not acting for NTA.

## The honest questions to ask before you choose Chhingchhip

**Is your child ready for a 5:45 AM start, six days a week?** The routine is the education as much as the classes are. Children who thrive here are the ones who wanted the discipline, not the ones who were sent for it.

**Are you a Mizoram family that hasn't applied because the seats feel out of reach?** The school itself is telling you the opposite: its home-state seats go unfilled. The bigger risk is not applying at all.

**What about the NDA?** Honestly, Chhingchhip is still writing that chapter. The first batch joined in 2017, so the first Class 12 batch passed out only recently, and no headline NDA selections have been publicly reported yet. What the school does offer is the full preparation — NDA-oriented academics, NCC, physical training — in a state that had no Sainik School at all before 2017. Judge it on that, not on a league table that doesn't exist yet.

**What if your child is a girl?** She would be joining the school that proved girls belong in Sainik Schools — the one with the longest track record of teaching them, and 12 Class 6 and 10 Class 9 girls' seats this year. Read our [girls' quota guide](/blog/girls-quota-sainik-schools-explained-2026-10-22/) for how the 10-percent rule works across all 33 schools.

Chhingchhip is not the oldest Sainik School, the biggest, or the most famous. It is the one that changed the institution — and the one with the widest Class 9 door in India this year. For a Mizoram family, that combination is hard to beat.

## FAQs

**How many seats does Sainik School Chhingchhip have for 2027-28?**
Chhingchhip has 120 Class 6 seats — 75 for Mizoram boys, 33 for boys from other states, and 12 for girls (7 Mizoram, 5 other states) — and 90 Class 9 seats (55 Mizoram boys, 25 other-state boys, 10 girls). Its 90 Class 9 seats are the largest Class 9 intake of any Sainik School in India. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin, Appendices VI-A and VI-A1.

**When was Sainik School Chhingchhip founded?**
It was established on 21 April 2017 and inaugurated on 17 May 2017 by the Chief Minister of Mizoram. It is the 26th Sainik School in India and the fourth in the Northeast. The first batch was 60 cadets in Class 6. Some older pages on this site said 21 January 2018 — that was wrong, and has been corrected here.

**Why is Sainik School Chhingchhip famous for girls' admission?**
On 4 June 2018, six girls joined Chhingchhip as a pilot project — the first girl cadets in any Sainik School in India. The pilot's success led the government to open girls' admission in all 33 Sainik Schools from the 2021-22 session, when 312 girl cadets were admitted across the country.

**What are the fees at Sainik School Chhingchhip?**
A new Class 6 cadet pays Rs 2,49,866 for 2026-27 (Rs 2,48,366 for SC/ST), as per the school's official fee document: tuition Rs 1,28,615, dietary charges Rs 78,175, term deductions Rs 18,000, security money Rs 3,000 (Rs 1,500 SC/ST) and clothing up to Rs 22,076. Existing cadets pay Rs 2,24,790. Fees rise every year, so confirm the 2027-28 figure with the school before paying.

**How do I get my child admitted to Sainik School Chhingchhip?**
Only through AISSEE, the national entrance exam run by NTA. For 2027-28, apply online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society by 2 December 2026 (11:50 PM). Class 6 needs age 10 to 12 on 31 March 2027; Class 9 needs age 13 to 15 and a passed Class 8. There is no direct admission or management quota.

**Do Mizoram children get preference at Sainik School Chhingchhip?**
Yes — 67 percent of seats are reserved for Mizoram-domicile children and 33 percent for other states. But the school's own staff said at a press meet in Aizawl in November 2026 that the Mizoram seats often go unfilled and are then taken by children from other states, so they are urging more Mizo families to apply.
