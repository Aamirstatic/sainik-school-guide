## You paid. That is not the same as being in.

Here is the mistake I hear about every year, usually in the second week of December, when it is already too late to fix cheaply: a parent pays the fee, sees the money leave the account, and assumes the form is done. Then the admit card never arrives in January, and the portal shows no application at all.

Payment is one step. The application is complete only when the **confirmation page is generated**. That is the rule in the bulletin, and both independent guides that read it in full say the same thing. So if you have already submitted — or you are about to — run these five checks tonight. Each one takes a few minutes. Together, they are the difference between a form that counts and a form that does not.

## 1. The confirmation page — download it, don't just see it

After the fee is paid, the portal generates a confirmation page. **Download it and save a copy somewhere you will find it in January** — your phone, your email, a printed copy in the file with the certificates. A screenshot of a "payment successful" message is not the same thing. If the page never appeared, your application is not complete, even if the money left your account.

## 2. The application number — write it down in two places

The confirmation page carries your child's **application number**. You will need it for everything that follows: checking the exam city on 11 January, downloading the admit card on 25 January, and any call to the NTA helpline. Write it in your phone's notes and on paper. Parents who lose it in December spend the worst week of January trying to recover it.

## 3. The fee receipt — match it against your bank statement

The fee is **₹850 for General, OBC (NCL) and defence wards, and ₹700 for SC/ST**, and it is non-refundable. Check that the amount debited matches the fee for your category, and keep the transaction reference. If the money was debited but no confirmation page appeared, **log back in and check the application status before paying again**. NTA keeps the latest form if two are paid for the same class — a second payment in a panic can leave you with a duplicate form instead of a fixed one.

## 4. The details on the form — read them once more, slowly

Open the submitted form and read the name, date of birth, category, domicile, school choices and the four exam-city preferences against the original documents. The name and date of birth must match the birth certificate exactly. The question-paper medium for Class 6 — one of 13 languages — **cannot be changed after it is exercised**, so check that it is the language your child actually studies in. The exam city has no link to the school you want, so pick cities you can actually reach on 31 January.

## 5. The correction window — know what it is, and don't lean on it

The correction window runs from **7 to 9 December 2026**, up to 11:50 PM on 9 December. That is your safety net for errors in the particulars — but treat it as a net, not a plan. 🔶 **EXPECTED:** based on NTA practice in earlier cycles, the window covers corrections to details in the form, not a fresh submission or a change of the locked medium. Submit a correct form now; use December only for genuine slips you spot on a re-read.

## FAQs

**How do I know my AISSEE 2027 form is submitted?**

Your form is complete only when the confirmation page is generated after fee payment. Download it and keep it safe — a bank debit alone does not prove the application went through.

**I paid the AISSEE fee but got no confirmation page. What should I do?**

Log back in to the NTA portal and check your application status before paying again. If two forms are paid for the same class, NTA keeps the latest one, so a hasty second payment can create a duplicate instead of fixing the first.

**When is the AISSEE 2027 correction window?**

7 to 9 December 2026, up to 11:50 PM on 9 December. Use it to fix errors in the particulars — but do not count on it for everything, and submit a correct form the first time if you can.

**What is the last date to apply for AISSEE 2027?**

2 December 2026, up to 11:50 PM, including fee payment. A late window runs from 3 to 6 December 2026 with an additional late fee of Rs. 500.
