## The smaller door most families forget about

Everyone talks about Class 6. It is the big door — **3,160 seats** across the 33 Sainik Schools, and most of the advice, coaching and panic is aimed at it. But if your child is in Class 8 right now, the door you are walking through is a different, much smaller one: **731 seats** for Class 9 across the same 33 schools.

That is roughly one Class 9 seat for every four Class 6 seats. And the paper is longer, harder and entirely in English. If you are filling the form for Class 9 this week, do it with your eyes open.

## A different paper: 400 marks, English only, three hours

Here is the thing most Class 9 parents discover too late: this is not the Class 6 paper with a few extra questions. It is a different exam.
| | Class 6 | Class 9 |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | 150 minutes | 180 minutes |
| Questions | 125 | 150 |
| Total marks | 300 | 400 |
| Medium | 13 languages | English only |
| Maths | 50 Q / 150 marks | 50 Q / 200 marks |
| Other sections | Language, Intelligence, GK — 25 Q / 50 marks each | English, Intelligence, Science, Social Science — 25 Q / 50 marks each |

Two things jump out. First, **Mathematics is half the paper** — 200 of the 400 marks. Second, the whole paper is in **English only** — no Hindi or regional-language option — so a child who studies science and social science in another medium needs to be comfortable answering in English.

The good news is the same as Class 6: **no negative marking**, and the qualifying bar is **25% in each section and 40% in aggregate** (the section-wise minimum does not apply to SC/ST candidates).

## Who can apply — and the Class 8 rule that trips families up

Your child must be **13 to 15 years old on 31 March 2027** — born between **1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014** — and must have **passed Class 8 from a recognised school at the time of admission**. A child currently in Class 8 can apply, but the admission itself depends on passing Class 8, so keep the passing certificate ready the moment it arrives.

Girls can apply for Class 9, but be honest with yourself about the numbers: girls' Class 9 seats are listed school-wise in the bulletin and are very few in most schools. Check the appendix for your chosen school before you build your hopes around it.

## Four schools have no Class 9 seats at all

This is the detail that catches people out. Four of the 33 schools — **Bhubaneswar, Bijapur, Gopalganj and Jhunjhunu** — list **zero Class 9 seats** for 2027-28. If your child has their heart set on one of those four, Class 9 is not the way in this year. Pick another school on the form, and remember what the bulletin says: list the school you actually want even if a category shows zero, because tentative vacancies get redistributed — but make sure at least one of your choices has real Class 9 seats. We explained that zero-seat rule in detail in our [school-choice guide](/news/aissee-2027-8-days-left-school-choice-home-state-quota-zero-seat-rule-2026-11-24/).

For scale, the biggest Class 9 intakes are at schools like **Tilaiya (25 seats)** and **Kunjpura (30 seats)** — and even those are small next to their Class 6 intakes. Our full seat count is in [this morning's piece](/news/aissee-2027-3160-class-6-seats-731-class-9-seats-2026-11-24/).

## What to do in the next 8 days

- **Confirm the age window first.** If your child's birth date falls outside 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014, check before you pay.
- **Keep the Class 8 proof ready.** Know when the school issues the passing certificate, and have the school's contact at hand.
- **Budget for the English-only paper.** If your child studies in another medium, spend these last weeks reading science and social science in English — not learning new chapters, just getting comfortable with the language of the questions.
- **Apply before 2 December, 11:50 PM.** The fee is **₹850** for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards and **₹700** for SC/ST. Miss the date and the late window (3–6 December) costs an extra **₹500**.
- **Note the later dates now.** NTA announces the exam city on **11 January 2027** and the admit card on **25 January**; the exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027, at 2:00 PM**.

Eight days is enough — if the form is filled carefully and the child keeps revising maths daily. Class 9 is the smaller door, but it is still open.

## FAQs

**How many Class 9 seats are there in AISSEE 2027?**

731 Class 9 seats across the 33 Sainik Schools, against 3,160 Class 6 seats. The figures are tentative, as listed in the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin's seat appendices.

**What is the AISSEE Class 9 exam pattern?**

A 180-minute, 150-question, 400-mark paper in English only. Mathematics carries 50 questions for 200 marks, and English, Intelligence, General Science and Social Science carry 25 questions for 50 marks each. There is no negative marking, and the qualifying bar is 25 percent in each section and 40 percent in aggregate.

**Who is eligible for AISSEE Class 9 in 2027?**

Children aged 13 to 15 on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014 — who have passed Class 8 from a recognised school at the time of admission. Both boys and girls can apply, though girls' Class 9 seats are few and listed school-wise in the bulletin.

**When does the AISSEE 2027 form close, and what does it cost?**

Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM. The fee is Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards and Rs 700 for SC/ST. A late window runs from 3 to 6 December with an extra Rs 500, and a correction window from 7 to 9 December.
