## Half your paper is one subject — treat it that way

Here is the arithmetic that should shape the rest of October. In the AISSEE Class 6 paper, Mathematics is **50 questions of 3 marks each — 150 of 300 total marks**. Language, Intelligence and GK are 50 marks each. Put plainly: one good hour of Maths study moves your final score roughly as much as an hour each on all three other sections combined.

The qualifying rule makes it even more important: **25% in every section** plus **40% aggregate**. A child who is strong in Maths has a safety cushion; a child who neglects it and scrapes 20% in the section fails the whole paper no matter how brilliant the GK answers are.

With the exam 108 days out — **16 days left in October** — here is a realistic sprint plan for this subject.

## The 16-day Maths sprint (16–31 October)

**Days 1–8: close the chapter gaps.** Work through the Class 6 syllabus unit by unit — number system, fractions and decimals, ratio and proportion, mensuration, basic geometry — and be honest about which ones are genuinely shaky. One unit per day, NCERT Class 5 and 6 exercises first, then 20 AISSEE-style questions on that unit. If a topic takes two days, it takes two days; do not move on while the basics are wobbling.

**Days 9–13: mixed speed practice.** Stop doing chapter-wise sets. Sit with a timer and do 50 mixed Maths questions in 55 minutes — slightly faster than the real pace of 150 minutes for the full 125-question paper. Mark every question you skip or guess; those are your revision list.

**Days 14–16: error-book review.** Re-attempt only the questions you got wrong in the mixed sets. Most Class 6 maths errors are not concept errors — they are unit-conversion slips, misread questions and arithmetic hurry. Fixing those three habits is worth more than learning one new chapter.

## The three habits that decide the section

1. **Write the units down.** Converting km to m or hours to minutes is where the silly marks leak. A 30-second unit check per word problem pays for itself.
2. **Attempt everything.** With no negative marking, a blank and a wrong answer both score zero — an attempted guess at least has a 25% chance. Leave nothing blank in the Maths section.
3. **Protect the section cutoff.** When the 150 minutes get tight, many children sacrifice Maths questions to finish GK. Do the opposite: Maths is 150 marks and the section has a 25% minimum. Finish Maths first.

## What parents can do this week

Set a daily 60–75 minute Maths block that does not move — before dinner works for most families. Do not buy three new books this week; one Class 5–6 NCERT set plus one past-paper collection is enough at this stage. And keep the pressure on the process, not the score: "Did you do the timed set?" beats "Why only 38 out of 50?"

The NTA notification will land any week now, and when it does, the registration window (EXPECTED: October–November, fee EXPECTED ₹850/₹700 on 2026 rates) will demand your attention. The families who have Maths locked down by then will use that energy on the form, the photograph specs and the school choices — the ones still wrestling with fractions will have to split their focus. Use October.

## FAQs

**When is the AISSEE 2027 exam?**
31 January 2027, per the NTA exam calendar released 16 September 2026 — the date is marked tentative. The application notification has still not been released as of 15 October 2026.

**How many marks does Maths carry in AISSEE Class 6?**
150 of 300 marks — 50 questions of 3 marks each. That is half of the entire Class 6 paper, the highest-weight section by a wide margin.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**
No — wrong answers and blanks both score zero; nothing is deducted. In the Class 6 pattern you should attempt all 125 questions.

**What are the qualifying marks for AISSEE 2027?**
25% in each section and 40% aggregate (not applicable to SC/ST candidates), per the 2026 information bulletin — the 2027 bulletin is expected to repeat this.
