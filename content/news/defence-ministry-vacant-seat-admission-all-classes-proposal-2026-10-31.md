## What is being proposed

The Defence Ministry is considering an important change to how Sainik Schools admit students: instead of opening admission only in Class 6 and Class 9, schools could admit new students to vacant seats in every class, every year. The idea, reported on 30 October 2026, is simple — fill the seats that fall empty when a student leaves, rather than letting them sit vacant for years.

This matters because the current system has a hard gap. A child who joins in Class 6 and leaves in Class 7 leaves behind a seat that, under today's rules, nobody can take. Year after year, those empty seats add up, even while large numbers of families compete for a limited number of Class 6 and 9 places.

## Why seats stay empty

According to an official Defence Ministry document cited in the reports, about 7 percent of students enrolled in Sainik Schools leave their studies midway. The reasons given include the difficulty of hostel life away from family at a young age, strict rules and a disciplined routine, trouble adjusting to a new environment, and in some cases medical reasons.

The parliamentary Standing Committee on Defence has independently flagged the same problem. Its report on Sainik Schools, Rashtriya Military Schools and RIMC records an average dropout of 6.93 percent in Sainik Schools, and the committee has pressed the ministry to deal with seats that fall vacant when students drop out. The current proposal follows directly from that recommendation.

## What has NOT changed

This is the part parents need to be careful about. No new rule has been announced, no admission window for Class 7, 8, 10 or 11 has opened, and no entrance test for vacant seats has been notified. Until the Defence Ministry formally approves and notifies a change, the existing system stands: admission comes through AISSEE, for Class 6 and Class 9 only.

Any coaching centre, agent or social media post claiming that "admission is now open in all classes" is getting ahead of the facts. Do not pay any fee or fill any form on the basis of this proposal alone.

## What to do next

If your child missed Class 6 admission, or is currently in Class 7 or 8 and hoping for a Sainik School seat, the honest position today is: wait for an official notification, and keep preparing in the meantime.

- **For Class 6 and 9 aspirants:** nothing changes. Prepare for AISSEE as before; the 2027 notification is still awaited from NTA, so keep documents ready for a short application window.
- **For students in other classes:** build the skills any future entrance process would measure — maths fluency, English reading, general knowledge and physical fitness.
- **For parents:** check only official sources — the Sainik Schools Society and NTA portals — before acting on any admission claim. This site will update this story the moment a decision is notified.

## Why this proposal matters even before it is decided

Even as a proposal, this is a meaningful shift in thinking. For decades the Sainik School model has treated Class 6 as the single front door, with Class 9 as a smaller second one. Opening every class to fresh admission would turn vacant seats from wasted capacity into second chances — for students who discovered the armed-forces dream late, for families who moved cities, and for children who were not ready at age ten but are ready at thirteen.

It would also raise questions the ministry must answer before notifying anything: how would a Class 8 entrant be tested, and would vacant-seat admission follow the same reservation and quota rules as AISSEE? Those details do not exist yet — which is why parents should follow the official notification rather than the rumour mill.

## FAQs

**Can I apply for Sainik School admission in Class 7 or 8 right now?**

No. The proposal to admit students to vacant seats in classes other than 6 and 9 is only under consideration. Until the Defence Ministry approves and notifies new rules, admission remains open only in Class 6 and Class 9 through AISSEE.

**Why are Sainik School seats lying vacant at all?**

About 7 percent of enrolled students leave midway, according to a Defence Ministry document cited in news reports, and the parliamentary Standing Committee on Defence has recorded a 6.93 percent average dropout in Sainik Schools. Because fresh admission happens only in Class 6 and 9, a seat vacated in Class 7 or 8 cannot be filled today.

**Who suggested this change?**

The parliamentary committee attached to the Defence Ministry expressed concern about seats falling vacant when students drop out and recommended effective arrangements to fill them. Following that recommendation, the ministry is considering admission to vacant seats across all classes.

**When will a decision be announced?**

There is no announced date. The proposal is still under consideration, and any change would need formal approval and notification by the Defence Ministry before it takes effect. Watch the Sainik Schools Society and NTA portals for official word.
