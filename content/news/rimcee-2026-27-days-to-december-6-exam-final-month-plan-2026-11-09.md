## 24 days, one Sunday, three papers

The Rashtriya Indian Military College (RIMC) entrance exam — RIMCEE 2026 — is on **Sunday, 6 December 2026**. That is 24 days from today, and it is the first of December's three defence-school Sundays: RMS CET follows on 13 December, and the UP Sainik School exam is tentatively expected on 20 December.

RIMC is a different kind of target from the Sainik Schools most of our readers are preparing for. There is only one RIMC, in Dehradun, and entry is into **Class 8 only**, with the exam held twice a year, in June and December. Applications for this term closed on 30 September 2026, and the correction window closed on 11 October. If your child applied, the form is long locked — what remains is preparation, and the admit card.

## The paper pattern — and the trap in it

Here is the written exam, in full:

| Paper | Type | Marks | Duration | Minimum to qualify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | Descriptive | 125 | 2 hours | 50% (62.5 marks) |
| Mathematics | Descriptive | 200 | 1 hour 30 minutes | 50% (100 marks) |
| General Knowledge | Objective (MCQ) | 75 | 1 hour | 50% (37.5 marks) |
| **Total written** | | **400** | **4 hours 30 minutes** | |

Two things about this pattern decide results. First, it is **descriptive** where it counts: English and Maths are written answers, not ticked boxes, so working, presentation and handwriting all carry marks. Second — and this is the trap — the 50% rule applies **to every paper separately**. A child who scores 190 out of 200 in Maths and 120 in English but only 36 in GK is out. There is no compensation across papers, no aggregate rescue. Balanced preparation is not advice here; it is the qualifying rule.

After the written exam, qualifiers face a **50-mark viva voce** (interview), and then a medical fitness test. The final merit combines the written 400 with the viva 50. And there is **no negative marking** in the written exam — on the GK paper, an attempted question can only help.

## A realistic 24-day plan

Honestly, 24 days is not the time to start a new topic. It is the time to convert what your child already knows into marks. A plan that works:

**Week 1 (9–15 November): close the gaps.** One full paper per subject, timed, to find the weakest of the three. Most children discover it is either English writing or GK — Maths usually takes care of itself by now. Fix the weakest paper first, with daily work, not weekend bursts.

**Week 2 (16–22 November): full papers under exam conditions.** One complete 4-hour-30-minute sitting, in one stretch, with the real paper order and timings. This is the single most valuable exercise in the month — stamina across four and a half hours of descriptive writing is a skill, and it cannot be built in pieces.

**Week 3 (23–29 November): GK sprint and English polish.** GK is the paper that fails the most candidates, and it is the most fixable in a week: current affairs from the last six months, plus history, geography, general science and defence basics. English needs essay and letter practice, comprehension, and grammar drills — written out by hand, not read silently.

**Final week (30 November–5 December): taper.** Light revision, one short mock, and rest. Re-check the exam centre and reporting time, pack the admit card, photo ID and stationery, and stop studying the night before. A tired child writes worse English and makes dumber Maths errors than a rested one.

## The admit card: watch, don't wait

No admit-card date has been announced. The pattern from earlier in this cycle puts the city intimation slip roughly two weeks before the exam and the admit card in the final week — but that is an expected pattern, not a promise. Check the official portal, **exams.nta.nic.in/RIMCEE**, once a week now and twice a week from the last week of November. When the admit card appears, download it the same day and check the photograph, the spelling of the name, and the centre address. An error spotted on day one can be fixed; an error spotted on exam eve cannot.

One more practical note for families with two December exams: RMS CET is exactly one week after RIMCEE, on 13 December. The week between them should be light revision, one mock, and rest — not new chapters. And AISSEE preparation continues alongside, not instead of, December preparation: AISSEE 2027 applications close on **2 December 2026**, with the exam on **31 January 2027**.

## FAQs

**When is the RIMCEE 2026 exam?**

Sunday, 6 December 2026. It is the written entrance examination for admission to Class 8 at the Rashtriya Indian Military College, Dehradun, for the July 2027 term.

**What is the RIMCEE 2026 exam pattern?**

Three papers totalling 400 marks: English (125 marks, descriptive, 2 hours), Mathematics (200 marks, descriptive, 1 hour 30 minutes) and General Knowledge (75 marks, MCQ, 1 hour). The total duration is 4 hours 30 minutes, and there is no negative marking.

**What marks are needed to qualify in RIMCEE 2026?**

At least 50% in each paper — English, Mathematics and General Knowledge. A candidate who scores below 50% in even one paper does not move to the viva voce, however high the other scores are.

**What happens after the RIMCEE written exam?**

Candidates who clear the written papers are called for a 50-mark viva voce (interview), followed by a medical fitness test. Final selection is on the combined merit of the written exam and the viva.

**When will the RIMCEE 2026 admit card be released?**

No admit-card date has been announced yet. The pattern from earlier in this cycle puts the city slip roughly two weeks before the exam and the admit card in the final week — treat that as an expected pattern, not an announced date, and check the official portal exams.nta.nic.in/RIMCEE regularly.
