## Free coaching, in a place where coaching barely exists

Here is the thing most AISSEE guides never say out loud: for a large number of families, the real barrier is not the exam. It is the price of preparing for it. Private coaching in a city can cost thousands of rupees a month, and in a remote district like Doda in Jammu and Kashmir, even finding a good teacher is hard — let alone paying for one.

That is exactly the gap the Indian Army's new programme is trying to close. On Monday, 2 November 2026, the Army launched an eight-week free coaching programme for the Sainik School entrance exam in Doda district. It was inaugurated by Commander 9 Sector Rashtriya Rifles, Brigadier Vikrant Shinde, in the presence of students and their parents, at the battalion headquarters of the Assam Rifles in Thaleela village.

Sixty meritorious students from different schools across Doda district have been selected. They were not picked by lottery or by recommendation — they qualified through an examination held at their own schools. The specialised coaching will be delivered by teachers specially called from West Bengal, and Brigadier Shinde also distributed free study material to every selected student.

## Why this matters beyond Doda

The Commander's own words explain the thinking better than any commentary could. "I strongly believe that the difference between us as human beings is not in our abilities, but in the opportunities we receive," Brigadier Shinde told IANS. "The much-needed coaching programme is aimed at bridging educational gaps and offering equal opportunities for students in far-flung and remote regions to crack the All India Sainik School Entrance Exam."

Look, that sentence is worth sitting with. A child in Doda and a child in Delhi sit the same AISSEE paper. The Delhi child may have a coaching centre down the road; the Doda child may not. Programmes like this one do not change the paper, but they do change the starting line — and for a parent watching from the sidelines, that is the whole point.

There is a second, quieter lesson here. The students themselves told IANS that every child dreams of studying at a Sainik School, and thanked the Army for the platform. When a programme like this runs in your district, it is not just about marks. It tells children from far-flung areas that the exam is genuinely meant for them too.

## What this means with 90 days to the exam

The timing is no accident. With AISSEE 2027 tentatively set for 31 January 2027, the eight-week programme runs right through the final stretch — the exact window when serious preparation matters most. Eight weeks of guided study, with teachers and material provided free, is a meaningful head start for these 60 students.

For parents reading this from anywhere else in India, the honest position is this: this programme is specific to Doda district, and you cannot enrol a child from another state into it. But its existence is still useful news for you, for three reasons:

- **It proves free, quality help exists.** Army and paramilitary units, district administrations and schools do run such initiatives from time to time. They are rarely advertised nationally, so they are easy to miss.
- **It shows what to ask for locally.** If your district has an Army unit, a Sainik School alumni association or a district education office, ask whether any free preparatory support exists for AISSEE. The worst answer is a polite no.
- **It sets the standard for what "free" should mean.** Free coaching plus free study material, with selection on merit through a school-level test — that is the model worth looking for, rather than any paid course that promises selection.

## What to do next

If your child is preparing for AISSEE 2027, the programme in Doda does not change your plan — but the calendar should sharpen it. With roughly 80 days left to the 31 January exam date, this is the phase for full-length mock papers under timed conditions, not for starting new chapters. Our [80-day countdown plan](/news/aissee-2027-83-days-to-exam-countdown-plan-2026-11-09/) lays out a week-by-week approach, and if coaching fees are a worry at your end, our guide to [preparing without coaching](/blog/aissee-rural-students-no-coaching-no-problem/) covers how rural families manage with school teachers and self-study alone.

And one practical habit, borrowed from Doda: ask. Ask your child's school whether any free coaching, mock test or study-material support is running locally. These programmes often fill through school notice boards, not newspapers — the parents who ask early are the ones who hear about them first.

## FAQs

**What is the Indian Army's free AISSEE coaching programme in Doda?**

It is an eight-week free coaching programme launched by the Indian Army in Doda district of Jammu and Kashmir to help underprivileged students prepare for the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination. It was inaugurated on 2 November 2026 at the Assam Rifles battalion headquarters in Thaleela village.

**Who can join the free coaching programme in Doda?**

The 60 students in this programme were selected and shortlisted after qualifying an examination at their respective schools in Doda district. It is a district-level initiative, not an all-India admission, so students outside Doda cannot simply walk in.

**Is the Doda coaching programme really free?**

Yes, according to the IANS report of 2 November 2026, the programme is free, and the Army also distributed free study material to all 60 selected students. No fee is mentioned anywhere in the report.

**When is the AISSEE 2027 exam?**

31 January 2027, as per the NTA exam calendar for 2026-27. That date is tentative until the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin is released, so treat it as confirmed-tentative, not final.
