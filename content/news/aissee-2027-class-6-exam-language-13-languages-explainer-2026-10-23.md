The AISSEE 2027 application form will ask Class 6 parents one question that quietly decides how the entire paper reads on exam day: *which language should the question paper be in?* And unlike the exam city or a spelling mistake, this answer gets locked the moment you submit.

Most parents pick on autopilot — English, because "everything important is in English." That can be the right call. But it can also be the wrong one, and the official rules contain details most families never read until it's too late. Here's what the NTA bulletin actually says.

## The 13 languages, and who gets them

For admission to **Class 6**, the paper is offered in 13 languages:

| # | Language |
|---|---|
| 1–3 | English, Hindi, Assamese |
| 4–6 | Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada |
| 7–9 | Malayalam, Marathi, Odiya |
| 10–13 | Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu |

For **Class 9**, there is exactly one option: **English**. No choice, no regional papers.

Three rules from the bulletin matter more than the list itself:

1. **You can pick any of the 13 irrespective of the school you apply to.** A child from Tamil Nadu applying to a school in Rajasthan can still write in Tamil. The medium is not tied to the school's state.
2. **The choice is locked at application.** "Option of medium of Question Paper should be carefully chosen while filling in the Application Form. The option once exercised cannot be changed." There is no correction-window fix for this.
3. **You always get an English safety net.** Choose English or Hindi and you receive a bilingual English–Hindi booklet. Choose a regional language and you receive your opted language **plus English**. So the regional-language child effectively gets two versions of every question (except the Language section) — a genuine advantage if they can read English reasonably well.

## Why this decision deserves 10 minutes of thought

Two things make the medium choice load-bearing rather than cosmetic.

First, the **Language section is 50 of 300 marks** — 25 questions at 2 marks each, testing grammar, comprehension and vocabulary *in the medium you chose*. A child who picked Hindi as an afterthought but actually reads better in English is now fighting 50 marks in their weaker language.

Second, in any translation dispute, the **English version of a question is treated as final** (except Language-section questions), and the NTA's decision is final. That matters for regional-language papers where a mistranslated maths term can cost a question.

## A 4-question decision framework

Ask these, in order, before the form opens:

1. **Which language does my child read fastest under time pressure?** AISSEE Class 6 is 150 minutes for 125 questions. Reading speed in the chosen medium is the single biggest factor.
2. **How strong is the Language-section performance in each candidate language?** Pick the medium where grammar and comprehension scores are highest — that's 50 marks.
3. **Can the child cross-check in English?** If yes, a regional language is nearly risk-free, since the booklet includes English anyway.
4. **What is the school medium?** It doesn't decide anything (eligibility is the same whatever the medium), but aligning exam language with the child's school medium removes one more variable on exam day.

The lazy default — English for everyone — is fine for English-medium schoolchildren. For a Hindi-medium or Tamil-medium child, the regional paper plus the English backup is very often the smarter play.

## FAQs

**In which languages can a child write AISSEE Class 6?**
Thirteen: English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odiya, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu and Urdu. This list is from the AISSEE 2026 information bulletin and is expected to repeat for 2027, since the 2027 bulletin is not out yet.

**In which language is the AISSEE Class 9 paper held?**
English only. Unlike Class 6, which offers 13 languages, the Class 9 paper has a single medium: English.

**Can I change my exam language after submitting the AISSEE form?**
No. The medium of the question paper is chosen while filling the application form and the option, once exercised, cannot be changed. Choose carefully on form day.

**If I choose a regional language, do I get the paper only in that language?**
No. Candidates opting for a regional language receive a booklet in their opted language plus English. English or Hindi opters receive a bilingual English–Hindi booklet.
