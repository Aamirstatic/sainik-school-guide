Yesterday we published the headline numbers: 3,160 Class 6 seats in the 33 Sainik Schools for AISSEE 2027, split into 2,809 for boys and 351 for girls. Useful — but a parent in Lucknow doesn't compete for 2,809 seats. She competes for the seats her child is actually eligible for, in her home state, in her category. So here is the same matrix, broken down state by state, because that is the number that decides your child's real chances.

Two things jump out. First, Uttar Pradesh tops the list not because any one school is huge — Amethi has 80 boys' seats, Mainpuri 80, Jhansi just 40 — but because it has three schools. Second, the single biggest school in the country is Sainik School Tilaiya in Jharkhand, with 126 boys' seats, followed by Amaravathinagar in Tamil Nadu with 110.

## The number that matters more: home-state seats

Here is the part parents miss. Of those 2,809 boys' seats, **1,935 are reserved for home-state candidates and 874 for other states** — the 67/33 split. Your child competes first in the home-state list of the school in your state. A school with 108 seats in a small state can be a better bet than a school with 140 seats in a state where far more children apply.

The category split inside the boys' seats is: General 1,171, OBC-NCL 741, SC 390, Defence wards 349 and ST 158. And if your state has no Sainik School at all — Delhi is the big example — your child competes for other-state seats, with one guardrail: no single state can take more than 25% of a school's other-state seats.

## The new Sainik Schools change the picture completely

The 78 approved new Sainik Schools list **10,811 seats boys can apply for** — 4,856 marked for boys and 5,955 marked gender-neutral. That is nearly four times the 33 old schools combined. But the state distribution is very uneven:

| State | New schools | Seats boys can apply for |
|-------|-------------|--------------------------|
| Gujarat | 8 | **1,670** |
| Rajasthan | 5 | **1,429** |
| Maharashtra | 10 | **1,130** |
| Madhya Pradesh | 7 | **1,100** |
| Haryana | 6 | **1,060** |
| Tamil Nadu | 6 | **710** |
| Chhattisgarh | 5 | **693** |
| Uttar Pradesh | 4 | **570** |
| Andhra Pradesh | 4 | **495** |
| Bihar | 4 | **430** |

Gujarat alone has more new-school seats than the entire 33-school network has for boys. But read the fine print before you celebrate: in new schools, at least 40% of seats go by All India merit and up to 60% can go to students already studying in that school. So a new school showing 120 seats may offer only about 48 to an outsider. And the bulletin does not define "gender neutral" — confirm with the school before you plan around those seats. Our [school-choice guide](/blog/aissee-2027-which-sainik-school-to-aim-for-choice-guide/) walks through the 40/60 route in detail.

## What to do with this table tonight

1. **Find your state's row** and note the home-state seats — that is your child's main list.
2. **Check the 25% other-state cap** if you are eyeing a school outside your state; your child competes with every other state for those seats.
3. **Do not ignore the new schools** in your state, but check each school's fees and domicile rules first — they are set by the school, not by NTA.
4. **Fill the form before 2 December 2026 (11:50 PM).** The exam is on 31 January 2027, and the [home-state quota rules](/blog/home-state-quota-sainik-schools-67-percent-explained-2026-10-17/) decide which list your child lands in.

Last week, a parent from Bihar told us he had been aiming only at Gopalganj because it was the school he knew. His son's home-state list there is 67 seats. Nalanda, the state's other school, adds 52 more home-state seats — and the new schools in Bihar add 430 seats on top. He had been planning around one school when his child had three lists to play with. The matrix is not just numbers. It is the map of where your child can actually get in.
