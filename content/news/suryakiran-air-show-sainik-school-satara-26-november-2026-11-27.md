## Nine jets, thirty minutes, one school ground full of children

Yesterday morning, the sky above Satara did something it rarely does. At **11:15 am**, the Indian Air Force's Suryakiran Aerobatic Team — nine jets flying in tight, synchronised formations — opened its display over the grounds of **Sainik School Satara**, with the Sahyadri ranges behind it.

The school, working with the district administration, had opened its grounds to the public. Thousands of students from schools around Satara were invited, with seating arranged for them and for the general public and dignitaries. The show ran for about half an hour. In the evening, an IAF band performance and drill display at Shahu Stadium gave residents a second taste of the day.

This was not a one-off flypast. It was the first of three consecutive displays: the team flies over **Air Force Station Lohegaon in Pune today, 27 November**, and over the **National Defence Academy at Khadakwasla tomorrow, 28 November** — a display timed to coincide with the passing-out parade of the NDA's 151st course.

## Two new formations — and a team that is half NDA alumni

The team arrived in Pune with something new. A Suryakiran spokesperson told The Indian Express that the team had introduced **two new formations and manoeuvres** for this season, alongside its signature moves, and that every season brings a chance to revisit the display sequence and improve synchronisation.

Here is the detail that matters most to a Sainik School parent: **50 percent of the current Suryakiran team members are NDA alumni**. One of them told the paper that performing over the NDA — "where many of us began our journey as officers, where our values were shaped" — is an exceptionally special and emotional experience.

That is the whole point of the Sainik School system, in one sentence. The school your child is applying to exists to feed the NDA. The pilots pulling nine-aircraft formations over a school ground yesterday walked the same path — Sainik School, NDA, commission — that an AISSEE aspirant starts on 31 January 2027.

## Why the IAF flies these shows over schools

A senior officer of the team told The Times of India that the display had a larger mission than spectacle: to plant the dream of flying in young minds and inspire students to join the armed forces. "To instil this dream, our team has been performing across the country," he said.

Satara was a deliberate choice. The district has a long association with the armed forces and has produced generations of military personnel and officers. Lt Col R. R. Jadhav (retd), former director of the Maharashtra Sainik Welfare Department, called it a big honour for the district. Lt Col Satish Hange, former Sainik Welfare Officer of Satara district, said the performance would give youngsters a chance to witness the IAF's capabilities at close quarters — and that "this show is one of its kind and holds significance for the town."

For most of the children watching, it was half an hour of noise and colour. For some of them, as The Times of India put it, "it could spark a lifelong ambition to fly." That is exactly what a parent should take from it — not the aerobatics, but the fact that the dream is planted young, and that the first step toward it is a form that closes in five days.

## What to do with this week

If your child watched the display — or watched the videos of it doing the rounds since yesterday — use it. The AISSEE 2027 form closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 pm**, and the exam is on **31 January 2027**. The late-fee window (3–6 December, ₹500 extra) exists, but it is a safety net, not a plan.
