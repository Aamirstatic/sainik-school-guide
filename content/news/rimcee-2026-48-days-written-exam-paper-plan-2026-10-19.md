## The milestone

The RIMCEE 2026 written examination — the gateway to Class 8 at the Rashtriya Indian Military College, Dehradun — is **48 days away**, on Sunday, 6 December 2026. Applications closed weeks ago, so this is no longer a "should we apply" question. It is a preparation question, and with seven weeks left, the honest answer is: the paper pattern itself should be deciding what gets studied this week.

## The three papers, exactly as the bulletin lays them out

| Subject | Type | Questions | Marks | Duration | Minimum to qualify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | Descriptive, pen and paper | 45 | 125 | 2 hours | 50% |
| General Knowledge | Objective (MCQ) | 75 | 75 | 1 hour | 50% |
| Mathematics | Descriptive, pen and paper | 30 | 200 | 1 hour 30 mins | 50% |
| **Total** | | **150** | **400** | **4 hrs 30 mins** | |

Two things in this table surprise most families. First, **Mathematics carries half the total marks** (200 of 400) in just 30 descriptive questions — roughly 6–7 marks per question. Second, the qualifying bar is **50% in each subject separately**, not on the total. A candidate can score 300/400 overall and still be out if English lands at 45%. That per-subject rule, more than any cutoff, is what ends campaigns. (CONFIRMED)

The paper is bilingual (English and Hindi) except the English section itself, there is **no negative marking**, and if a question's Hindi translation is ambiguous, the English version is treated as final per NTA. Syllabus-wise, Maths draws on Class 6–7 topics (LCM/HCF, fractions, ratio and proportion, percentage, profit and loss, simple interest, area and perimeter, time and work), English covers unseen passages, composition, picture writing, letters, grammar and idioms, and GK spans current affairs, sciences, social sciences and logical questions. (CONFIRMED from the bulletin as described by two portals)

## What to finish this week (19–25 October)

With 48 days left, this is the week to lock the mechanics, not learn new chapters:

1. **Maths working speed.** Thirty descriptive questions in 90 minutes is three minutes per question — with full written working shown. Do two timed 30-question sets this week and measure the actual minutes-per-question, not the score.
2. **English writing volume.** Two papers are pen-and-paper, not MCQ ticks — handwriting speed, clear sentences and neat working matter as much as knowing the topic. One unseen passage + one composition, written out fully, every day.
3. **The 50% floor per subject.** Identify the weakest subject honestly and give it the extra hour — the exam is three separate 50% gates, not one 400-mark race.
4. **GK as a daily habit, not a cram.** Seventy-five MCQs in one hour rewards breadth; 20 minutes of current affairs plus science/social-science revision daily beats a weekend binge.
5. **No negative marking = attempt everything.** Make this a reflex now: on the GK paper especially, an unanswered question is a wasted mark.

After the written, qualifiers face a **50-mark viva voce** (also 50% to pass, conducted through the State Governments) and then a medical examination — we covered the viva stage in detail [last week](/news/rimcee-2026-viva-voce-after-written-2026-10-18/).

## FAQs

**When is the RIMCEE 2026 written exam?**

6 December 2026, a Sunday — 48 days from 19 October 2026. The written test has three papers totalling 400 marks over 4 hours 30 minutes.

**What is the qualifying mark in the RIMCEE written exam?**

At least 50% in each subject separately — English, General Knowledge and Mathematics. A candidate can score 300 out of 400 overall and still fail to qualify if any one subject is below 50%.

**Is there negative marking in RIMCEE 2026?**

No. The official information bulletin states there is no negative marking, and the English version of a question prevails if there is any ambiguity in the Hindi translation.

**What happens after the RIMCEE written exam?**

Candidates who qualify the written test face a 50-mark viva voce conducted through the State Governments (also 50% to pass), then a medical examination. Only medically fit candidates reach the final merit list.
