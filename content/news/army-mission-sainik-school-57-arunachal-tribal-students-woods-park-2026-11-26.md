## What happened

On Wednesday, Arunachal Pradesh Chief Minister Pema Khandu publicly thanked the Indian Army for something that rarely makes headlines: **57 tribal students from the state's remote areas have secured admission to Woods Park Sainik School** through the Army's 'Yuva Sashaktikaran: Mission Sainik School' initiative.

"The Indian Army is not just guarding our borders; it is also shaping the future of the communities that inhabit them," Khandu said in a social media post, praising the programme. The 57 students come from the **first two batches** of the initiative — children from far-flung tribal areas where, as the Chief Minister put it, difficult terrain and remoteness often cut families off from specialised coaching and exam preparation altogether.

The mentoring is done by the **Sela Warriors** — the local name for the Gajraj Corps' Mission Sainik School batches, named after the Sela Pass in Tawang district. The Corps identifies talented students in border districts, coaches them for the AISSEE, and walks them through the admission stages after the exam.

## Why this number matters

Fifty-seven is not a rounding error. It is the second hard number this programme has put on record in a month. On 28 October 2026, the Chief Minister's Office commended the same initiative and said **59 students had already secured Sainik School admissions through it, with 75 aspirants preparing for AISSEE 2027**.

A word of caution, because the two numbers look confusing side by side: they are different counts from different dates. The 57 is the Woods Park figure for the first two batches; the 59 was the programme-wide total the CMO quoted in October. Don't add them together — and don't treat either as a final total, because the 75 aspirants preparing for AISSEE 2027 are still in the pipeline.

The pipeline has been building for a while. In August 2025, PTI reported that the Army's Blazing Sword Division, also under Gajraj Corps, had mentored **32 students from Dirang and Jang** in West Kameng and Tawang districts into Woods Park Sainik School. A year earlier, the Spear Corps picked **33 students from remote hamlets** — including Sarli in Kurung Kumey district — gave them 88 theory classes and 18 rounds of testing and counselling, and The Hindu reported in September 2025 that **32 of the 33 qualified at the national level**, with Milli Yabi of Sarli becoming the first to secure final admission — to Sainik School East Siang, the school this website is named after.

## What it means for parents in remote districts

Here is the honest takeaway. AISSEE is a national exam, and a child in a border village competes against children with coaching centres on their street. Mission Sainik School exists precisely because that gap is real — and the numbers now show it is closing, at least for the children the Army reaches.

If your child is in a remote or border district of Arunachal Pradesh, **ask whether a batch runs near you**. The practical route is your child's school, your district education office, or your nearest Army unit. The programme is free at the point of use for the families it serves — the Army runs it as outreach, not as a paid course — but places in a batch are limited, and the earlier you ask, the better your child's chances of getting in.

And the wider picture is worth noting too. Arunachal Pradesh has established its first Sainik School at Pasighat — Sainik School East Siang — and a second is being developed at Tawang under the public-private partnership model. The state government is also working with the Army on structured coaching to prepare Arunachali youth for the NDA and CDS examinations after school.

## What to do next

The 75 aspirants already preparing for AISSEE 2027 are a reminder of the calendar. The AISSEE 2027 form closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026, at 11:50 PM**. The late window (3–6 December, with a ₹500 late fee) and the correction window (7–9 December) follow, and the exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. If your child is in a Mission Sainik School batch, the form is part of the preparation the mentors handle — but check the confirmation page yourself. Nobody else can do that for you.

## FAQs

**How many Arunachal students got into Sainik School through Mission Sainik School?**

Fifty-seven tribal students from the first two batches of the programme have secured admission to Woods Park Sainik School, Chief Minister Pema Khandu said on 25 November 2026. The Chief Minister's Office had separately said in October that 59 students had secured Sainik School admissions through the programme overall — a different count from a different date, so the two figures should not be added together.

**What is Mission Sainik School in Arunachal Pradesh?**

It is the Indian Army's preparatory coaching programme for tribal youth from Arunachal Pradesh's border districts, run by the Gajraj Corps under the local name Sela Warriors. The Army identifies, mentors and prepares students for the AISSEE and supports them through the admission stages.

**Where is Woods Park Sainik School?**

Woods Park Sainik School is in Hamirpur, Himachal Pradesh. It is one of the newer Sainik Schools under the public-private partnership model, and the Army's Arunachal batches have been sending students there since 2025.

**Can my child join a Mission Sainik School batch?**

The batches run in Arunachal Pradesh's remote and border districts through the Army's Gajraj Corps. Ask your child's school, your district education office or your nearest Army unit whether a batch runs near you — and keep the AISSEE 2027 form in mind, because it closes on 2 December 2026.
