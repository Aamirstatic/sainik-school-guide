## If your daughter is applying, this is the number that matters

Seven days remain before the AISSEE 2027 form closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026, at 11:50 PM**. If you are filling it for your daughter, here is the fact that should shape every choice you make on that form: there are **351 Class 6 seats for girls** across the 33 Sainik Schools — and your daughter will compete for them **only against other girls**.

NTA prepares the merit list separately for boys and girls, school-wise and category-wise. That one rule changes the maths completely. Your daughter is not chasing a seat in a pool of 3,160 children. She is chasing one of 351 seats in a girls-only list, at a school where the girls' intake is usually 10 to 14 seats.

## Where the 351 seats actually are

Every one of the 33 schools has Class 6 seats for girls. The rule is simple: **10% of the Class 6 strength, or 10 seats, whichever is higher**. Most schools sit at exactly 10 — five for home-state girls, five for girls from other states. A few go higher — our full [seat matrix breakdown](/news/aissee-2027-seat-matrix-3160-class-6-seats-2026-11-14/) has the numbers for boys and girls together:

| School | Class 6 girls' seats |
|---|---|
| **Tilaiya (Jharkhand)** | **14** (8 home + 6 other) |
| Amaravathinagar, Balachadi, Chhingchhip, Goalpara, Kapurthala, Kunjpura, Punglwa, Purulia | **12 each** |
| Gopalganj | **11** |
| All other 33 schools, including Chittorgarh, Ghorakhal and Bhubaneswar | **10 each** |

The 67% home-state quota applies to girls exactly as it does to boys — so a Rajasthan-domiciled girl applying to [Sainik School Chittorgarh](/blog/sainik-school-chittorgarh-complete-parents-guide-2027-2026-11-25/) competes for 5 of its 10 girls' seats, while a girl from another state competes for the other 5.

## Class 9 is a different story — check before you assume

This is where parents get caught out. In Class 9, girls' admission is **subject to availability of vacancies**, and this year only **21 of the 33 schools have any Class 9 seats for girls at all — 69 seats in total**. Twelve schools show zero:

Amaravathinagar, Bhubaneswar, Bijapur, **Chittorgarh**, Gopalganj, Imphal, Jhunjhunu, Kalikiri, Kodagu, Nagrota, Purulia and Sujanpur Tira.

If your daughter is in Class 8 and you are counting on a Class 9 seat at one of those twelve, the bulletin says plainly: there is none this year. The schools with the most Class 9 girls' seats are Chhingchhip (10), followed by Goalpara, Mainpuri, Ambikapur and Nalanda (5 each). Class 6, with ten times the seats, is the real door — for girls exactly as for boys. The [girls' quota explainer](/blog/girls-quota-sainik-schools-explained-2026-10-22/) walks through how unfilled girls' seats move in later counselling rounds.

## The rules that are the same — and the one that is not

The age rules do not change for girls: **10 to 12 years on 31 March 2027 for Class 6** (born 1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017, studying in Class 5), and **13 to 15 for Class 9** (born 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014, studying in Class 8). The exam is the same paper, on the same day — **Sunday, 31 January 2027** — with the same qualifying bar of 25% in each section and 40% aggregate. The fee is the same: Rs 850 for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, Rs 700 for SC and ST.

The one thing that is different is the competition pool, and it cuts in your daughter's favour. A separate merit list means her rank is measured against girls alone. It does not make the exam easy — coaching portals report Chittorgarh's 2025 girls' cutoffs running higher than the boys' (general girls around 284–287 out of 300 at home-state level, against 275–277 for boys), because fewer seats concentrate the competition — but it does mean the seat she is chasing is a real, counted seat, not a hope.

Seven days is enough time to fill the form carefully. It is not enough time to fill it twice. ## FAQs

**How many seats are there for girls in AISSEE 2027?**

351 Class 6 seats and 69 Class 9 seats for girls across the 33 Sainik Schools. Every school has Class 6 seats for girls; only 21 schools have Class 9 seats for girls.

**Does my daughter compete with boys for a Sainik School seat?**

No. NTA prepares a separate merit list for girls, school-wise and category-wise. Your daughter competes only with other girls for the girls' seats at each school.

**Which Sainik School has the most seats for girls in Class 6?**

Sainik School Tilaiya in Jharkhand, with 14 Class 6 girls' seats — 8 for home-state girls and 6 for girls from other states. Eight schools have 12 each, and most others have 10.

**Can girls apply for Class 9 in every Sainik School?**

No. Only 21 of the 33 schools have Class 9 seats for girls in 2027-28. Twelve schools — including Chittorgarh, Bhubaneswar, Jhunjhunu and Nagrota — have none this year.
