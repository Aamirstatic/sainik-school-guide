## The Window Is Shut — Here Is What Happens Next

If your child applied for the Rashtriya Indian Military College Entrance Examination (RIMCEE) 2026, the form-filling phase is over. The regular window closed on 30 September, the late window (with its extra Rs 500 fee) closed on 7 October, and there is no indication of any further extension.

That can feel like a relief — or a worry, if you rushed the form at the last minute and are now unsure whether everything in it is correct. Either way, the next milestone is already fixed: the correction window opens on **9 October**, the day after tomorrow, and runs for just three days, till **11 October 2026**.

This is the only chance to fix your application before the exam machinery takes over. Use it deliberately.

## Correction Window: 9–11 October — Your 3-Day Checklist

NTA describes this as correction of the details filled in the application form, **on the website only**. The exact list of editable fields will appear on the portal when the window opens, so do not assume everything can be changed — read the on-screen notice first. Then work through this checklist:

1. **Download your confirmation page today** (if you have not already) and print it. Comparing the form against a paper copy is how you catch errors your eyes skipped on screen.
2. **Cross-check the big three against documents:** the candidate's name spelling exactly as on the birth certificate, the date of birth, and the category. These are the fields that cause the most trouble at document verification later.
3. **Look closely at the uploads.** Is the photograph recent and clear? Is the signature legible? Blurry uploads are a classic last-minute rejection risk.
4. **Keep your login credentials ready** before 9 October — application number, password/date of birth, and the registered mobile number for OTPs.
5. **After making corrections, download the updated confirmation page.** The corrected copy is your proof if anything is disputed later.

One honest note: a correction window fixes typos and wrong selections. It does not fix ineligibility — if the age or Class 7 criteria do not hold, no correction can save the application. If you are unsure about eligibility, re-read our [RIMCEE 2026 pattern and eligibility explainer](/news/rimcee-2026-dec-6-exam-pattern-vs-aissee-2026-10-02/) before the window opens.

## The Exam Itself: 400 Marks, 4.5 Hours, Zero Margin for a Weak Subject

Since the form is done, the only thing that moves the needle now is preparation. The Stage-I written exam is pen-and-paper, held across 39 cities:

- **English** — 125 marks, descriptive, 2 hours
- **Mathematics** — 200 marks, descriptive, 1.5 hours
- **General Knowledge** — 75 marks, objective (MCQ), 1 hour

Two rules matter more than the syllabus: there is **no negative marking**, but you need **at least 50% in each subject** to qualify. A brilliant Mathematics score cannot rescue a failed English paper. Answers can be written in English or Hindi, except the English paper itself.

Qualifiers move to the Stage-II viva voce (50 marks, also needing 50% to pass), tentatively in April 2027, followed by a medical examination. Final merit is based on the written exam plus the viva voce.

The fee structure for the record: Rs 1,000 for General, defence wards and OBC-NCL candidates, Rs 750 for SC/ST candidates, with the Rs 500 late fee already paid by those who applied in the 1–7 October window.

## FAQs

**When is the RIMCEE 2026 correction window?**

The correction window runs from 9 to 11 October 2026, on the NTA website only. It is the last opportunity to fix mistakes in your submitted RIMCEE 2026 application form.

**When is the RIMCEE 2026 written exam?**

The Stage-I written examination is on 6 December 2026 (Sunday). It is a pen-and-paper exam held across 39 cities in India, exactly 60 days after the application window closed.

**What is the age limit for RIMCEE 2026?**

Candidates must be between 12-and-a-half and 14 years old as on 1 July 2027 — born between 2 July 2013 and 1 January 2015 — and studying in or having passed Class 7 from a recognised school.

**Is there negative marking in RIMCEE 2026?**

No, there is no negative marking. But you must score at least 50 percent in each subject — English, Mathematics and General Knowledge — so a weak subject cannot be hidden behind a strong one.
