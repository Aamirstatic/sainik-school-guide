## Class 6: 300 marks, 150 minutes

The Class VI paper (for children studying in Class 5) has **125 questions worth 300 marks**, to be finished in **150 minutes** (2:00 PM to 4:30 PM), all on an OMR sheet.

| Section | Subject | Questions | Marks per question | Total marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Language | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| B | Mathematics | 50 | 3 | 150 |
| C | Intelligence | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| D | General Knowledge | 25 | 2 | 50 |
| | **Total** | **125** | | **300** |

## Three facts that should change how your child prepares

**1. Maths is half the battle.** In Class 6, Mathematics alone is 150 of 300 marks; in Class 9, it is 200 of 400 marks. And each maths question pays more — **3 marks** in Class 6, **4 marks** in Class 9 — while every other section pays 2. A child who is merely average at maths is leaving the biggest chunk of the paper on the table. We went deep on this earlier this week: read our [108-day Class 6 maths sprint](/news/aissee-2027-108-days-class6-maths-sprint-2026-10-15/).

**2. You get 72 seconds per question — in both classes.** Class 6: 150 minutes ÷ 125 questions. Class 9: 180 minutes ÷ 150 questions. Identical. That number tells you everything about strategy: questions are meant to be solvable quickly, and spending five minutes wrestling one hard problem is mathematically worse than moving on. Practice must be timed, not just correct.

**3. There is no negative marking — so attempt everything.** A wrong answer and a blank both score zero (CONFIRMED — AglaSem, Careers360). The last 10 minutes of the paper should never find a single unmarked bubble. Guessing is free; leaving blanks is voluntary surrender.

## The language question

Class 6 candidates can take the paper in **English, Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Assamese, Telugu, Odiya, Punjabi, Kannada, Bengali, Tamil or Urdu** (CONFIRMED — AglaSem). Class 9 is **English only**. If your child is in Class 5 and still reading hesitantly in the language they will answer in, that — not more maths — is the first thing to fix in the next 106 days.

## What to do this week

1. **Work the 72-second rule into every practice session.** Start timing individual questions, not just whole papers.
2. **Recalibrate study time toward maths.** It carries half the marks at higher per-question weight — it deserves roughly half the study hours.
3. **Pick the exam language deliberately** (Class 6 only) and practise reading comprehension in that language daily.
4. **Track the notification.** The form window is expected in October–November 2026 (EXPECTED — 2025 pattern, 10 Oct–30 Oct). We are monitoring it daily in our [notification watch](/news/aissee-2027-notification-watch-nta-opens-2027-season-2026-10-16/) so you do not have to.

## FAQs

**What is the AISSEE 2027 exam pattern for Class 6?**

125 multiple-choice questions for 300 marks in 150 minutes: Language 25 questions (50 marks), Mathematics 50 questions (150 marks), Intelligence 25 questions (50 marks), and General Knowledge 25 questions (50 marks).

**What is the AISSEE 2027 exam pattern for Class 9?**

150 multiple-choice questions for 400 marks in 180 minutes: Mathematics 50 questions (200 marks), Intelligence 25 (50 marks), English 25 (50 marks), General Science 25 (50 marks), and Social Science 25 (50 marks).

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**

No. Wrong answers and blank answers both score zero, so every question should be attempted — there is no penalty for guessing.
