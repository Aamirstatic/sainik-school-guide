## What happened

The Karnataka Cabinet, meeting at the Vidhana Soudha in Bengaluru on Thursday, 29 October 2026, approved a grant of ₹228.95 crore for the construction and infrastructure development of the Krantiveera Sangolli Rayanna Sainik School at Sangolli, in Belagavi district. Parliamentary Affairs Minister Krishna Byre Gowda briefed reporters after the meeting, and the decision was reported the same evening by The Hindu and carried the next morning by The New Indian Express and Daijiworld.

The money is earmarked for the physical campus: school buildings, laboratories, the library, sports facilities and other essential infrastructure. The reports do not mention a construction timeline, a phased release schedule, or any change to the school's intake — so treat those details as not yet announced rather than assuming them.

## The school in brief

The Sangolli school is a young one by Sainik School standards. It commenced operations in 2022 and currently has 540 students studying from Classes 6 to 10, according to both The Hindu and The New Indian Express. That makes the funding decision a story about consolidation rather than a launch: a school that already exists, already teaches, and is now getting the permanent infrastructure to match its roll.

Sangolli, in Bailhongal taluk of Belagavi district, is named for Krantiveera Sangolli Rayanna, the 19th-century freedom fighter. For Karnataka families, the school sits alongside the state's older Sainik School at Bijapur (now Vijayapura) as a second in-state option — our [complete guide to Sainik Schools in Karnataka](/blog/sainik-schools-in-karnataka-complete-guide-2027/) covers both, including the 67% home-state quota rule that applies across Sainik Schools.

## Why it matters for AISSEE aspirants

First, infrastructure is not cosmetic in a residential military school. Laboratories, a library and sports facilities are where a large part of the Sainik School model actually happens — academic preparation for the NDA route, and the physical and team training parents cite as the reason for choosing the school. A funded permanent campus removes the uncertainty of temporary classrooms and shared facilities for future batches.

Second, the decision is a signal of intent. State governments do not put ₹228.95 crore into a school they expect to shrink. For a family weighing AISSEE against a local private school, a cabinet-level infrastructure commitment is a reasonable, if indirect, indicator that the school is being built up, not wound down. It does not, however, create new seats or change AISSEE rules — and no coaching-centre claim to that effect should be believed without an official NTA announcement.

## What to do next

**How much money did the Karnataka Cabinet approve for the Sangolli Sainik School?**
