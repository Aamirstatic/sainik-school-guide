## A report card for the schools themselves

Maharashtra's 38 sainik schools are about to be graded the way their students are. Chief Minister **Devendra Fadnavis** chaired a high-level meeting at **Varsha residence, Mumbai, on Friday, 20 November 2026**, to review the report of a committee formed under the state's Sainik School Improvement Policy — and the directives that came out of it leave little room for underperformers.

The committee's central recommendation is a **performance grading system** that sorts all 38 schools into bands, from A at the top to D at the bottom, on three things: academic outcomes, administrative health, and military-training results. Schools at the top earn formal recognition, incentives, additional grants, and infrastructure upgrades such as solar-energy projects. Schools in the middle get a structured window to improve. Schools that consistently trail face what the Chief Minister's Office bluntly called "strict administrative action".

Fadnavis backed the bulk of the committee's findings and told departments to act on them without delay. His instruction, in his own words, was for a system that is *"गुणवत्तापूर्ण, पारदर्शक आणि उत्तरदायी"* — quality-driven, transparent, and accountable. School Education Minister **Dadaji Bhuse** and senior officials from the relevant departments were present.

## NDA entry is the number that matters

The clearest performance metric running through every directive is the rate at which Maharashtra students clear the **National Defence Academy (NDA)** entrance and earn commissions in the armed forces. That is the whole point of a sainik school, and it is where Maharashtra's state-run schools have been falling short.

This is not a new worry. In June 2025, the state set up a seven-member committee to review the functioning of these same 38 schools, after inspections following an October 2024 policy revision found several of them failing to meet essential standards — students not receiving adequate military training and guidance, the very things NDA preparation depends on. The government resolution at the time was blunt: the schools have "a meagre share in sending their students to the National Defence Academy". The committee was asked to physically inspect the schools, study their problems, and report back. Friday's meeting was the review of that report.

To keep the pressure on, the reform proposes two pieces of machinery: a **state-level monitoring committee** and a **Sainik School Regulatory Board**, designed to track NDA intake and push it upward. The meeting also set an annual rhythm — the entire network is to be assessed once a year, with implementation of recommendations tracked at each cycle.

## Which schools does this cover — and which does it not?

One distinction matters for parents. The 38 schools in this reform are the state's own network — government-aided but privately managed, with around 12,400 students between them, one per district under a 1995 government resolution. They are **not** the same as Maharashtra's two central Sainik Schools, **Satara** (established 1961) and **Chandrapur** (started 2019), which admit through AISSEE like the other 31 central schools. [Our Satara profile covers its fees, seats and admission process.](/blog/sainik-school-maharashtra-satara-fees-seats-admission-2026-10-17/)

If your child is applying to a Maharashtra school through AISSEE 2027, nothing about the exam changes: the form still closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**, and the exam is still on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**. [Our comparison of what changed this year — and what didn't — is here.](/news/aissee-2027-11-days-left-what-changed-this-year-2026-11-21/) What the reform changes is what happens *after* admission: the school your child joins will now be graded every year, rewarded if it performs, and pushed hard if it does not.

## What to watch next

The next concrete marker is whether the monitoring committee and the regulatory board are formally constituted — and whether the first annual review triggers real consequences for the schools at the bottom of the grade sheet. We will update this story when either happens.

## FAQs

**What did Maharashtra announce for its sainik schools on 20 November 2026?**

Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis ordered an overhaul of the state's 38 sainik schools: a performance grading system, incentives and extra grants for top schools, an improvement window for middle-ranked schools, and strict administrative action for chronic underperformers. A state-level monitoring committee and a Sainik School Regulatory Board were also proposed.

**How many sainik schools are there in Maharashtra?**

There are 38 state-run sainik schools covered by this reform, with around 12,400 students. Maharashtra also has two central Sainik Schools — Satara (established 1961) and Chandrapur (started 2019) — which admit through AISSEE and are separate from the 38.

**What is the grading based on?**

Academic outcomes, administrative health and military-training results. The clearest metric running through the reform is the rate at which students clear the NDA entrance and earn commissions in the armed forces.

**Does this change AISSEE 2027 for Maharashtra students?**

No. AISSEE 2027 is unchanged: the form closes on 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM and the exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027. The Maharashtra reform is about how the state's own 38 schools are run and reviewed, not about the entrance exam.
