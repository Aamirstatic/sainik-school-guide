## What happened in Dimapur on Saturday

Nagaland Chief Minister Dr Neiphiu Rio spent his Saturday with the old boys of Sainik Schools — and he did not come empty-handed. Addressing the 8th General Body Meeting-cum-Get Together of the Old Boys' Association of Sainik Schools Nagaland Chapter (OBASSNC) in Dimapur on **21 November 2026**, Rio announced a personal contribution of **₹50 lakh** to the association and called for a concerted effort to strengthen **Sainik School Punglwa** and develop it into a centre of excellence.

Punglwa, in Peren district, is the Northeast's first Sainik School (1970) and Nagaland's only one. Rio's message was blunt: the school needs collective intervention to become more vibrant, attractive and academically competitive. He also announced that the state government will provide a **prefabricated multipurpose building** for the association's complex, to be used for meetings, sports, coaching and recreational activities.

Rio is himself a Sainik School product — an alumnus of Sainik School Purulia — and he spoke from that experience, recalling his days as a class monitor and the discipline that hostel life, PT and parades drilled into him.

## The number that matters most: 10–20 empty seats a year

The headline figure is the ₹50 lakh. But for a parent in Kohima or Dimapur reading this, the more important number is the one Rio flagged next: **around 10 to 20 or more seats from Nagaland's quota at Punglwa remain vacant every year** before being filled by students from outside the state.

In a state where parents worry about getting their child into a good residential school, the home-state quota at its only Sainik School is not being fully used. Rio attributed the gap partly to parents' concerns over the **cost of education**, and called for greater awareness about the opportunities the school offers.

Here's the thing — under the Sainik Schools admission system, **67% of seats** at Punglwa are reserved for Nagaland-domicile candidates. If those seats are not filled by local children, they go to other-state applicants. Every empty Nagaland seat is a local child who did not apply, did not know, or assumed they could not afford it.

## What the Chief Minister proposed next

Rio did not stop at the money. He proposed forming a **committee to study financial assistance, entrance-exam coaching and other support** for deserving students from economically weaker families, and said the state government is willing to extend assistance within the prescribed financial assistance criteria. He also urged the alumni association to move beyond reunions and become an organisation that mentors younger generations, prepares students for entrance examinations and guides them toward armed-forces careers — including through a **corpus fund** built from voluntary contributions.

Be clear about what this is and is not. The committee is a **proposal**. No scholarship scheme, no fee waiver and no coaching programme has been announced yet. If anyone tells you they can get your child a Punglwa seat or a fee concession for a payment, that is a scam — admission is only through the AISSEE.

## Why this is good news for AISSEE 2027 applicants

Punglwa is not a school to overlook. Two of its cadets made the **NDA-157 merit list** this year — Dev Dagar at AIR 61 and Anshik Yadav at AIR 125 — which is a serious return from a small school. And with the AISSEE 2027 form open until **2 December 2026 (11:50 PM)**, Nagaland families still have ten days to apply. The late-fee window runs **3–6 December** with an extra ₹500, the correction window is **7–9 December**, and the exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**.

If your child is in Class 5 or Class 8 in Nagaland right now, the message from Dimapur is simple: the seats are there, the quota is yours, and the state is finally paying attention. Apply.
