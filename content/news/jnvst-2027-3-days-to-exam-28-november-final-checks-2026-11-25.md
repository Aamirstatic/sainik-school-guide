## Three days out — the preparation is done, the checks are not

Saturday is the day. The Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya Selection Test (JNVST) 2027 for Class 6 is on **Saturday, 28 November 2026, from 11:30 AM to 1:30 PM**, in a single phase for both summer-bound and winter-bound Navodaya Vidyalayas. If your child is appearing, the studying is essentially over. What is left is the small, boring stuff — and that is exactly the stuff that goes wrong on exam morning.

So spend the next three days on logistics, not on new chapters. No new topic learned in the last 72 hours will change the result. A wrong photo on the admit card, a missing pen, or a centre you have never visited — those can.

## First: check the admit card tonight, not on Saturday

The admit cards were released on **28 October 2026** on the NVS admission portal, linked from the official website **navodaya.gov.in**. Download it with the child's **registration number and date of birth**. If you have not downloaded it yet, do it tonight — the portal gets slow when thousands of families do the same thing on the last day.

Then read the card the way the invigilator will read it:

- **Name, photo and date of birth** — the invigilator will match the photo with the child sitting in front of them.
- **Exam centre name and address** — no change of centre is allowed, so if the centre is in another town, plan the travel now.
- **Medium/language of the test** — the question booklet must be in the language you chose at the time of the form.

If anything is wrong, contact the **principal of your district's JNV** right away. That is the route NVS itself points families to — do not wait for Saturday morning to discover a mismatch.

## Exam-day rules that catch families out

Children must be **seated in the exam room by 11:00 AM**. Latecomers will not be allowed in. Aim to reach by 10:30 AM — and if you have never been to the centre before, do a trial run tomorrow.

What to carry:

- **The printed admit card.** A printed copy is strictly mandatory for entry. NVS does not send hall tickets by post, and a phone screenshot is not a substitute.
- **The child's Aadhaar card**, or the parent's residence certificate that was uploaded with the form.
- **A blue or black ballpoint pen.** Pencils are not allowed on the OMR sheet, and answers cannot be changed or erased once marked. Carry two pens, not one. Divyang candidates get **40 extra minutes**.

## The paper itself — what your child will face

The test is **80 objective questions for 100 marks, over two hours, with no negative marking** — 1.25 marks per correct answer. The split:

| Section | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Mental Ability | 40 | 50 |
| Arithmetic | 20 | 25 |
| Language | 20 | 25 |
| **Total** | **80** | **100** |

No negative marking changes the strategy completely: every question deserves an attempt, even a guess, because a wrong answer costs nothing. Tell your child that on Friday night, not on Saturday morning — it is the one piece of exam technique that genuinely matters at this stage.

## What happens after Saturday

The result is 🔶 **EXPECTED by the end of March 2027** on the same NVS portal, after machine evaluation of the OMR sheets and document verification of qualifying students. Admission follows only after that verification — so keep the documents you uploaded with the form in one folder, and do not lose the registration number.

Families preparing for both JNVST and AISSEE often ask which exam matters more. They are different doors to different schools — Navodaya is free residential schooling, Sainik School is a defence-oriented residential school with fees. Our [AISSEE vs JNVST comparison](/blog/aissee-vs-jnvst-two-national-boarding-exams-compared/) explains the trade-offs plainly. For this week, though, forget the comparison. Saturday belongs to JNVST.
