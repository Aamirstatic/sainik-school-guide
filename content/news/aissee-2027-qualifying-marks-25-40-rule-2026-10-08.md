## The 25-40 Rule, in Plain Words

Most parents think of AISSEE as one total score — 300 marks for Class 6, 400 for Class 9. But there is a floor under each part of the paper: your child must score **at least 25% in every section** and **at least 40% in the total**. Fail either condition, and the paper does not count for merit, no matter how strong the total. Think of it as four or five small doors instead of one big gate — each door has to open.

## What the Sections Look Like

For the Class 6 paper — 300 marks, 150 minutes, pen-and-paper OMR — the sections are Language, Mathematics, Intelligence and General Knowledge (50, 150, 50 and 50 marks). That makes the 25% floor about 13 marks in the small sections and 38 in Mathematics, with at least 120 overall.

The Class 9 paper — 400 marks, 180 minutes — spreads across Mathematics (200 marks), Intelligence, English, General Science and Social Science (50 each). Same rule: 25% in each section, 40% (160 marks) overall. These section marks follow the AISSEE 2026 pattern; the 2027 bulletin will confirm whether the structure holds.

## Why the Section Floor Changes How You Prepare

This is the part that quietly sinks good students every year. A child who is brilliant at Mathematics but blanks out in General Knowledge can walk out with 210 out of 300 — a score that looks like a comfortable pass — and still fail, because one section fell below its floor. So the preparation lesson is balance: GK deserves more attention than most families give it, because it is where strong maths students most often slip below 25%.

And one small mercy: there is no negative marking. A guessed answer can be the difference between 24% and 25% in a section — and that difference can decide the whole attempt. Attempt every question.

## The Tie-Breaker Parents Rarely Know About

When two children land on the same total, NTA first compares Mathematics marks; if those are equal too, it compares Intelligence. Two identical totals can end up pages apart on the merit list because of how the marks were distributed. This is the strongest practical argument for front-loading Mathematics: it is half the paper and the first tie-breaker — no other section does that much work.

## The SC/ST Exception (and Its Limit)

For the 33 Sainik Schools, SC and ST students are exempt from the minimum marks — admission depends on rank within their category rather than the 25-40 floor. But for New Sainik Schools, the exemption does not apply: the minimum marks are required of every student. Check the 2027 bulletin to see which side of this line your chosen school falls on.

## What to Do With This Now

The 25-40 rule rewards steady, even preparation over spiky talent. If your child's mock tests show one section consistently near the floor, fix that section before pushing the strongest one higher. A child at 65% everywhere is safer than a child at 90% in Maths and 22% in GK. The exam rewards breadth first, depth second.

## FAQs

**What are the qualifying marks for AISSEE 2027?**

Based on the AISSEE 2026 pattern, a student must score at least 25% in each section and 40% overall to qualify. The 2027 bulletin will confirm whether this stays the same.

**Is there negative marking in AISSEE 2027?**

No. There is no negative marking in AISSEE — wrong answers and blank answers both score zero, so every question should be attempted. This follows the AISSEE 2026 pattern.

**Do SC and ST students have the same qualifying marks?**

For the 33 Sainik Schools, SC and ST students are exempt from the minimum qualifying marks, based on the AISSEE 2026 pattern. For New Sainik Schools, the minimum marks apply to all students including SC and ST.

**What happens if two students get the same total score in AISSEE?**

The tie is broken by Mathematics marks first, then Intelligence. That is one more reason Mathematics — which already carries half the paper's marks — deserves the most preparation time.
