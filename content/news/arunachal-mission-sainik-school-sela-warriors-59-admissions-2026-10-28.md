## The Chief Minister's Office speaks up

Arunachal Pradesh's Chief Minister's Office (CMO) on Wednesday, 28 October 2026, publicly commended the Indian Army's 'Sela Warriors' initiative under Mission Sainik School — and in doing so, put two numbers on the record. According to the CMO's post, as reported by Nation Press, **59 students have already secured admissions to Sainik Schools through the programme**, while **75 aspirants are currently preparing for AISSEE 2027**, the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination. The CMO closed its message with "sincere gratitude to the Indian Army."

For parents in Arunachal's border districts, this is not a routine government pat on the back. It is public confirmation that a coaching pipeline built for remote, tribal communities is actually converting preparation into seats — and that a fresh batch of 75 is already in the system for the next exam cycle.

## Who the Sela Warriors are

The name is deliberate. Sela Warriors is the local branding used by the Army's **Gajraj Corps** for its Mission Sainik School coaching batches in Arunachal Pradesh, named after the Sela Pass in Tawang district — the high-altitude gateway the corps' area covers. Gajraj Corps is headquartered at Tezpur in Assam and holds responsibility for the Arunachal Pradesh and Assam sectors along the Line of Actual Control.

The logic of the programme is straightforward, and it is the same logic behind every Sainik School success story from a remote district: cracking AISSEE without structured coaching, mock tests and guidance through documents and counselling is a steep ask for a child in a village with thin school infrastructure. Mission Sainik School exists to close that gap — coaching first, then hand-holding through the exam and admission stages.

## This is not the first Arunachal success story

The Sela Warriors numbers land on top of an earlier, well-documented Arunachal mentorship effort. In May 2024, the Army's Spear Corps picked 33 students from remote hamlets including Sarli in Kurung Kumey district and prepared them for the Sainik School entrance examinations, with 88 theory classes, 18 rounds of testing and counselling sessions. The Hindu reported in September 2025 that **32 of the 33 qualified at the national level**, and that Milli Yabi of Sarli became the first to secure final admission — to Sainik School East Siang, the school this very website is named after. Latestly's agency report of the same story records the same structure: structured classes, mock tests, documentation help, and an escorted trip to Itanagar for the examination.

That history matters for two reasons. First, it shows the model works: the same ingredients — coaching, mocks, documents, escort to the exam centre — keep producing admissions. Second, it shows the pipeline is not a one-off: from the Sarli batch of 2024–25 to 59 admissions now and 75 aspirants for AISSEE 2027, the effort has scaled.

## What this means for parents elsewhere

If your child is in a remote or border district — in Arunachal or anywhere else — the lesson is practical, not inspirational. Structured preparation plus help with the paperwork beats talent alone, because AISSEE rewards syllabus coverage and exam practice, and the form itself rejects careless applicants. Ask your school, your district education office or your nearest Army unit whether a similar coaching or mentorship batch runs near you, and keep the AISSEE documents ready: birth certificate, domicile certificate and category certificate, as the AglaSem guide to the 2027 cycle advises.

One caution, though. The AISSEE 2027 notification itself is **still awaited**: as of the checks reported on 24 October 2026, the National Testing Agency had not released it on the official portal, exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society. Apply only through that portal once the bulletin appears, and treat any "notification out" claim on other sites with the scepticism this site's earlier fact-checks have earned — the October 13 release claim was already debunked here on 18 October.

## FAQs

**What is Mission Sainik School in Arunachal Pradesh?**

It is an Indian Army preparatory coaching programme for tribal youth from Arunachal Pradesh's border districts, preparing them for the All India Sainik Schools Entrance Examination (AISSEE) and supporting them through admission.

**Who are the Sela Warriors?**

Sela Warriors is the local name used by the Army's Gajraj Corps for its Mission Sainik School coaching batches in Arunachal Pradesh, named after the Sela Pass in Tawang district.

**How many students has the programme helped so far?**

According to the Arunachal Pradesh Chief Minister's Office, as flagged on 28 October 2026, 59 students have secured Sainik School admissions through the programme and 75 aspirants are preparing for AISSEE 2027.

**Is the AISSEE 2027 notification out yet?**

No. As of the most recent checks reported on 24 October 2026, the National Testing Agency had not released the AISSEE 2027 notification, and parents should apply only through the official NTA portal once it appears.
