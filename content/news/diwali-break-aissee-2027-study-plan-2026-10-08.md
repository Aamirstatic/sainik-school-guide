## Why This Matters Now

Here is a date most parents have not yet done the maths on: **Diwali 2026 is 31 days away** (Sunday, 8 November), and it lands squarely inside the final stretch to AISSEE 2027, tentatively scheduled for 31 January 2027.

Every year, the same thing happens. October is productive — the admission forms are being filled, the weather cools, the child is in rhythm. Then the festival stretch arrives, school gives a few days off, guests come, and preparation silently slips by three weeks. By the time the rhythm returns, it is late November, and the exam is two months away.

The festivals are not the enemy. The enemy is the **unplanned break** — stopping without deciding when to restart.

## The Two-Phase Plan

**Phase 1 — Before Dhanteras (8 Oct to 5 Nov): finish the syllabus.** This is the non-negotiable part. Twenty-eight days is enough to close every pending chapter in Class 6 maths, English grammar and GK if the child studies at normal pace. Do not save "one chapter for after Diwali" — leftover chapters are what turn December into a panic.

**Phase 2 — Diwali week (6 to 10 Nov): switch to maintenance mode.** Do not attempt new topics between Dhanteras and Bhai Dooj. Instead, keep one daily habit that takes under 90 minutes total:

- 30 minutes: mental-maths revision (tables, squares, quick division)
- 30 minutes: read the day's current affairs and 10 GK flashcards
- 30 minutes: one timed section of a previous-year paper (intelligence or English)

This is not a "study holiday" — it is a **hold-the-line holiday**. A child who revises for 90 minutes a day through the festivals returns to full pace on 11 November feeling sharp instead of restarting cold.

## The Restart Rule

Agree on the restart **date** with your child before the break, not during it. Tuesday, 10 November (Bhai Dooj) or Wednesday, 11 November is a natural restart point. Write it on the wall calendar. The data from our [115-days explainer](/news/aissee-2027-notification-not-out-115-days-2026-10-08/) is worth remembering here: once the NTA notification drops — expected any day now in October — the form-filling itself will take mental bandwidth, so having the study machine already running makes the double load manageable.

One more practical point: the Diwali holidays are the perfect window for one **full-length mock exam** under real conditions. The [qualifying-marks rule](/news/aissee-2027-qualifying-marks-25-40-rule-2026-10-08/) — 25% in every section and 40% aggregate — is what a mock teaches best. Find the weak section in November, fix it in December, polish in January.

## FAQs

**When is Diwali 2026?**

Diwali 2026 falls on Sunday, 8 November, with Dhanteras on 6 November. It comes about 31 days after this article's publication date.

**How many days are left for AISSEE 2027?**

The AISSEE 2027 exam is tentatively listed for 31 January 2027 in NTA's examination calendar, which is about 115 days from 8 October 2026.

**Should my child study during the Diwali holidays?**

Study through the holidays, but lighter than usual — keep revision of formulas and mental maths going daily, and plan a full-speed restart the week after Bhai Dooj. A zero-study break costs far more than it feels like.

**What is the single best use of the Diwali week for AISSEE?**

Timed full-length mock papers. A student finishing the syllabus by Dhanteras can spend the break testing speed and accuracy, which is exactly what the AISSEE rewards.
