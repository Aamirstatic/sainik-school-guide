## The number that should change the conversation at home

Here is a number worth reading twice: **35**. That is how many women cadets from Haryana have joined the National Defence Academy since it opened its doors to women in August 2022 — more than any other state in India. Uttar Pradesh is next with 28, then Rajasthan with 13 and Maharashtra with 11. Of the 126 women who have joined the NDA across five courses, more than one in four is from Haryana.

The figures come from the Ministry of Defence itself, in a written reply in the Rajya Sabha on 17 March 2025. There is no state quota at the NDA — selection is through the same national written examination and Services Selection Board for everyone. Haryana's lead is not a reservation. It is what happens when families in one state decide, in large numbers, that their daughters belong in uniform.

That is the context for an appeal made this month in Haryana. Sonia Mor, BJP Vice-President from Sonipat, has urged parents across the state to encourage their daughters to apply for Sainik School admission through AISSEE 2027, saying military-oriented schooling builds the confidence, discipline and leadership that a future in the armed forces needs. Her own family lives the argument: her son is posted in Kargil, her husband, Colonel Ashok Mor (Retd), served in the Army for 28 years and was Registrar at Sainik School Rewa, and her father-in-law also served.

## 420 seats for girls — and how they actually work

For the 2027–28 session, the 33 existing Sainik Schools list **420 tentative seats for girls: 351 in Class 6 and 69 in Class 9**. Approved New Sainik Schools add 1,221 Class 6 and 383 Class 9 seats for girls, plus gender-neutral seats. Every number here is tentative — the bulletin says so, and final availability follows the official rules.

Three things about those seats matter more than the total:

**Your daughter does not compete with boys.** NTA prepares a separate merit list for girls, school-wise and category-wise. For the girls' seats, she competes only with other girls. That single fact changes the odds completely — and it is the detail most parents miss.

**The 10 percent rule protects the smallest schools.** Every Sainik School keeps 10 percent of its Class 6 vacancies, or 10 seats, whichever is more, for girls. That is why even a small school has at least 10 girls' seats in Class 6.

**Class 9 is different — check before you choose.** Girls can apply for Class 9 only where vacancies exist. This year, 21 of the 33 schools have Class 9 seats for girls and 12 have none. In Haryana itself, Sainik School Kunjpura lists 16 tentative seats for girls — 12 in Class 6 (seven for Haryana, five for other states) and four in Class 9 (two and two). Do not fill a Class 9 preference for a school that has no girls' seats there; the bulletin vacancy list is the check.

## What to do before 2 December

The AISSEE 2027 form closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. A late-fee window runs from 3 to 6 December with an extra Rs 500, and corrections are allowed from 7 to 9 December. The exam is on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**.

If your daughter is eligible — Class 6 applicants must be 10 to 12 years old on 31 March 2027, Class 9 applicants 13 to 15 and must have passed Class 8 — there are two checks to do tonight, not next week. First, confirm her date of birth against the bulletin window (1 April 2015 to 31 March 2017 for Class 6; 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014 for Class 9). Second, look at the school-wise vacancy list and pick schools that actually have girls' seats in her class. Then apply at the NTA portal, exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society, and save the confirmation page.

Haryana's 35 cadets did not start at the NDA. They started with a form filled by a parent who believed a daughter could wear the uniform. Twelve days remain to be that parent.

## FAQs

**How many seats are there for girls in Sainik Schools for 2027-28?**

The 33 existing Sainik Schools list 420 tentative seats for girls — 351 in Class 6 and 69 in Class 9. Approved New Sainik Schools list 1,221 Class 6 and 383 Class 9 seats for girls, plus gender-neutral seats. All figures are tentative and come from the AISSEE 2027 information bulletin.

**Does my daughter compete with boys for a Sainik School seat?**

No. NTA prepares a separate merit list for girls, so your daughter competes only with other girls for the girls' seats in each school. The standing rule gives girls 10 percent of vacancies or 10 seats per school, whichever is more.

**Which Sainik Schools have Class 9 seats for girls?**

Only 21 of the 33 Sainik Schools have Class 9 seats for girls in 2027-28; 12 schools have none this year. In Class 6, all 33 schools admit girls. Check the school-wise vacancy list in the bulletin before filling your preferences.

**What is the last date to apply for AISSEE 2027?**

2 December 2026, up to 11:50 PM. A late-fee window runs from 3 to 6 December with an extra fee of Rs 500, and corrections can be made from 7 to 9 December. The exam is on Sunday, 31 January 2027.
