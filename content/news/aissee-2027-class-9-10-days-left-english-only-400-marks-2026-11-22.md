## Ten days left — and Class 9 is a different exam

The AISSEE 2027 form closes on **Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. Ten days sounds like plenty. It is — for the form. But most of the Class 9 advice floating around is actually Class 6 advice, and the two exams are not the same. If your child is in Class 8 now, here is what is different, and the traps that catch families every year.

## The paper your child will actually sit

Class 9 is a **180-minute, 150-question, 400-mark** paper in **English only**. Mathematics is 50 questions for **200 marks** — half the paper on its own. English, Intelligence, General Science and Social Science are 25 questions for **50 marks each**. There is **no negative marking**, so a blank answer and a wrong answer both score zero. And the qualifying rule is the same as Class 6: **25% in every section and 40% in aggregate** — which means 100 marks out of 400 in total, and at least 7 correct answers in each 50-mark section to clear its floor. That rule does not apply to SC/ST candidates.

## The traps

**1. Assuming the paper is bilingual.** The Class 6 paper can be written in 13 languages. Class 9 cannot — it is English only. A child who has done all their Science and Social Science in Hindi will understand the concepts and still stumble on the words. If that is your child, the next ten days should include English-medium questions, not just English lessons. Our [Class 9 English bridge guide](/blog/aissee-class-9-english-only-hindi-medium-72-day-bridge/) explains the switch in detail.

**2. Treating Maths as just another section.** Maths is 200 of the 400 marks. A child who scores well in the other four sections but leaves Maths weak cannot make it up — there are not enough marks elsewhere. The reverse is also true: strong Maths carries the paper. Give it the biggest share of the remaining 70 days.

**3. Ignoring a 50-mark section.** Science and Social Science look small next to Maths, but each carries its own 25% floor. Seven correct answers in a section is all it takes to clear it — and a child who scores zero in one section fails the paper however well they did elsewhere. Our [Class 9 72-day plan](/blog/aissee-class-9-72-day-study-plan-400-marks-2026-11-20/) shows how to keep all five sections moving together.

**4. Getting the eligibility wrong.** Class 9 needs a child aged **13 to 15 years as on 31 March 2027** — born between **1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014** — studying in Class 8 now, and passed Class 8 from a recognised school at the time of admission. Check the date of birth against the birth certificate before you pay the fee, because the fee is generally non-refundable.

**5. Waiting until 2 December to submit.** The portal is always busiest on the last day. Submit this week, while it is calm. If you do miss 2 December, the late window runs **3 to 6 December** with an extra **Rs 500** on top of the regular fee — **Rs 850** for General, OBC-NCL and defence wards, **Rs 700** for SC/ST. After 6 December there is no further window. Read our [late-fee window guide](/news/aissee-2027-late-fee-window-dec-3-6-worth-paying-2026-11-08/) before deciding to wait, and our [correction-window guide](/news/aissee-2027-correction-window-7-9-december-audit-confirmation-page-2026-11-18/) so you know what you can still change between 7 and 9 December — and what you cannot.

## What to do this week

Fill the form, pay the fee, and download the confirmation page. Then, before the exam on **Sunday, 31 January 2027**, put your child through at least one full 180-minute paper under real timing — English medium, OMR sheet, no phone in the room. Seventy days is enough to fix a weak section. It is not enough to fix a wrong form.

## FAQs

**When does the AISSEE 2027 Class 9 application form close?**
Wednesday, 2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM. The fee must also be paid by the same time. Candidates who miss it can still apply from 3 to 6 December with a late fee of Rs 500.

**Is the AISSEE Class 9 paper in English only?**
Yes. The Class 9 paper is in English only, unlike the Class 6 paper which can be written in 13 languages. Children from Hindi-medium schools should start reading and solving in English now, not in January.

**What is the AISSEE 2027 Class 9 exam pattern?**
180 minutes, 150 questions, 400 marks. Mathematics is 50 questions for 200 marks, and English, Intelligence, General Science and Social Science are 25 questions for 50 marks each. There is no negative marking.

**Who can apply for AISSEE 2027 Class 9?**
Children aged 13 to 15 years as on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014 — who are studying in Class 8 now and will have passed Class 8 from a recognised school at the time of admission. Both boys and girls can apply.
