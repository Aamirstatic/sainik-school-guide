## What happened

Nothing new broke today — which is exactly why this rule matters. With **119 days to the AISSEE 2027 exam on 31 January 2027** (CONFIRMED tentative, NTA exam calendar), most families are counting total marks. But AISSEE does not work on total marks alone — every year, candidates learn too late that clearing 40% overall was never enough.

## The rule, stated plainly

To qualify for admission to Sainik Schools, a candidate must score at least **25% marks in each section** AND at least **40% marks in aggregate**. Both conditions must be met together. Miss either one and the result says "not qualified" — no matter how strong the other looks.

In raw numbers for the 2026 pattern (EXPECTED to repeat for 2027):

**Class 6 (300 marks):** Maths ≥ 37.5 of 150; Language, Intelligence, GK ≥ 12.5 of 50 each; aggregate ≥ 120.

**Class 9 (400 marks):** Maths ≥ 50 of 200; English, Intelligence, General Science, Social Science ≥ 12.5 of 50 each; aggregate ≥ 160.

## Why it matters

The trap is obvious once you see it. Mathematics carries half the Class 6 paper, so a Maths-strong child can cross the 40% aggregate on one section's strength — then fail the whole exam because General Knowledge or Intelligence stayed under 25%.

Example: a Class 6 candidate scoring 140 in Maths, 45 in Language, 40 in Intelligence, but only 10 in GK totals 235 out of 300 — a respectable 78% — and still does not qualify.

For preparation planning, this flips the usual advice. With four months left, the highest-return work is lifting the weakest section above 25% — not polishing the strongest one. A child scoring single digits in any section is sitting on a disqualification, not a low score.

## The SC/ST and New Sainik Schools nuance

The exemption clause is easy to misread. For the **33 established Sainik Schools**, SC and ST candidates are exempt from the minimum qualifying marks. But for **approved New Sainik Schools**, the 25% per section and 40% aggregate rule applies to **all** candidates, including SC and ST.

Note the difference between qualifying marks and cutoffs: qualifying marks decide whether you appear on the merit list at all. The school-wise closing cutoffs decide whether you actually get a seat. Qualifying is necessary; it is not sufficient.

## What to do next

1. **Run the numbers this week.** Take your child's latest mock test and mark each section against 25%. Anything at or below the line is the emergency, not the total.
2. **Rebalance the timetable.** Give the weakest section daily time until it sits safely above 25%, with roughly 15 marks of headroom on the 50-mark sections.
3. **Do not abandon the strong sections.** A comfortable buffer can shrink fast under exam pressure.

## FAQs

**What are the qualifying marks for AISSEE 2027?**

25% marks in each section and 40% marks in aggregate. This was the qualifying criterion in the AISSEE 2026 information bulletin and is expected to continue for 2027.

**Does the 25% rule apply to every section of the AISSEE paper?**

Yes — Maths, Language, Intelligence and GK for Class 6, not just 40% overall.

**Is there any relaxation in qualifying marks for SC/ST candidates?**

For the 33 established Sainik Schools, SC and ST candidates are exempt from the minimum qualifying marks. For New Sainik Schools, the 25% per section and 40% aggregate rule applies to all candidates including SC/ST.

**If my child scores 60% overall but fails one section, do they qualify?**

No — not for the 33 Sainik Schools. Scoring below 25% in any single section disqualifies the candidate even if the overall aggregate is well above 40%.
