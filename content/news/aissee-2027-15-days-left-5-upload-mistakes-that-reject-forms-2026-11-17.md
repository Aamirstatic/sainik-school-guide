## 15 days left — and the form is where parents slip up

Here is the thing most parents do not realise until it is too late: the AISSEE form is not hard to fill, but it is easy to get wrong. The questions are simple. The uploads are where forms get stuck — a photo that is too large, a signature scanned at the wrong size, a certificate saved as a JPG instead of a PDF. And the portal will not always explain the problem clearly; sometimes it just refuses the file.

The window opened on 3 November and closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**, fee payment included. There is a late window from 3 to 6 December with an extra ₹500, and a correction window from 7 to 9 December — but neither is a reason to submit a sloppy form now and hope to fix it later. The correction window covers particulars; the safest approach is to upload everything correctly the first time.

If you have not applied yet, [run the four ten-minute checks from our 13 November guide](/news/aissee-2027-19-days-left-4-checks-before-you-submit-2026-11-13/) first, then come back here for the upload details.

## The 5 upload mistakes

### 1. A photograph outside the 10–200 KB range

The bulletin wants a **recent photograph in JPG format, between 10 KB and 200 KB**. The most common failure is a phone photo straight from the gallery — those are usually 2–5 MB, far too large. Compress it before you start the form. And use a recent photo of your child, not one from two years ago: the photo on the form is the photo on the admit card, and it should look like the child who walks into the exam hall on 31 January.

### 2. A signature that is too big — or scanned off a ruled page

The candidate's **signature must be a JPG between 10 KB and 50 KB**. Parents often scan the signature at high resolution and wonder why the portal rejects it. Sign on plain white paper with a dark pen, scan or photograph it cleanly, and compress it into the range. Remember, this is the *child's* signature, not the parent's.

### 3. Forgetting the thumb impression — or using the wrong thumb

This is the upload most first-time applicants do not expect: the **candidate's left-hand thumb impression, JPG, 10 KB to 50 KB**. The bulletin permits the right thumb only if the left thumb is unavailable. Take it with a stamp pad on white paper, the same way you would for any government form, and keep the scan inside the size limit.

### 4. Certificates uploaded as images, or over 300 KB

Every certificate — date of birth, domicile, caste or category certificate, and the defence service certificate or PPO where applicable — must be a **PDF between 50 KB and 300 KB**. A phone photo of a certificate saved as a JPG will not do. Scan the originals (or photograph them flat and convert to PDF), and compress each file into the range before uploading. The domicile certificate matters more than most parents think: it decides the home-state quota, which covers 67% of seats.

### 5. Paying the fee and assuming the form is done

This one costs parents sleep. The fee is **₹850 for General, OBC (NCL) and defence wards, and ₹700 for SC/ST** — but payment alone does not complete the application. **The form is complete only when the confirmation page is generated.** Download it and keep it safe. If the money left your account but no confirmation page appeared, log back in and check the application status before paying again: NTA keeps the latest form if two are paid for the same class, so a hasty second payment can create a duplicate instead of fixing the first one.

## Three things that cannot be fixed later

- **The question-paper medium.** Class 6 candidates choose from 13 languages. Once selected, it cannot be changed — pick the language your child actually studies in, not the one that sounds impressive.
- **One form per child, per class.** Register with a working email and mobile number you will still have in January; NTA sends admit-card and counselling messages there.
- **The name and date of birth.** Both must match the birth certificate exactly. Class 6 is for children born between 1 April 2015 and 31 March 2017; Class 9 is 1 April 2012 to 31 March 2014, with Class 8 passed by admission time.

## What to do this week

- **Today:** gather the photo, signature, thumb impression and all certificates, and get every file into the right format and size before you open the portal.
- **Before you pay:** re-read the name, date of birth, category, school choices and the four exam-city preferences against the original documents. [The city slip comes on 11 January and the admit card on 25 January](/news/aissee-2027-exam-city-slip-jan-11-admit-card-jan-25-what-to-check-2026-11-13/) — the details you enter now are the details printed there.
- **After you pay:** download the confirmation page immediately. No confirmation page, no completed form — that is the one check that matters most.

## FAQs

**What is the last date to apply for AISSEE 2027?**

2 December 2026, up to 11:50 PM, including fee payment. A late window runs from 3 to 6 December 2026 with an additional late fee of Rs. 500.

**What size should the AISSEE 2027 photograph be?**

A recent photograph in JPG format, between 10 KB and 200 KB. The signature and the left-hand thumb impression must each be JPG files between 10 KB and 50 KB.

**In what format should AISSEE certificates be uploaded?**

All certificates — date of birth, domicile, caste or category, and defence service certificates where applicable — must be uploaded as PDF files between 50 KB and 300 KB each.

**I paid the AISSEE fee but did not get a confirmation page. Is my form submitted?**

No. The application is complete only after the confirmation page is generated. Log back in and check your application status before paying again — NTA keeps the latest form if two are paid for the same class.
