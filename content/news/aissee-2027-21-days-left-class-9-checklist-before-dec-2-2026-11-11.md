## 20 days left — and Class 9 is the tighter race

The AISSEE 2027 application window closes on **2 December 2026 at 11:50 PM**. Today, 12 November, that leaves 20 days. The exam itself is on Sunday, **31 January 2027** — 80 days away.

Most of the deadline talk this month has been about Class 6, and understandably so: it has the bigger seat count. But Class 9 is the tighter race, and it catches families out. There are far fewer Class 9 seats — the bulletin lists 732 tentative Class 9 seats across the 33 existing Sainik Schools, against 3,160 for Class 6 — and the paper is longer, harder, and entirely in English. If your child is in Class 8 now and aiming for Class 9, these 20 days deserve a plan of their own.

## The Class 9 checklist

**1. Age — check it first, because there is no flexibility.** Your child must be 13 to 15 years old as on 31 March 2027, which means born between **1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014**, both days inclusive. The bulletin is explicit that there is no age relaxation for any category, including SC/ST and defence wards. If the date of birth on the birth certificate falls outside that window, the form will not save the application — check the certificate, not your memory.

**2. Class 8 must be passed by admission time.** The bulletin requires Class 9 applicants to have passed the Class 8 examination from a recognised school at the time of admission. A child currently in Class 8 is eligible to apply now; the pass certificate is what matters at admission.

**3. Know the paper you are signing up for.** Class 9 is a single paper of **180 minutes (2:00 to 5:00 PM)**, 150 questions, **400 marks**, all in English: Mathematics 50 questions for 200 marks, and Intelligence, English, General Science and Social Science at 25 questions and 50 marks each. There is **no negative marking** — every question is worth attempting. To qualify, a candidate needs 25% in each section and 40% in aggregate; SC/ST candidates are exempt from the minimum in the existing Sainik Schools, though in New Sainik Schools all categories must meet it.

**4. Fee and documents.** The fee is **Rs. 850** for General, OBC (NCL) and wards of defence personnel and ex-servicemen, and **Rs. 700** for SC/ST candidates, payable online. Keep ready: a recent photograph (JPG, 10–200 KB, taken on or after 1 July 2026), signature and left-thumb impression (JPG, 10–50 KB each), and the date-of-birth, domicile and category certificates as PDFs (50–300 KB). Defence wards need the service certificate or PPO.

**5. Four exam cities, in order.** The form asks you to pick four exam cities in order of preference. Choose with care — it is one of the hardest things to change later. The city intimation slip comes on **11 January 2027** and the admit card on **25 January 2027**.

Apply only at **exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society** — the official NTA portal. And a word of caution from the Education Times report: some third-party websites are currently listing a lower fee (Rs. 800 / Rs. 650). The bulletin says Rs. 850 / Rs. 700. Trust the bulletin.

Twenty days is enough for a Class 9 application — if this week is the week the documents get ready. The families who struggle are rarely the ones who started late. They are the ones who started on the last day.

## FAQs

**What is the last date to apply for AISSEE 2027 Class 9?**

2 December 2026, up to 11:50 PM. Applications are accepted only online at exams.nta.nic.in/sainik-school-society. A late window runs from 3 to 6 December with an extra fee of Rs. 500.

**What is the age limit for AISSEE 2027 Class 9?**

13 to 15 years as on 31 March 2027 — born between 1 April 2012 and 31 March 2014. There is no age relaxation for any category.

**Does a Class 9 applicant need to have passed Class 8?**

Yes. The candidate must have passed the Class 8 examination from a recognised school by the time of admission.

**Is the AISSEE Class 9 paper in Hindi or English?**

English only. Class 9 has five sections — Mathematics, Intelligence, English, General Science and Social Science — for 400 marks in 180 minutes, with no negative marking.
